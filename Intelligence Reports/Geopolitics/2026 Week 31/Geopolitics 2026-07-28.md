## Story 2: Houthi strikes force Aramco to suspend operations at Abqaiq, the most systemically important node in global oil supply

**Source:** Middle East Eye (regional) | https://www.middleeasteye.net/live-blog/live-blog-update/saudi-arabia-halt-operations-major-oil-facility-after-houthi-attack ; Washington Post (Western) | https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/07/25/houthis-claim-attack-saudi-oil-refinery-iran-war-widens-red-sea/
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Ventures]], [[Comparison Network]]

Saudi Aramco has suspended operations at Abqaiq, its largest crude processing and stabilisation facility, after a Houthi strike — the same facility whose 2019 drone attack briefly knocked out roughly half of Saudi output and moved oil markets sharply. This follows Houthi strikes on Jizan and Yanbu earlier in the week that opened a new Red Sea/Saudi front in the wider Iran war, with Houthi leader Abdul Malik al-Houthi threatening all Saudi oil facilities if Riyadh escalates its own involvement. The Abqaiq suspension itself is the new, today-dated escalation beyond the earlier strikes.

This is arguably a bigger near-term oil-price risk than the Hormuz transit dispute this report has been tracking for weeks — Abqaiq is a single point of failure for global crude supply in a way Hormuz, a chokepoint rather than a facility, is not. Direct relevance to the property/mortgage risk premium the comparison network prices in: watch this over the next 24-48 hours ahead of any Hormuz talks conclusion.
