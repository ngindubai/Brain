## Story 1: UN fact-finding mission finds "reasonable grounds" the US committed war crimes in Iran strikes that killed 178 civilians

**Source:** UN News | https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/09/1168358 | RT | https://www.rt.com/news/645915-iran-us-war-crimes/

Independent investigators appointed by the UN Human Rights Council reported today that they found reasonable grounds to believe the US committed war crimes in two strikes during the Iran conflict: an attack on a school in Minab that killed roughly 150 people, including around 120 schoolchildren, which the investigators found was a "clearly identifiable civilian object" with no evidence of military use; and a missile strike that dispersed tungsten pellets over a residential/sports complex area in Lamerd. Combined, the two strikes are cited as responsible for at least 178 civilian deaths. The same report, in the same document, found that Iran's own government committed crimes against humanity — murder, imprisonment and torture — in crushing the 2025-26 domestic protest movement, which matters for reading this as an evidentiary document rather than a one-sided political instrument. RT's coverage of the US finding was close to the UN's own language rather than visibly spun, which is itself worth noting: Moscow's state media needs little embellishment to make use of an international body accusing Washington of war crimes. These findings are not a court judgment — they are not binding and create no immediate legal consequence — but they are the kind of evidentiary record that feeds future accountability proceedings (ICC referrals, domestic prosecutions in third countries, sanctions triggers) and will likely be cited by critics of the war regardless of what happens on the ceasefire track.
**Relevance:** Indirect — no immediate oil or Hormuz-transit effect, but a genuine reputational and legal cost accumulating against the US side of the conflict, worth watching for how it affects Washington's leverage in the diplomacy discussed in Story 3 below.

## Story 2: Gulf security architecture hardens — $24.3bn F-35 sale to Saudi Arabia cleared, Pakistan says time has come to implement the Mecca defence pact

**Source:** Al-Monitor | https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2026/09/trump-administration-approves-24b-sale-f-35-jets-saudi-arabia | Breaking Defense | https://breakingdefense.com/2026/09/us-clears-24b-f-35-sale-for-saudi-arabia/ | Middle East Eye | https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/pakistan-says-time-has-come-implement-mecca-defence-pact

Two distinct but related developments landed together. First, the US State Department formally cleared a possible $24.3bn sale of 48 F-35 Lightning II jets plus 49 F135 engines to Saudi Arabia — the approval Trump first floated in November 2025 ahead of a White House meeting with Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, now cleared through the formal congressional-notification process, though lawmakers retain a window to block it. Second, and more immediately operational, Pakistan's defence minister said today that "the time has come" to actually implement the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement — the NATO Article 5-style pact binding Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkey since August — as Houthi attacks on the kingdom intensify, with Pakistan reaffirming its commitment to defend Saudi Arabia and the holy cities. Read together, these are two different timelines of the same signal: the F-35 sale is a multi-year modernisation program that does nothing for the current Houthi fight, while the Mecca pact activation talk is about immediate manpower and deterrence. Neither is a done deal — the F-35 sale can still be blocked by Congress, and "time has come to implement" is a political statement, not a deployment order — but both point at Gulf states and their partners betting on hard security rather than diplomacy to manage the Houthi/Yemen front, even as the separate Iran-US diplomatic track (Story 3) continues in parallel.
**Relevance:** Direct — a durable shift toward militarisation on the Saudi side of the conflict this network's Hormuz/oil exposure depends on; worth tracking whether Congress moves to block the F-35 sale and whether Pakistan's rhetoric translates into any actual force posture change.

## Story 3: Iran's diplomatic track shifts toward Beijing as Araghchi and Pakistan's Munir talk again, with China positioning itself as a mediator

**Source:** Asal Media (Iran-adjacent outlet, read for framing) | https://www.asalmedia.com/iran-fm-calls-pakistan-army-chief-china-mediation/ | cross-referenced against yesterday's Al Jazeera coverage of Trump's "direct contact" claim

While in Beijing, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi held a call with Pakistan's Field Marshal Asim Munir to discuss de-escalation, continuing the Pakistan-mediated channel this brief has tracked since August — but the venue is the new detail: Araghchi making this call from Beijing, rather than Tehran, alongside China's Wang Yi telling Beijing talks that China supports Iran while cautioning against further Yemen escalation (carried forward from Wednesday), suggests China is angling for a visible role in the mediation architecture rather than staying on the sidelines. This sits directly next to yesterday's still-unconfirmed story: Trump's claim of "direct" US-Tehran contact, which Iran has not confirmed. Today's Beijing-routed diplomacy is a different channel entirely — Pakistan-mediated, not a US-Iran direct line — and its emergence the same week China is hosting Araghchi is a plausible signal that Tehran is diversifying its diplomatic options rather than moving toward the US-only track Washington has been signalling. No breakthrough or new proposal was reported; this is a channel confirmation, not a deal.
**Relevance:** Direct — any genuine diplomatic movement on Hormuz reopening is the single biggest lever on oil pricing and the Gulf risk premium this network is exposed to; a China-routed channel, if it produces anything, would be a materially different outcome than a US-brokered one for how sanctions and reconstruction get structured afterward.
