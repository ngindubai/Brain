## Story 1: Trump says US is "hopefully toward the end" of the Iran war, claims direct Tehran contact; Iran does not confirm it

**Source:** Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/world/mena/trump-says-us-hopefully-toward-the-end-of-war-with-iran-claims-direct-talks-as-tehran-stresses-caution-1.500677545

Trump told reporters today that Washington has been in "direct" contact with Iranian officials and that the US is "hopefully toward the end" of the war, a more specific and more optimistic claim than his prior line that the conflict would end "around the midterms." Tehran's public position, as reported alongside the US account, is markedly more cautious: Iranian officials have repeatedly declined to confirm the "direct" characterisation and continue to insist any process must protect Iranian sovereignty over Hormuz management and the disposition of frozen funds, the same two issues that broke the June memorandum of understanding's 60-day deadline back on 17 August. The gap between Trump's framing (progress, direct channel, near-term resolution) and Iran's framing (caution, no confirmed direct channel, unresolved core disputes) is the story: Washington has an incentive to signal progress ahead of the midterms, given only 31% of Americans support the war per last month's polling; Tehran has an incentive to avoid looking like it capitulated while Hormuz remains its one point of real leverage. No new IRGC or Press TV statement responding to today's Trump comments was found in a defensible search; the silence from Tehran's state media on this specific claim is itself notable given how quickly Press TV normally rebuts US "progress" narratives.

**Relevance:** Direct — this is the single biggest lever on the oil price and Hormuz risk premium this network is exposed to. A genuine de-escalation would ease the barrel price and the regional risk premium underpinning Gulf property and mortgage demand; today's claim is unconfirmed by the other side and should not be treated as one.

## Story 2: Israel and Morocco agree to upgrade ties, open full embassies

**Source:** Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/16/israel-and-morocco-agree-to-open-embassies-upgrading-diplomatic-ties | Morocco World News | https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/09/338583/israel-says-it-agrees-with-morocco-to-upgrade-ties-appoint-ambassadors/

Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar and Moroccan counterpart Nasser Bourita agreed at a US-brokered trilateral summit in New York, hosted by US Ambassador to the UN Mike Waltz, to raise their liaison offices (open since 2021 but never upgraded despite a 2021 pledge) to full embassies with appointed ambassadors, plus investment-protection and double-taxation agreements to be finalised by year end and expanded flights between the two countries. Al Jazeera's framing notes this sits inside the broader Abraham Accords architecture and follows years of the upgrade being promised and not delivered; US conservative outlets (Breitbart) frame it as reaffirming accords momentum, while Al Jazeera's own coverage is more measured, noting the gap between past pledges and actual delivery without editorialising further. Rabat has domestic sensitivities here, given public opinion on Gaza, that make the flight and trade provisions more politically deliverable in the near term than the embassy opening itself.

**Relevance:** Indirect — no immediate Gulf oil or property channel, but it is a genuine widening of the Abraham Accords footprint at a moment when the wider region is absorbing an active Iran-US war and Houthi-Saudi exchange; worth watching as a signal that Gulf-adjacent normalisation tracks continue to move independently of the Iran conflict.
**Related vault notes:** None.

## Story 3: Congress sends Trump a sweeping Russia/Iran sanctions bill naming China and India as targets

**Source:** NBC News | https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/house-passes-russia-sanctions-bill-named-lindsey-graham-sending-trump-rcna598124 | CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/russia-sanctions-bill-lindsey-graham-congress.html

The House passed the "Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026" 262-159 on 16 September, following the Senate's 86-11 vote last month; a White House official says Trump intends to sign it. The bill lets the president impose tariffs on the largest buyers of Russian oil and gas, a category that explicitly includes China and India, to cut the revenue funding Russia's war on Ukraine. US wire coverage (NBC, CNBC) presents this as bipartisan momentum honouring the late Senator Graham; The Hill and CBS coverage flag more pointedly that the tariff-on-third-countries mechanism could hit US allies and partners' own trade relationships with China and India as collateral damage, a tension the bill's sponsors have not resolved. No Chinese or Indian government statement on the bill's passage was found dated today; both governments' predictable response, given the pattern on prior sanctions rounds, is likely to be rejection of extraterritorial tariff authority rather than compliance, but that is inference, not a confirmed reaction.

**Relevance:** Indirect — a genuine escalation in the sanctions and critical-minerals contest between Washington and Beijing/Delhi, worth tracking for how China responds given its parked rare-earth export-control lever; no direct Gulf channel today.
