## Story 1: Hormuz transit collapses to roughly a sixth of normal — the chokepoint stops being a risk premium and becomes an actual supply cut
**Source:** War on the Rocks (via search) | https://warontherocks.com/global-risks-heating-up/ ; NPR | https://www.npr.org/2026/07/24/g-s1-135417/us-iran-war-updates ; CENTCOM | https://www.centcom.mil/MEDIA/PUBLIC-RELEASES/Article/4554310/us-concludes-13th-night-of-strikes-on-iranian-military-targets/

Only six ships transited the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday, down from 15 the day before, against a pre-war baseline of roughly 88 vessels a day — commercial transit is running under a fifth of normal. CENTCOM confirmed a 13th consecutive night of strikes on Iranian military targets as of 23 July (command centres, drone storage, coastal surveillance, maritime capability), with more than 50,000 US personnel now deployed across the theatre, and NPR frames the conflict as nearing its fifth month with Trump reportedly weighing a further escalation. Separately, the Houthis have resumed attacks on Saudi tankers in the Red Sea, reopening the second chokepoint threat flagged in Wednesday's reporting. The UK and US are reportedly planning a London meeting next week on an international coalition to protect Gulf shipping — the first concrete sign of a multilateral response to the transit collapse rather than unilateral US strikes.

This is the one slot this report gives the Iran war's daily detail — yesterday's report covered the oil-price mechanics of the $100 Brent breach in full; today's genuinely new fact is that the shipping data has deteriorated well past what the price alone was signalling. ## Story 2: Russian missile strike kills at least 21 as Kyiv opens a criminal probe into its own defence-event security
**Source:** Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/24/at-least-11-killed-in-ukraine-as-moscow-and-kyiv-continue-to-trade-attacks

A Russian missile hit a Ukrainian defence-industry event in the Kyiv region, killing 10, while a separate strike near Sloviansk killed 5 more from aviation bombs; Ukraine's own strikes on Russia's Kirov region killed 6 and wounded 26, and Ukrainian drones hit Wildberries warehouses in Saint Petersburg, Leningrad region and occupied Crimea. Russia's defence ministry claims it intercepted 571 Ukrainian drones overnight across more than a dozen regions — a scale of exchange notably heavier than the diplomatic-focused reporting earlier in the week. The most striking detail: Ukraine's own prosecutor general has opened a criminal investigation into who authorised holding the defence event at all and what risk assessment was done under martial law — a rare instance of Kyiv publicly scrutinising its own decision-making rather than only Russia's targeting.

Zelenskyy separately warned that Russia is preparing a "massive" missile attack within 48 hours and a "significant" mobilisation wave for autumn — a claim from one side, not independently verified, but consistent with the intensity of today's exchange.

## Story 3: White House confirms Trump will host both Zelenskyy and Netanyahu on the same Tuesday
**Source:** Kyiv Post (via search) | https://www.kyivpost.com/post/81011 ; Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/24/netanyahu-to-meet-trump-at-white-house-next-week-amid-iran-war-escalation

The White House confirmed Zelenskyy will meet Trump in Washington on Tuesday, 28 July, alongside a separately-scheduled Netanyahu visit the same day — Netanyahu's seventh Oval Office meeting since Trump's return to office, more than with any other foreign leader. Both meetings coincide with a memorial service for the late Senator Lindsey Graham. On Ukraine, US and Ukrainian officials have reportedly been discussing a proposed air ceasefire to put to Russia as part of a fresh peace push, following Friday's inconclusive Lavrov-Rubio meeting in Manila. On the Israel side, Netanyahu's visit is billed to cover both Gaza and Iran; UN agencies estimate 1.4 million people (67% of Gaza's population) remain in crisis-or-worse acute food insecurity, and local health authorities put the war's Palestinian death toll at over 73,000.

This is a genuinely new diplomatic development, not a continuation of yesterday's Manila non-breakthrough — it's the first concrete follow-up scheduled since that meeting, and worth watching as the week's actual decision point on Ukraine.

## Story 4: China and Philippines clash a third time this week at Scarborough Shoal; Rubio calls Beijing's actions "disturbing"
**Source:** Bloomberg (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/china-coast-guard-reports-third-clash-with-philippines-this-week ; South China Morning Post | https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3361723/south-china-sea-beijing-and-manila-trade-blame-second-day-scarborough-shoal-clashes ; CNN | https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/24/asia/china-philippines-south-china-sea-scarborough-intl-hnk

A Chinese vessel closed to within seven metres of a Philippine fisheries boat at Scarborough Shoal on Friday — the third clash of the week following Monday's baton incident at Second Thomas Shoal and Wednesday's water-cannon incident — with a CNN crew aboard the Philippine vessel for this latest encounter. The clashes came as ASEAN foreign ministers wrapped their Manila meetings, attended by both China's Wang Yi and US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who publicly called Beijing's South China Sea conduct "disturbing" and reaffirmed the US-Philippines defence treaty. Triangulation divergence: Manila's coast guard calls the pattern harassment of lawful resupply and fishing activity; Beijing says it used "control measures" against vessels that "ignored repeated warnings" in Chinese-claimed waters.

Three incidents in one week, timed around a regional diplomatic forum, reads as a deliberate pressure campaign rather than incidental friction — worth watching whether a fourth incident follows before the week is out.

## Story 5: Trump's new tariff wave hits 80+ countries including Taiwan, the EU, Japan and South Korea
**Source:** Irish Times | https://www.irishtimes.com/world/us/2026/07/24/trump-imposes-sweeping-round-of-fresh-tariffs-on-more-than-80-countries/ ; TIME | https://time.com/article/2026/07/24/trump-tariffs-forced-labor-explainer/

A fresh wave of US tariffs, ranging 10-12.5%, took effect Friday on more than 80 countries, replacing a global 10% tariff set in February that expired at midnight. The stated rationale is forced-labour import enforcement: countries that have committed to adopt and enforce forced-labour import prohibitions (Canada, the EU, India, Mexico, the UK) face the lower 10% rate, while non-exempt goods from the EU, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan get an additional charge bringing the total to 10-12.5%. Taiwan's inclusion lands the same week as its own domestic news cycle (a state-sponsored birthrate savings scheme, a whole-of-society defence airsoft competition) and alongside continued Czech-Taiwan diplomatic manoeuvring after a Czech official signalled no further official Taiwan visits following Beijing meetings.

Relevance lens: this is a genuine cross-cutting economic story — a trade-policy move layered directly on top of an already-tense Taiwan Strait/South China Sea dynamic (Story 4), worth tracking for how it interacts with any critical-minerals or semiconductor supply-chain exposure.

## Story 6: DRC's Ebola outbreak passes 1,000 deaths — fastest an Ebola epidemic has ever reached that toll
**Source:** Medical Daily | https://www.medicaldaily.com/drc-ebola-death-toll-1000-bundibugyo-fastest-outbreak-2026-476321 ; NPR | https://www.npr.org/2026/07/20/g-s1-134548/ebola-congo-deaths ; UN News | https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/07/1167983

As of 23 July, DRC health authorities report 1,033 deaths and 2,536 confirmed cases from the Bundibugyo-strain outbreak declared 15 May in Ituri province — a genuinely new milestone since yesterday's report cited the 15 July WHO count of 828 deaths. The outbreak passed 1,000 confirmed cases within 40 days of response activation, versus roughly 235 days for the comparable 2018 North Kivu outbreak to hit the same mark — the fastest-moving Ebola epidemic on record. No approved vaccine or treatment exists for the rarer Bundibugyo strain, leaving case isolation and contact tracing as the only tools, while the same instability, displacement and militia conflict driving the region's security crisis is actively hampering the response. WHO separately notes some signs of stabilisation even as the outbreak continues expanding geographically.

Spanish hotel group Meliá formally ended all Cuban operations on 24 July, its Portuguese subsidiary ceasing services across the remaining properties in a 34-hotel, 14,000-room portfolio. The company cites persistent operational, legal and financial difficulty doing business on the island, tracing back to Executive Order 14404 (signed 1 May 2026) targeting the GAESA military-linked tourism conglomerate. This closes out a process that began 3 June with the exit from 15 GAESA-linked properties. A concrete, dated example of US sanctions pressure materially reshaping a foreign company's investment decision rather than remaining rhetorical.
