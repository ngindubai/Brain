## Story 1: US pauses Iran strikes after 13 nights as Oman-brokered Hormuz talks advance — first de-escalatory signal since the war restarted
**Source:** Axios via Iran International | https://www.iranintl.com/en/202607250090 ; South China Morning Post | https://www.scmp.com/news/world/middle-east/article/3361857/us-pauses-nightly-iran-strikes-houthis-clash-saudis ; CBS News | https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-us-trump-strait-hormuz-middle-east/

After CENTCOM confirmed a 13th consecutive night of strikes on 23 July, no new strikes were reported overnight Friday into Saturday — the first quiet night in two weeks. A Pentagon source told CNN operations are "on hold," not terminated, and it's genuinely unclear whether this is a real off-ramp or a pause before renewed escalation. What's driving it: Trump told reporters Friday the US and Iran "are talking" and that Tehran is "getting more and more serious," and two regional sources told CBS that Omani officials travelled to Tehran the same day and made real progress on reopening the Strait of Hormuz and clarifying territorial-water protocols — the timing of that outreach and the strike pause reads as connected rather than coincidental. Muddying the picture: the Houthis attacked a Saudi-flagged tanker in the Red Sea the same day, unclaimed as of this report, and Israeli officials are reportedly sceptical, believing Trump will be forced back into escalation soon regardless.

This is the single most consequential geopolitical fact for Orwell today — not because it resolves anything, but because it's the first crack in a two-week trajectory that had only moved one direction. See [[Markets]] for the oil-price read-through.

## Story 2: Russian drone strike on a Sea of Azov holiday camp kills 12 including 5 children; Ukrainian strike kills 3 in Sumy
**Source:** Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/25/at-least-11-killed-in-ukrainian-and-russian-strikes-as-holiday-camp-hit

A coordinated drone strike hit a resort area in Russian-occupied Kyrylivka on the Sea of Azov coast on 25 July, killing 12 people including 5 children and wounding 19 — Russian-appointed governor Yevgeny Balitsky called it a deliberate Ukrainian strike on civilians, which Kyiv has not directly addressed. Separately, a Ukrainian strike in Sumy region killed 3 drivers for courier service Nova Poshta and set a building ablaze; Ukraine says the target was a civilian courier facility, while Moscow's defence ministry insists it was a drone storage and launch site — a direct factual contradiction neither side has resolved. This follows Zelenskyy's Friday warning, unverified independently, of an imminent "massive" Russian missile attack and an autumn mobilisation wave, and comes on top of Friday's separate strike on a Kyiv defence-industry event that killed 15+ (covered in yesterday's report).

Triangulation note: this is now the second consecutive day of high-casualty exchanges with directly contradictory target claims from both sides — a pattern worth watching for whether it's random wartime confusion or a deliberate framing contest ahead of Tuesday's Trump-Zelenskyy meeting.

## Story 3: Israeli strike kills Gaza's top Hamas-led police official; civil defence says 40 killed Saturday territory-wide
**Source:** Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/25/head-of-hamas-led-police-in-northern-gaza-killed-by-israeli-strike ; Times of Israel | https://www.timesofisrael.com/head-of-hamass-northern-gaza-police-killed-in-israeli-strike/

An Israeli strike on Saturday killed Brigadier-General Abdel-Nasser Al-Maqadma, head of the Hamas-led police force and de facto governor of northern Gaza, in the Sheikh Radwan area of Gaza City — the IDF says several "October 7 terrorists" were eliminated in the strike. Gaza's civil defence agency put Saturday's total dead across the territory at 40, from a mix of airstrikes, drone strikes and gunfire during aid-distribution operations; separately, Israeli forces detained more than 70 suspects following deadly West Bank violence. This continues a pattern of near-daily lethal incidents despite the October 2025 ceasefire technically remaining in effect — Gaza's Government Media Office had already logged 3,689 recorded ceasefire violations and over 1,100 deaths since the truce began, before today's toll.

Sets up as the backdrop to Tuesday's scheduled Trump-Netanyahu meeting in Washington, which is billed to cover both Gaza and Iran.

## Story 4: FBI director meets China's top police official in Beijing as Washington and Beijing both signal restraint ahead of September's Trump-Xi summit
**Source:** South China Morning Post | https://www.scmp.com/news/us/article/3361811/fbi-director-meets-chinas-public-security-chief-beijing-crime-crackdown ; Bloomberg (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/china-set-to-look-past-trump-s-new-tariffs-before-xi-visit-to-us

FBI Director Kash Patel met China's state councillor and public security minister Wang Xiaohong in Beijing on Friday — a previously unreported trip that drew domestic criticism over his private jet usage but produced concrete commitments on fentanyl-precursor interdiction, anti-fraud cooperation, and a joint fugitive task force, following two recent US-China fugitive repatriations despite the two countries having no extradition treaty. Separately, Bloomberg reports Beijing is deliberately choosing to look past Friday's new 80-country Trump tariff wave (which hit China at 12.5%) rather than retaliate, prioritising the runway to a Trump-Xi summit expected in Washington in late September. Both developments point the same direction: both governments are visibly managing friction rather than escalating it, in contrast to the sharper rhetoric around Scarborough Shoal this week (Story 5, open loop).

Triangulation note: Xinhua's framing of the Patel meeting emphasises cooperative language ("deepen cooperation") with no mention of broader US-China friction — a notably softer register than Beijing's water-cannon rhetoric toward the Philippines the same week, suggesting Beijing is compartmentalising the US relationship from its regional disputes rather than letting one bleed into the other.

## Story 5: UN General Assembly hands Volker Türk a rare second term as UN rights chief, over open US, Israeli and Russian objection
**Source:** Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/25/how-volker-turk-won-a-new-term-as-un-rights-chief-despite-israel-opposition ; US News (AP wire) | https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-07-24/un-human-rights-chief-faces-opposition-in-vote-to-extend-his-term-for-another-four-years

The UN General Assembly voted 144-10 (13 abstentions) on Friday to grant Volker Türk a second four-year term as UN human rights chief, defeating both a US proposal to delay the vote and a Russian bid to shorten the new term to end-of-year only. Türk becomes the first person to serve two full terms in the role since it was created in 1993. His outspoken criticism of Israel's conduct in Gaza and Lebanon, Russia's conduct in Ukraine, and unspecified US human-rights concerns put him at odds with all three opposing states — a genuinely rare instance of Washington, Moscow and Tel Aviv aligned against the rest of the General Assembly.

Cross-cutting significance: the 144-10 margin is a clean read on where the UN's broader membership sits relative to the three powers most engaged in the week's other major stories (Iran, Ukraine, Gaza) — worth watching whether any of the three actually reduces cooperation with Türk's office in response.

## Story 6: Venezuela's interim government withdraws from the International Criminal Court
**Source:** France24 | https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20260724-venezuela-to-withdraw-from-international-criminal-court ; Xinhua | https://english.news.cn/20260725/863bfcac5d734b3e95b97e11cafbe741/c.html

Venezuela's Foreign Minister Félix Plasencia announced on 25 July that Caracas has formally notified the UN of its "firm and irrevocable" withdrawal from the ICC, calling the court's work geographically biased against Africa and Latin America. The move comes under interim leader Delcy Rodríguez, who took over after US forces ousted Maduro in January, and directly follows an ongoing ICC investigation into alleged crimes against humanity under the former Maduro government. Withdrawal takes a year to formally take effect under ICC rules, meaning the current investigation continues in the meantime.

Notable for the optics: a US-aligned interim government pulling out of the same court whose Venezuela investigation targets the ousted regime it replaced — worth watching whether this is read regionally as shielding the new government from future scrutiny rather than closing the book on the old one.

## Story 7: DR Congo's Ebola death toll jumps more than 40% in five days — now the fastest-spreading Ebola outbreak ever recorded
**Source:** Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/25/ebola-deaths-in-drc-surge-past-1300-as-virus-spreading-like-a-wildfire

DRC's Bundibugyo-strain Ebola outbreak has surged to 1,354 confirmed deaths and 3,075 cases as of Saturday — up from 1,033 deaths reported just five days earlier in Wednesday's report, a genuinely alarming acceleration rather than a steady climb. The outbreak took roughly two months to reach 2,000 confirmed cases, versus ten months for the comparable 2018-19 North Kivu outbreak to hit the same mark, making this unambiguously the fastest-moving Ebola epidemic on record. More than 100 health workers have been infected and 35 have died since the outbreak was declared in May; healthcare staff at several Ituri facilities have now gone on strike over unpaid wages and unsafe conditions, actively degrading the response at the exact moment it needs to scale up. No approved vaccine or treatment exists for the rarer Bundibugyo strain.
