## Story 1: BRICS summit opens in Delhi, Iran pushes de-dollarisation as Modi juggles Moscow and Beijing

**Source:** Business Today | https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/brics-summit-2026-delhi-traffic-advisory-for-september-12-roads-to-avoid-diversions-alternatives-555025-2026-09-12 ; Outlook India | https://www.outlookindia.com/national/brics-summit-2026-live-updates-delhi-pm-modi-xi-jinping-vladimir-putin-latest-news ; Business Today (Pezeshkian) | https://www.businesstoday.in/world/story/irans-pezeshkian-pitches-brics-financial-architecture-to-accelerate-de-dollarisation-554925-2026-09-11 ; The Week | https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2026/09/12/xi-jinping-modi-india-brics-summit-india-china-relations.html

The 18th BRICS summit opened today at Bharat Mandapam, India's fourth time chairing the bloc, with 13 heads of state including Xi Jinping, Vladimir Putin and Iran's Masoud Pezeshkian in attendance. Putin arrived Friday for bilateral talks with Modi; the day's marquee event is a 5:45pm Modi-Xi meeting, their first in seven years, built on recent LAC patrolling arrangements and resumed direct flights, a real, if narrow, thaw in India-China relations that the Western press has under-covered relative to its size. Pezeshkian used his platform to press BRICS partners toward local-currency trade and leaning harder on the New Development Bank, explicit framing that sanctions exposure is now a structural risk for any economy transacting in dollars. The divergence to flag: Indian outlets cast today as evidence of Delhi's "strategic autonomy" and multi-alignment (India sits in BRICS, Quad, G20 and the SCO simultaneously, a hedging posture unique among major powers); the framing that will come from Washington, if precedent from mid-2025 holds, treats BRICS de-dollarisation rhetoric as anti-American and grounds for tariff retaliation. No New Delhi Declaration has been issued yet, that lands tomorrow, so today is positioning, not outcomes. Worth noting for the AI-tech reader: this summit is unfolding on the same day the Anthropic-Houthi story (Story 3) is dominating adjacent headlines, an uncomfortable juxtaposition between "Global South cooperation" messaging and one BRICS-adjacent state's documented weapons misuse of a Western AI model.

**Relevance:** Indirect. Currency and sanctions-architecture trends here shape the dollar-pegged dirham's operating environment over years, not days, but a serious de-dollarisation push is a standing item for the Comparison Network's macro backdrop.

## Story 2: Houthis complete Red Sea coast takeover, Saudi shuts Hormuz-bypass pipeline, Trump declines to strike

**Source:** Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/11/can-the-houthis-close-the-red-sea-after-seizing-the-yemen-coast ; NBC News | https://www.nbcnews.com/world/middle-east/houthis-control-bab-el-mandeb-yemen-red-sea-saudi-iran-war-oil-rcna597186 ; Middle East Eye | https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/saudi-arabia-says-it-closed-vital-east-west-pipeline-after-attacks ; Bloomberg | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-11/saudi-oil-pipeline-that-bypasses-hormuz-shut-after-attacks ; CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/saudi-arabia-shut-down-east-west-crude-oil-pipeline.html

The Iran-backed Houthis have finished a week-long offensive that gives them full control of Yemen's entire Red Sea coastline, including Perim Island and the Bab al-Mandab gateway, the most significant Yemen-war shift since the US-Israel-Iran war opened in February. The follow-through hit home on 10-11 September: drones launched from Iraq struck Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline (7 million bpd capacity, the kingdom's only crude export route that bypasses Hormuz entirely, running to Red Sea terminals at Yanbu), forcing Riyadh to shut it as a precaution after fires and injuries. Saudi Arabia is deliberately not retaliating against Baghdad, giving the Iraqi government "an opportunity to take the necessary measures," a sign Riyadh wants to avoid a second front. Separately, MBS called Trump twice on 10 September asking for US strikes on the Houthis; Trump refused, with officials saying Washington does not intend to intervene directly. That refusal is the story within the story: with Hormuz itself already down to a handful of transits a day against a normal roughly 85, the US bypass valve now half-closed too, and Washington choosing restraint over escalation, the market is pricing genuine supply risk rather than just headline risk. Framing divergence: Gulf/Saudi-aligned coverage treats this as unprovoked Iranian-proxy aggression against energy infrastructure; Al Jazeera's analysis is more circumspect about whether the Houthis can actually convert coastal control into a Red Sea chokehold given naval limitations. Brent was trading around $108-109 and WTI near $104 into today, a four-month high, with the pipeline closure now stacked on top of the Hormuz premium rather than replacing it. See today's [[Markets]] report for Friday's actual close, where oil pulled back slightly on separate Iran-Gulf talk news, a genuinely different and more hopeful signal than this pipeline story, both are live at once.

**Relevance:** Direct. This is the single most consequential Gulf-energy story of the week for the UAE lens: a Hormuz-bypass route is now down at the same time the strait itself is effectively closed to commercial transit.

## Story 3: Anthropic confirms Yemen-based cell used Claude to develop guided missiles

**Source:** Bloomberg | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-11/anthropic-says-yemen-group-used-claude-in-missile-development ; Middle East Eye | https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/houthis-used-ai-platform-claude-develop-ballistic-missiles ; Dawn | https://www.dawn.com/news/2029255/group-in-yemen-used-claude-ai-for-missile-projects-anthropic-says ; Arab News | https://www.arabnews.com/middle-east/yemens-houthis-used-claude-ai-to-build-guided-weapons-3001380

Anthropic disclosed it identified a northern-Yemen-based cell, not formally named as the Houthis but matching their territory, running three parallel weapons programmes: a guided rocket using a phone-class flight computer with terminal homing, a multi-stage ballistic missile targeting a range above 2,000km, and an "R2000" programme including a hypersonic glide vehicle variant. The cell used Claude Code in place of human software engineers, running multiple instances in parallel roles "akin to how a lead would delegate work on a small engineering team," to write guidance and control software, build firmware, and run flight simulations, while actively trying to conceal intent from the model. Anthropic says a live rocket test failed, and it has no evidence any weapon was successfully fielded; it banned the accounts on detection and disclosed the same report also covered a foiled bio-weapons attempt and a suspected Russia-linked cyber-espionage campaign against Ukraine. The divergence worth flagging: this is Anthropic's own disclosure, so the "we caught it" framing is self-serving by design even where the underlying detection appears genuine, no independent verification of the technical claims exists yet, and coverage from TRT World and Al-Monitor is notably more sceptical about how much capability uplift Claude actually provided versus the group's existing know-how. For Orwell specifically: this is the first documented case of a Yemen-based armed group treated as a "small engineering team" via a commercial LLM, and it is a live signal that agentic AI misuse detection and account-banning are now operational realities, not theoretical safety-team exercises. Worth a note for anyone pitching AI-agent capability to a nervous enterprise client, expect this story to surface in procurement conversations.

**Relevance:** Direct. Orwell's entire build stack runs on Claude; this is a live case study in AI-agent misuse detection and the reputational and regulatory environment the AI tooling Gareth depends on now sits inside.

## Story 4: Putin says European troops in Ukraine would mean "direct war" with Russia

**Source:** The Hill | https://thehill.com/policy/international/6045983-pence-zelensky-ukraine-defense/ (search aggregation of wire coverage, Reuters/AP direct fetch blocked, corroborated via multiple outlets)

Putin issued a direct warning to European capitals on Friday, 11 September, that deploying European troops into Ukraine, under any "reassurance force" or peacekeeping banner, would constitute direct war with Russia. This lands as European governments continue quiet planning around post-ceasefire security guarantees for Kyiv, and it is a deliberate attempt to pre-empt that planning before it hardens into policy. On the ground, Russian drone and missile strikes continued against Ukrainian cities, with a Zaporizhzhia residential strike killing one woman, a continuation of, not an escalation past, the pattern from earlier in the week (Kyiv region strikes, the Holosiivskyi toll). The divergence: Russian state framing (TASS) presents this as a defensive redline against NATO creep; Kyiv and most European governments read it as an attempt to veto sovereign European decisions about their own troop deployments on Ukrainian soil, which Russia has no legal standing to do. No European government had responded on the record to today's specific threat as of writing.

**Relevance:** Indirect. A rhetorical escalation, not yet a battlefield one; matters more for European risk sentiment than Gulf/UAE exposure.

## Story 6: UN Security Council renews Sudan sanctions for one month only, as US-Russia-China split hardens

**Source:** UN Press | https://press.un.org/en/2026/sc16450.doc.htm ; Security Council Report | https://www.securitycouncilreport.org/whatsinblue/2025/09/sudan-vote-on-a-draft-resolution-extending-the-sanctions-regime-2.php

The Security Council unanimously adopted Resolution 2828 on 11 September, a bare technical rollover extending Sudan's 1591 sanctions regime and arms embargo by just one month (rather than the usual year), with the Panel of Experts mandate extended only to 9 November. Unanimity masks real division: the US, via envoy Massad Boulos, pushed to expand the existing Darfur-only arms embargo to cover all of Sudan; Russia and China blocked it, with Russia warning it would "not allow even a hint of broadening" and China objecting to "forced adoption of texts that are clearly contentious." The one-month rather than one-year renewal is itself the signal, it buys time for a fight neither side has yet won, rather than resolving it. Meanwhile the underlying crisis is unchanged and grim: 11.8 million displaced, 24.6 million facing acute hunger, RSF drone strikes continuing on El Obeid and other cities. This is a genuine update to the standing Sudan open loop, not a re-tread.

**Relevance:** Indirect. No direct portfolio exposure, tracked for Global South and middle-power realignment pattern.
