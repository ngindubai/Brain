## Story 1: Iran and Oman put numbers on Hormuz — a revenue-sharing deal and a seven-mile corridor

**Source:** Bloomberg / gcaptain.com | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/iran-oman-agree-to-share-strait-of-hormuz-revenue-irgc-says | https://gcaptain.com/iran-oman-agree-to-share-strait-of-hormuz-revenue/
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]] · [[Comparison Network]] · [[Priorities]]

Yesterday's report flagged the "interim framework" language from Tuesday's Araghchi-Albusaidi joint statement as a genuine but mechanically thin first step — no coordinates, no fees mentioned. That gap closed on Wednesday evening: Iran's IRGC confirmed an agreement on each country's share of the strait's waters and on splitting the revenue from managing them, and Iran's deputy foreign minister for legal affairs, Kazem Gharibabadi, detailed the structure — a seven-mile-wide transit corridor, inbound traffic through Iranian territorial waters, outbound through Omani waters, military vessels excluded from the arrangement. Coordinated mine-clearance is slated to run over the following 30-60 days.

Tehran is still not calling this a reopening. Iranian officials continue to insist that normalising traffic requires Washington to make good on the lapsed 17 June memorandum of understanding — lifting the US naval counter-blockade, dropping oil sanctions, unfreezing Iranian assets — none of which is on the table. But this is now a mechanically specific de-escalation step rather than a rhetorical one, and the market is treating it that way: Brent has traded below $88 and WTI near $82 today, both down for a third straight session, a roughly 6% decline over that stretch. Read alongside the still-unresolved Yemeni Houthi attacks on Saudi shipping in the Red Sea this month (a separate but adjacent chokepoint risk this brief has tracked), the Gulf's overall risk premium is easing on the Hormuz side specifically, not uniformly across the region.

## Story 2: CIA director's secret Moscow trip collides with Ukraine's biggest drone barrage on the capital this year

**Source:** CNN / Axios / Washington Post (via search) | https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/25/politics/cia-director-visits-moscow | https://www.axios.com/2026/08/25/cia-director-ratcliffe-visits-moscow
**Related vault notes:** [[Priorities]]

CIA Director John Ratcliffe made an unannounced trip to Moscow this week (Monday through Wednesday), meeting Russian intelligence officials — the Kremlin confirmed he did not meet Putin — and speaking by phone with the head of Russia's SVR foreign intelligence service, the first such CIA-SVR contact in more than two years. Trump characterised the visit as "routine" efforts to end the Ukraine war. Washington reportedly asked Ukraine to hold off drone and missile strikes on Moscow and northern Russian cities for the duration of the trip.

Ukraine appears to have complied only partially, or resumed immediately after: more than 600 Ukrainian drones flew toward the Moscow region overnight, with Moscow's mayor claiming 180 shot down — one of the largest single-night barrages of the year, aimed at the revenue base funding Russia's war effort. Zelensky, after speaking with Trump, said publicly he believes Putin is bluffing about wanting to end the war and that no ceasefire is possible without a three-way leaders' meeting. The juxtaposition is the story: a genuinely rare diplomatic overture (first SVR contact in two years) running in parallel with, not instead of, an intensifying military campaign — read this as both sides testing whether the other blinks first, not as movement toward an actual ceasefire.

## Story 3: Zambia's Chief Justice refers a disputed election petition to the Constitutional Court

**Source:** Lusaka Times / Zambian Observer (via search) | https://www.lusakatimes.com/2026/08/26/petition-or-no-petition-zambias-courts-hold-the-real-verdict/

The procedural standoff flagged in yesterday's report moved forward: a lawyer's attempt to file a petition challenging Hichilema's re-election bounced when submitted electronically to the court registry before Tuesday's deadline, was then forwarded to the Chief Justice, who has now referred the documents to the Constitutional Court for a formal determination on whether the submission counts as a valid filing. The government maintains "there is no Presidential Petition before the Constitutional Court" — a position now directly in tension with the Chief Justice's own referral. Hichilema's swearing-in remains scheduled for Tuesday, 1 September; if the Constitutional Court accepts either petition as validly filed, the constitution gives it 14 days to rule — a window that runs past the inauguration date. This is the cleanest institutional test case in the region right now: courts that were physically barricaded on the filing deadline are now being asked to rule on whether a bounced-and-forwarded submission counts at all.
