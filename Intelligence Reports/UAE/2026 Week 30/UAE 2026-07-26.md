## Story 1: First quiet night in thirteen; Qatar reopens Gulf waters to shipping

**Source:** Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/world/mena/us-iran-conflict-evening-update-what-uae-residents-need-to-know-today-july-25-1.500620251 (published 25 July 2026, 23:14 UAE time)
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Iran reported a peaceful night with no new US strikes overnight into Saturday 25 July, ending a run of thirteen consecutive nights of American military action. Qatar announced full resumption of maritime navigation in its waters from today, Sunday 26 July, the first concrete easing of shipping restrictions reported this week. It is not, however, a clean de-escalation: UK Maritime Trade Operations logged a fresh tanker-military incident in the Gulf of Oman overnight, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they stopped four vessels in the Strait of Hormuz after firing warning shots, and Houthi forces kept striking Saudi Aramco facilities at Jizan and Yanbu. Qatar and Oman are reported to be pushing Iran toward a softer negotiating position to head off a larger US operation that Trump has reportedly held back to allow more time.

Read this as a pause, not a resolution. Qatar reopening its own waters is a genuinely new, dated development, but it doesn't extend to the Strait of Hormuz itself, which multiple trackers still show as functionally closed to commercial tankers since late February. For UAE residents and Orwell's [[Comparison Network]] insurance content, the practical picture is unchanged from Friday's report: EASA's Gulf airspace warning still runs to 31 August, war-risk premiums stay elevated, and one quiet night doesn't reset that five-week runway.

## Story 2: Dubai International tops global rankings for July international seat capacity

**Source:** Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/business/aviation/dubai-airport-tops-worlds-busiest-international-airports-in-july-1.500619822 (published 25 July 2026, 13:23 UAE time)
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Dubai International Airport (DXB) logged 4.35 million international seats in July 2026, putting it ahead of Heathrow (4.23 million, down 1% year on year), Amsterdam Schiphol (3.82 million, up 1%), Incheon and Charles de Gaulle. DXB's own capacity is up 18% year on year for the month, though still running below pre-conflict 2025 levels by the same measure once the earlier airspace disruption is accounted for. It marks DXB's twelfth consecutive year as the world's busiest international hub by passenger volume, backed by continued investment in biometric gates and the "Red Carpet" corridor (6-14 second passport processing).

This lands the same week as the flight cancellations to Kuwait, Bahrain and parts of Saudi Arabia covered in prior reports, and the contrast is the real story: DXB's headline capacity keeps climbing even while individual regional routes stay disrupted by the war. It's more evidence for the pattern flagged repeatedly this week, Dubai's tourism and aviation apparatus is actively engineering resilience through the conflict rather than just weathering it. No direct product angle for the comparison network today, filed as context for how confidently "business as usual" messaging can be pitched against the war backdrop.
