## Story 1: Dubai gold extends third straight rise to Dh542 a gram as Hormuz hedging persists

**Source:** Khaleej Times (live gold-forex page, updated today) | https://www.khaleejtimes.com/gold-forex, corroborated against Khaleej Times market coverage from Thursday | https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/markets/dubai-gold-prices-september-3-2026
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

24-karat gold in Dubai priced at Dh542 per gram this evening, extending a rally that has now run for a third consecutive session: Dh522 early last week, Dh528.25 Wednesday, Dh535 at Thursday's open, Dh542 today. International spot gold sits above $4,400 an ounce, roughly $115 (2.7%) off its recent low near $4,285, though still well short of late August's $4,700 peak set when Fed rate-cut expectations spiked.

The drivers are the same ones that have shaped the metal's whole month: US Treasury yields easing back toward 4.78% from near 5%, a dollar index that has softened to the 99.1-99.2 range even after touching a seven-week high earlier in the week, a weak US ADP print (38,000 private-sector jobs added in August against a 48,000 forecast), and continued central-bank buying (289 tonnes in Q2) alongside $3 billion of ETF inflows in July. Layered on top of all of that is plain geopolitical hedging tied to the still-live Hormuz war, which remains the dominant risk factor pricing into every Gulf-adjacent asset right now.

For Orwell this is a watching-brief item rather than an action item. Sustained gold strength is generally a soft positive read for UAE household wealth and retail jewellery demand, and it is one more data point (alongside the dollar's own recent strength) showing markets are still actively pricing Hormuz risk six months into the war rather than having priced it in and moved on. No direct [[Comparison Network]] angle today; flagged mainly to keep the macro backdrop current for [[Priorities]] tracking.
