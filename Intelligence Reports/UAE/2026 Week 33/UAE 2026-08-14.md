## Story 2: Strait of Hormuz traffic collapses to 14 vessels a day as global oil cushion drains

**Source:** Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/world/mena/hormuz-drains-oil-cushion-will-consumers-be-hit-again-1.500640084

**Related vault notes:** [[Priorities]]

New data shows Strait of Hormuz crossings fell to 14 vessels on Tuesday, against a pre-war average of roughly 120 a day — the tightest the chokepoint has been throughout this crisis. The UK Maritime Trade Operations has now logged 54 damage reports across the Strait, Arabian Gulf and Gulf of Oman, with 3 confirmed total losses and 13 near misses. The Strait normally carries around 20% of the world's oil; the article frames the global price cushion that's absorbed the disruption so far as now running out, meaning further disruption (like today's Story 1) is more likely to show up directly in consumer fuel prices than it has to date.

**Signal for Orwell:** Reads directly against yesterday's IEA note that 2026 global oil demand growth is being trimmed 1.6 million barrels a day — a softening-demand backdrop is exactly what's kept prices from spiking harder despite supply this tight. Today's attack (Story 1) plus this data point together are the clearest sign yet that the cushion is thinning; worth tracking Brent specifically over the next few days rather than assuming today's price holds.

## Story 3: UAE banks' back-to-school fee instalment plans — thirteen banks, zero interest, hidden fees

**Source:** Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/your-money/saving-investment/back-to-school-which-uae-banks-let-parents-pay-fees-in-instalments-1.500640244

**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Thirteen UAE banks now offer zero-interest school fee instalment plans running 3 to 24 months, but products vary meaningfully: ADCB charges a flat Dh50-plus-VAT processing fee; Abu Dhabi Islamic Bank and First Abu Dhabi Bank waive processing fees entirely on eligible cards through Q3; Emirates NBD charges Dh49 for 6-12 months but a 3% fee for a 24-month plan on transactions above Dh5,000; HSBC UAE charges a flat 2.10% processing fee regardless of tenure. The article's own framing is the useful bit: "zero interest isn't always free" once processing and VAT charges are counted.

**Signal for Orwell:** Directly relevant to [[Comparison Network]] — this is the same structural pattern as the Rent Now, Pay Later scheme flagged yesterday (a genuine product-comparison category, not a rate-comparison one, since the products differ by fee structure rather than headline interest rate). ## Story 4: ADNOC's XRG takes equal stake in Venezuela's offshore Loran gas licence

**Source:** Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/business/energy/adnocs-xrg-enters-venezuela-with-stake-in-major-offshore-gas-licence-1.500640296

**Related vault notes:** [[Priorities]]

XRG, ADNOC's international investment arm, acquired an equal-partner stake alongside bp and UCC in Venezuela's offshore Loran gas licence, which holds over 4 trillion cubic feet of proven reserves. The deal is subject to regulatory approval and involves a transfer of interest from state-owned PDVSA Gas. XRG frames the appeal as proximity to existing Trinidad and Tobago liquefaction infrastructure, avoiding the need to build new export capacity.

**Signal for Orwell:** No direct action item. A scale marker on ADNOC's continued push to diversify its international gas portfolio beyond the Gulf, consistent with the broader pattern of UAE state energy capital moving into new geographies this year.

## Story 5: UAE sets Dh2.56 trillion Islamic finance asset target for 2031

**Source:** Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/business/banking/uae-targets-dh256-trillion-in-islamic-finance-assets-by-2031-1.500639617

**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]], [[CRM Systems]]

The UAE's Islamic Finance and Halal Industry Strategy (Cabinet-approved in May 2025) now has a formal number attached: Dh2.56 trillion in local Islamic finance assets by 2031, up from Dh1.4 trillion today across 43 licensed institutions and over 280 Shari'ah standards already issued. CBUAE is coordinating implementation across federal and local entities, with a focus on strengthening the legal/Shari'ah framework and developing sukuk and Islamic money markets.

**Signal for Orwell:** Loosely relevant background for [[Comparison Network]]'s Islamic home finance comparison content (already tracked in the standing mortgage-rate line) — an 83% growth target over five years signals sustained institutional push behind the product category, worth remembering as a tailwind rather than a one-off policy note.
