## Story 1: Hormuz talks reach "final stages," US says deal possible today or tomorrow

**Source:** Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/8/5/iran-war-live-tehran-oman-talks-on-hormuz-positive-ship-hit-in-red-sea ; Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/4/us-says-deal-on-reopening-hormuz-close-as-iran-oman-hold-positive-talks

Iran says Oman-mediated talks on reopening the Strait of Hormuz to safe shipping are proceeding "positively." US Secretary of State Marco Rubio confirmed progress, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent went further today, saying an agreement could be reached "today or tomorrow." Diplomatic sources in Tehran reportedly suggested the same timeline. No deal has actually been signed as of this report. The talks are focused specifically on establishing safe inbound and outbound shipping lanes through the strait rather than a broader ceasefire settlement.

This is the most concrete timeline this report has had on Hormuz in weeks of "imminent" language that never quite lands. Two of Washington's most senior economic and diplomatic voices naming a 24-48 hour window is a real escalation in specificity, even if the pattern this month has been optimistic language followed by delay. Worth checking again before this file is read.

**Signal for Orwell:** No direct action, but this is the single variable that determines whether flight cancellations (Story 2) and war-risk pricing across the Gulf unwind fast or drag on. If a deal lands, expect rapid normalisation across shipping, aviation and insurance pricing within days.

## Story 2: Emirates and Etihad extend Bahrain/Kuwait flight cancellations; wider carrier picture confirmed

**Source:** The National | https://www.thenationalnews.com/travel/2026/08/04/emirates-etihad-bahrain-kuwait-cancellations-us-iran/

Emirates has suspended all services to Bahrain and Kuwait (7 daily flights) through at least today, 5 August. Etihad has cancelled its Abu Dhabi–Bahrain and Abu Dhabi–Kuwait routes citing "the safety and comfort of our guests and crew." Beyond the UAE carriers, the picture is now clearer on how far this has spread: British Airways has postponed its Dubai/Bahrain/Amman resumption to 25 October, KLM has suspended Dubai, Riyadh and Dammam services until 23 August, Lufthansa Group (including Swiss) has adjusted its wider Middle East schedule, and Air France has suspended Saudi routes. Qatar Airways separately extended its own Bahrain/Kuwait/Erbil suspension to 7 August. flydubai and Air Arabia continue operating with the risk of ad hoc cancellations.

The new detail here isn't that cancellations continue, that's been true for weeks, it's the specificity: European long-haul carriers are now planning suspensions in months (BA to October), not days, which is a much more pessimistic signal from outside the region than anything Gulf carriers themselves are saying.

**Signal for Orwell:** No direct action for the SEO portfolio or comparison network today, but worth flagging for anyone tracking Gulf travel-adjacent content: European carriers pricing in an October resumption is a harder data point than "cancellations extended again."

## Story 3: AMF forecasts Kuwait and Qatar hit hardest by war; Saudi and UAE largely insulated

**Source:** Arabian Gulf Business Insight | https://www.agbi.com/economy/2026/08/kuwait-and-qatar-likely-to-face-deepest-economic-hit-from-war/

The Arab Monetary Fund's latest regional forecast puts Qatar's 2026 contraction at -5.9% and Kuwait's at -2.9%, the two economies with the least diversification (oil and gas exports are over two-thirds of both governments' revenue) and the least ability to route around Hormuz. Qatar in particular controls the world's third-largest gas reserves but has been unable to export most of its LNG output through the closure, compounded by Iranian strikes on its energy facilities. Kuwait has resorted to increased borrowing on cash shortages. By contrast, the AMF still projects growth for Saudi Arabia (3.2%), the UAE (1.7%), Oman (2.9%) and Bahrain (1.4%) this year, all four benefiting from larger non-oil sectors and, for Saudi and the UAE specifically, pipeline infrastructure that bypasses Hormuz entirely. All six GCC states are forecast to rebound sharply in 2027 (UAE +9.8%, Saudi +4.2%).

This fills in the picture Saudi Arabia's -4.8% Q2 GDP print (covered in yesterday's report) only hinted at: the pain from this war is being distributed very unevenly across the Gulf, and the UAE sits on the fortunate side of that split largely because of infrastructure decisions made years before this conflict started.

**Signal for Orwell:** No direct action for [[Comparison Network]] or [[SEO Portfolio]] today, but useful context: UAE-focused products aren't exposed to the worst of this regional shock the way Kuwait- or Qatar-adjacent business would be, one more reason UAE remains the right base for a Gulf-facing operation through this period.
