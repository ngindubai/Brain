## Story 1: Fed hikes to 3.75%-4.00%, CBUAE mirrors to 3.9% base rate

**Source:** Federal Reserve / CNBC / Gulf News | https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/fed-rate-decision-september-2026.html | https://gulfnews.com/business/banking/uae-central-bank-raises-base-rate-to-39-after-us-fed-rate-hike-1.500677315
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

The open loop from yesterday is closed. The FOMC voted 12-0 to raise the federal funds rate 25 basis points to 3.75%-4.00%, its first hike since 2023, with Chair Kevin Warsh citing persistent above-target inflation despite pressure from the White House to hold. No dissents. Markets are now pricing at least one more 25bp move before year-end, with Fed officials' own year-end projections clustering at 4.1%-4.4%.

CBUAE followed within hours, as it must under the dollar peg. The Base Rate on the Overnight Deposit Facility rises 25 basis points to 3.9% from 3.65%, effective today, 17 September. The rate on short-term liquidity borrowed from the central bank stays anchored at 50 basis points above the Base Rate across all standing credit facilities. This is pure mechanical transmission, not an independent UAE policy call, and it is the second such mirrored move this year.

The signal worth logging: even with a Fed chair as openly hawkish and politically confrontational as Warsh, the peg mechanism transmitted cleanly and instantly. No lag, no local hesitation. That's a data point for anyone modelling UAE rate risk off US politics rather than US data.

## Story 2: DFM hits record close on rate-hike relief; UAE fixed mortgages hold steady

**Source:** Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/business/markets/dubai-index-outperforms-emerging-markets-amid-fed-hike-1.2283452
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

The Dubai Financial Market General Index closed 1.81% higher today at 2,825.76, a record close and the best performer among emerging markets, a day after the Fed's move. Emirates NBD led gains, closing 6.15% higher at Dh9.50, with Dubai Islamic Bank up 1.13% and buying also concentrated in Emaar Properties. The logic is straightforward: a wider spread between the Fed's funds rate and what banks charge borrowers lifts expected loan profitability, and Gulf banks got repriced on that basis within a single session.

On the mortgage side, nothing dramatic moved for existing borrowers. Major UAE banks are currently quoting one-year fixed rates from around 3.75%, two-year fixed around 3.78%, and three-year fixed around 3.95%. Anyone already locked into a fixed term keeps their contracted rate until it expires; only new lending and rate resets feel today's 25bp move directly. For [[Comparison Network]] purposes, that's a case for a short "what today's CBUAE hike actually means for your mortgage" explainer on MortgageCompare, most people conflate a central bank base-rate move with an immediate repricing of their own loan, and today is a clean, current example to correct that.

## Story 3: Gulf tension keeps grinding on oil and flights

**Source:** Oilprice.com / Gulf News aviation status page | https://oilprice.com/futures/brent/ | https://gulfnews.com/business/aviation/uae-flights-status-emirates-etihad-air-arabia-flydubai-delays-and-cancellations-1.500670657

Brent held in the $107-109 range today, easing off session highs but staying near a four-month peak. Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline remains offline after last week's attacks, with the US Energy Secretary calling the outage temporary while independent analysts still expect several weeks of repair time. Riyadh continues rerouting exports through the Strait of Hormuz with US naval assistance rather than waiting on the pipeline.

The knock-on for UAE aviation is ongoing rather than new today, but it is live: Emirates, Etihad, flydubai and Air Arabia are all still advising passengers to check status before travelling, with flydubai and Air Arabia cancelling services to Bahrain and Kuwait and Etihad reporting technical delays on two long-haul routes. None of this is a single dramatic event, it's a low-grade drag that has now run for weeks with no resolution timeline. Worth flagging only if it starts hitting UAE-origin freight or insurance claims volumes directly.

## Story 4: Insurance Law transition deadline passes silently for a second day

**Source:** Al Tamimi & Company / Lexology | https://www.tamimi.com/news/insurance-licensing-in-uae-what-has-changed-in-2026/ | https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d58fa7ec-6047-4a76-8ac0-6bab99aa55c2
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

No update. The one-year transitional deadline under the new CBUAE Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 6 of 2025) landed 16 September, folding standalone insurance regulation into the consolidated CBUAE framework alongside banking, payments and open finance. Maximum fines under the new regime rise to AED 1 billion, with Article 170 criminal liability as backstop, and CBUAE retains discretion to extend the transition period if it chooses. As of today there is still no public CBUAE statement confirming enforcement posture, extension, or a compliant-firm list. Firms operating in a licensing grey zone right now have no clarity on what happens next, which is itself notable for anyone tracking InsureCompare's regulatory environment.
