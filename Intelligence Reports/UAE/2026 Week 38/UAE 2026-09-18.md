## Story 1: UAE launches second Sovereign Retail T-Sukuk issuance, five-year tenor

**Source:** WAM / Khaleej Times | https://www.wam.ae/en/article/c2ajejd-ministry-finance-announces-second-issuance-under | https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/ministry-of-finance-announces-second-issuance-under-sovereign-retail-t-sukuk-programme-with-five-year-tenor-2
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

The Ministry of Finance announced a second issuance under the UAE's Sovereign Retail T-Sukuk Programme, this time with a five-year tenor, open to UAE nationals and residents from a minimum subscription of AED 1,000. It is fully government-backed and Shariah-compliant; once allocation and settlement complete, it lists for secondary trading on Nasdaq Dubai. The profit rate is due to be announced on 22 September. The inaugural issuance earlier this year attracted subscription orders of AED 445 million, roughly nine times the initial target size, which is the benchmark this one will be measured against.

The retail framing is the point: this isn't aimed at institutional investors, it's aimed at ordinary residents looking for a low-minimum, government-backed savings instrument, which puts it in loose competition with the kind of products MoneyCompare covers. If demand tracks anywhere near the first issuance's 9x oversubscription, that's a genuine signal of retail savings appetite worth a short MoneyCompare piece once the 22 September profit rate lands and there's an actual number to write about.

## Story 2: Brent eases as Saudi Arabia's Oman workaround takes pressure off the East-West pipeline outage

**Source:** AGBI / CNBC | https://www.agbi.com/oil-and-gas/2026/09/oil-supply-fears-ease-on-saudi-workaround-via-oman/ | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/17/oil-prices-today-wti-brent-hormuz-iran-war.html

Brent fell roughly 1.2% to around $104.6-104.8 a barrel today, easing off the four-month highs of the past week, as Saudi Arabia scaled up ship-to-ship crude transfers to Asian refiners off Oman's Sohar port, bypassing the drone-damaged East-West pipeline entirely. Transfer volumes through the Gulf of Oman have risen to roughly 2.7 million barrels per day, up from 1.5 million in August. Saudi Aramco is reportedly working to restore about half the pipeline's capacity within days, with full repair estimated at roughly six weeks. UAE flight disruptions tied to the wider regional tension (Emirates, Etihad, flydubai, Air Arabia routes to Bahrain and Kuwait) continue on a similar low-grade basis to recent days, with no material change today.

This is the first genuine de-escalation signal on the oil side since the pipeline attack, not a resolution. The workaround is holding better than the market initially priced, which is the actual news: risk premium easing because logistics adapted faster than expected, not because the underlying tension resolved. Nothing for the portfolio to act on; flagging only because a full week of "tension keeps grinding" coverage finally produced a number moving the other direction.

## Story 3: Insurance Law transition deadline stays silent, day three

**Source:** Al Tamimi & Company / Lexology (checked again today for any update) | https://www.tamimi.com/news/insurance-licensing-in-uae-what-has-changed-in-2026/
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Checked specifically for a CBUAE statement today, as flagged in each of the last two reports. There is none. The one-year transitional deadline under Federal Decree-Law No. 6 of 2025 landed 16 September, folding standalone insurance regulation into the consolidated CBUAE framework with maximum fines up to AED 1 billion and Article 170 criminal liability as backstop. Three days on, there is still no public confirmation of enforcement posture, no extension announcement, and no compliant-firm list. Industry legal commentary continues to advise firms to simply treat 16 September as the operative deadline in the absence of guidance.

This is the third consecutive report noting the same silence, so it earns one more full mention before dropping to a straight open-loop line: three days of no communication on a deadline carrying billion-dirham fine exposure is unusual even by CBUAE's normal pace, and if it stretches into a full week it becomes a standalone story about regulatory drift rather than a footnote. Relevant background, not yet action-relevant, for InsureCompare.
