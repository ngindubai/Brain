## Story 1: Dubai's free zones post 96% occupancy, company count up 13% in H1 2026

**Source:** Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/business/markets/dubai-economic-zones-hit-96-occupancy-company-count-rises-13-1.500643597

The Dubai Integrated Economic Zones Authority (DIEZ) — covering Dubai Airport Freezone, Dubai Silicon Oasis and Dubai CommerCity — reported H1 2026 occupancy of 96% across its three zones, with the number of registered companies up 13% year on year and the combined workforce up 24% against H1 2025.

The venture arm, Oraseya Capital, backed 15 startups in the period, a 25% increase on H1 2025, and ranked as the UAE's most active investor by deal count for the third consecutive year (second across MENA by MAGNiTT's ranking), with a particular tilt towards AI, proptech and e-commerce. New company registrations at the Dubai Technology Entrepreneur Campus specifically were up 57% on H1 2025, and AI-focused registrations within that cohort climbed 95%.

This is a genuinely strong number and it is a company-formation signal, not just a real estate one. A 13% rise in company count against 96% occupancy means the zones are close to physically full — expect either new zone capacity announcements or upward pressure on setup/licensing fees as scarcity bites. For companyformation.ae, the AI and proptech tilt in what's actually registering is worth building content around: generic "set up a free zone company" content is saturated, but "AI company formation in Dubai" and "proptech startup licensing UAE" are underserved angles with a real data hook behind them today.

## Story 2: Salama's H1 profit more than triples to Dh28.3 million

**Source:** Khaleej Times | https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/salama-h1-profit-more-than-triples-to-dh283-million-on-stronger-underwriting-pricing-and-expense-management

Islamic Arab Insurance Company (Salama), the Al-Futtaim-backed takaful insurer, reported H1 2026 profit after tax of Dh28.3 million, up from Dh8.2 million a year earlier — a more than threefold increase. The insurance service result swung to a Dh28.3 million gain from a Dh1.9 million loss in H1 2025, a Dh30.2 million turnaround. Q2 alone delivered Dh14.3 million profit after tax against Dh7.9 million a year earlier — the second consecutive profitable quarter. Total equity rose 9% to Dh6.19 billion from Dh5.66 billion in H1 2025.

Notably, insurance revenue actually fell, to Dh469.7 million from Dh515.4 million, which management is framing as a deliberate move: pricing discipline and portfolio optimisation over chasing volume. That's the more interesting story than the headline profit figure — Salama is choosing margin over market share, which fits a pattern flagged in earlier reports of smaller UAE insurers outgrowing the majors by being more selective rather than more aggressive.

For InsureCompare, this is a useful data point: an insurer visibly repricing and trimming weaker business lines is one that will be more selective about which comparison-site leads it wants. Worth checking whether Salama's product mix on the platform still matches what they're now underwriting for.

## Story 3: Tax Star raises $1.75m seed round to scale as an approved UAE e-invoicing provider

**Source:** Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/business/corporate-news/tax-star-raises-175m-seed-round-to-scale-as-a-pre-approved-accredited-service-provider-for-uae-e-invoicing-1.500643578

Tax Star, an AI-powered UAE corporate tax and e-invoicing compliance platform, closed a $1.75 million seed round backed primarily by angel investors, on the back of its status as a pre-approved Accredited Service Provider (ASP) for UAE e-invoicing under the Ministry of Finance framework. Funds go to go-to-market expansion, product development and simplifying compliance workflows for businesses facing the rollout.

This lands directly on the open e-invoicing deadline loop: large businesses (Dh50m+ annual revenue) must select an ASP by 30 October 2026 and start using e-invoicing from 1 January 2027; smaller businesses have until 31 March 2027 to choose a provider and 1 July 2027 to go live. A funded, pre-approved provider actively going to market now is a signal the compliance deadline is starting to bite commercially — expect a wave of similar ASP marketing pushes through autumn as the October deadline approaches.

For legalconsultants.ae and companyformation.ae, this is a live content and partnership opportunity: businesses will be searching "UAE e-invoicing provider" and "e-invoicing deadline UAE" in growing volume between now and October. Worth a content push ahead of competitors rather than after the deadline crunch.

## Story 4: Emirates NBD launches UAE's first Transition Finance Framework, adds BNPL split-payments

**Source:** Khaleej Times | https://www.khaleejtimes.com/life-and-living/banking-in-uae/emirates-nbd-launches-uaes-first-transition-finance-framework-to-support-lower-carbon-investment

Emirates NBD introduced what it says is the UAE's first dedicated Transition Finance Framework, aimed at corporate and institutional clients in hard-to-abate sectors (manufacturing, mining, power, real estate, transport, agriculture, IT) that don't yet qualify as "green" but are taking credible decarbonisation steps. It supports the bank's target of $30 billion in sustainable and transition finance by 2030, feeding into the UAE Banking Federation's Dh1 trillion sustainable finance ambition for the same year.

Separately, and more relevant to day-to-day retail: Emirates NBD rolled out an interest-free split-payments option at checkout for its customers, joining the wider Gulf push into buy-now-pay-later style consumer credit.

Neither is dramatic on its own, but together they show Emirates NBD moving on two fronts at once — institutional ESG-linked lending and retail point-of-sale credit. The BNPL move is the one with more direct read-through for MoneyCompare: another major bank formalising split-payment products narrows the gap between dedicated BNPL fintechs and the incumbent banks, and it's worth tracking whether other UAE banks follow within the month.

## Story 5: Airline suspensions to the Gulf extend deeper into 2026 and 2027

**Source:** Gulf News (aviation updates) | https://gulfnews.com/business/aviation/uae-flight-status-today-emirates-etihad-and-air-arabia-cancellations-and-advisories-1.500642383

The rolling airline disruption picture has hardened rather than eased. Air Canada has now pushed its Dubai suspension out to mid-January 2027 (well beyond the previously flagged near-term window), and Singapore Airlines has extended its Dubai resumption date to 24 October, matching Lufthansa Group's existing suspension of Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Bahrain, Riyadh, Doha and Dammam services, also to 24 October. Etihad has Bahrain cancellations running to 21 August, and Air Arabia continues to suspend selected Sharjah and Abu Dhabi routes to Kuwait, Bahrain, Doha and Abha. Turkish Airlines has resumed Dubai and Abu Dhabi flights, though its Iran routes remain suspended.

The direction of travel matters more than any single airline's schedule: carriers are no longer treating this as a short-term disruption to ride out in weeks — Air Canada's mid-January 2027 date is a five-month-plus suspension from today. That's a planning horizon shift, not a rolling extension, and it should inform any UAE-exposed travel or logistics assumptions for Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 rather than being treated as week-to-week noise.

## Story 6: DP World's Tartus port revamp in Syria hits crane milestone

**Source:** Economy Middle East / Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/business/markets/dp-world-accelerates-800m-tartous-port-overhaul-as-syria-pushes-trade-recovery-1.500540312

DP World's $800 million, 30-year concession to modernise Syria's Port of Tartus is progressing towards its first operational milestone: the first of three new harbour cranes arrived in June, with the remaining two expected imminently, which together are projected to lift the port's cargo-handling capacity by roughly 40% once fully commissioned. The project sits alongside AD Ports' and Khorfakkan's eastern-coast Hormuz-hedging moves flagged in recent reports — a reminder that UAE port operators are diversifying trade infrastructure exposure across the wider region, not just around the Strait.

This is a slower-moving structural story rather than a today-dated news event, but it is part of the same pattern this brief has been tracking all month: UAE state-linked logistics and ports capital is actively redistributing away from Hormuz-dependent single points of failure. Background only — no new headline development today, included for continuity with the AD Ports/Khorfakkan thread.
