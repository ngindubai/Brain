## Story 1: ADIA's 2025 annual report shows improved returns, tech still the top investment theme

**Source:** The National, dated 10 September | https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/markets/2026/09/10/abu-dhabi-wealth-fund-adia-says-tech-remains-major-investment-theme-this-year/
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Abu Dhabi Investment Authority's 2025 annual report shows its 20-year annualised return rising to 6.6% (from 6.3%) and 30-year return to 7.2% (from 7.1%), with managing director Sheikh Hamed bin Zayed stating technology's economic benefits are "now spilling over into the broader industrial landscape." Portfolio split: developed-market equities 32-42%, emerging-market equities 15-17%, private equity 15-20%; 63% internally managed; North America still dominant at 45-60% of allocation. Global SWF estimates ADIA's total assets at roughly $1.1 trillion. Recent deals cited include the $1.5bn GLP investment (August 2025) and the €3.6bn Froneri stake (October 2025).

Routine but useful annual context: one of the two largest Abu Dhabi sovereign funds is explicitly reaffirming tech as its lead theme rather than rotating away from it, which sits alongside yesterday's ADGM data point ($100bn+ in AI-focused investment now domiciled there) as further confirmation that Abu Dhabi's institutional capital is not treating AI as a bubble to de-risk from.

## Story 2: IHC and Adani Group sign $25bn pact with India's Odisha government across 14 projects

**Source:** The National, dated 10 September | https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/economy/2026/09/10/ihc-and-adani-group-sign-pact-with-indias-odisha-government-to-invest-25bn-in-14-projects/
**Related vault notes:** none directly

Abu Dhabi's International Holding Company (1,300+ subsidiaries) and India's Adani Group signed an agreement with Odisha's state government during the chief minister's UAE visit to invest roughly $25bn (2.1 trillion rupees) across 14 projects spanning renewable energy, healthcare, tourism, metals/chemicals and skills development, with the state citing a potential 8.6 million jobs created. This builds on a separate $11.5bn aluminium joint venture (50-50 IHC-Adani stake) announced in July, covering a refinery in Rayagada and a smelter in Sundargarh.

Logged under the daily Gulf-economy sweep as a scale data point on how aggressively Abu Dhabi capital is flowing into India specifically, rather than anything with a direct [[Comparison Network]] or portfolio angle — no consumer-facing UAE product touches this deal.

## Story 3: Majid Al Futtaim posts 25% jump in H1 operating profit despite UAE retail softness

**Source:** The National, dated 9 September | https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/economy/2026/09/09/majid-al-futtaim-expects-strong-2026-performance-as-first-half-profit-jumps-25/
**Related vault notes:** none directly

Majid Al Futtaim reported H1 2026 net operating profit of $490m (up 25% year-on-year) on Dh17.5bn revenue (up 1%), with malls revenue up 12% and EBITDA up 11% to Dh2.5bn, even as UAE retail revenue fell 6% on "challenging consumer conditions." Development revenue surged 38% on Dubai and Cairo projects, including a Dh62bn Dubai South partnership and a $3.1bn Cairo development with Midar. CEO Ahmed Galal Ismail said the group remains "confident about the outlook for 2026" across its 14 markets despite Iran-conflict-related Gulf supply-chain disruption.

The UAE retail softness sitting alongside strong mall and development performance is a useful real-economy data point against the property-cooling narrative this report has tracked: discretionary retail spend looks to be where UAE household-level pressure is actually showing up, not just in home prices.

## Story 4: Azizi Developments enters Sharjah with Dh30bn ($8.16bn) Azizi Florence project

**Source:** The National, dated 9 September | https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/2026/09/09/dubais-azizi-developments-makes-first-foray-into-sharjah-with-dh30-billion-project/
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]], [[SEO Portfolio]]

Flagging this a day later than its dateline because it wasn't caught in Wednesday's report: Dubai-based Azizi Developments announced its first Sharjah project, Azizi Florence — 1,130 villas, over 6,000 townhouses and 3,500 apartments around a 1.7 million sq ft central park, three-bedroom townhouses starting at Dh1.89m. No construction start date given. It lands against a backdrop of strong Sharjah transaction growth (Dh29.5bn, up 9.3% year-on-year; 59,460 transactions, up 23.7%, H1 2026) — a major Dubai developer's first Sharjah entry is itself a signal that Sharjah's growth is now pulling in Dubai-based capital rather than just local developers.

Worth a note for [[Comparison Network]]'s mortgage/property products: if Sharjah is starting to draw major Dubai developer investment while Dubai's own residential prices are in their first annual decline since 2021, that's a genuine relative-value shift worth having an opinion on for any UAE property-comparison content.
