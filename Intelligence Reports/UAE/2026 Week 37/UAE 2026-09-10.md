## Story 1: Houthi strikes on Saudi energy facilities push Brent to $100 a barrel — UAE condemns "flagrant violation" of Saudi sovereignty

**Source:** NPR, US News, Yahoo Finance, Gulf News (via search), dated 8-9 September | https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-09-08/saudi-led-coalition-in-yemen-says-73-injured-in-houthi-attacks-on-the-kingdom
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Iran-aligned Houthi forces launched drone and ballistic-missile strikes on 8 September against civilian and economic facilities in Abha, Jazan, Najran and Khamis Mushait in southern Saudi Arabia, wounding 73 people and hitting the 400,000 barrel-a-day Jazan refinery among other sites. Saudi authorities halted operations at some affected facilities. The strikes put the Bab al-Mandeb strait's commercial traffic at renewed risk on top of the Strait of Hormuz disruption already running from the Iran war — a genuine dual-chokepoint scenario, since Hormuz traffic has been largely halted for weeks and Bab al-Mandeb now carries fresh attack risk too. Brent crude responded by climbing from around $98.61 immediately after the strikes to touch $100.71 on 9 September, its first return above $100 since July. The UAE's Ministry of Foreign Affairs issued a strong condemnation the same day, calling the attacks "a flagrant violation of the sovereignty of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia" and reaffirming solidarity with Riyadh; it separately condemned continued Houthi targeting of Red Sea commercial vessels as a threat to safe maritime navigation.

This is the update this report's standing Hormuz open loop has been waiting for: a second, geographically separate escalation (Saudi energy sites, not just the Strait of Hormuz) that widens the risk picture rather than just extending it. Lloyd's-market underwriters were already rewriting Red Sea and Saudi-touchpoint cover after the first round of Houthi strikes on Aramco sites in late July; expect further tightening on marine and cargo cover for anything transiting the southern Gulf or Red Sea. No direct hit to [[Comparison Network]] products today, but worth watching whether sustained $100+ oil feeds through to UAE fuel and freight costs, and by extension into cost-of-living and consumer-spending signals relevant to any lead-gen product tracking discretionary UAE household spend.

## Story 2: FTA issues new input-VAT supplier-verification rules, effective 1 October

**Source:** Khaleej Times, VATupdate (via search), Federal Tax Authority Decision No. 13 of 2026 | https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/uae-introduces-mandatory-vat-supplier-and-supply-verification-checks
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

The Federal Tax Authority's Decision No. 13 of 2026 requires VAT-registered businesses to verify suppliers — identity, authorised representatives, and genuine business premises — on first dealing and at least every 12 months thereafter, with enhanced due diligence (bank confirmation letters, media and risk screening) required for suppliers with annual supplies over AED 375,000. Businesses must also assess each individual transaction for commercial justification, pricing consistency and licensed-activity alignment, and keep documented evidence of the checks. A narrow carve-out exempts transactions under AED 10,000 where total annual supplies from that supplier stay below AED 100,000. The rule takes effect 1 October 2026 — businesses claiming input VAT after that date need more than a valid tax invoice; they need a paper trail proving the verification actually happened.

This is a genuine new compliance deadline, distinct from the CBUAE insurance and Central Bank Law deadlines already tracked below, and it applies far more broadly — any VAT-registered UAE entity, not just financial-sector ones. Worth a short note on whether this touches any Orwell-adjacent UAE entity's supplier relationships before 1 October, since the retroactive documentation burden (proving checks were done, not just that a valid invoice exists) is the part likely to catch businesses out.

## Story 4: Dubai August home sales fall 11.5% month-on-month to 11,147, value down to AED 21.43bn

**Source:** Projectory/DLD data (via search), dated early September | web search
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]], [[SEO Portfolio]]

Dubai Land Department data shows 11,147 home sales worth AED 21.43bn in August 2026 — down 11.5% in volume from July, with off-plan registrations falling 10.8% to 8,270 and ready-home sales down 13.6% to 2,877. AED 10 million-plus transaction activity grew even as overall volume fell, meaning the softening is concentrated at the lower and middle bands rather than uniform across the market. Year-to-date transaction value through August reached AED 523.44bn across 148,564 transactions — already 57% of the whole of 2025's annual total in eight months, so the market remains large in absolute terms even as the month-on-month trend softens.

This confirms rather than adds to the cooling trend already flagged as the lead story two reports ago (first annual price decline since February 2021).
