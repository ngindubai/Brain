## Story 1: Houthis seize Mokha; Brent spikes to $109 as Gulf shipping traffic collapses

**Source:** The National, dated 11 September | https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/energy/2026/09/11/oil-prices-set-to-end-the-week-above-100-as-houthis-seize-key-port-city/
**Related vault notes:** none directly

Houthi forces captured Mokha, a Red Sea port city near the Bab Al Mandeb Strait critical to Saudi Arabia's oil exports to Asia, on Thursday. Brent crude spiked to $109 intraday, its highest level since the April peak of $126.41, before settling around $105; WTI traded near $100.50. Ship traffic has collapsed at both regional chokepoints: only 7 vessels crossed the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday, down from 18 on Tuesday and over 100 daily before the conflict began in February; Bab Al Mandeb crossings fell to 27 from 32. An analyst at Phillip Nova framed the new question as not whether Brent breaks $100 (it has) but whether it stabilises below $120 or keeps climbing.

This is a genuine escalation of the standing Hormuz/Houthi open loop, not a continuation: a physical port seizure is a materially different risk level than the shelling and shipping-lane harassment tracked in recent reports. For the UAE specifically, worth watching fuel and freight cost pass-through and, more acutely, whether Fujairah (the UAE's own alternative export/bunkering point outside the Strait) sees any knock-on effects if Hormuz traffic keeps falling. Nothing to action today, but this now reads as a live supply-shock risk rather than a slow-burn one.

## Story 4: The real cost of UAE freelancing: permits, insurance and lost mortgage access

**Source:** The National, dated 11 September | https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/2026/09/11/from-salary-to-self-employed-counting-the-cost-of-freelancing-in-the-uae/
**Related vault notes:** none directly

A detailed explainer on the true cost of going freelance in the UAE: a basic professional-services permit runs Dh6,500 + VAT for someone with an existing residence visa, a full first-year package (permit, fees, insurance) typically Dh14,888-Dh18,000, and full business setup via a free-zone flexi-desk (DMCC cited) up to Dh43,780. Beyond licensing, freelancers absorb health insurance (Dh1,000-Dh20,000 a year depending on tier) and lose easy access to credit: lenders typically require three years of audited accounts before extending mortgage financing to a freelancer.

Filed under the company-formation/employment sweep. The mortgage-access point is the one worth flagging: it is a structural reason a growing freelance/gig cohort in the UAE is mortgage-excluded regardless of income, a segment [[Comparison Network]]'s mortgage-comparison content is not currently built to address (it likely assumes salaried applicants as the default case).

## Story 5: China says no conflict between Belt and Road and India's IMEC corridor: UAE sits in both

**Source:** The National, dated 11 September | https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/economy/2026/09/11/chinas-belt-and-road-expects-no-friction-with-india-led-trade-corridor/
**Related vault notes:** none directly

Belt and Road Initiative commissioner Nicholas Ho said publicly that China sees no conflict between the BRI and India's competing India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC), the US/EU/UAE/Saudi/France/Germany/Italy-backed route designed to link South Asia, the Gulf and Europe while bypassing traditional maritime chokepoints. The UAE holds membership in both initiatives simultaneously; India's objection to BRI is specifically the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor's route through disputed Kashmir, not BRI generally.

Filed under the wider-Gulf-economy sweep as a logistics/trade-corridor data point: the UAE's dual membership in what are nominally rival infrastructure blocs is a deliberate hedge, and IMEC's chokepoint-bypass design is directly relevant background to Story 1's Hormuz/Bab Al Mandeb disruption, since IMEC's entire pitch is that it is not exposed to exactly that kind of chokepoint risk.
