## Story 1: Iran-Oman Hormuz talks add a 30-60 day timeline, oil stays soft
**Source:** Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/26/top-official-says-iran-oman-have-agreed-new-temporary-hormuz-route | The Hill | https://thehill.com/policy/international/6051291-iran-oman-strait-of-hormuz-plan/
**Related vault notes:** [[Priorities]]

The framework reported yesterday — a temporary joint navigational corridor plus a mine-clearing operation — now has more routing and timing detail attached. The proposed temporary inbound route (Gulf of Oman into the Persian Gulf) would run entirely through Iranian waters; the outbound route would split between Iranian and Omani waters. Iran and Oman have set 30-60 days for further talks toward a permanent corridor, a standing traffic-management mechanism, and a decision on who administers the strait long-term.

This is incremental, not new in substance — it fills in the "technical negotiations continue" line from yesterday's report with an actual timeframe, which at least gives a date range to watch rather than an open-ended one. Brent held below $89 and WTI around $81 through the week on the same de-escalation pricing already noted; no fresh move today. Nothing here changes the UAE-Iran trade suspension, and the underlying risk (only two tankers transited Hormuz on Monday, ~80% of traffic still running dark) is unresolved. Downgrading to a shorter open-loop entry from here unless the corridor is actually signed.

## Story 2: Saudi Arabia's H1 2026 trade surplus tops $41bn, non-oil economy still expanding despite Q2 GDP contraction
**Source:** Zawya (via web search) | https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/gcc/saudi-arabias-trade-surplus-surges-60-to-over-24bln-in-q1-2026-i81tctzc and related H1 coverage

Saudi Arabia's first-half 2026 trade surplus is reported at over $41bn, building on a Q1 surplus already up 60% year-on-year to $24.1bn. The surplus print sits awkwardly alongside a genuinely contractionary second quarter — Saudi real GDP shrank 4.8% year-on-year in Q2, the kingdom's first quarterly contraction since 2023, while the non-oil PMI has still shown four straight months of improvement (53.1 in July). Qatar's economy is separately forecast to contract 5.9% this year, largely attributed to Hormuz-related LNG export disruption and Iranian strikes on Qatari energy infrastructure.

Read together, the Gulf's largest economies are absorbing real cost from the Iran-Israel/Hormuz standoff even while headline trade numbers look strong — the trade surplus is partly a function of import compression, not pure export strength. No direct UAE data point today, but it's useful context for how much of the wider Gulf economy is still running on a war footing five-plus months in, and it's a reminder the UAE's own relative stability through this period is not the regional norm.
