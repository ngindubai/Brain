## Story 1: Abu Dhabi's sovereign fund L'imad completes 100% takeover of Taqa, delisting it from ADX
**Source:** The National | https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/energy/2026/08/26/taqa-to-be-delisted-from-adx-as-limad-completes-acquisition/ | https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/markets/2026/08/26/ad-ports-board-backs-limads-dh625-a-share-bid-for-full-control/
**Related vault notes:** [[Priorities]]

L'imad — Abu Dhabi's sovereign wealth fund, stood up last year — has taken full ownership of Abu Dhabi National Energy Company (Taqa) through its subsidiary Abu Dhabi Power Corporation, building on an earlier 98.12% stake. Taqa is now waiting on regulatory approval to delist from the Abu Dhabi Securities Exchange. Taqa runs power/water generation, water treatment, transmission and distribution, and upstream/midstream oil and gas across 26 countries — this is not a minor listing.

The same fund's board also just backed a Dh6.25-a-share bid for full control of AD Ports, a separate ADX-listed company. Read together, L'imad is running a genuine consolidation programme: taking strategic infrastructure (energy, ports) fully private under sovereign control rather than leaving it exposed to public markets. That's consistent with the broader Gulf sovereign-fund pattern of buying certainty during a period (Hormuz, regional conflict spillover) where public-market valuations for exposed infrastructure assets are harder to hold steady. No direct product relevance to the comparison network, but it's a real data point on how much of Abu Dhabi's listed corporate landscape is being absorbed back into sovereign hands this year — worth remembering next time ADX listing counts or index composition come up.

## Story 2: Union Properties' turnaround CEO resigns
**Source:** The National | https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/2026/08/27/union-properties-chief-executive-amer-khansaheb-resigns/
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Amer Khansaheb stepped down as Union Properties CEO effective 24 August, citing a wish to focus on his own business interests — not a forced-out departure on the reporting available. It's a notable exit because of what he leaves behind: since his December 2021 appointment he repaid roughly Dh1.47bn ($400m) of legacy debt, relaunched major Dubai project pipelines (Takaya, Mirdad in Motor City), and got the company's first shareholder dividend in about 11 years approved in April. That turnaround followed a period of genuine governance crisis — a UAE prosecutor's investigation into alleged financial irregularities that removed the former chairman and several board members.

A transitional committee under chairman Sheikh Nasser Almoalla takes over with limited authority over major strategic calls until a permanent successor is named. Nothing here moves MortgageCompare or the comparison network directly, but it's a useful marker of how far one of Dubai's more troubled listed developers has come — worth watching whether the project pipeline (and dividend policy) holds through the transition, since that's the more market-relevant question than the resignation itself.

## Story 3: InsuranceMarket.ae confirms CEO succession — founder steps up to Chairman
**Source:** Health Magazine (press release syndication) | https://healthmagazine.ae/press_release/hitesh-motwani-appointed-ceo-of-insurancemarket-ae-as-founder-avinash-babur-moves-to-chairman-role/
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Hitesh Motwani, a 13-year company veteran (CMO from 2018, Deputy CEO from February 2025), has been appointed CEO of InsuranceMarket.ae (AFIA Insurance Brokerage Services), with CBUAE formal approval. Founder Avinash Babur moves to Chairman, staying on as Founder/Chairman of parent Alfred Holdings, freeing him to focus on that group's expansion.

This is the one story today with a direct line to InsureCompare: InsuranceMarket.ae is a real competitor in the UAE online insurance-comparison/brokerage space, and a planned, CBUAE-approved succession (not a crisis exit) suggests the company is stable and investing in its next growth phase rather than distracted by leadership turmoil. Worth a note if InsureCompare ever needs to benchmark competitor positioning or partnership approaches — a newly installed CEO with an operations/marketing background (rather than pure insurance-broking) may signal a growth-and-distribution push worth watching for.
