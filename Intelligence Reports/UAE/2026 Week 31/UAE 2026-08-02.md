## Story 1: Dubai delivers a record 24,800 homes in H1 2026 as the market shifts from launches to handovers

**Source:** Khaleej Times | https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/dubai-delivers-record-24800-new-homes-in-h1-2026-as-market-matures | data compiled by Cavendish Maxwell
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Dubai handed over 24,800 residential units in H1 2026, up 37.6% year-on-year and 12.1% higher than H2 2025 — the strongest half-year completion total on record. Apartments accounted for roughly 18,900 of those (up 43% YoY), villas and townhouses the remaining 5,900 (up 22.6%). The completion rate against scheduled units was 41.3%.

Sales activity ran alongside it rather than instead of it: 79,300 transactions worth Dh221.4 billion, split 74.8% off-plan (59,300 deals) and the rest ready homes (20,000 deals). The number that matters most for the comparison network: 22,500 mortgage transactions worth Dh51.3 billion, up 17.4% year-on-year. That is a genuine volume signal for MortgageCompare, not noise — mortgage-financed purchases are growing faster than the transaction count overall, which points to buyers leaning harder on debt even as average prices only crept up 1.9% YoY to Dh1,639/sq ft. Rents rose faster, up 7.8% to Dh75.7/sq ft, keeping gross yields attractive at 6.9% for apartments and 5.0% for villas citywide (Dubai Investments Park topped the table at 9.7% for apartments).

The more interesting structural signal is the pullback in new launches: developers brought 124 projects (~28,000 units) to market in H1 2026, against 2025's record 410 projects. Cavendish Maxwell's Ronan Arthur frames this as the market "transitioning to a new cycle" rather than a confidence problem — completions are catching up with several years of aggressive launching, and developers are choosing to digest that pipeline (47,000 more units expected in H2 2026, 162,500 in 2027) rather than keep adding to it. Off-plan concentration in areas like Dubai South (7,306 transactions) and DAMAC Islands 2 (3,192) shows demand is still there; it is supply behaviour that has changed.

This is genuinely useful, not just descriptive: a market moving from launch-driven to delivery-led typically means less speculative froth, steadier completion-linked mortgage demand, and — if the 2027 pipeline of 162,500 units actually lands — renewed downward pressure on price growth from 2027 onward. Worth flagging to MortgageCompare and MoneyCompare as the underlying volume trend to watch through H2; nothing here changes InsureCompare's picture directly.

## Story 2: Sheikh Mohamed and Zelensky discuss UAE-Ukraine CEPA and mediation efforts

**Source:** Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/sheikh-mohamed-zelensky-discuss-uae-mediation-efforts-1.500627979
**Related vault notes:** none directly — filed for completeness under wider Gulf economy

UAE President Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan held a call with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy today, reviewing cooperation under the UAE-Ukraine Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (in force since June 2026, expected to add roughly $1bn to combined GDP over six years) and discussing the state of the Ukraine crisis. Filed here because it is the only confirmed diplomatic movement dated today and because the CEPA channel is a live one (Dr Thani Al Zeyoudi has flagged up to seven CEPAs closing by year-end), but there is no direct read-through into the portfolio today.

## Story 3: UAE records year's hottest day at 50°C as peak summer nears its end

**Source:** Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/uae/weather/uae-hits-50c-as-middle-east-enters-hottest-phase-of-summer-1.500627738 | Khaleej Times | https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae/weather/50-8c-uae-records-hottest-day-of-the-year
**Related vault notes:** none directly

Al Dhafra region (Owtaid, Abu Dhabi) touched 50.2°C today, with Khaleej Times separately citing a 50.8°C reading as the year's hottest so far — both mark 2026's peak so far, arriving as the wider Middle East enters what forecasters are calling the hottest phase of summer. The National Centre of Meteorology has simultaneously extended its unsettled-weather outlook, forecasting scattered rain and dust over eastern and western regions through Monday 3 August alongside the extreme heat — an unusual combination rather than a contradiction, and one it flagged as continuing from Saturday's report.

Nothing here is a standalone economic story, but it is context worth holding: peak heat this late in summer typically means peak DEWA/ADDC grid demand, and the mandatory midday outdoor-work break (in force since mid-June) remains the operative labour rule through the period. No portfolio site has direct exposure to either.
