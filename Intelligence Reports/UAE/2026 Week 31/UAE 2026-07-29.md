## Story 1: Iran and Oman exchange concrete proposals to manage Strait of Hormuz shipping

**Source:** Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/28/iran-and-oman-exchange-proposals-to-manage-strait-of-hormuz
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

This is the first genuinely substantive movement on the Hormuz file in the week we've been carrying it as an open loop. Oman proposed a joint regional mechanism modelled on the Strait of Malacca system — Indonesia, Malaysia and Singapore's arrangement for voluntary fee collection funding navigation, environmental protection and rescue operations — specifically designed to prevent Iran exercising sole control over the strait. Oman's plan lays out three parallel traffic lanes: one through Iranian territorial waters, one international, one through Omani territorial waters.

Iran's counter is less accommodating: Tehran rejected an equal division of transit routes and instead wants to manage shipping through its own territorial waters while Oman handles only part of the opposite lane, with mine-clearance responsibility reportedly falling to Iran as well — a genuine sticking point given the mined and contested state of the strait after weeks of conflict. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi was blunt that Tehran has "no plans to negotiate with the US" and will consider "any action" to maintain control, including resuming war, even as sources describe Iran as showing "some flexibility" behind the public posture.

This sits against the backdrop of Gulf carriers (Emirates, Etihad, Air Arabia) still running suspended or curtailed Kuwait and Bahrain routes, a disruption that started with the 18 July Kuwait International Airport drone-attack closure and has not fully unwound. The EASA advisory covering the region runs through 31 August. None of this is resolved, but for the first time there's an actual mechanism on the table rather than a ceasefire holding on goodwill.

## Story 2: DIFC posts H1 2026 results — 30% organic growth in registered companies, seventh in the Global Financial Centres Index

**Source:** PR Newswire via Manila Times | https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/07/28/tmt-newswire/pr-newswire/dubai-international-financial-centres-industry-leading-h1-2026-achievements-reinforce-its-position-at-the-forefront-of-global-finance-in-the-middle-east-africa-and-south-asia/2392839
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

DIFC's active registered companies hit 10,018 at end-H1 2026, up 2,318 new registrations and 30% organic growth over 12 months. Regulated financial services firms grew 16% to 1,134. The centre climbed to seventh in the Global Financial Centres Index. The wealth-management numbers are the standout: family-related entities up 36% to 1,408, foundations up 67% to 1,409 — DIFC positioning itself hard as the region's private-wealth centre, not just a financial-services free zone. The Innovation Hub (fintech-adjacent) grew 39% to 1,933 companies.

None of this is a single-day headline, but it's a genuine data point on UAE company-formation demand that a comparison-network or company-formation content strategy should reference directly rather than working from stale figures. Governor Essa Kazim's framing — DIFC as the gateway connecting Asia, Europe, Africa and South Asia capital — is consistent messaging with the broader UAE positioning strategy (same instinct as G42 joining every AI coalition, covered yesterday).
