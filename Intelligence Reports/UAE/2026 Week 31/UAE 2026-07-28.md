## Story 1: Federal Tax Authority sets three-year AI roadmap, EmaraTax to absorb e-invoicing

**Source:** The National | https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2026/07/28/from-vat-to-ai-uaes-push-to-shape-future-of-tax/
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

The Federal Tax Authority used the 29th St Petersburg International Economic Forum today to lay out a three-year plan to embed AI into UAE tax administration — not just chatbots and faster query resolution, but AI-driven risk scoring and audit selection. The stated aim is to "identify and manage issues as early as possible," i.e. flag non-compliant businesses before problems compound rather than catching them at year-end.

Two concrete threads matter more than the AI framing. First, EmaraTax — the FTA's existing portal — will integrate electronic invoicing directly, rather than requiring businesses to bolt on a separate accredited service provider layer. That's a meaningful simplification given the e-invoicing mandate's own timeline is already in flux (ASP appointment deadline pushed from 31 July to 30 October 2026, full go-live still targeted for 1 January 2027 — see Research pipeline). Second, the FTA is exploring digitised VAT refunds for tourists, explicitly benchmarking against the UK's post-Brexit retail export scheme, which suggests the UAE sees a competitive opening in tourist-VAT UX now that the UK has made its own offer less generous.

None of this is a today-only headline — it's a policy signal. For a comparison-network operator, the relevant read is that the UAE is moving toward earlier, automated compliance flagging for VAT-registered entities. Company formation content and any small-business advisory angle on the network should track the EmaraTax e-invoicing integration closely: once it lands, the pitch to SME clients shifts from "get compliant" to "get compliant before the algorithm notices you're not."

## Story 2: Fed decision eve — CBUAE base rate expected to hold at 3.65%, no relief for borrowers yet

**Source:** Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/your-money/saving-investment/will-uae-us-interest-rates-rise-or-fall-tomorrow-what-analysts-expect-next-1.500621904
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Published today, ahead of tomorrow's Federal Reserve decision (Wednesday 29 July). Consensus is a hold at 3.5%–3.75% Fed funds, which under the dirham peg means the CBUAE almost certainly holds its own Base Rate at 3.65% — unchanged since 17 June. This is the article that matters most for the mortgage-comparison side of the network, even though the actual headline is "nothing happened."

Two things worth flagging for MortgageCompare content. First, the immediate practical line: a hold brings "little immediate relief" for Eibor-linked variable mortgages, and fixed-rate borrowers inside their lock period see no change either way — so there's no urgent rate-story hook for tomorrow. Second, the medium-term split view is more interesting than the base case: markets price some chance of a Fed hike in September if oil stays elevated and inflation proves sticky (plausible given the Hormuz-driven crude volatility — Brent swung from sub-$70 in June to above $100 in July and is now sliding back toward $87), while Morgan Stanley's house view is no change through 2026 with two cuts in 2027. That's a genuine fork for anyone weighing fixed vs variable right now, and it's the kind of divergence-of-expert-view piece that performs well as comparison-site content: "lock in now at 3.75% fixed, or wait" is a live, honestly-uncertain question through Q3.

## Story 3: G42 joins Nvidia's Open Secure AI Alliance

**Source:** The National | https://www.thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2026/07/28/open-secure-ai-alliance-nvidia-g42/
**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]]

Abu Dhabi's G42 signed on today to Nvidia's newly launched Open Secure AI Alliance, a cybersecurity-focused coalition backing open-weight AI models, alongside roughly 37 other founding members including IBM, SpaceX, Hugging Face, Cloudflare and Salesforce. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang announced it directly; G42 CEO Peng Xiao's line was that "frontier open models are an essential part of that future, alongside frontier closed models."

This is a positioning story more than a deal story — no capital figure, no product. But it's consistent with the pattern the UAE has been running all year: attach G42 (and by extension Abu Dhabi/MGX capital) to every major US AI-infrastructure coalition as it forms, so the UAE is inside the standard-setting room rather than a rule-taker later. The "open, secure, sovereign" framing also does double duty domestically — it's the same language the UAE uses to justify its own sovereign AI compute build-out (Stargate UAE, MGX). Not directly actionable for the comparison network, but worth a line in [[Agent Org]] tracking since it signals which AI coalitions G42/Abu Dhabi are prioritising — relevant if Orwell ever touches sovereign-AI-adjacent tooling.
