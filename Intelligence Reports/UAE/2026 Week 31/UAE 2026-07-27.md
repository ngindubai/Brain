## Story 1: Oman-Iran talks in Tehran and a crude price drop mark the first diplomatic movement since the pause began

The US-Iran pause held through a second day, and today added the first concrete diplomatic development on top of Saturday's ceasefire covered in yesterday's report: Iran's deputy foreign minister met Oman's counterpart in Tehran to discuss operational mechanisms for safe Strait of Hormuz passage, described by both sides as constructive. Crude prices fell sharply today as traders reduced the near-term risk premium on the back of the pause, though average US pump prices are still elevated at $4.11/gallon. Commercial shipping through Hormuz remains below normal despite the talks, and Etihad and Air Arabia continue adjusting Kuwait and Bahrain routes into a third day.

Nothing here is a resolution yet, Gulf News's own reporting stresses officials still call this an operational pause rather than a peace deal, reversible if strikes resume. But an actual bilateral meeting on Hormuz mechanics is a genuinely new marker after two weeks of pure military exchange, worth tracking closely since it's the variable that would move the EASA advisory and war-risk insurance picture InsureCompare content is currently built around.
