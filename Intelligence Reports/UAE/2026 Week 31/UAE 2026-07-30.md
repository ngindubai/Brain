## Story 1: CBUAE confirms base rate hold at 3.65% as Gulf bank H1 2026 earnings season closes

**Source:** Gulf News / centralbank.ae / The Asian Banker (via WebSearch synthesis) | https://www.centralbank.ae/en/news-and-publications/news-and-insights/press-release/cbuae-maintains-the-base-rate-at-3-65-1/
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

The CBUAE confirmed on Wednesday it is holding the Base Rate at 3.65%, unchanged since 17 June, mirroring the US Federal Reserve's own hold — as expected under the dirham peg and flagged as pending in yesterday's report. This closes out the rate-decision open loop with no surprise: no immediate MortgageCompare-relevant repricing expected.

Alongside it, Gulf bank H1 2026 results have now largely reported, and the divergence is the more interesting data point. Abu Dhabi Commercial Bank extended its growth streak to 20 consecutive quarters, with H1 profit before tax up 28%. Emirates NBD's profit before tax rose a slower 5%, First Abu Dhabi Bank's rose 3%, while Saudi's Al Rajhi Bank grew net income 14% against Saudi National Bank's 7%. That spread — ADCB and Al Rajhi pulling well ahead of Emirates NBD, FAB and Saudi National Bank — suggests loan-book composition and fee income are starting to separate winners from laggards more than the base-rate environment itself, which is identical for all of them.

**Signal for Orwell:** confirms the base-rate environment is stable and unlikely to move mortgage pricing near-term; the bank-earnings divergence is background context, not actionable for the comparison network today.

## Story 2: Dubai's H1 2026 property market: volumes down a third, price per square foot up, mortgages shifting to fixed rate

**Source:** AGBI / Khaleej Times (via WebSearch synthesis) | https://www.agbi.com/analysis/real-estate/2026/07/dubai-property-sales-tumble-but-market-settling-at-true-value/
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

More than 38,000 residential transactions closed in Q2 2026, down almost a third from the year-ago all-time high, with total sales value down nearly 40% to AED110.4bn. Despite the volume drop, price per square foot is up 6.5% — a market cooling in transaction count while holding (or gaining) on a per-unit basis, consistent with AGBI's framing of the market "settling at true value" after several years of rapid expansion rather than a genuine downturn. Average residential rental yields remain attractive at 6.58% (6.9% apartments, 5.1% townhouses, 4.5% villas).

On the mortgage side, banks are now offering one-year fixed rates from 3.75%, two-year around 3.78%, three-year around 3.95% — and buyers are increasingly locking in fixed terms to protect against future rate volatility rather than riding variable rates, a meaningful behavioural shift for anyone selling mortgage products into this market.

**Signal for Orwell:** directly relevant to MortgageCompare — the fixed-rate uptake trend is a live content and product angle (comparing fixed vs. variable products as demand shifts), and the volume-down/price-up divergence is worth citing accurately in any market-commentary content rather than working from older "boom" framing.

**Source:** Arab News / Kamco Invest (via WebSearch synthesis) | https://www.arabnews.com/node/2652711/business-economy

Kamco Invest data has Saudi Arabia leading GCC fixed-income maturities over 2026-2030 at $167.4bn, ahead of the UAE at $157.4bn and Qatar at $89bn — a rolling refinancing wall the region will need to manage as rates stay elevated. ## Story 4: Al Wathba becomes first UAE insurer with direct Lloyd's of London market access

**Source:** Atlas Magazine / Reinsurance News (via WebSearch synthesis) | https://www.atlas-mag.net/en/category/pays/emirats-arabes-unis
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Al Wathba Insurance has secured access to the Lloyd's of London market through the London Bridge 2 protected cell company structure, becoming the first UAE-based insurer to do so. This gives it a direct route to Lloyd's syndicated capacity and underwriting expertise rather than working solely through reinsurance intermediaries — a genuine capability upgrade for a UAE-domiciled insurer, and a sign of the market maturing toward international standards.

**Signal for Orwell:** a real, checkable data point for InsureCompare content on which UAE insurers are expanding international underwriting capacity — worth a line if the comparison network covers Al Wathba or discusses Lloyd's-backed products.
