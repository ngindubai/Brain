## Story 1: Second Sovereign Retail T-Sukuk profit rate confirmed at 5.06%, subscriptions open through 28 September

**Source:** Gulf News, corroborated by Khaleej Times, Zawya, Entrepreneur Middle East, Emirates 24|7 | https://gulfnews.com/business/banking/uae-sets-yearly-profit-rate-of-506-for-second-retail-t-sukuk-investments-1.500683993
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

The Ministry of Finance confirmed the profit rate on its second Sovereign Retail T-Sukuk issuance at 5.06% annually, well above the 4.30% rate on the first issuance earlier this year — though the tenor has also stretched from two years to five, so the two aren't directly comparable on rate alone. Subscriptions opened 23 September and run through 28 September, minimum investment Dh1,000, open to UAE citizens and residents, profit distributed semi-annually, Shariah-compliant, government-backed. The issuance is scheduled to list and begin trading on Nasdaq Dubai from 1 October.

This closes the open loop flagged in Tuesday's report cleanly, and it's genuinely useful for MoneyCompare: a government-backed, five-year, 5.06% fixed-income product beats most bank fixed-deposit rates and sits well above the CBUAE's 3.9% base rate, which makes it a strong comparison point against fixed-deposit and conservative investment content. The subscription window closes 28 September — four days out — so any content push needs to land inside that window to catch search interest while it's still actionable, not after the fact.

## Story 3: UAE corporate tax deadline now six days out, FTA continuing filing push

**Source:** Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/business/corporate-tax/uae-corporate-tax-deadline-2026-nears-fta-urges-filing-by-september-30-1.500660597
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]] (company formation angle)

Taxable persons with a financial year ending 31 December 2025 must file and pay via EmaraTax by 30 September — now six days out, down from eight in Tuesday's report. No extensions outside genuine emergencies (medical, natural disaster), and applications for those must be filed before the deadline, not after. Penalty structure is unchanged: AED 500/month for the first 12 months of delay, rising to AED 1,000/month thereafter, accruing from the day after the deadline with no grace period.

Same practical read as before, now sharper with the window closing: any company-formation content touching corporate tax should be pushing the 30 September deadline hard this week, since search interest on "UAE corporate tax deadline" typically peaks in the final days before a hard filing date. This is the last report before the deadline itself lands.
