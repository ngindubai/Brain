## Story 1: StepFun (Jieyue Xingchen) launches Step 5 Preview, an open-weight model at 1/8 the cost of Claude Opus 5

**Source:** KuCoin News Flash (aggregating StepFun/Jieyue Xingchen's own release) | https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/step-5-preview-released-by-jieyue-cost-is-1-8-of-claude-opus-5 ; additional detail via https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/step-5-preview-launches-with-600b-parameters-and-100k-token-context
**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]]

Chinese lab StepFun (corporate name Jieyue Xingchen) released Step 5 Preview today: a 600-billion-parameter model with 27 billion activated per inference, image input support, and a context window reported at up to 1 million tokens depending on source. It targets programming, software engineering and finance specifically, and lands in the top three of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index among open-weight models. The headline number is cost: Step 5 Preview runs at roughly 1/8 the price of Claude Opus 5, and StepFun says it will fully open-source the weights on 15 October.

This is the pattern that's been building all year rather than a one-off: a Chinese lab ships a model that benchmarks near the frontier at a fraction of the API cost, with a real open-source date attached rather than a vague promise. For anyone running AI-agent-heavy operations, the practical read isn't "switch models," it's that the cost floor for capable models keeps dropping, which matters directly for margin on any Orwell product that bills per-agent-task rather than per-seat. Worth a benchmark comparison against whatever's currently driving cost on the CRM and comparison-site agent workloads once the October open-source release lands and independent evals exist, rather than trusting the lab's own numbers today.

## AGENTIC WORKFLOW WATCH

The one clear item today: LangChain published "Jev-as-a-Judge for Agent Evals" (https://www.langchain.com/blog/jev-agent-evals-langsmith), a head-to-head test of Jev, a "System One" typed-output evaluator from TypeSafe AI, against LLM-judge approaches using GPT-5.6 Luna, Terra, and Claude Sonnet 4.6. The pitch: code-based evaluators only handle narrow deterministic cases, and LLM judges are slow, expensive and noisy (LangChain measured 92-913x higher variance than Jev across test cases). Jev instead returns calibrated probabilities and typed answers across three question types (Choice, Score, Noul) rather than generating free text to parse. LangChain's own numbers: 0.44 seconds and $0.00035 per call versus $28.17 total for the Claude-judge run across the same test set, and 100% accuracy on binary pass/fail decisions.

Read cautiously since this is LangChain's own benchmark of a partner product, not independent, but the direction matters regardless of the exact numbers: eval infrastructure that doesn't burn an LLM call per judgment is exactly the kind of "harness" layer LangChain has been pushing all month (see the open loop below), and it's directly relevant to any Orwell-internal agent-ops tooling that currently leans on an LLM-as-judge pattern for QA. No MCP or A2A protocol news today; the last substantive spec movement remains the 22 August roadmap update.
