## Story 1: OpenAI pauses new ChatGPT Pro sign-ups — Astra demand "unprecedented"

**Source:** TechCrunch, dated 10 September | https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/10/openai-puts-pro-subscriptions-on-hold-due-to-astra-demand/
**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]]

OpenAI has temporarily closed new sign-ups for its $200/month ChatGPT Pro tier, citing infrastructure strain from demand for Astra (GPT-6), which launched 3 September and which OpenAI has been calling the start of the "AGI era." Product lead Thibault Sottiaux said Pro accounts "put the most strain on its systems" and that "demand for Astra is really unprecedented... I've not seen anything like it until now." API, Go and Plus tiers remain open; no timeline has been given for reopening Pro, and OpenAI has not published daily sign-up figures to quantify the surge. This follows last month's Codex usage-limit increase, suggesting sustained rather than one-off demand growth.

For Orwell, the read is capacity risk rather than opportunity: a top-tier subscription from the market leader being paused on demand grounds is a reminder that frontier-model access, even paid access, isn't guaranteed to scale smoothly with usage — worth factoring into any plan that assumes Pro-tier OpenAI access will always be purchasable on demand.

## Story 2: Anthropic publishes its own numbers on Chinese distillation campaigns — nearly 200 million exchanges across five operations

**Source:** TechCrunch, dated 10 September | https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/10/anthropic-details-distillation-campaigns-from-alibaba-moonshot-ai-and-deepseek/
**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]]

A day after yesterday's NSA/CISA/FBI joint advisory (AA26-251A) named six Chinese firms in an industrial-scale distillation campaign, Anthropic published its own threat-intelligence detail: nearly 200 million exchanges linked to five separate campaigns, the largest an Alibaba-linked operation running 151 million exchanges between May and July (peaking at ~3 million a day, 3,500 accounts), and a Moonshot AI-linked campaign of roughly 300,000 requests over 10 days from 5,000 accounts Anthropic says were routed from Chinese military infrastructure. The technique: framing requests as translation tasks specifically to surface Claude's internal chain-of-thought reasoning, which Anthropic normally summarises rather than exposes raw.

This is the genuinely new piece on yesterday's open loop, not a re-telling: it puts hard numbers behind the "industrial scale" language the government advisory used, and confirms the extraction method (prompt-engineered reasoning-trace exposure) rather than leaving it abstract. It reinforces the standing point already on file: any high-volume, unusually uniform Claude API usage risks resembling this pattern to Anthropic's own defences, worth being aware of for anything Orwell ever runs at scale.

## Story 3: Salesforce previews "Enterprise AI Harness" and "AI Control Plane" for agent governance at scale

**Source:** SiliconAngle, dated 10-11 September | https://siliconangle.com/2026/09/10/salesforce-introduces-enterprise-ai-harness-ai-control-plane/
**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]], [[CRM Systems]]

Salesforce previewed two enterprise-agent offerings: Enterprise AI Harness, a six-part toolkit covering trusted data context, data-quality lineage, access control, cost/accuracy-aware model routing, deterministic reliability controls, and external-app integration; and AI Control Plane, a centralised dashboard for monitoring agent performance, inference cost and security-policy compliance across a fleet of deployed agents. Partial rollout starts now, with full availability slated for Salesforce's fiscal 2028 (from February 2027).

Grouped into the Agentic Workflow Watch below given its direct relevance to [[Agent Org]] design, but flagged here too because it's the second major vendor in a week (after Certinia's "System of Action," logged yesterday) shipping a dedicated governance/control layer for running many agents reliably rather than shipping a new agent framework — the standing pattern this report keeps returning to.

## Story 4: Meta's Muse hits No. 2 in the US App Store within 48 hours of launch

**Source:** TechCrunch, dated 10 September | https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/10/metas-ai-agent-muse-is-now-the-no-2-app-in-the-us/
**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]]

Muse, Meta's personal AI agent (launched 8 September on Muse Spark, covered in Wednesday's report), climbed from fourth to second on the US iOS App Store within two days, with over 83,000 downloads per Sensor Tower — a fast climb by consumer-agent standards, though still well behind Threads' 4.3 million launch-day downloads and modest on Google Play (No. 338 in Productivity). ## Story 5: OpenAI restricts rival AI tools from advertising inside ChatGPT, even as it opens ad inventory to Amazon

**Source:** Search Engine Land, dated 10 September | https://searchengineland.com/openai-reportedly-blocks-rival-ai-tools-from-advertising-in-chatgpt-488019
**Related vault notes:** none directly

OpenAI has quietly told advertising partners that competing standalone image- and voice-generation products (Adobe among them) can no longer advertise inside ChatGPT, while video-generation tools remain permitted — a distinction that happens to exempt the category OpenAI's own Sora product doesn't yet fully compete in while restricting the ones it does (image and voice). Separately, and the same day, Amazon began piloting ChatGPT ad inventory access through its own DSP, letting US advertisers buy text/image ad units beneath organic ChatGPT answers on a CPC/CPM basis, OpenAI retaining placement control.

The pairing is the story: OpenAI is simultaneously closing its platform to rivals it competes with directly and opening it to a non-competing ad-tech partner it needs for scale — standard platform-power behaviour once a product has enough distribution to monetise selectively. Nothing actionable for Orwell today, but a data point for [[SEO Portfolio]] and [[Comparison Network]] on how quickly conversational AI surfaces are adopting the same gatekeeping dynamics as search once did.

4. **Agents Trust Tools Too Much: Measuring Reliance on Unreliable Tools** — quantifies how readily an LLM agent over-trusts a tool's output even when the tool is known to be unreliable, a direct reliability risk for any agent role that calls external APIs or scrapers. (cs.AI, 10 September)
5. **Beyond Prompts: Measuring and Optimizing LLM Tool-Agent Harnesses** — proposes measuring the harness (the scaffolding around a tool-using agent) as its own optimisation target, separate from the underlying model or the prompt — relevant methodology for evaluating any Orwell agent role's actual production harness rather than just its prompt.
