## Story 1: Anthropic signs $9.1bn, 20-year power deal with Bitcoin miner Riot Platforms

**Source:** Bloomberg (via Investing.com, The Block, TradingView) | https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/anthropic-signs-91-billion-cloud-deal-with-riot-bloomberg-reports-4850722 · https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-08-10-riot-platforms-ai-deal-anthropic-411358

**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Priorities]]

Anthropic has agreed to buy 191 megawatts of compute capacity from Riot Platforms' Rockdale, Texas campus under a 20-year deal Riot expects to generate $9.1bn in revenue, with two five-year extension options that could push total value to $16.1bn. Riot — until recently a pure-play Bitcoin miner — has been converting mining capacity to AI data-centre hosting as the economics of GPU hosting outrun crypto mining. Delivery is phased: the first 96 megawatts by December 2027, the full 191 megawatts by June 2028, running through June 2048. AMD separately exercised an option to expand its own existing capacity deal with Riot the same day. Riot shares jumped 26% in after-hours trading once the Anthropic connection was confirmed as the "mystery" customer behind the deal.

The structural signal is the buyer, not the number. Crypto miners hold something labs now need more than GPUs themselves: grid interconnection and power contracts that took years to secure. Riot still has another 1GW campus at Corsicana under a letter of intent for a further tenant. Expect more of this pattern — AI labs buying capacity from whoever already has the power secured, regardless of what that company's original business was.

**Signal for Orwell:** Context for [[Ventures]]' GPU-compute thinking rather than a direct action item. The read-through: power and interconnection, not chip supply, is now the binding constraint on frontier AI capacity, and the buyers with the least conventional profile (crypto miners) are becoming the sellers with the most leverage. Worth tracking whether this repeats with other miners (Core Scientific, Cipher, etc.) as a signal of how tight the power market actually is.

## Story 2: OpenAI completes $7bn employee share buyback, holds valuation at $852bn

**Source:** TechCrunch | https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/10/openai-reportedly-completed-a-7-billion-employee-tender-offer/ · CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/10/openai-wraps-7-billion-share-sale-ahead-of-potential-ipo-.html

**Related vault notes:** [[Priorities]]

OpenAI has completed a tender offer letting current and former employees sell roughly $7bn of shares, funded by OpenAI itself rather than outside investors — a departure from earlier tenders that brought in Thrive Capital and SoftBank as buyers. The valuation held flat at $852bn, unchanged from its most recent funding round. Keeping the buyback self-funded avoids adding new names to the cap table just as OpenAI prepares for a possible IPO; the company filed confidentially with the SEC in June. That it didn't need fresh outside capital for a $7bn buyback signals ample cash on hand post its March raise.

**Signal for Orwell:** No direct action item, but useful context for reading OpenAI's competitive position against Anthropic: one lab spending cash to keep its equity structure clean pre-IPO, the other spending cash to lock in two decades of power capacity. Both are managing scale in different ways from Orwell's — worth noting only as scale calibration, not a signal that changes anything here.

## Story 3: TSMC's July revenue jumps 44.7% as AI chip demand outruns capacity

**Source:** CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/10/tsmc-revenue-surge-ai-chip-big-tech.html

**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]]

TSMC reported July revenue of NT$467.58bn ($14.5bn), up 44.7% year-on-year, with high-performance computing — the AI chip revenue line — now 66% of wafer revenue. The same day, South Korea announced a 5 trillion won ($3.52bn) fund targeting chip materials, components and fabless design firms specifically (not Samsung or SK Hynix directly), plus 5 trillion won in export trade finance, part of a wider $576bn semiconductor megaproject Seoul unveiled in June. Separately, Sony and TSMC confirmed a $6.32bn joint venture to build a Japan fab for next-generation image-sensing chips. Apple, meanwhile, is reportedly testing China-made memory chips for iPhones and MacBooks because it can't secure enough of its preferred supply — a sign the capacity crunch has reached even the most well-resourced buyers.

**Signal for Orwell:** Confirms the same upstream-constraint pattern as Story 1: capital is chasing fabrication, materials and power, not model training, because that's where the actual bottleneck sits. Nothing actionable for Orwell today, but keep reading every AI capex story through this lens — the ceiling for how cheap agentic AI gets is being set in fabs and power-purchase agreements right now, not in model releases.

## Story 4: Meta releases Muse Glimmer, a 30B open-weight model built for local agentic use

**Source:** Washington Post | https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/08/10/meta-ai-mark-zuckerberg-artificial-intelligence/266f5128-94d2-11f1-9ef9-1be722184483_story.html

**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]], [[Products]]

Meta shipped Muse Glimmer, a 30B-parameter dense multimodal model under a permissive Apache 2.0 licence, alongside a Zuckerberg manifesto arguing against AI capability concentrating under a handful of companies or governments. Glimmer is tuned specifically for local agentic tool use, coding and LLM-as-judge tasks, with a 131K context window and support for 100+ languages; 4-bit quantisation compresses it under 20GB, small enough to run on a single consumer GPU. Meta also confirmed developer access to a more powerful sibling, Muse Spark 1.2. Artificial Analysis published independent benchmarks the same day.

**Signal for Orwell:** Worth a look for any Orwell agent role that could run locally rather than via API — a 30B model that fits on one consumer GPU and is tuned for tool use and judge tasks is a genuine option for cost-sensitive, always-on agent roles (monitoring, triage, first-pass classification) where API spend adds up. Not urgent, but flag for the next Agent Org cost review.

## Story 5: Alibaba opens Qwen to third-party developers as an agent-building platform

**Source:** Caixin Global | https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-08-11/alibaba-opens-qwen-platform-to-outside-developers-in-push-to-build-ai-agent-ecosystem-102473024.html

**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]]

Alibaba launched the Qianwen Open Platform, giving third-party developers access to build custom AI agents across mobile, PC and smart glasses on top of Qwen — positioning Qwen less as a model and more as an agent-building ecosystem, following the Qwen3.8-Max release earlier this month. Separately reported: Alibaba is planning a revenue-sharing requirement for large commercial users of its next Qwen release rather than a flat free/open-weight model, a shift one commentator called "an API business model wearing an open source jacket."

**Signal for Orwell:** A second major lab (after OpenAI's GPT Store and Anthropic's own agent tooling) treating "agent platform" as the product, not the model underneath it. Consistent with the packaged-agent-skills trend flagged in recent reports — the competitive layer is moving up from raw model quality to who owns the agent-building surface.

## What this means for Orwell

Today's real story is where the money is going: Anthropic buying two decades of power from a Bitcoin miner, TSMC's AI revenue share climbing again, South Korea and Japan both throwing new state and joint-venture money at chip capacity. None of that is training-model news — it's infrastructure news, and it confirms the trend flagged repeatedly in recent reports that the constraint on cheap, abundant agentic AI is upstream in power and fabrication, not in model quality. On the product side, Meta's Muse Glimmer and Alibaba's Qwen platform both point the same direction: labs increasingly compete on who owns the agent-building surface rather than raw benchmarks, which matters directly for how Orwell should be thinking about build-vs-buy on agent tooling.
