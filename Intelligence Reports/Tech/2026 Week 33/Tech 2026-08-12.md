## Story 1: Anthropic signs $9.1bn, 20-year compute deal with bitcoin miner Riot Platforms

**Source:** Bloomberg | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-11/anthropic-strikes-9-billion-deal-with-cloud-computing-firm-riot · CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/11/riot-platforms-signs-anthropic-deal-as-miners-shift-to-ai-infrastructure-.html

**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]], [[Ventures]], [[Products]]

Riot Platforms — a bitcoin miner that only recently pivoted into selling AI data centre capacity — has signed a 20-year deal to supply Anthropic with 191 megawatts from its Rockdale, Texas campus, enough to power roughly 143,000 homes at any given moment. The contract runs to June 2048 and is expected to generate $9.1bn in revenue, with two five-year extension options that could push total value to $16.1bn. Riot brings capacity online in stages: 96MW by December 2027, the full 191MW by June 2028. Riot's shares jumped 25% on the news. This is the latest in a run of compute deals Anthropic has struck this year to keep pace with Claude demand it has struggled to fully serve — and the Riot angle specifically confirms a trend flagged in prior reports: crypto-mining infrastructure converting wholesale into AI data centre capacity, because the power connections already exist and the AI labs' hunger for megawatts now outbids anything crypto mining pays for the same power.

**Signal for Orwell:** Pure infrastructure-economics context rather than a direct action item, but it sharpens the picture for [[Ventures]]' GPU-compute thinking: the bottleneck really is power and siting now, not chip supply, and repurposed crypto-mining sites are becoming a recognised category of AI compute supply rather than a one-off.

**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]]

Brad Lightcap — OpenAI's COO since 2022, CFO for four years before that, and a Sam Altman colleague going back to Y Combinator — is leaving. **Signal for Orwell:** Not directly actionable, but worth holding as a data point on how much internal churn sits under the surface at the labs Orwell builds its whole stack on top of — a second data point this year, not yet a trend to draw conclusions from.

## Story 3: Google's Gemini app crosses 1 billion monthly users

**Source:** TechCrunch (Lauren Forristal) | https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/11/googles-gemini-app-surges-to-one-billion-users/

**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]], [[Products]]

Gemini becomes Google's 14th product to cross 1 billion monthly active users — a figure that counts only the standalone app, not AI usage folded into Search or other Google surfaces. That's up from 950 million reported at Google's Q2 earnings call just weeks ago, with daily actives tripling over the past year. Notable usage detail: 63% of Gemini users now engage via voice, and the app generates over 150 million images a day. This puts Gemini roughly level with ChatGPT, which crossed 1 billion MAU in June.

**Signal for Orwell:** Confirms the consumer AI-assistant market has genuinely become a two-horse race at scale (plus Claude at the frontier-capability end) rather than one dominant product — worth keeping in view for anything Orwell builds that depends on which assistant a client's own staff or customers already default to.

## Story 4: General Catalyst leads $1.1bn round into 2-month-old River AI, built around personally-trainable agents

**Source:** TechCrunch (Julie Bort) | https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/11/general-catalyst-leads-1-1b-round-into-2-month-old-river-ai/

**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]]

River AI, founded by ex-xAI co-founder Igor Babuschkin (also ex-DeepMind, ex-OpenAI), raised $1.1bn in seed/Series A funding led by General Catalyst and AMP PBC, with Nvidia, AMD Ventures, Y Combinator and Temasek also in. The pitch: rebuild the AI stack — training, models, product, hardware — around agents individual users can fine-tune and control themselves, rather than renting a fixed assistant someone else built. The API lets developers fine-tune open-weight models via reinforcement learning and LoRA, with a claimed 15-20 minute complex RL run and no dedicated infrastructure team required, at 2-4x the cost of closed-source alternatives.

**Signal for Orwell:** Worth a watching-brief entry rather than action — "personally-trainable agents you own the weights for" is a philosophically different bet to the humanless-company model (which relies on renting frontier capability, not owning smaller fine-tuned ones), but a well-funded team validating that bet is useful signal for where cheap, controllable agent capability might sit in 12-18 months.

## Story 5: An unreleased Anthropic model advances the bound on the Riemann Hypothesis

**Source:** TechCrunch (Russell Brandom) | https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/11/an-unreleased-anthropic-model-made-progress-on-one-of-maths-biggest-unsolved-problems/

**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]]

An Anthropic staffer with limited maths background prompted an unreleased model to "take a real stab" at the 150-year-old Riemann Hypothesis (a $1m Clay Institute problem), then let it run autonomously for 1.5 days. The run spun up 60 subagents testing 650 distinct approaches across 31 million output tokens — 2 subagents developed the key mathematical ideas, 13 contributed supporting ideas, 30 tried and failed, 13 validated the arguments, 2 drafted the paper. The result didn't solve the hypothesis but meaningfully raised the lower bound of where it's proven to hold, formalised in Lean and checked by Anthropic's in-house mathematicians. Reaction is split: signatories of June's Leiden Declaration worry about erosion of mathematical authorship norms; Fields Medallist Timothy Gowers pushed back, comparing it to how astronomical discoveries don't need to be credited to the telescope.

**Signal for Orwell:** The organisational shape of the run — dozens of subagents with distinct roles (ideation, validation, drafting) coordinated toward one goal over a day and a half unsupervised — is closer to a live case study of the multi-agent "org chart" pattern than most published research gets. Worth a look for [[Agent Org]] design reference: it's a real example of role specialisation and internal QA (13 dedicated validators) inside a single autonomous run.
