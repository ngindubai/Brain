## Story 1: Nvidia heads into its make-or-break print with the stock finally off the mat
**Source:** Yahoo Finance / Benzinga / Kiplinger live blog | https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/live/nvidia-earnings-live-updates-and-commentary-august-2026

Nvidia reports Q2 FY27 after the US close tonight (results will land in the small hours Dubai time, so nothing is confirmed as this report goes out — treat this as the set-up, not the outcome). Consensus sits at roughly $92-95bn revenue (up ~96-97% year-on-year) and about $2.08-2.09 EPS, against Nvidia's own guide of $91bn ±2%. Data centre revenue alone is expected near $85-87bn.

The stock snapped a seven-session losing streak on Tuesday, closing up 2.2%, and was edging higher again in Wednesday premarket trade — a partial unwind of the slide that took Nvidia down for its longest losing run since 2022. That slide was driven by reports that some of Nvidia's biggest customers had been warned of AI chip price hikes above 15%, and by broader investor nerves that AI infrastructure capex might be "peaking faster than expected." Micron, AMD and Broadcom all fell alongside it, and the semiconductor ETF (SOXX) shed 2.7% in the same stretch.

The setup is genuinely two-sided: beats have triggered post-earnings drops in six of Nvidia's last eight reports (four in a row most recently), because expectations are now so large that a clean beat-and-raise is treated as merely adequate. Watch commentary on Blackwell/Vera Rubin ramp pace, gross margin trend, and any read-through on China access (the offshore-rental loophole US regulators are chasing is a live undercurrent, see open loops).

## Story 2: OpenAI trims ChatGPT's model lineup as o3 goes dark today
**Source:** OpenAI ChatGPT release notes (via help.openai.com, fetched indirectly) | https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes

OpenAI o3 is formally retired from ChatGPT today, 26 August, closing a 90-day sunset window opened in May. The change is ChatGPT-only — the o3 API endpoint is untouched — but it's the clearest signal yet that OpenAI wants ChatGPT's user-facing model list simplified around GPT-5-class defaults rather than a sprawl of legacy reasoning models.

Alongside the retirement, OpenAI is rolling an updated GPT-5 Instant to ChatGPT users and has pushed GPT-5-Codex into the Responses API, making the agentic-coding-tuned variant available anywhere Codex runs (cloud tasks, code review, CLI, IDE extension) rather than only inside dedicated Codex surfaces. OpenAI is also previewing "Private Safety Processing," a mechanism designed to spot abuse patterns across related conversations without giving OpenAI staff access to the underlying content — a direct answer to the recurring criticism that safety monitoring and user privacy are in tension.

None of this is a single dramatic story, but together it reads as OpenAI doing quiet product hygiene the same week Nvidia's print could reset AI valuations — tidying the product surface while the market's attention is elsewhere. Worth a glance if any Orwell workflow still hard-codes o3 as a fallback model; check and repoint to a supported model if so.

## Story 3: Perplexity ships an on-device agent that runs your dev workflow locally
**Source:** SiliconANGLE | https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/25/perplexity-ai-launches-portable-computer-on-device-ai-agent/

Perplexity launched "Portable Computer," an on-device AI agent that decomposes multi-step tasks — building a web app, running data science, coding — into subagent chunks, verifies its own output, and only calls out to cloud models when local compute or context runs short (while explicitly protecting on-device files from ever leaving the box). Launch hardware is the Nvidia DGX Spark (Blackwell GPU, 20-core CPU, 128GB RAM), with Windows/RTX support coming next. It ships with open-source Qwen 3.8 27B and a Perplexity-tuned PPLX 27B variant, plus context compaction to work around the 256k-token window ceiling, and permissioned GitHub integration.

This lands squarely in the "local-first agentic computer use" lane that Claude Cowork, ChatGPT's agent mode, and various open-source computer-use stacks are all now competing in. The strategic tell is the DGX Spark exclusivity at launch — it reinforces the reporting from earlier this week that Nvidia is in talks to put real money and technology licensing behind Perplexity (the ~$30bn valuation conversation, still an open loop below). If that deal firms up, Nvidia gets a flagship on-device agent showcase for its own hardware, and Perplexity gets a distribution and compute lifeline.

Relevant to [[Agent Org]] as a data point on where "run this locally, escalate to cloud only when needed" architecture is heading — the same pattern worth considering for any Orwell agent stack that currently sends everything to a hosted model by default.

## Story 4: Stability AI's big three music-label backers put $76m behind the AI everyone loves to sue
**Source:** SiliconANGLE | https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/25/major-record-labels-amd-back-76m-round-for-stability-ai/

Stability AI closed a $76m round backed by Sony Music Group, Universal Music Group and Warner Music Group — the three largest record labels — alongside AMD Ventures and Electronic Arts. The money is earmarked for creative-professional tooling and growing applied research/professional services. Universal and Warner already have live partnerships with Stability on AI music production tools, and the funding follows Stability's May release of Stable Audio 3.0 (up to 6:20 of generated audio).

The notable thing is who's writing the cheque, not the size of it. The labels that spent 2024-25 suing generative AI companies over music training data are now co-funding one of them directly — the clearest sign yet that "license the labels' catalogue and cut them into the upside" has beaten "fight it out in court" as the majors' preferred strategy. AMD's presence is the other tell: chip vendors backing model companies to lock in inference workloads is now a running pattern (Nvidia-Perplexity below, Nvidia-OpenAI's Nemotron ecosystem, and others), and AMD has been comparatively quiet in this game until now.

## Story 5: India's Ringg raises again to push "voice AI" into "outcome AI"
**Source:** TechCrunch | https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/25/indias-ringg-gets-backing-from-peak-xv-as-it-pushes-voice-ai-past-the-phone-call/

Ringg (formerly DesiVocal, a text-to-speech company) closed a $10m Series A extension from Peak XV, taking its Series A to $15.5m. Ringg's platform now handles 20 million call attempts a month for enterprise customers including Cred, Flipkart, Practo, Groww, PolicyBazaar and Shell, covering appointment booking, cart recovery and KYC verification. Co-founder Siddharth Tripathi was candid about the pivot logic: pure voice-agent commodity use cases "are not sticky... it's always going to be a price game," so Ringg is repositioning as "a platform for agents that bring outcomes or get things done rather than voice agents" — chasing higher-value tasks like merchant onboarding and L1/L2 support where quality and consistency, not just price, win the deal.

Plans include expanding into chat/WhatsApp, building proprietary speech models, and targeting Global Capability Centres as a partnership channel, with 40+ hires underway focused on forward-deployed engineers and ML researchers.

This is a useful comparable for anyone running commoditised AI service lines (voice, chat, lead-gen): the "outcome, not channel" repositioning is the same lesson underneath the whole [[SEO Portfolio]] and [[Comparison Network]] thesis — the value is in the completed transaction, not the interface that delivered it.
