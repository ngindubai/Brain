## Story 1: Open-weight AI coalition grows from 25 to 35 signatories in a day

**Source:** ts2.tech, citing coalition tracking | https://ts2.tech/en/open-weight-ai-alliance-expands-by-40-while-nvidia-nasdaqnvda-microsoft-nasdaqmsft-meta-nasdaqmeta-approach-earnings-results/ (published 25 July 2026)
**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]], [[Ventures]]

Yesterday's report covered the 25-organisation open letter led by Nvidia, Microsoft and Meta urging Washington not to impose "premature restrictions" on open-weight AI models. Overnight the signatory list grew by roughly 40%, from 25 to 35 organisations, a fast expansion for a policy letter and a sign the coalition is actively recruiting rather than treating Thursday's launch as the finished product. No new substantive argument has been added; the growth itself is the news; it signals the industry sees this as a live fight worth pushing hard on ahead of any Commerce Department move on Chinese open-weight models.

This remains a thread to track rather than act on. If the coalition wins the argument, the field of cheap, usable open-weight models (Kimi, Llama, Mistral) that make sense for [[Ventures]]-style low-budget experimentation stays open. If Washington moves toward restriction regardless, that field narrows. Nothing changes this week either way.

## Story 2: TechCrunch's 2026 AI-layoffs tracker adds Monday.com

**Source:** TechCrunch | https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/25/the-running-list-major-tech-layoffs-in-2026-where-employers-cited-ai/ (updated 26 July 2026, 01:30 UTC)

TechCrunch's running list of 2026 tech layoffs that name-checked AI as a factor added Monday.com in its latest update, published in the small hours of 26 July. The list now runs well past 20 companies for the year, spanning software, media and logistics firms citing AI-driven efficiency as part of headcount reductions. It's a thin update on its own, one more entry on an existing tracker, but the list itself is a useful standing reference for how normalised "AI efficiency" has become as a stated reason for cuts, not a hypothetical trend piece.

No direct Orwell angle: the businesses in the portfolio are staying lean by design rather than cutting from a larger base, but it's a data point on how comfortable large employers now are attributing layoffs to AI publicly, worth keeping in view for hiring and market-positioning language on [[Products]] and client-facing work.
