## Story 1: IJCAI-ECAI 2026 main technical programme opens in Bremen — keynote on "The Social Future of Agentic AI"

**Source:** IJCAI 2026 official programme / Tech Times | https://2026.ijcai.org/at-a-glance2/ and https://www.techtimes.com/articles/324425/20260814/eu-ai-act-enforcer-joins-ijcai-ecai-2026-worlds-oldest-ai-conference-opens-saturday.htm
**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]], [[Intelligence Feed Sources]]

The 35th International Joint Conference on Artificial Intelligence moved from its workshop/tutorial phase (15–17 August) into the main technical programme today, running through Friday 21 August at Congress Centrum Bremen. Around 4,000 researchers and practitioners from 40+ countries are attending, with 990 accepted papers across the main and five special tracks (human-centred AI, AI for social good, AI-enabling critical tech, AI and health, AI and robotics).

Today's headline session was the keynote from Professor Nick Jennings of Loughborough University, this year's IJCAI Award for Research Excellence winner, titled "The Social Future of Agentic AI". Jennings is one of the founding figures of multi-agent systems research — his work from the 1990s on agent negotiation and coordination underpins a lot of what today's LLM-agent frameworks are quietly reinventing. A keynote from him specifically on the social and coordination risks of agentic AI, at the exact moment the industry is racing to ship autonomous multi-agent products, is a useful signal: the academic multi-agent community sees governance and inter-agent trust as the open problem, not raw capability.

Also notable per Tech Times' pre-conference coverage: a representative from the EU AI Office (the body now empowered, since 2 August, to investigate and fine GPAI providers) is attending — the first time an EU AI Act enforcer has had a visible presence at IJCAI. That is a small but real data point on regulators embedding themselves in research communities rather than just issuing rules from Brussels.

**Why it matters for Orwell:** nothing actionable today, but the Jennings keynote content (once slides/paper land, likely this week) is worth a look for Agent Org — Orwell's entire model is multi-agent coordination inside a single company, and the failure modes academic multi-agent research worries about (miscommunication, goal drift between agents, accountability when an agent acts on stale context) are exactly the failure modes a humanless company has to design against. Flagging for the next Agentic Workflow Watch to follow up once conference papers publish.

## Story 2: Baidu Q2 2026 earnings — AI-native revenue growing fast, legacy search ads still shrinking

**Source:** Baidu Investor Relations / multiple financial press | https://ir.baidu.com/news-releases/news-release-details/baidu-report-second-quarter-2026-financial-results-august-18
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Markets]]

Baidu reported Q2 2026 results before the US market open today. Wall Street had modelled roughly $4.74bn revenue and $1.46 EPS, with a mixed run-up of downward analyst revisions going in. The number the market is actually watching sits inside the segments: in Q1, Baidu Core's AI-powered business grew 49% year-on-year to RMB13.6bn ($2.01bn), and AI-native marketing services grew 36% YoY to RMB2.3bn ($341m) — while traditional online marketing (classic search ads) fell 22% YoY over the same period. Today's Q2 print is the next data point on whether that split (AI cannibalising Baidu's own legacy ad business while growing faster than it shrinks) is accelerating or stabilising.

This is the clearest live example anywhere of what AI-native search does to a legacy search-ads business, from a company that has both models simultaneously. Baidu is both a search engine under AI answer-engine pressure and an AI lab racing to build the thing replacing its own core business.

**Why it matters for Orwell:** direct relevance to the SEO portfolio. Baidu's Core segment is the world's largest live experiment in "what happens to search ad revenue when AI answers cannibalise the click", and the 22% YoY decline in traditional online marketing revenue (against 49% AI growth) is the sharpest concrete number yet for how fast that shift can move once it tips. Worth a line in the next SEO brief cross-referencing this against Google's own search/AI-mode trajectory — pet-transport, funeral-repatriation and the other lead-gen sites live or die on exactly this transition.

## Story 4: New governance protocol for signed AI agent mandates — "Mandato"

**Source:** arXiv cs.AI | https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14074
**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]]

Today's arXiv cs.AI batch included "Mandato: Protocol-Level Enforcement of Digitally Signed Mandates on AI Agent Actions with Cryptographically Chained Audit Trails" — a proposal for a governance proxy that sits between an LLM agent and the tools/actions it can take, enforcing that every action the agent performs is backed by a digitally signed "mandate" (a scoped, revocable authorisation) and logging every action into a cryptographically chained, tamper-evident audit trail.

This sits in the same lane as the Agentao paper covered yesterday (governed local-first runtimes for tool-using agents) — it's now a clear pattern across multiple independent research groups this month: agent governance/audit-trail infrastructure is the thing academia and industry are converging on simultaneously, ahead of (or in response to) regulators like the EU AI Office asking "how do you prove what your agent did and why".

**Why it matters for Orwell:** this is squarely Agent Org territory. A humanless company staffed by agents in every department needs exactly this — a way to prove, after the fact, which agent took which action under what authorisation, especially anywhere money, client data or public-facing content is involved. Worth reading the full paper before the next Agent Org design pass; cryptographically chained audit trails are a cheap, buildable pattern even without adopting the whole protocol.

## Story 5: "Demystifying Agent Skills: Why They Work — Until They Don't"

**Source:** arXiv cs.AI | https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14036
**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]], [[SEO Portfolio]]

Also in today's cs.AI batch: a paper analysing when skill-based enhancements (reusable, packaged capability modules bolted onto an LLM agent — the same pattern as Claude Skills) stabilise agent execution and when they silently break it, across different task contexts. The core finding, per the abstract-level summary, is that skills which are narrowly scoped to the exact context they were authored in perform reliably, but degrade sharply and unpredictably when the same skill is reused in an adjacent-but-different context — without any obvious failure signal to the operator.

**Why it matters for Orwell:** directly relevant. Gareth runs Claude Code with a live Skills library across multiple projects and repos. This paper is effectively a warning against assuming a skill built for one project (say, a CRM workflow) will generalise cleanly to a different one (say, a comparison-site build) without silent degradation. Worth a proper read and, if the finding holds up, worth auditing the Skills folder for over-general skills that quietly assume context they don't state.

## arXiv watch

- **Mandato: Protocol-Level Enforcement of Digitally Signed Mandates on AI Agent Actions** — a governance proxy that requires every agent tool-call to carry a signed, scoped authorisation and logs actions to a tamper-evident chain. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14074
- **Demystifying Agent Skills: Why They Work — Until They Don't** — finds packaged agent "skills" work reliably in the narrow context they were built for but degrade silently when reused elsewhere. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14036
- **HELIX: Model-Harness Co-evolution for Recursive Self-Improvement** — proposes jointly evolving an agent's underlying model and its surrounding harness/scaffolding, rather than treating them as separate layers. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13951
- **Second Thought: Reasoning in Parallel as LLM Agents Act and Observe** — lets an agent keep reasoning in a background thread while its current action is still executing/being observed, instead of blocking. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13667
- **A Graph-Based Reinforcement Learning Framework for Structured Drift Diagnosis and Recovery in Autonomous LLM Agents** (cs.MA) — targets diagnosing and correcting behavioural drift in long-running autonomous agents, a direct concern for any agent left running unattended for weeks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14109
