## Story 1: Israeli government agency built a fake think tank to train AI chatbots on its Gaza narrative

**Source:** Responsible Statecraft | https://responsiblestatecraft.org/israel-influence-chatgpt/
**Author:** Nick Cleveland-Stout | **Published:** 17 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~7 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Per the granularity rule this is a catch-up: it carries yesterday's date and had not yet been reported.

Responsible Statecraft reports that a self-styled "Hanover Institute for Public Policy," which has published more than a hundred reports on Israel-Palestine topics since 6 August 2026, is not an independent research body at all but a production run by Israel's own Government Advertising Agency. The site carries no bylines and mimics academic form, footnotes, tables of contents, a flat neutral tone, and its questions are phrased the way a chatbot user would type them ("What Caused Palestinian Displacement in 1948?"). Run through GPTZero, eleven of twelve randomly sampled articles were flagged as AI-written with high confidence. A disclaimer buried on the site itself acknowledges the Israeli government connection.

Cleveland-Stout traces the production chain through Piro, Inc., a firm co-founded by Daniel Rosenberg, and Havas Media, the French PR group handling subcontracting, with Department of Justice FARA filings recording $900,000 paid for the work. A LinkedIn post from Rosenberg, cited directly, describes the approach as "reverse-engineering" how AI systems select and weight sources, the clearest on-record admission that the goal is not to reach human readers but to seed the training and retrieval corpus that chatbots draw on when asked about the conflict. NewsGuard analyst Alice Lee is quoted assessing the operation's design. The piece situates the find alongside earlier Politico reporting and a Drop Site News investigation into related Israeli influence operations, suggesting this is one node in a wider effort rather than a one-off.

The evidentiary core, that the Hanover Institute is an Israeli government production rather than an independent think tank, rests on the site's own buried disclaimer and DOJ FARA filings, both primary documents rather than anonymous sourcing. The AI-generation finding rests on one detection tool's output on a small sample (eleven of twelve), a reasonable but not definitive method. Rosenberg's "reverse-engineering" framing is his own public statement, not disputed or explained further by any of the named parties.

## Story 3: MI5 and MI6 fight to keep themselves outside Britain's new Hillsborough Law

**Source:** Declassified UK | https://www.declassifieduk.org/can-andy-burnham-make-mi5-respect-the-hillsborough-law/ (site inaccessible; reconstructed from search-engine snippets, no full-text access achieved)
**Author:** Richard Norton-Taylor | **Published:** 17 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~6 min (estimated)

Declassified UK's own site and its /feed/ path returned HTTP 403 again today, the twenty-fourth consecutive failure since 20 July, so this account is built from search-engine snippets and corroborating third-party reporting rather than a direct read of the piece.

Norton-Taylor, the Guardian's former security editor now writing for Declassified UK, argues that holding MI5 accountable will be the toughest test of any Whitehall "rewiring" push, framing it through Andy Burnham's long campaign for a Hillsborough Law after the 1989 stadium disaster. The Public Office (Accountability) Bill, which imposes a statutory duty of "candour, transparency and frankness" on public officials and criminalises misleading the public, passed the Commons in July, but senior MI5 and MI6 officers pushed to have the intelligence agencies exempted wherever they judge disclosure would damage "national security." A government amendment would have made any intelligence-officer cooperation with future inquiries conditional on sign-off from the head of their own service, a provision campaigners and some Labour MPs treated as gutting the point of the law; the government pulled the final debate after the backlash. Bereaved Hillsborough families argue MI5 and MI6 should be fully covered, citing cases where the Security Service is said to have supplied false information to past inquiries.

The evidentiary core here, the wording of the government's proposed carve-out amendment and the sequence of the bill's passage and pulled debate, is independently corroborated by mainstream reporting (the Washington Post's 14 July piece on the Commons vote, HuffPost UK's explainer) surfaced via search, so the underlying facts are solid. What could not be verified today is Norton-Taylor's own analysis and framing, since neither the original article nor a republication could be read directly, only reconstructed from search snippets and summaries. Treat the framing, not the underlying facts, as the weaker part of this account.

**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Byline Times reports that Reform UK's Nadhim Zahawi told ITV's Good Morning Britain that comedy Clacton by-election candidate Count Binface had received "£180,000 from Dale Vince, who is a big Labour supporter," and that Binface's campaign had drawn help from Conservative and Labour activists. Both named parties denied it on the record: Count Binface (comedian Jon Harvey) called the claim "untrue," saying "Dale Vince has never donated any money to me"; Vince told Byline Times directly it was "untrue, totally false, a fantasy" and that he had donated nothing. ITV broadcast an on-air correction the following morning confirming Vince had once offered support that was never taken up, and that Binface denied soliciting other parties' activists. Mortimer traces the claim's origin to a 9 July Telegraph report describing a donation offer that evidently never materialised into an actual payment.

The evidentiary core rests on on-record denials from both people Zahawi's claim was about, plus a broadcaster's own on-air correction, about as solid a basis as a fact-check gets. This is the fourth Byline Times piece since late July tracking Reform UK's finances and public claims (23 July Farage-funder story, 7/8 August Tice loan-conversion story, 11 August leaked training-material story), continuing a standing beat rather than opening a new one.
