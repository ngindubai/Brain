## Story 1: Pentagon's own civilian-harm staff call its Yemen boat-strike report "a total whitewash"

**Source:** The Intercept | https://theintercept.com/2026/08/11/war-department-civilian-casualties-boat-strikes-yemen/
**Author:** Nick Turse | **Published:** 11 August 2026, 3:51pm ET | **Read time:** ~7 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Per the granularity rule this is a catch-up: it carries yesterday's date and had not yet been reported.

The Intercept reports that the Pentagon's unreleased annual Section 1057 report on 2025 civilian casualties records zero civilian deaths from US boat strikes, the campaign of attacks on alleged drug-smuggling vessels that has run since 2025, despite independent monitors documenting over 220 deaths from the same strikes. Two anonymous government officials briefed on the report's contents gave Turse its findings on air and naval operations more broadly: 153 civilians killed and roughly 250 wounded across Operation Rough Rider in Yemen, only three incidents formally determined to have "likely" caused civilian harm, and fifteen further Yemen cases still under review. One strike on an Ethiopian migrant-detention centre alone killed 68 people.

Both officials were blunt about the document's credibility. One called it "a fucking joke"; the other called it "a total whitewash" and "sickening," adding "they have absolutely no credibility." One raised a forward-looking concern: with the civilian-harm office still unable to close out its Yemen caseload, it has no visible capacity to process the much larger volume of cases expected from the Iran conflict, asking "how will they get through hundreds of cases for Iran if their one civilian harm person still hasn't finished Yemen?"

Turse sets the Pentagon's zero-boat-strike finding against corroborating outside material: Yemen Data Project figures of 238-plus killed and 467-plus injured, an Amnesty International report on the detention-centre strike, a Pentagon Inspector General report from May 2026 that already found failures in the department's civilian-protection processes, a July letter from Democratic lawmakers to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth demanding answers, Airwars monitoring data on the Iran conflict, and a World Health Organization estimate of over 3,375 deaths in that war. Hegseth is named as having overseen cuts to the civilian-harm programme's staffing and remit. A Pentagon spokesperson's only on-record response was "we have nothing to preview or announce at the moment."

The evidentiary core, the boat-strike death toll and the internal assessment of the report's reliability, rests on two anonymous officials with direct knowledge of an unreleased government document, corroborated in scale (though not in the specific zero-deaths finding) by independent monitors including Yemen Data Project and Airwars. The reporting does not have the unreleased report itself, only officials' description of its contents, and the Pentagon has not confirmed or denied the figures on the record.

## Story 2: Leaked Reform UK training materials warn activists their own members are the party's biggest liability

**Source:** Byline Times | https://bylinetimes.com/2026/08/11/reform-uk-tells-its-activists-dont-drink-and-tweet-because-the-biggest-threat-to-reform-is-the-people-in-it/
**Author:** Josiah Mortimer | **Published:** 11 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~6 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Per the granularity rule this is a catch-up: it carries yesterday's date and had not yet been reported.

Byline Times reports on leaked material from Reform UK's internal "Centre of Excellence" digital training catalogue, produced for activists between July and August 2025. Trainers tell members directly that "the biggest threat to Reform is the people in it" and instruct them to vet every social media post as if it will end up "sat on the desk of an anti-Reform journalist". Further guidance in the leaked materials tells canvassers to ignore "no junk mail" signs while leafleting and to remain "unidentifiable" while doing so, to avoid political commentary on personal social media altogether, and describes deliberately delaying by-election announcements to maximise the party's campaigning window. The vetting process described for prospective candidates and activists checks online profiles only, not criminal records.

Mortimer pairs the leaked training material with Reform UK's own 2025 accounts: donations of £22.87 million, up from £5.86 million in 2024 and concentrated among a small number of large donors including cryptocurrency-sector backers, against almost £9 million spent on "office costs" the piece says the accounts do not itemise further, and a reported £10.2 million surplus. No Reform representative is quoted responding to either the leaked training content or the spending breakdown; the piece is built entirely on the leaked documents and the party's own filed accounts rather than on-record comment.

The evidentiary core, the wording of the leaked training material, sits on documents Byline Times says it has obtained rather than public filings, so their authenticity rests on the outlet's own sourcing rather than independent corroboration; the financial figures, by contrast, come from Reform UK's own Companies House and Electoral Commission filings and are independently checkable. No party response is recorded to either strand.

## Story 4: US quietly expands its military footprint in Puntland, Somalia, as Red Sea tensions build

**Source:** Responsible Statecraft | https://responsiblestatecraft.org/us-military-base-somalia/
**Author:** Blaise Malley | **Published:** 11 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~6 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Per the granularity rule this is a catch-up: it carries yesterday's date and had not yet been reported.

Responsible Statecraft reports that the top US special operations commander for Africa has signed a security agreement with the president of Puntland, Somalia's semi-autonomous northeastern region, to expand the American military installation at Bosaso. The only public confirmation is a statement from the Puntland government itself, saying the expanded base will "improve operations against terrorism and to help protect maritime security"; there is no public text of the agreement, and US Africa Command has not confirmed the arrangement. The piece notes the deal appears to bypass Somalia's federal government in Mogadishu, which is not reported as a party to it.

Named analysts supply the interpretive framing the primary documents don't provide. Samar Al-Bulushi of UC Irvine and the Quincy Institute reads the deal's informality as strategically deliberate, preserving room for the US to avoid full sovereign accountability for what happens at the base. Cameron Hudson, a former National Security Council director for African affairs, says the US now appears to want "a kind of wholly owned, wholly operated" facility rather than a shared one. Michael Woldemariam of the University of Maryland argues the move is "really only understandable in terms of the Red Sea question," tying it to the wider contest over access near the Bab al-Mandab Strait following the Iran conflict and ongoing Houthi activity in the Red Sea.

The evidentiary core, that an agreement exists and covers a base expansion at Bosaso, rests entirely on the Puntland government's own public statement; neither AFRICOM nor Washington has confirmed, denied or detailed it, and Mogadishu's position is not recorded at all. The strategic significance attached to the move, that it is Red-Sea-driven and structured to sidestep Somali federal sovereignty, is analysts' informed reading rather than anything stated in the agreement or by US officials, since no agreement text is public.
