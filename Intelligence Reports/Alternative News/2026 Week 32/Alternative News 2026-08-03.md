## Story 1: Metropolitan Police reopens war crimes probe into Britons who fought for Israel in Gaza

**Source:** Declassified UK, republished in full by ScheerPost | https://scheerpost.com/2026/07/31/war-crimes-case-re-opened/ (original at declassifieduk.org returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch, as it has since 18 July)
**Author:** John McEvoy (Declassified UK chief reporter) | **Published:** 31 July 2026 | **Read time:** ~5 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Per the granularity rule this is a catch-up, not today's story: it carries a 31 July date and only surfaced today because Declassified UK's domain remains inaccessible and a full third-party republication (ScheerPost, crediting the original and linking back) had not previously been located.

The Metropolitan Police has reversed an April 2026 decision to close its investigation into British nationals accused of committing war crimes while serving in the Israeli military in Gaza. The reversal follows a threat of judicial review from the Public Interest Law Centre (PILC), which submitted a 240-page referral in August 2025 alongside the Palestinian Centre for Human Rights (PCHR). That referral named ten UK nationals and alleged offences including wilful killing, extermination, attacking civilians, and forcible transfer, drawing on Geneva Convention grave-breach categories. The Met had narrowed its focus to four identifiable individuals before telling the claimants in April that no effective investigation could be mounted and there was no realistic prospect of conviction. In correspondence with PILC described as arriving "last week" relative to the article's 31 July date, the force said it will now carry out "a fresh scoping exercise" on those four.

McEvoy's reporting draws on Freedom of Information data showing 2,069 Britons have served in the Israel Defense Forces since October 2023, a petition exceeding 26,000 signatures from public figures calling on the UK government to formally track citizens serving in the IDF, and on-record comment from UN special rapporteur-adjacent human rights expert Chris Sidoti and veteran human rights barrister Michael Mansfield KC, both pressing the Met to draw on the full range of available evidence, explicitly including intelligence-service material and satellite or surveillance imagery, rather than relying solely on what claimants can independently gather.

The piece sits inside Declassified UK's established beat connecting UK state and civil institutions to accountability gaps around British involvement in the Gaza war, a line this routine has tracked through the outlet's earlier reporting on the Fairford ammunition resupply chain (written up 1 August) and its own prior Met Police referral coverage (logged in the source-health file on 24 July, before the block set in properly). The story's real interest for this register is procedural rather than novel: it is a UK police force reversing itself under legal pressure rather than new evidence of wrongdoing being unearthed, and the piece is transparent that the "fresh scoping exercise" language stops well short of a reopened criminal investigation.
