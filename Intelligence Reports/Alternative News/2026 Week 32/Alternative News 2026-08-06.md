## Story 1: UK Charity Commission opens inquiry into eight charities over Israeli settlement funding, following Declassified UK's own reporting

**Source:** Declassified UK, recovered via full republication on Palestine Uncensored, corroborated by Middle East Eye and Middle East Monitor | https://www.declassifieduk.org/uk-charities-face-inquiry-over-israeli-settlement-funding/ (direct fetch returned HTTP 403, as it has since 18 July; full republication at https://www.palestineuncensored.org/uk-charities-face-inquiry-over-israeli-settlement-funding-2026/) | corroboration: https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/uk-charity-regulator-investigate-donations-illegal-israeli-settlements, https://www.middleeastmonitor.com/20260805-uk-charity-regulator-opens-inquiry-into-funding-of-illegal-israeli-settlements/
**Author:** Phil Miller | **Published:** 5 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~5 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Per the granularity rule this is a catch-up, not today's story: it carries yesterday's date and had not surfaced when the 5 August report was written.

The UK Charity Commission has opened a statutory inquiry into eight England and Wales-registered charities suspected of channelling money to illegal Israeli settlements in the West Bank. The regulator's own announcement follows a formal complaint from Labour MP Melanie Ward alleging that 32 charities had donated at least £28 million to settlement-linked causes, and it lands directly on the back of Declassified UK's own prior undercover reporting on Shivat Zion, an Israeli organisation that solicited UK donations via Gift Aid (worth an extra 25 percent on top of each gift) and routed them through a linked UK charity, UK Toremet Ltd, while its staff were recorded telling supporters about the settlement of Efrat in the West Bank. Two of the charities the Commission is now examining are alleged, per Middle East Monitor's figures, to have sent roughly £5.7 million to a yeshiva in Susya and roughly £200,000 to one in Hebron.

Charity Commission assistant director Stephen Roake said on the record that "serious allegations have been made about UK charities operating in illegal Israeli settlements in Palestine, so our first step is to establish the facts." Labour MP Grahame Morris said he was "appalled that UK taxpayers may have been funding an organisation that helps people move to illegal Israeli settlements" and called for the government to act swiftly. UK Toremet's own spokesperson maintained its grants comply with English charity law and were made at full trustee discretion. Middle East Eye separately reports the Commission has opened more than 200 regulatory cases connected to Gaza-related concerns since October 2023, putting this inquiry inside a much larger and growing caseload rather than a one-off.

The evidentiary core, an official statutory inquiry opened by a UK government regulator, a named MP's formal complaint, and prior undercover reporting establishing the donation-routing mechanism, is solid and multiply sourced; the £28 million and 32-charity figures rest on Ward's complaint rather than independent Charity Commission confirmation at this stage, and the story could not be independently checked against Declassified UK's own page, which remains blocked, so the byline and precise wording are as carried by the crediting republication rather than verified against the primary source directly.

## Story 2: Undercover investigation exposes a paid fraudulent-copyright racket used to remove critical journalism from social platforms

**Source:** OCCRP | https://www.occrp.org/en/news/i-terminated-this-channel-inside-the-fake-copyright-racket-silencing-news-outlets
**Author:** Monitori and OCCRP | **Published:** 5 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~6 min

Per the granularity rule this is a catch-up: it carries yesterday's date and had not surfaced when the 5 August report was written.

OCCRP, working with the Georgian outlet Monitori, reports on an underground market in which operatives charge $3,000 to $5,000 to strip critical reporting off social platforms by filing fraudulent copyright complaints. Monitori's own YouTube channel was taken down after three of its videos, covering luxury goods, sanctioned judges and tax policy, were hit with false infringement claims; pages belonging to the Kyrgyz outlet Radio Azattyq were also removed by the same method. A reporter posed as a prospective client and made contact via WhatsApp with the operative behind the claims, an India-based individual identified as Aditya Singh, who confirmed "I terminated this channel" and, once identified as a journalist rather than a client, offered to withdraw the complaints for $380 per video. Citizen Lab researcher Alberto Fittarelli is quoted describing the mechanics of the abuse.

The story's real value is showing how thin the barrier is between a platform's copyright-enforcement tooling, designed for genuine infringement, and a commercial service for silencing reporting: Singh operated through a referral network of unnamed intermediaries and gave no indication of who was ultimately commissioning the takedowns of Monitori's and Radio Azattyq's material, leaving the paying clients unidentified.

The evidentiary core, the WhatsApp exchange in which Singh confirms responsibility and prices the withdrawal, plus the platform-side record of the takedowns themselves, is strong and directly documented via undercover contact; the identity and motive of whoever ultimately paid for the takedowns of Monitori's and Radio Azattyq's content remains unestablished, which the piece is upfront about.

## Story 3: Field report documents two years of Russian drone attacks on Kherson civilians, now feeding a crimes-against-humanity case

**Source:** Byline Times | https://bylinetimes.com/2026/08/05/two-years-on-the-world-witnesses-human-safari-in-kherson/
**Author:** Zarina Zabrisky | **Published:** 5 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~8 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Per the granularity rule this is a catch-up: it carries yesterday's date and had not surfaced when the 5 August report was written.

Byline Times reports from Kherson on what Ukrainian officials and residents call a "human safari": first-person-view drones deliberately hunting civilians in the city and surrounding region, a tactic now documented for more than two years. The immediate news hook is a video, released by Kherson Police, of a drone chasing a vegetable seller named Yuri through a farmers' market; Zabrisky interviewed Yuri hours after the attack, and the reporting crew itself came under drone fire while working in the field. The piece draws on three UN reports, a Human Rights Watch investigation, and figures from Ukraine's Prosecutor General: 7,787 recorded FPV drone attacks on civilians in 2025 and 2,614 more in the first seven months of 2026, with July 2026 alone accounting for 29 civilians killed and 395 injured in the Kherson region. Ukrainian prosecutors have opened more than 16,000 criminal cases and put the cumulative toll, through 2026, at 967 civilians killed, including 10 children, with nearly 7,000 people affected in total.

Prosecutor General Ruslan Kravchenko is quoted on the documentation effort, which the piece reports is now being assembled into Ukraine's first crimes-against-humanity case targeting senior Russian political and military leadership specifically over the drone-hunting pattern, rather than individual strikes treated as isolated incidents. The piece also notes the tactic has since spread beyond Kherson to Donetsk, Dnipropetrovsk, Sumy, Kramatorsk and Zaporizhzhia.

The evidentiary core, the volume and consistency of the attack data across UN, Human Rights Watch and Ukrainian prosecutorial sources, independently corroborated by the reporter's own on-the-ground experience of drone fire, is strong; the claim that this specific pattern is being built into a crimes-against-humanity case rests on the Prosecutor General's own statement rather than an independently verifiable court filing, so treat the prosecution's exact legal framing as reported rather than confirmed by a filed document.
