## Story 1: Israel is sponsoring ads on Ted Cruz's podcast, and the money goes to a pro-Cruz super PAC

**Source:** Responsible Statecraft | https://responsiblestatecraft.org/ted-cruz-israel/
**Author:** Connor Echols and Nick Cleveland-Stout | **Published:** 22 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~5 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Per the granularity rule this fills the 22–23 August gap, no report ran either day.

Responsible Statecraft reports that the Israeli government is buying advertising on Senator Ted Cruz's podcast "Verdict with Ted Cruz" as part of a "$7 million 'I am Israel'" tourism campaign launched in June, aimed at persuading Christian listeners to visit biblical sites. The ads, fronted by co-host Ben Ferguson, carry no disclosure that "Visit Israel" is a foreign government tourism ministry. The mechanism that makes this more than an ordinary ad buy: iHeartMedia, which has syndicated Cruz's show since 2022, routes ad revenue from the programme not to Cruz but to Truth and Courage PAC, a super PAC supporting him, a structure that has already paid the PAC at least $1.7 million in iHeartMedia digital revenue since 2023.

Campaign finance lawyer Craig Holman told the outlet that, absent a formal earmarking agreement between Israel and the PAC, the arrangement sits in a legal grey zone: "the money is considered iHeartRadio funds, with which it can do as it pleases." The piece notes the FEC has already cleared Cruz once on a similar complaint, finding no evidence he "solicited, directed, received, transferred, or spent the funds." The reporters set this against Cruz's record of pro-Israel and Christian Zionist positioning, past six-figure support from Miriam Adelson, and his own on-record acknowledgment that AIPAC "raises a lot of money for me," and against Israel's wider, well-documented push to shore up support among American evangelicals as younger evangelicals drift away.

The evidentiary core, the ad campaign's existence and value, the iHeartMedia-to-PAC revenue arrangement, and the $1.7 million cumulative PAC total, rests on the campaign's own stated budget, iHeartMedia's disclosed revenue-sharing structure, and the PAC's own filings, independently checkable financial records rather than anonymous sourcing. The "is that legal" framing in the headline is accurately hedged in the body: Holman's own read is that no clear violation exists without a formal earmarking agreement, and the FEC has already ruled favourably for Cruz on a comparable complaint once. This is a story about a loophole, clearly presented as such, not an allegation of a proven violation.
