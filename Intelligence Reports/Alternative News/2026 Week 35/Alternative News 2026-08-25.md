## Story 1: A CENTCOM official confirms the Pentagon blacklists journalists, then threatens to add one to it live on the phone

**Source:** The Intercept | https://theintercept.com/2026/08/24/pentagon-military-journalist-blacklist-press-media/
**Author:** Nick Turse | **Published:** 24 August 2026, 5:52am ET | **Read time:** ~7 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Reporting on CENTCOM's silence over a failed Iran strike and alleged cover-ups of US casualties, Turse got CENTCOM executive assistant Richard Allee to admit, unprompted and by name, that the command keeps a list of journalists to ignore, then told Turse directly: "You'll be put on a list of people to disregard." Asked to confirm that was a threat, Allee said "Yes." Asked which reporters were already on it, he declined to say but conceded "it's not a list that we publicize." CENTCOM's press office later denied by email that any "blacklist" exists. A separate anonymous US official told Turse that War Secretary Pete Hegseth's office has standing orders not to engage with him at all, and would deny the list if challenged, which is exactly what happened.

Turse has form here: US Africa Command blacklisted him in 2018 after he exposed torture of prisoners held in Cameroon, with an officer telling him at the time "we don't consider you a legitimate journalist, really." The piece sets this single admission inside Hegseth's wider record: evicting the New York Times and NPR from their Pentagon offices, a no-unauthorised-newsgathering pledge for credentialed reporters, mandatory escorts, the closure of a decades-old press room, and internal polygraphing to hunt leakers. Seth Stern of the Freedom of the Press Foundation makes the significance explicit: "This should put to rest the Pentagon's prior claims that its anti-press policies are somehow content neutral."

The evidentiary core is about as strong as this register gets: a named official's own words, given knowingly on the record, followed by an official denial that directly contradicts them, plus an independent anonymous source corroborating the pattern of stonewalling. There is no reconstruction or inference here, Allee said what he said.

## Story 2: The journalist ICE shot in the arm has now been held over 300 days, longer than any US journalist on record

**Source:** The Dissenter | https://thedissenter.org/detained-for-300-plus-days-journalist-shot-by-ice-could-lose-arm/
**Author:** Kevin Gosztola | **Published:** 24 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~5 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Carlitos Ricardo Parias, a Los Angeles citizen journalist who reported on TikTok as "Richard LA," was shot in the arm by an ICE agent during an October 2025 vehicle stop after agents boxed in his car and broke its windows. He was charged with assaulting a federal officer; a judge dismissed the charge in December, finding the government had denied him access to counsel before trial. ICE detained him anyway and moved him to the Adelanto ICE Processing Center, where he remains, now surpassing the previous record for longest US journalist detention (Josh Wolf, 226 days) by more than three months.

Congresswoman Sydney Kamlager-Dove told Congress in July that Parias's hand had turned purple with atrophied fingers; doctors are now weighing amputation against risk of bone infection. Family video shows him continuing to describe contaminated water conditions inside the facility despite his situation. Gosztola frames this against a wider pattern the outlet tracks of immigration enforcement used against people documenting it.

Sourcing rests on a federal judge's own dismissal order, on-record congressional testimony, and family-sourced video, a solid documentary basis, though it stops short of an independent medical assessment of the amputation risk, which comes from the family's account of doctors' concerns rather than a named clinician on record.

## Story 3: A celebrated study on treating opioid-addicted pregnant women was flawed enough for a fraud lawyer to call the misrepresentation "not minor"

**Source:** ProPublica | https://www.propublica.org/article/craig-towers-knoxville-tennessee-newborn-withdrawal-study
**Author:** Amy Yurkanin | **Published:** 24 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~8 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Dr Craig Towers, a University of Tennessee high-risk obstetrician, published a study finding naltrexone helped pregnant opioid users stay clean, work that shaped clinical practice across several states and drew dozens of citations. A university audit obtained by ProPublica found he never got the required institutional review board approval, supplied "inaccurate and/or unverifiable, contradictory source documentation" when asked, withheld his underlying database, and described a study as prospective that was actually retrospective, meaning cases were assembled after the fact rather than tracked as they happened. The American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology issued a 78-word correction three years after publication rather than retracting it; publisher Elsevier says that met its standards.

Eight addiction and pregnancy specialists compared notes and wrote to the university in 2020; six of the eight would only speak to ProPublica anonymously, but two went on record. Research-fraud attorney Eugenie Reich reviewed the audit and called it plainly: "The misrepresentation is not minor. It runs throughout the piece." Retraction Watch co-founder Ivan Oransky put the journal's light-touch response bluntly: "You have a situation where the fox is guarding the henhouse." A former patient, Katie Carringer, told Yurkanin she felt pressured into naltrexone despite being drug-free for an extended stretch; Towers denies pressuring any patient. Dr Mishka Terplan, one of the two named specialists, put the stakes in plain terms: "each day the article(s) is out there, potential harm to a vulnerable population accumulates."

The evidentiary core, the audit's findings on IRB non-compliance and contradictory documentation, is drawn from a university's own audit report and an IRB determination citing "serious noncompliance," both primary institutional documents. The six anonymous specialists weaken the piece's attributability somewhat, but two named experts corroborate on the record, and Towers's own denial is included rather than omitted.

## Story 4: Two dozen ex-lawmakers and staff cashed out to defence contractors, one of them now overseeing the Navy programmes he lobbies on

**Source:** Responsible Statecraft | https://responsiblestatecraft.org/defense-contractors-revolving-door/
**Author:** Julian Cooper | **Published:** 24 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~6 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

A Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists report tracked 31 former Capitol Hill lawmakers and staff, mostly off the House and Senate Armed Services Committees, into defence-industry jobs since 2015. The sharpest case: Chris Vieson, once staff director of the House Armed Services Committee, now sits as a commissioner on the National Commission on the Future of the Navy advising on unmanned-systems modernisation, while simultaneously lobbying for two unmanned-vehicle startups, Saronic Inc. and Whiskey Project Group, whose business is that exact modernisation. Former representatives Mike Gallagher and Jeff Miller now work for Palantir; Christian Brose is chief strategy officer at Anduril. The same report flags STOCK Act disclosure violations by sitting Armed Services members trading defence stocks: Austin Scott (GE Aerospace), Lisa McClain (SpaceX, via her husband, ahead of its IPO), and Tommy Tuberville (Lockheed Martin, Honeywell).

The evidentiary core sits on federal lobbying disclosures, stock-trading records, and public commission appointments, independently checkable public filings rather than leaked material or anonymous sourcing, a solid basis even though the piece itself is a secondary write-up of the Bulletin's underlying report rather original document-mining by Responsible Statecraft.

## Story 5: Israel is buying ads on Ted Cruz's podcast through a structure built to dodge a law against paying senators directly

**Source:** Responsible Statecraft | https://responsiblestatecraft.org/ted-cruz-israel/
**Authors:** Connor Echols, Nick Cleveland-Stout | **Published:** 22 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~5 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

iHeartMedia has syndicated Ted Cruz's podcast since 2022 under an arrangement that, because it cannot legally pay a sitting senator directly, instead routes advertising revenue into Truth and Courage PAC, Cruz's own re-election vehicle, at least $1.738 million since 2023. Israel's ministry of tourism has now bought into that stream: a $7 million "I am Israel" campaign launched in June runs ads on Cruz's show promoting Christian pilgrimage tourism to biblical sites, without disclosing that Visit Israel is a foreign government entity. Craig Holman of Public Citizen says the arrangement likely survives US campaign-finance law's ban on foreign nationals funding candidates only because Israel never explicitly earmarked its ad spend for the PAC, calling it a "court-created loophole" traceable to Citizens United. The FEC has already dismissed a prior complaint against Cruz over the iHeartMedia structure, finding no evidence he personally solicited or directed the funds.

The reporting explicitly frames this as targeting evangelical Christians, a bloc whose support for Israel is measurably softening, which the authors connect to the same push covered in this desk's 18 August report: an Israeli government advertising agency separately built a fake think tank to seed pro-Israel content into AI chatbot training data (see Threads).

The evidentiary core, the PAC donation total and the campaign's cost, comes from campaign-finance filings and the Israeli tourism ministry's own disclosed budget, solid public-record sourcing. The legality question is explicitly presented as unsettled, a named expert's interpretation of a grey area rather than a settled finding, which the piece is careful not to overstate.

## Story 6: Leaked emails show Moscow spending hundreds of thousands of dollars a year running a Kremlin culture centre inside Belarus

**Source:** OCCRP | https://www.occrp.org/en/news/moscow-funds-pro-kremlin-influence-hub-in-belarus
**Authors:** Alexander Yarashevich (Buro Media), Alena Koroleva (OCCRP) | **Published:** 24 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~6 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

More than 270,000 emails from the Moscow House of Compatriots, obtained by Danish broadcaster DR and analysed by OCCRP's Belarusian partner Buro Media, show Moscow budgeted over $250,000 for 2026 alone to run the House of Moscow in Minsk, with prior annual event budgets above $400,000 between 2021 and 2023. The centre hosted exhibitions promoting narratives about "Ukrainian neo-Nazi crimes in Donbas" and conferences pitching investment in annexed Crimea. Investigators identified 13 contracts worth roughly $270,000 between the Moscow House of Compatriots and a Minsk events firm founded by Sergei Lushch, who separately leads organisations pushing closer Belarus-Russia integration. EU-sanctioned speaker Mikhail Palei, linked to pro-war fundraising, also appears in the material. A parallel Kremlin-linked fund, Pravfond, put over 20 million roubles into a Minsk legal-advice centre that specifically guides people on doing business in occupied Ukrainian territory.

The evidentiary core rests on a large leaked email cache cross-referenced against named contracts and named recipients, one of the stronger documentary bases in this register. The framing quote, that Moscow "views soft power as one of the tools for transforming Belarus," is attributed to an unnamed analyst rather than drawn from the leaked material itself, worth noting as the one softer element.

## Story 7: Leaked MoD briefing shows a Trump-linked drone firm going from 40 to over 150 UK staff in eighteen months

**Source:** openDemocracy | https://www.opendemocracy.net/anduril-trump-linked-us-defence-firm-eyes-uk-military-contracts/
**Author:** Ethan Shone | **Published:** 21 August 2026, updated 24 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~6 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Anduril Industries, the autonomous-weapons firm founded by Palmer Luckey with Peter Thiel backing and Donald Trump Jr as an investor via 1789 Capital, has expanded its UK headcount from around 40 to over 150 in eighteen months. A government briefing note prepared for then-investment minister Poppy Gustafsson, obtained by openDemocracy, shows Anduril initially refused to invest in the UK without guaranteed contracts and government co-investment. The Ministry of Defence has since awarded it £2.5 million to develop "Wingman" autonomous drones, on top of existing UK work: a £30 million Ukraine drone-delivery contract, Border Force software and surveillance towers, and RAF base security systems, alongside Project ASGARD and its Lattice command-and-control software. New hires include former UK Space Command head Major General Paul Tedman CBE and staff advertising classified-intelligence and multi-billion-pound portfolio backgrounds on LinkedIn. Khem Rogaly of the Transition Security Project makes the throughline explicit: "Anduril should not be mistaken for a 'UK-based company.'"

The evidentiary core, the government's own reluctance and the co-investment terms it eventually agreed to, comes from an internal briefing document openDemocracy obtained directly, primary-source material rather than reconstruction. The staffing and hiring detail is drawn from public LinkedIn profiles and official contract announcements, both independently checkable.

## Story 8: Angela Rayner has taken cash and speaking fees from a firm that pitches investors on war-zone reconstruction

**Source:** openDemocracy | https://www.opendemocracy.net/angela-rayner-takes-cash-from-war-profiteering-advisory-firm-courting-labour/
**Author:** Ethan Shone | **Published:** 21 August 2026, 1:54pm | **Read time:** ~4 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Signum Global Advisors, run by Charles Myers, has given Angela Rayner £20,000 in recent months, on top of a £10,000 speaking fee in March that Rayner donated to charity, and hosted her at its "Westminster Day" event. Signum's business is arranging investor access to reconstruction opportunities in conflict and post-conflict states; Myers has described a hypothetical US strike on Venezuela as "a major infrastructure play. I could be as big as $500bn over the next 10 years." The firm has also courted Labour figures Jonathan Reynolds, Lucy Rigby, Angela Eagle and Dan Tomlinson, and, on the other side of British politics, Reform's Richard Tice and Robert Jenrick. Because Signum operates outside statutory lobbying registration, none of this access has to be publicly disclosed as lobbying.

This is the thinner piece in today's set: it establishes the donation and the pattern of access but does not allege any specific decision Rayner made in exchange for it, nor does it quote her or a spokesperson in response.

The evidentiary core, the donation and speaking-fee amounts, is drawn from the statutory register of MPs' financial interests and party donation disclosures, independently checkable public records. The characterisation of Signum as courting political access for undisclosed clients is the reporter's own framing of the pattern rather than a specific documented instance of influence being exercised.
