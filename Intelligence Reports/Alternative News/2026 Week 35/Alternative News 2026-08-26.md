## Story 1: Every regional commander told Hegseth to keep the Pentagon's civilian-casualty programme. He scrapped it anyway

**Source:** ProPublica | https://www.propublica.org/article/hegseth-civilian-harm-mitigation-response-trump-defense-department
**Authors:** Megan Rose, Hannah Allam (research: Kirsten Berg) | **Published:** 25 August 2026, 11:05am | **Read time:** ~8 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

An internal poll memo reviewed by ProPublica shows all nine US regional combatant commands were asked in early 2025 to choose between preserving, scaling back, or eliminating the Civilian Harm Mitigation and Response (CHMR) programme, the unit built after years of botched drone strikes to reduce civilian deaths. Every command chose to keep it; two asked for it to be kept in full. Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth eliminated it anyway. A Central Command memo warned the cut would cause "decreased efficiency of U.S. lethal operations due to an increased risk of target misidentification." Months later, the Pentagon's own 2025 annual report recorded 153 civilian deaths and 243 wounded from the Yemen campaign alone. Eleven Democratic senators wrote to the department in April raising the pattern; Under Secretary of Policy Elbridge Colby then proposed partially restoring the programme at reduced capacity. Hegseth, defending the Yemen operation elsewhere, called it a "clear, limited mission executed with ruthlessness, full stop." Current and former officials spoke to ProPublica only anonymously, citing fear of retaliation from the administration.

The evidentiary core, the commanders' actual poll responses and the CENTCOM memo, is drawn from internal Pentagon documents ProPublica obtained directly, corroborated by the department's own published casualty report and a documented congressional letter. The anonymous-official quotes describing the retaliation climate are the one part resting on unnamed sourcing, disclosed as such rather than hidden.

## Story 2: A kidney transplant paid for by an Indian mining firm, then a $160 billion mine handed over without a bidding process

**Source:** OCCRP | https://www.occrp.org/en/investigation/a-kidney-transplant-ignored-advice-and-a-presidents-surprise-choice-to-run-a-160-billion-pacific-island-mine
**Authors:** Michael E. Miller, Carmel N. Pilotti (OCCRP), with Aubrey Belford, Allan Gioni | **Published:** 25 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~9 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Bougainville President Ishmael Toroama bypassed the official selection process run by state-owned Bougainville Copper Ltd (BCL) and handed development rights to the Panguna copper-and-gold deposit, valued at roughly $160 billion, to Lloyds Metals and Energy, an Indian company with little mining experience that BCL had already flagged as under-qualified against five rival bidders (BCL had recommended Chinese firm CMOC instead). Toroama told OCCRP directly that Lloyds managing director Balasubramanian Prabhakaran had offered to pay for his wife Betty's kidney transplant in India, performed in December 2025; a Coimbatore Kidney Centre administrator, Kabilan Natarajan, confirmed "Lloyds" covered the cost. Toroama signed the memorandum of understanding with Lloyds weeks after accepting the offer, and insists the two were unconnected. Ten days before that signing, BCL's chairman sent a confidential letter warning the move would "discredit BCL." Lloyds had signed a non-disclosure agreement in April 2025 then gone quiet, never filing a formal expression of interest, before an unauthorised helicopter visit to the mine pit with Toroama in June 2025, corroborated by two anonymous sources with direct knowledge. Lloyds also offered to build a hospital in Arawa and funded trips to India for Bougainvillean officials.

Panguna sits at the centre of Bougainville's push for full independence, with a referendum expected by the end of this month; landowners whose legal consent is required were not meaningfully consulted, and some have already blocked drilling.

The evidentiary core is strong: Toroama's own on-record admission, a named hospital administrator confirming payment, and a documented confidential BCL warning letter. The unauthorised helicopter visit rests on two anonymous sources, disclosed as such, and Toroama's denial of any quid pro quo is included rather than omitted.

## Story 3: Sixty more US soldiers wounded in the Iran war, and a government official calls the Pentagon's slow count "an accounting trick"

**Source:** The Intercept | https://theintercept.com/2026/08/25/military-soldiers-wounded-casualties-iran-war/
**Author:** Nick Turse | **Published:** 25 August 2026, 11:15am ET | **Read time:** ~6 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

The Defense Casualty Analysis System now records 776 US troops wounded or killed in the Iran war, sixty more than the last public tally, with 345 of those casualties occurring since 7 July, when a second ceasefire collapsed. Government contracting documents on sam.gov analysed by The Intercept show the Pentagon separately soliciting additional casualty and mortuary services, consistent with an undercount still working through the system. An anonymous US government official told Turse the department wanted "the heat on them to slow down after the really devastating attacks" and called the reporting pattern "an accounting trick." Hegseth's office declined to comment. Named among the dead are Pvt. Isabella Gonzales, Sgt. Benjamin Pennington and Maj. Sorffly Davius.

The evidentiary core, the DCAS figures and the sam.gov contracting documents, is independently checkable official and procurement data. The "accounting trick" characterisation rests on a single anonymous official, the one soft point, though it is consistent with the pattern this desk covered on 24 August (see the CENTCOM blacklist story in yesterday's report) of the same department stonewalling casualty reporting on this war.

## Story 4: Declassified files show Britain trained the Russian army during the Chechnya war, and one trainee was later expelled as a spy

**Source:** Declassified UK | https://www.declassifieduk.org/when-the-uk-trained-the-russian-army-and-a-russian-spy/ (site returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch; reconstructed from search-engine snippets, no republication route found)
**Author:** Mark Curtis | **Published:** 25 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~5 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

National Archives files show Britain ran an "intensive military cooperation" programme with Russia in September 1995, training thirty-seven Russian army officers in Britain, including driving tanks and firing weapons, funded by the UK under John Major's government, at the same time Russian forces were killing civilians in the first Chechen war. A Ministry of Defence document states the programme aimed to help Russia develop "well-trained, democratically accountable forces" and that it was "important not to isolate Russia at present," with the contacts programme continuing "bearing events in Chechnya very much in mind." One of the thirty-seven officers named in the files, Maxim Nikolaevich Yelovik, listed as a lieutenant in a Russian motor rifles unit, is identified by Curtis as the same individual later posted to London as a defence attaché for a decade before being expelled from the UK in 2024, denounced by then home secretary James Cleverly as an "undeclared military intelligence officer."

The evidentiary core rests on primary declassified Ministry of Defence and National Archives documents, a strong documentary basis consistent with Declassified UK's usual standard. This particular writeup is weaker than the site's normal sourcing only in the access sense: it is reconstructed entirely from search-engine snippets rather than a direct read of the full piece, since neither the original nor a republication could be reached, so some framing and quotation beyond what is captured here may exist in the original that this summary cannot confirm.

## Story 5: A sanctioned Russian cargo ship has been running an unexplained shuttle route through West African ports since March

**Source:** Bellingcat | https://www.bellingcat.com/news/africa/2026/08/25/patria-sanctioned-russian-vessel-russia-us-africa-cameroon-gabon-lagos-nigeria/
**Author:** Bellingcat Investigation Team, with Youri van der Weide, Galen Reich, Yörük Işık | **Published:** 25 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~6 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

The cargo vessel Patria, sanctioned by the US, Ukraine and Canada, has shuttled four times since March between Douala, Cameroon, and Owendo, Gabon, with two additional stops off Lagos, Nigeria, and time spent off Equatorial Guinea. Bellingcat cross-referenced Planet Labs and Copernicus Sentinel-2 satellite imagery against Lloyd's List Intelligence AIS tracking data, and used draught measurements (the ship sitting lower when loaded, higher when unloaded) to show cargo was consistently offloaded at Douala and loaded at Owendo, ruling out an ordinary point-to-point trading pattern. The vessel's listed owner and manager are Hanro Shipping and Sakhalin Shipping Company; prior reporting has linked the Patria to weapons deliveries supporting Russia's Africa Corps operations in Mali. Former US naval officer Charlie Brown told Bellingcat the pattern, "repeated regional port calls, extended periods at anchor, and an apparent absence of a normal point-to-point trading cycle," is "atypical for a merchant vessel."

The evidentiary core, satellite imagery cross-referenced against independently sourced AIS data and physical draught measurements, is a solid open-source methodology with a documented chain of evidence. The conclusion that this indicates sanctions evasion or military logistics rather than ordinary shipping is inference from the pattern, clearly flagged as such by the named naval expert rather than asserted as fact.

## Story 6: Kazakhstan's new parliament was elected in what a press-freedom group called "grave silence, as quiet as a graveyard"

**Source:** OCCRP | https://www.occrp.org/en/news/kazakhstans-election-brings-a-new-parliament-and-more-pressure-on-journalists
**Author:** Olga Likhogray | **Published:** 25 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~5 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Kazakhstan's first election for its restructured unicameral parliament (the Kurultai) returned the pro-presidential Adilet party with 71.17% of roughly 6.5 million votes on a claimed 74% turnout, under a new proportional-representation system that favours party lists over independent candidates. Constitutional changes bundled into the same reform reset President Tokayev's term count, clearing the way for another run in 2029. Nazira Darimbet of human rights group Adil Soz described the campaign period as passing "in grave silence, as quietly as a graveyard"; Diana Okremova of the Legal Media Center said "everyone was simply afraid to say or do anything out of line," and had her own Facebook account blocked during the period. OCCRP documents specific cases: Orda editor Gulnara Bazhkenova sentenced to eight years on embezzlement and false-information charges; Azamat Omarov arrested for "inciting social, national, racial, or religious discord" after posting satirical videos; and travel bans imposed on journalists Lukpan Akhmedyarov and Dinara Yegeubayeva.

The evidentiary core, the named journalists' sentences, arrests and travel bans, is drawn from documented legal cases and named on-record testimony from two press-freedom organisation heads, a solid basis. The turnout and vote-share figures come from Kazakhstan's own Central Election Commission, official but not independently audited in this piece.

## Story 7: White phosphorus, bulldozers and glyphosate: how Israel is making southern Lebanon uninhabitable

**Source:** Responsible Statecraft | https://responsiblestatecraft.org/israel-destroy-south-lebanon/
**Author:** Janet Abou-Elias | **Published:** 25 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~7 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Since late 2024, Israeli operations in southern Lebanon have combined three tactics documented separately by outside investigators: white phosphorus shelling that Al Jazeera tied to forest fires destroying centuries-old olive groves and contaminating soil with heavy metals, with Lebanon's National Council for Scientific Research estimating $25 billion in environmental damage for 2023-24 alone; systematic village demolition, with a Guardian review documenting mass single-blast detonations in Taybeh, Naqoura and Deir Seryan after Defence Minister Israel Katz said all border-village homes would be destroyed, comparing the plan explicitly to Gaza; and glyphosate herbicide spraying across eleven miles of the border in early 2026, with Lebanon's agriculture ministry measuring concentrations 20 to 50 times normal agricultural levels, a chemical the WHO classifies as "probably carcinogenic to humans." CNN satellite imagery has separately identified new Israeli forward operating bases built inside Lebanese territory. UN human rights experts characterised the overall pattern as "consistent" with the Gaza campaign. Green Southerners' Dr Hisham Younes called it a "systematic process of destruction and place erasure." An estimated 600,000 residents cannot return to destroyed villages.

The evidentiary core draws on multiple independent investigators, Al Jazeera on phosphorus and fires, the Guardian on demolitions, Lebanon's own agriculture ministry lab testing on herbicide levels, and CNN satellite imagery on new bases, a genuinely corroborated multi-source picture rather than a single outlet's claim. The $25 billion damage estimate comes from a single Lebanese government research body and has not been independently verified elsewhere in this reporting.

## Story 8: Conservative legal groups back left-wing Minneapolis protesters, arguing ICE targeted them for their politics

**Source:** The Intercept | https://theintercept.com/2026/08/25/minneapolis-ice-protesters-undercover-agents/
**Author:** Matt Sledge | **Published:** 25 August 2026, 4:10pm ET | **Read time:** ~5 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

The Cato Institute, the Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression, and the Society for the Rule of Law filed a joint amicus brief backing defendants in the "Minneapolis 15" case, arguing federal prosecutors' investigation originated from Donald Trump's National Security Presidential Memorandum 7 (NSPM-7), issued after the assassination of activist Charlie Kirk, which the groups say "uses the domestic terrorism label to justify criminal investigations into disfavored groups." Their brief states: "The limited public disclosures here confirm the investigation for this case originated under NSPM-7." Court filings referenced in the piece show ICE agents infiltrated mainstream organisations including labour unions and the Sunrise Movement, and earlier Intercept reporting is cited describing undercover ICE agents encouraging confrontational tactics among protesters. Federal prosecutors dispute the framing, arguing the defendants actively conspired to shut down federal facilities and were "openly hostile" toward peaceful demonstrators. A magistrate judge is due to consider a disclosure request on the underlying investigation this Thursday.

The evidentiary core, the existence and content of the amicus brief and the underlying court filings on ICE infiltration, is drawn from primary court documents, solid sourcing. The characterisation of NSPM-7 as the investigation's actual origin is the amicus groups' own legal argument, not yet ruled on by any court, and the piece includes the prosecution's contesting position rather than presenting the brief's claim as settled fact.
