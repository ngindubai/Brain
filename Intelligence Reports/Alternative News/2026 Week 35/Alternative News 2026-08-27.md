## Story 1: An Army factory took $533 million and produced not one usable artillery shell

**Source:** ProPublica | https://www.propublica.org/article/army-artillery-factory-fiasco-takeaways
**Author:** Jesse Coburn | **Published:** 26 August 2026, 6:00am | **Read time:** ~7 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

The US Army paid General Dynamics $533 million to build an artillery-shell factory in Texas using untested production equipment from Turkish manufacturer Repkon, fast-tracked outside normal competitive bidding to expand shell output for Ukraine. Since opening in 2024 the plant has not produced a single shell that passed specification testing. Robotic arms caught fire and swung uncontrollably, a forging device meant to shape shell noses instead left "soft-serve ice cream" swirls in the steel, and workers resorted to sledgehammers to force repairs. The building itself cracked and flooded. The Army kept paying anyway, including a $26.3 million "progress payment" in December 2025 for production lines that still did not work, and the same General Dynamics division has since been awarded $2.5 billion in new contracts. A former Army official called it "an absolute disaster." The Army's own statement points to "rigorous oversight" and says it is "evaluating contract performance, seeking recoupment of funds"; General Dynamics says the reporting "fundamentally mischaracterizes the circumstances."

The evidentiary core rests on 36 interviews, including 12 former factory workers speaking on the record about conditions inside the plant, internal company documents, a Defense Department Inspector General report, and photographs and video from inside the facility. The Army's and General Dynamics's on-record denials are included rather than omitted. The one softer element is the "absolute disaster" characterisation, which comes from a single anonymous former official, though it is consistent with the on-record worker accounts and the documented lack of a passed specification test.

## Story 2: Idaho has let faith-healing parents watch fifteen children die since 2015, and the law protecting them was written on purpose

**Source:** ProPublica (co-published with The Oregonian/OregonLive) | https://www.propublica.org/article/faith-healing-idaho-deaths-followers-of-christ and https://www.propublica.org/article/idaho-law-faith-healing-exemption
**Author:** Audrey Dutton | **Published:** 25 and 26 August 2026, 5:00am | **Read time:** ~12 min combined
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

ProPublica gathered coroner and law-enforcement death records from five Idaho counties and shared redacted versions with more than a dozen medical specialists, who found at least fifteen children of the Followers of Christ sect died of treatable conditions between 2015 and 2025: untreated bacterial heart and lung infections, a pituitary tumour, sepsis from infections antibiotics would have cured. Five sets of parents lost more than one child, a pattern not previously reported. Caldwell church elder Nathan Kangas, who lost his wife, his last child in labour and three grandchildren, told Dutton the sect's view plainly: "It isn't necessarily that we're supposed to have a joyous life here. It's actually quite the opposite." Retired Idaho paediatrician David Peterman asked the obvious question: "How is this tolerable?" A companion piece traces why the law protects this: Idaho's 1971 and 1976 rewrites, following a wave of Christian Science-backed lobbying and a 1974 federal mandate later reversed under Reagan, added exemptions for parents choosing "prayer or spiritual means alone," at a time most other states have since stripped such exemptions out. Idaho's legislature has instead rejected repeal attempts every year since 2014.

The evidentiary core is unusually strong for this kind of story: primary death records independently reviewed by more than a dozen named medical specialists working from the same case files, on-record quotes from a sheriff, retired physicians and the sect's own elder, and a documented legislative record spanning 1887 to the present. Nothing here rests on anonymous sourcing; the weight is entirely in named experts and public records.

## Story 3: Israeli soldiers arrested the workers building a fence to protect a Palestinian home, and let the settlers who attacked it walk free

**Source:** The Intercept | https://theintercept.com/2026/08/26/settlers-israel-soldiers-west-bank-siege-qusra/
**Author:** Kavitha Chekuru | **Published:** 26 August 2026, 12:27pm ET | **Read time:** ~6 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Palestinian-American homeowner Loui Abu Ridi hired workers to build a protective fence around his house in Qusra, in the West Bank, after months of settler attacks that began in January 2026. Security-camera footage reviewed by The Intercept shows Israeli soldiers arriving alongside the settlers on 4 August, blindfolding and restraining the fence workers while the settlers moved freely around the property; Loui's brother Qusai, who witnessed it, says a soldier told him "Shut up, not a word, do not say a word." An Israeli military spokesperson confirmed troops are "permanently stationed" in the area but declined to address the detention. UN and human-rights-group data cited in the piece put settler attacks in 2026 at roughly 190 a month. US Ambassador Mike Huckabee has separately called the settlers "terrorists," while the State Department declined specific comment on this incident.

The evidentiary core, multiple pieces of security-camera footage reviewed directly by The Intercept plus two named eyewitnesses, is solid documentary and testimonial sourcing. The military's own confirmation that troops were present, paired with its refusal to address the actual detention, functions as a partial corroboration rather than a denial.

## Story 4: Malta's accused mastermind says "zero" involvement in a journalist's murder, and a Dubai shell company sits in the middle of the money trail

**Source:** OCCRP | https://www.occrp.org/en/news/i-had-no-involvement-zero-accused-mastermind-denies-killing-maltese-journalist
**Author:** Julian Bonnici | **Published:** 26 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~6 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Yorgen Fenech, on trial for ordering the 2017 car-bomb murder of investigative journalist Daphne Caruana Galizia, testified he had "no involvement, zero," while admitting he funnelled roughly €500,000 to middleman Melvin Theuma and passed him leaked police intelligence, including advance warning of raids. Prosecutor Godwin Cini pressed him directly: "Who are you trying to fool? Your fingerprints are all over this." The case draws in former prime ministerial chief of staff Keith Schembri, whom Fenech accuses (Schembri denies it, and police say he was never a suspect), and former energy minister Konrad Mizzi, both linked in evidence to 17 Black, a Dubai-registered shell company owned by Fenech that prosecutors say was designed to funnel millions into Panama-based companies tied to the two men. Confidential Europol files recovered from Fenech's phone and internal police records showing leaked raid warnings form part of the evidence against him.

The evidentiary core, recovered phone data, internal police documents and direct courtroom testimony under cross-examination, is strong and largely documentary. Fenech's own denial and his account of the payments are necessarily self-interested testimony from a defendant, which the piece presents as such rather than as settled fact.

## Story 5: Armenia's ex-president and his son were detained, then a court ordered the son held as the corruption probe widened

**Source:** OCCRP, reporting from Hetq | https://www.occrp.org/en/news/armenia-detains-ex-president-kocharyan-in-corruption-probe and https://www.occrp.org/en/news/armenian-court-orders-son-of-ex-president-held-in-sweeping-graft-probe
**Author:** Samson Martirosyan (Hetq) | **Published:** 25 and 26 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~6 min combined
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Armenia's Anti-Corruption Committee detained former president Robert Kocharyan and his son Sedrak on 25 August, alleging Kocharyan used his 1998-2008 term to acquire state property below market value and launder the proceeds through affiliated companies; four others were held alongside them. A Yerevan court ordered Sedrak held pending trial the next day, as investigators alleged wire transfers to him totalling roughly $392 million, €77 million and 18 million roubles between 2001 and 2019, and opened parallel tax-evasion inquiries into family-linked businesses including Toyota Yerevan and Orange Fitness. Nine others face related charges, including a former prime minister and a former Yerevan mayor. Kocharyan's office called it "another attack by the authorities against President Kocharyan and his family"; his lawyer, Alexander Kochubayev, called the case an "extralegal prosecution." A previous, similar bribery case against Kocharyan was dismissed in court for insufficient evidence.

The evidentiary core so far rests on the Anti-Corruption Committee's own statements and court filings; the specific wire-transfer figures are attributed to investigators rather than to independently obtained banking records at this stage. Kocharyan's denial and his history of a prior dismissed case are both included, useful context given Armenia's record of using corruption cases against former ruling-party figures.

## Story 6: A US-brokered Libya peace push is running into assassinations and drone strikes it didn't plan for

**Source:** Responsible Statecraft | https://responsiblestatecraft.org/libya-trump/
**Author:** Khaled Mahmoud | **Published:** 26 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~5 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Massad Boulos, Trump's adviser for Arab and African affairs, has pushed through a unified Libyan budget for the first time in thirteen years and staged joint military exercises as part of a US-led stabilisation push. That effort is now colliding with events on the ground: violent protests over living conditions have hit cities controlled by Prime Minister Abdul Hamid Dbeibeh in the west, drone strikes on energy facilities in Zawia prompted General Electric to evacuate technical staff from a power plant, and Khalifa Haftar's military intelligence chief, Major General Fawzi al-Mansouri, was assassinated near Benghazi by unidentified assailants. UN envoy Hanna Tetteh warned the Security Council of "institutional fragmentation" and resource mismanagement. Boulos says the attacks "underscore the need for unified, professional military and security institutions," but the piece argues his initiative risks legitimising militias rather than dismantling them if security issues stay secondary to political deal-making.

This is analysis rather than original document-based reporting: it draws on a UN Security Council briefing and public statements from Boulos and Libyan political figures, all independently checkable, but the central argument about the initiative's design flaw is the author's own interpretation of the pattern rather than a documented internal finding.

## Story 7: A new book argues the Israel lobby's money has grown as its persuasive power has shrunk

**Source:** Responsible Statecraft | https://responsiblestatecraft.org/israel-lobby-book/
**Author:** John B. Judis | **Published:** 26 August 2026 | **Read time:** ~5 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Reviewing Eli Clifton and Ian Lustick's "Israel's Lobby: America in the Grip of a Foreign Power," Judis lays out the book's documentary spine: since Citizens United, pro-Israel groups have shifted from direct campaign contributions to super-PAC spending, with the United Democracy Project alone spending $104 million between January 2025 and July 2026, and nine megadonors contributing over $1.7 billion since 2012. The book argues that as Israel's US popularity has declined since October 2023, the lobby's tactics have hardened, from engaging critics to branding them antisemitic, and its alliances have shifted rightward toward Republican and Netanyahu-aligned politics. Judis's own read is that the lobby's actual influence has fallen even as its spending has risen, forcing a reliance on "deceptive advertising" rather than persuasion. The piece connects directly to this desk's 25 August story on Israeli tourism-ministry ad spending routed through Ted Cruz's podcast, and the 18 August Hanover Institute find on manufactured pro-Israel content aimed at AI training data: three different accounts of the same actor building indirect, disclosure-avoiding channels of influence.

This is a book review, not original reporting: the underlying donor and spending figures are Clifton and Lustick's research, drawn from disclosed campaign-finance filings, a solid public-record basis, but Judis is presenting and endorsing their argument rather than independently verifying it himself.
