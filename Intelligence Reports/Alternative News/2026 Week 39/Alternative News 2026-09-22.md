## Story 1: Families of three UK aid workers killed by an Israeli drone press a British coroner to release RAF surveillance footage the MoD is withholding

**Source:** Declassified UK | https://www.declassifieduk.org/israel-killed-three-uk-citizens-now-their-families-want-answers/
**Author:** John McEvoy | **Published:** 21 September 2026 (missed by yesterday's feed-only check, caught today via homepage) | **Read time:** ~6 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

James Henderson (33), James Kirby (47) and John Chapman (57), all working for World Central Kitchen, were killed alongside four colleagues in an Israeli drone strike on their aid convoy in Gaza on 1 April 2024. Their families are now pushing the Avon Coroner's Court in Bristol to open a full investigation into the IDF's targeting and deconfliction decisions that day, and to force the Ministry of Defence to release RAF surveillance footage the article says the MoD holds but has refused to hand over.

McEvoy sets the families' case against the IDF's own account. An IDF criminal investigation cleared the strike, and Colonel Nochi Mendel, named in the piece, said he is "not sorry" for what happened. Against that, the piece cites an Australian government investigation into the deaths of its own citizen among the seven aid workers killed, and a Guardian investigation that found the strike pattern consistent with Israel's broader "incrimination" policy for targeting aid convoys, both used to argue the IDF's internal clearance does not settle the question. Lawyers for the Henderson family argue the IDF's public narrative doesn't hold up against the available evidence. The Campaign Against Arms Trade adds a UK-specific angle, alleging the drone involved used UK-made engine components, which would put Elbit Systems' UK supply chain in the frame if confirmed.

The piece leans on a precedent: the 2006 inquest into the killing of British activist Tom Hurndall, where a jury returned a finding of unlawful killing by the IDF despite Israel's own internal clearance of the soldier involved, the same pattern McEvoy suggests is repeating here. A second pre-inquest review is scheduled for 5 March 2027, so this is the opening move in what will be a slow-moving coronial process, not a resolution.

This is a UK institutional-accountability story rather than a fresh factual revelation: the strike itself and the seven deaths are already established, established by the same investigation and Guardian reporting the piece cites. What's new is the coroner's court becoming the venue where the MoD's own footage and the CAAT arms-supply-chain allegation will be tested, if the families' push succeeds.

## Story 2: A Forensic Architecture satellite analysis finds Israel expanding and formalising bases for five armed factions it created inside Gaza's ceasefire zone

**Source:** Drop Site News | https://www.dropsitenews.com/p/israel-gaza-palestinian-militias-compounds-yellow-line-bases
**Author:** Forensic Architecture and Sharif Abdel Kouddous | **Published:** 21 September 2026 (missed by yesterday's search-based check, caught today via direct archive access) | **Read time:** ~7 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Forensic Architecture's satellite and video analysis finds that compounds housing at least five Israeli-backed Palestinian militias, operating in the Israeli-controlled zone east of the Gaza ceasefire's "yellow line," have been expanded and upgraded in the roughly year since the ceasefire took hold. The upgrades reportedly mirror Israeli military infrastructure: berms, barracks, perimeter fencing and new permanent buildings. One compound in East Rafah has gone further, adding shops, air-conditioned classrooms and a playground, markers of a settlement being made permanent rather than a temporary armed outpost.

The piece names the five groups and their leaders by compound: the Northern Forces/Popular Army under Ashraf Al-Mansi in Beit Lahia, the Popular Defense Forces under Rami Halas in Shujaieya, the Free Homeland Forces under Shawqi Abu Nasira in Deir al-Balah, the Counter-Terrorism Strike Force under Hussam al-Astal in Khan Younis, and the Popular Forces/Counter-Terrorism Service under Ghassan Al-Duhaini in East Rafah. It traces the leaders' backgrounds to former gang networks, past jihadi-group associations, or sidelined Palestinian Authority security officers, a lineage the piece says the IDF and Shin Bet actively assembled. Gaza-based analyst Muhammad Shehada, a European Council on Foreign Relations fellow, is quoted on the record: "The IDF and the Shin Bet were the ones that recruited, funded, trained, provided shelter, accommodation, food, and so on to these gangs, brought them together, and designated each one of them a very specific turf that they operate in."

The evidentiary backbone is Forensic Architecture's own satellite and video analysis, a methodology with a strong track record in this register, supplemented by social media footage of construction work and militia operations. The piece's argument is that these compounds function as Israel's control infrastructure over eastern Gaza: while material for reconstruction and shelter is kept out of western Gaza, resources are flowing into permanent basing for armed groups answerable to Israel rather than to any Palestinian governing authority, raising the question of what "ceasefire" is actually stabilising on the ground.
