## Story 1: Declassified files show the Foreign Office called Israeli conduct a "pattern" of war crimes in 2002, two decades before Britain deepened military ties with Israel

**Source:** Declassified UK, investigation by John McEvoy dated 7 September 2026 (direct fetch blocked, reconstructed via Al Jazeera's 11 September writeup and its wire pickups) | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/11/declassified-documents-show-uk-aware-of-israeli-war-crimes-for-24-years
**Author:** John McEvoy, Chief Reporter, Declassified UK | **Published:** 7 September 2026 (per Al Jazeera's citation); surfaced to this desk 13 September | **Read time:** ~5 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

UK Foreign Office files, declassified and public since 2024 but only reaching this register's coverage this week, record British diplomats and military officers describing Israeli conduct in the occupied West Bank as a "pattern" of "human rights abuses" as early as March 2002, during Israel's Operation Defensive Shield. A Foreign Office official's contemporaneous assessment used that "pattern" language directly. A senior British army officer went further in an internal file, calling Israeli forces "a second-rate, ill-disciplined, swaggering and bullying force" that "routinely use excessive force such as firing at the 'legs' of stone throwers or at 'car tyres', with the inevitable stream of ambulances ferrying youths to hospital with fatal bullet wounds." The same files document Israeli attacks on Red Cross staff and ambulances obstructing medical access, a practice McEvoy's reporting says went on to become standard over the following two decades of Israeli operations.

The files date to the Blair government, and reporting folded into the same investigation shows Blair's government secretly suspended some UK arms exports to Israel in 2002 after concluding British-supplied equipment had been used in the occupied territories in breach of assurances Israel had previously given. Named figures from the period include Blair himself, then-Israeli prime minister Ariel Sharon, Blair's Middle East envoy Lord Michael Levy, and Israeli defence minister Binyamin Ben-Eliezer. The throughline McEvoy draws, per the reporting surfaced today, is that the Foreign Office's own contemporaneous language, "pattern," "war crimes," sat alongside a British state that went on to deepen military and arms cooperation with Israel for the next twenty-four years regardless.

This is McEvoy's long-running method at Declassified UK: setting National Archives and Foreign Office file releases against what the same government said and did in public, the approach behind his Mandelson/Syria piece covered in this report on 11 September. What is different here is the discovery route, not the method. Declassified UK's own site has now been unreachable for this desk for eight weeks, and this specific investigation, dated 7 September, went unnoticed until Al Jazeera's 11 September writeup credited it, which was then itself picked up by several wire-aggregator sites (Garowe Online, Axadle Times, 234Radio) that add no independent reporting of their own. No full, credited long-form republication of McEvoy's original piece, of the kind ScheerPost or Palestine Uncensored have provided for past Declassified UK stories, was found this time.

The evidentiary core, declassified Foreign Office and military files quoted directly, with specific officials' contemporaneous language rather than retrospective characterisation, is solid and documentary, consistent with Declassified UK's usual standard. The access gap is the honest caveat: this report cannot confirm McEvoy's exact framing, full quote context, or any additional files beyond what Al Jazeera excerpted, and the quotes above should be read as accurately relayed by the sources found rather than independently verified against the original.
