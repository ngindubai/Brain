## Story 1: US ammunition ship resupplied RAF bases in Britain during the Iran strikes

**Source:** Declassified UK | https://www.declassifieduk.org/us-ammunition-ship-docked-in-wales-during-iran-strikes/ (direct fetch returned HTTP 403; reconstructed from search-engine-indexed fragments of the article, no third-party republication located)
**Author:** Not confirmed — search results attribute Declassified UK generally; one earlier search suggested Phil Miller and John McEvoy but did not confirm this specific piece's byline. **Published:** 30 July 2026 (per search indexing; not independently verified against the primary page, which remains inaccessible) | **Read time:** ~4 min
**Related vault notes:** none directly relevant.

Per the granularity rule this is a catch-up, not today's story: it carries yesterday's date and only surfaced via search after Declassified UK's block prevented it appearing in the 31 July report.

Declassified UK reports that the MV Sagamore, a container ship used to resupply US airbases in Britain with bombs, docked in Newport, south Wales, during the period the US was flying strikes on Iran from RAF Fairford. The ship's cargo was destined for Welford, an ammunition depot roughly 30 minutes from Fairford with its own private junction off the M4, which normally resupplies the base by lorry once a year. According to evidence the piece attributes to Declassified and Bloomberg reporting, US combat sorties from Fairford were frequent enough to run down bomb stocks at the base, prompting the resupply run. The strikes followed a decision, reported elsewhere as taken by new prime minister Andy Burnham, to permit continued use of Fairford for what the US and UK have characterised as "defensive strikes" on Iran, after Iran's IRGC had reportedly turned to B-1 bombers flying from Fairford once cruise-missile stocks aboard Indian Ocean vessels were exhausted.

The piece situates the Sagamore's 2026 visit against its 2024 and 2025 calls at the same port, both also Friday-evening arrivals lasting roughly 36 hours, framing this as a recurring but low-visibility logistics chain that keeps a UK base in the US strike rotation rather than a one-off.

This continues the run of Declassified UK reporting on the UK's practical enablement of the US-Iran air campaign (the outlet's own 24 July piece, cited by the 24 July source-health log, took the same Met Police war-crimes-referral angle on a related matter), and sits alongside coverage from Al Jazeera and Middle East-focused outlets on bunker-busting bombs loaded at UK airbases earlier this year.
