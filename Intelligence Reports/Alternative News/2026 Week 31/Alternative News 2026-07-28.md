## Catch-up: Palestine Action activists face terrorism-designation sentencing (dated 27 July, missed in the 27 July report)

**Source:** Declassified UK (republished by Palestine Uncensored) | https://www.palestineuncensored.org/five-more-palestine-activists-face-terrorist-sentencing-2026/ (original: declassifieduk.org, URL unreachable directly)
**Author:** John McEvoy | **Published:** 27 July 2026 | **Read time:** ~4 min

Per the granularity rule this is not written up as today's story, since it carries yesterday's date, but Declassified UK's ongoing block meant it did not appear in the 27 July report and is worth logging now rather than dropping it. It was recovered via a republication on Palestine Uncensored, since the outlet's own domain is still returning HTTP 403 on every path tested (homepage, feed, and this specific article URL).

Declassified UK's chief reporter John McEvoy reports that five Palestine Action activists, Brendon O'Hagan, Amanda Kelly, Hmeera Atiqnisar, Mohammed Malik and Alma Yaniv, were convicted last month of criminal damage over a 2024 protest at a Barclays branch in Burnley, where the group smashed windows and sprayed paint causing roughly £212,805 in damage. The protest targeted Barclays' financial ties to Elbit Systems, Israel's largest arms manufacturer; the prosecution itself acknowledges Barclays provided "investments, loans and other corporate financial services" to Elbit. The protest took place before Palestine Action was proscribed as a terrorist organisation. Prosecutors have now applied, after the guilty verdicts, to attach a terrorism connection to the case, with defendants due to be sentenced on 4 September 2026. One defendant, Hmeera Atiqnisar, said: "You go through a two-week trial only to be told after it's over that you were on trial for terrorism."

The story's significance is that this is reported as the first prosecution seeking a terrorism designation for property damage valued below £1 million, a threshold that, if it holds, would lower the bar for treating protest-related criminal damage as terrorism and potentially expose a much wider pool of activists to the same designation after conviction rather than at charge.
