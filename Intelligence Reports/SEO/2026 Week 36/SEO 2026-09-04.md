## Story 1: Performance Max AI video auto-resize opt-out deadline lands today

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-ads-will-use-generative-ai-to-resize-performance-max-video-ads-485252 (announced 17 August, deadline confirmed for today via relevantaudience.com and commonthreadco.com)
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Google announced on 17 August that it will use generative AI to auto-generate missing aspect-ratio versions of existing Performance Max video assets, on by default for all advertisers. Today, 4 September, is the deadline for advertisers who don't want Google reframing their creative to opt out, either via account management or a form, or by changing video settings directly inside Google Ads.

Not an SEO story in the organic sense, but relevant anywhere in the portfolio running PMax with video assets, most plausibly on [[Comparison Network]] properties where paid acquisition sits alongside organic. The risk is brand control, not performance: AI-reframed video crops and pans existing creative into new aspect ratios without a human reviewing the result before it serves. If no PMax account runs video today, this is a non-event; if one does, the opt-out window closes today and the setting has been live by default since 17 August, meaning any auto-generated variants have already had over two weeks to serve. Worth a five-minute check of Google Ads video settings on any comparison-network account running PMax, same spirit as the Cloudflare audit below — a small, easily-missed setting with a hard date attached.

## Story 2: Google Assistant's mobile shutdown begins today

**Source:** Search Engine Roundtable | https://www.seroundtable.com/google-assistant-shutting-down-41822.html
**Related vault notes:** none

Google begins removing Google Assistant access on mobile devices today, 4 September, with Gemini taking over on Wear OS, compatible headphones/earbuds, and Android Auto. Google says the rollout to all users will take a few weeks rather than happening instantly.

Marginal but not zero relevance: this is the last major voice-assistant surface still branded separately from Gemini, and its retirement finishes folding Google's entire consumer AI surface, Search, Assistant, and now the last holdout, into one Gemini-branded experience. For anything in the portfolio thinking about voice or in-car query behaviour (a longer-horizon GEO consideration, not urgent), it's one more data point that "voice search" as a distinct channel has effectively been absorbed into general Gemini/AI Mode behaviour rather than persisting as its own thing. Nothing to action today.

## Story 3: Google commits to fixing Gemini 3.8 Flash's AI Mode citation bug

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-to-fix-citation-bug-with-gemini-3-8-flash-in-ai-mode-486892
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

New development on yesterday's open thread, not a re-tell: a day after Gemini 3.8 Flash rolled into AI Mode, users found it had stopped linking to or citing sources for top-of-funnel queries, a regression against Google's stated push to link out more. Robby Stein, Google Search's VP of Product, acknowledged the bug on X on 3 September: "This isn't working as intended, and we'll roll out a fix soon," with no committed date. Currently limited to AI Pro/Ultra subscribers testing the new model.

Worth two lines because it's a live illustration of exactly the citation fragility [[SEO Portfolio]] content depends on for any AI Mode visibility: a single model swap silently dropped outbound links for a subset of query types, and it took public pressure rather than a changelog entry to get an acknowledgement. No action needed; just a reminder that citation behaviour inside AI Mode is not yet stable enough to plan around with any confidence.

## Story 4: Semrush MCP tested directly — no Sensor or aggregate volatility report exists

**Source:** Direct tool investigation, 4 September (`mcp__Semrush__domain_overview`, `get_report_schema`)
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

This report has a live Semrush MCP connector for the first time, so it was tested directly against the standing "can't read Sensor" open loop rather than just re-noting the gap. Result: the connector exposes seven toolkits — domain overview, organic research, backlinks, competitors, keywords, paid search, audience, plus position tracking and site audit gated behind a Projects lookup — and every one of them is scoped to a specific domain, URL, or keyword the caller supplies. There is no toolkit, and no report inside any toolkit, that returns an aggregate cross-industry SERP volatility score of the kind Semrush Sensor publishes on its own dashboard (sensor.semrush.com). Domain-level rank history (`resource_rank_history`) can show whether one specific portfolio domain's own Semrush Rank moved day to day, which is a usable proxy if anyone wants it, but it is not the same signal as an industry-wide volatility spike.

This closes the loop with a precise answer rather than a repeated shrug: the gap is structural (the API product doesn't expose Sensor, not that this session lacks access to it), and the fix if a live volatility read is ever wanted is either the Sensor dashboard itself (browser session, not API-reachable) or running `resource_rank_history` against specific [[SEO Portfolio]] and [[Comparison Network]] domains to watch for self-reported rank swings as a lagging, narrower substitute. Recommend closing this open loop as "answered: no aggregate feed exists via MCP" rather than carrying it forward as an open question.
