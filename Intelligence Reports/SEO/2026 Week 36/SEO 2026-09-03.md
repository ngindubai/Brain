## Story 1: Google avoids ad tech breakup as judge orders behavioural remedies instead

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-avoids-adx-breakup-486625 (also TechCrunch, CNBC, AdExchanger)
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

US District Judge Leonie Brinkema, who ruled in April 2025 that Google illegally monopolised the publisher ad tech market, decided on the remedy on 2 September: no forced divestiture of AdX, Google's ad exchange. Instead she adopted behavioural remedies largely drawn from proposals both sides submitted, restricting some of Google's conduct without breaking up the business. The judge did not spell out operational specifics for how Google must adjust its practices, which is itself the open question for the weeks ahead.

This is the second high-profile case this year where the DOJ won the underlying monopoly finding but lost the structural remedy, echoing the pattern in the search-distribution case. For publishers, including anyone in the [[SEO Portfolio]] or [[Comparison Network]] monetising through Google's ad exchange or AdSense, this is a status quo outcome: no forced restructuring of the auction infrastructure, but a real prospect of new behavioural constraints (on self-preferencing, data use or bidding practices) landing over the coming months without much warning. Nothing to act on today, but worth watching for the specifics of what Google is actually required to change, since those specifics could touch how ad inventory is priced or auctioned on any AdSense-monetised portfolio page. This also sits alongside yesterday's AdSense Begin-to-Render impression-counting change (effective February 2027) as a second sign that Google's ad stack is under real pressure to change shape.

## Story 2: Gemini 3.8 Flash now powers Google AI Mode

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/gemini-3-8-flash-rolling-out-in-google-search-486630
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Dated 2 September. Google has swapped Gemini 3.7 Flash for Gemini 3.8 Flash inside AI Mode, with a Google product VP citing improvements in "software engineering, agentic tasks, and multi-step reasoning." It is currently gated to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers, selectable via the model dropdown, with a wider rollout expected in the coming weeks or months, matching the pattern of prior Gemini version bumps.

Model swaps inside AI Mode are frequent enough now that they are not individually actionable, but the direction is consistent: Google is iterating AI Mode's reasoning quality faster than its citation or attribution behaviour is changing. Nothing here changes how [[SEO Portfolio]] content should be structured for AI Overviews or AI Mode visibility, but it is another data point that the underlying model is moving fast while the measurement layer (Search Console's AI Performance reports, flagged yesterday) is only now catching up to give visibility into the effect.

## Story 3: Google Trends Explore page adds category filtering without a query

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-trends-adds-category-filters-486582
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Dated 2 September. Google Trends' Explore page now lets users apply a category filter (for example "All Books & Literature") without entering a search term first, surfacing category-specific top searches by region and timeframe. This is aimed at content teams doing topic discovery within a niche rather than starting from a known keyword.

Minor but genuinely usable for content planning across the [[SEO Portfolio]] verticals: pet transport, funeral repatriation, close protection, bus hire and pest control each map to a Trends category, and this makes it faster to pull seasonal or regional demand signals for a vertical without guessing at seed keywords first. Not urgent, but worth a five-minute look next time a content brief is being scoped for any portfolio site.

## Story 4: Google's John Mueller confirms the disavow tool "does have effects"

**Source:** Search Engine Roundtable | https://www.seroundtable.com/google-disavow-tool-effects-42004.html
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Dated 2 September. Responding to a site owner who saw ranking drops after disavowing links bought via guest posts and Fiverr, Mueller said: "There's a lot of hate on the disavow tool here, but it has its place & time, it does have effects." He clarified that disavowing a small number of links alone would not cause a whole-site drop, and recommended checking the disavow file itself, any concurrent spam algorithm activity, and technical issues as alternative causes before assuming the tool caused the drop.

Useful confirmation rather than a policy change: Google has historically downplayed the disavow tool as more likely to harm than help most sites, so a direct statement that it "does have effects" is worth filing if any [[SEO Portfolio]] site ever needs a link cleanup after a past guest-post or PBN phase. The practical read is unchanged: use it narrowly, on demonstrably manipulative links, not as a blanket response to a ranking drop.

## Story 5: Microsoft ships a Windows 11 app whose sole purpose is pushing Bing as default search

**Source:** Reported 2 September 2026 via cybersecuritynews.com, corroborated by windowslatest.com
**Related vault notes:** none

Dated 2 September. Microsoft has built and is distributing a standalone Windows 11 utility called "Microsoft Recommended Search Settings" (a 22.2MB installer, MicrosoftSettings.exe, hosted on Microsoft's own download servers) whose first screen defaults to switching a user's search engine to Bing, across Edge, Firefox and Chrome simultaneously, with the Bing toggle pre-set to "Yes." This lands the same week Windows 11 separately shipped a genuinely Bing-free local search option via KB5120998.

Two contradictory signals from the same company in the same week: Microsoft is simultaneously making it easier to remove Bing from Windows search and pushing a dedicated tool to force it back into browsers. Background rather than action for Orwell; flagged here only because default search engine share shifts, however small, eventually show up in referral traffic mix for anything with a UAE or Gulf audience, including [[Comparison Network]] properties, where Bing's share has historically been thin but not zero.

## Story 6: Cloudflare's 15 September crawler-blocking defaults, mechanics now clearer

**Source:** Web search synthesis (arcalea.com, ipregistry.co, hosting.com), corroborating the standing Cloudflare open loop below
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Not a today-dated story, but new detail worth recording against Gareth's own top-priority open loop. Cloudflare has reclassified bots by function rather than AI/non-AI: Search, Agent and Training. From 15 September, new domains and all existing free-tier customers default to blocking Training and Agent crawlers on ad-bearing pages while leaving Search crawlers allowed. The catch: Googlebot is a single multi-purpose crawler that does both search indexing and AI training crawling, so a site that blocks Training crawlers blocks Googlebot too, on those pages, unless the owner explicitly opts out before the cutover. The opt-out sits in Cloudflare's Security settings.

This is the concrete mechanism behind the audit Gareth already has flagged as open, overdue and top priority. It confirms the risk is real and specific: any [[SEO Portfolio]] or [[Comparison Network]] site on Cloudflare's free tier, or any new domain added since this policy shipped, is at genuine risk of losing Google indexing on ad-bearing pages from 15 September if nobody checks the Security tab before then. This is not a new deadline, it is the same one, but it is now clear the fix is a five-minute per-domain settings check, not a research project. Worth doing before the date rather than after.

## Story 7: Lily Ray on why SEO shortcuts still fail, including at scale

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/lily-ray-search-engine-land-20-for-20-interview-486614
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Dated 2 September, an interview rather than breaking news, included for its direct relevance to how the portfolio is built. Ray's core arguments: E-E-A-T is not a technical checklist but how competitors, reviewers and users actually perceive a brand's reputation; aggressive content scaling eventually draws penalties "including major brands"; and she explicitly rejects the idea that SEO fundamentals matter less in an AI-search world, arguing the opposite, that they matter more because AI systems still lean on the same trust signals to decide what to cite or recommend.

Directly relevant to how [[SEO Portfolio]] sites get built: the portfolio is programmatic, AI-assisted content at scale across five verticals, which is precisely the pattern Ray warns eventually draws Google's attention regardless of brand size. Not a reason to change course today, no enforcement action is attached to this, but a useful outside check against any temptation to push page-creation volume faster than the pre-publish diff-check open loop (below) is actually built to handle.
