## Story 1: Google Business Profile address changes can silently break local rankings

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-business-profile-address-changes-local-rankings-483537
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]] — pest control USA, global bus hire, close protection hire all run as trade/service listings that plausibly use Google Business Profile for local visibility

The piece, by Claudia Tomina, lays out a geocoding trap that's easy to walk into without noticing: when a business enters an address in GBP, Google's geocoding engine assigns a fixed physical coordinate to the listing, and that coordinate — not the address text itself — is what anchors local map-pack and local-finder rankings. Editing the visible address later doesn't necessarily move the coordinate.

The sharper problem is for service-area businesses (SABs) — the model most relevant to pest control, close protection, and bus hire operators who serve a radius rather than a storefront. When an SAB hides its street address in the GBP dashboard (standard practice for home-based or non-public-facing operators), the underlying map pin can revert to whatever address the profile was originally created with. Updating the address field afterwards does not re-anchor the coordinate, because the hidden-address setting decouples the visible listing from the geocoded location Google actually ranks against. The business owner sees a corrected address on the profile; Google is still ranking the old coordinate.

The downstream effect is what makes this dangerous: small shifts in the location signal change who shows up in the map pack, the local finder, and organic local results — not just for the business itself but for which competitors get preferenced instead. A business that moved premises, or that was set up under a placeholder/registered-agent address and never revisited it, could be quietly losing local visibility for months with no error state to alert them. There's no warning in the GBP dashboard when this desync happens.

Exposure for the portfolio: this is squarely relevant to pest control USA (classic SAB model, local intent-heavy), global bus hire (depot-based but often marketed by service area), and close protection hire (deliberately low-profile, address-hidden by design — the exact profile this bug targets). Funeral repatriation is less exposed since it's typically not competing on hyper-local map-pack visibility. Worth a direct check: for any GBP listing on these sites with a hidden address, verify current pin placement against actual service base using Google Maps directly rather than trusting the dashboard address field. This is a five-minute check per listing, not a project — flagging as a concrete to-do rather than a research note, since the article gives the exact diagnostic (compare visible address to pin location on the live Maps listing).

## Story 2: Search Engine Land publishes an "AI entity footprint" audit framework

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/how-to-audit-your-ai-entity-footprint (28 July 2026, via site listing)
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]] / [[Comparison Network]] — GEO/AI-visibility relevant to every property in the portfolio

A framework piece rather than news, but timely given how much of the daily brief lately has been GEO-adjacent. It proposes auditing how AI systems (Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini) understand a business across six dimensions: identity (is the entity clearly defined and disambiguated), differentiation (does the entity read as distinct from competitors), evidence (is there verifiable proof behind claims — reviews, citations, structured data), consistency (does the business's identity match across every surface it appears on), relationships (is the entity correctly linked to related people, brands, and locations via `sameAs` and structured data), and specialization (is expertise in the specific niche legible to the model, not just general business presence).

None of this is new territory conceptually — it's the same entity-authority argument that's been building all year — but the six-dimension framing is a usable checklist rather than the usual vague "be more authoritative" advice. The practical takeaway for a programmatic portfolio is that entity consistency across five niche sites run by one operator is a real risk: if pet transport, funeral repatriation, close protection, bus hire and pest control all trace back to the same underlying operator without clear separation, AI systems may either merge them into a confused single entity or fail to credit any of them with real specialization. Worth a Research note on how the portfolio's entity signals (NAP data, schema, about pages, sameAs links) currently read across sites — not urgent, but the six-dimension framework is a decent lens to audit against when there's time.

## Story 3: Google mandates passkeys for new Google Ads API refresh tokens from 5 August

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-makes-passkeys-mandatory-for-google-ads-api-users-483617
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]] (if running Google Ads programmatically for any lead-gen or comparison site)

Lower-relevance but worth a one-line flag since it has a hard date. From 5 August, anyone generating a new OAuth 2.0 refresh token through the Google Ads API will be required to authenticate with a passkey — replacing password-only and SMS/TOTP two-factor for that specific workflow. Existing refresh tokens keep working and don't need reauthorisation, so this only bites on new integrations or re-authorisations after the date. Newly created passkeys carry a seven-day trust delay before being fully usable, which matters for anyone onboarding a new Ads API connection close to a deadline. Service-account-based automated workflows are unaffected.

Relevance check: this is Ads API, not Search Console API or organic tooling, so it only matters if any portfolio or comparison-network site pushes Google Ads changes programmatically (bid management scripts, automated campaign tooling). If nothing in the stack touches the Ads API directly, this is a non-event — flagging only because the 5 August date is close enough to be worth a five-second mental note.
