## Story 1: AI models favour brands they already know — new research quantifies the incumbency bias

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/ai-models-favor-familiar-brands-search-study-484054
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]], [[Agent Org]]

New research (66 US buyer prompts, tested 60 times each between 29 May and 9 June, 3,960 responses, 13,281 fan-out searches, 1,416 brand-level observations) finds that AI models search for brands they already know roughly 3.2 times more often than unfamiliar ones, and 63% of brand-specific searches surfaced one of each model's five most-familiar brands. In plain terms: before the model even runs a fan-out search, it has already narrowed the field toward names it recognises from training data, and unfamiliar or newer brands start from a structural deficit that good on-page content alone does not fully offset. The authors are careful to note this shows a relationship between memory and search behaviour rather than proof of direct causation, but the practical effect is the same either way.

This sharpens a problem already being tracked in this brain: GEO visibility is not a level playing field the way blue-link SEO nominally is. A new comparison-network site or a niche portfolio site competing against an established incumbent (a national funeral-repatriation broker, a big-name pest control franchise) is fighting the model's prior, not just the SERP.

**Signal for Orwell:** every portfolio and comparison-network vertical has at least one entrenched incumbent brand. This is a reason to lean harder into named/proprietary data, structured entity signals, and citation-worthy specificity rather than generic category content — the tactics already flagged in the entity-consistency research thread — because outranking on relevance alone will not overcome a familiarity prior baked into the model. Worth a one-line addition to that research note: incumbency bias is now a measured, citable effect, not a hunch.

## Story 2: Google tests AI-generated descriptions on Shopping and Product ads

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-tests-ai-generated-descriptions-in-shopping-ads-484016
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

PPC specialist Brodie Clark spotted Google testing AI-generated descriptive text alongside Shopping and Product ads, extending an experiment that started on standard Search ads earlier this month. Google's own line: "This is a small experiment to see if adding AI-generated context to Search ads helps people make more informed decisions." No confirmation yet on whether or when it expands beyond the current test. The concern raised by advertisers is straightforward: product titles, descriptions and images are deliberately optimised inputs, and an AI-generated summary layered on top is an interpretation the advertiser does not control, with no visibility into how accurately it represents the product or how it affects click-through.

**Signal for Orwell:** relevant mainly as a watch item rather than an action — none of the current portfolio or comparison-network sites run Shopping ads today, but the comparison network's monetisation model (affiliate/lead-gen cards that function similarly to product listings) sits in the same conceptual space: a third AI-generated layer between the listing and the click is a pattern worth tracking as it matures, since it could eventually apply to how comparison cards themselves get summarised in AI Overviews or AI Mode.

## Story 3: Multi-location SEO — how to structure geographic pages without cannibalising rankings

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/multi-location-seo-structure-geographic-pages-483959
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Gaetano Pizzi lays out a structural framework for scaling location pages across a multi-market business without the common failure mode: turning every geographic keyword variant into its own thin URL, which dilutes relevance and invites self-cannibalisation in the SERP. The recommended approach is to build local presence around real locations, defined markets, and genuine service areas — standardised systems for citation consistency, review generation, and page architecture that scale without creating near-duplicate pages competing against each other. This is core, well-trodden local-SEO doctrine restated for scale, not a new mechanic, but the "real markets, not keyword variants" framing is a useful check against the most common way programmatic location-page rollouts go wrong.

**Signal for Orwell:** directly relevant to the SEO Portfolio's service-area sites — pet transport, funeral repatriation, close protection hire, bus hire, and pest control all run or could run location/service-area pages, and all are exactly the profile this piece warns about: high temptation to template out one page per city or postcode without checking for cannibalisation. Worth a quick structural audit against this framework next time any portfolio site's location-page set gets touched, particularly pest control and bus hire, which are the most geographically fragmented of the five.

## Story 4: Turning news articles into reusable assets for AI search

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/turn-news-articles-into-ai-search-assets-483950
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Agent Org]]

Joy Johnston, writing from a news-publisher SEO angle, argues that "liquid content" — journalism repackaged, distributed, personalised and monetised across formats rather than published once and left static — gives publishers more surface area to be found, cited, and reused inside AI search systems than a single static article does. The underlying logic applies beyond news: content built to be atomised into reusable facts, quotes, and structured claims is inherently easier for an AI system to lift, cite, and re-voice than a single long-form page that buries the citable material in prose.

**Signal for Orwell:** this is a content-architecture principle worth carrying into the Claude Code content-refresh pipeline flagged yesterday — when that pipeline gets built, "does this section survive being atomised into a standalone, citable fact?" is a cheap additional check to bake into the delta-brief step, on top of the Keep/Fix/Remove/Add logic already planned.

## Story 5: A working Python keyword-clustering tool, step by step

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/keyword-clustering-tool-python-483977
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Agent Org]]

Simone De Palma publishes a walkthrough for building a keyword-clustering tool in Python, grouping keywords by SERP or semantic similarity rather than manual eyeballing — the kind of tooling piece that is directly reusable rather than merely informative. The general approach (SERP-overlap or embedding-based similarity, clustering via something like DBSCAN to avoid having to pre-guess a cluster count, output grouped by search intent) matches the broader pattern seen across the Python SEO-tooling space this year.

**Signal for Orwell:** this is the kind of small, self-contained script that fits Orwell's operating model well — a Claude Code session could stand this up against any portfolio site's keyword universe in an afternoon, feeding directly into the location-page structuring problem in Story 3 (cluster first, then decide which clusters actually deserve a dedicated page, rather than templating one page per raw keyword).

## Story 6: "Selling SEO or AI services? Your sales team needs more than a pitch deck"

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/selling-seo-ai-services-sales-team-pitch-deck-483942
**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]]

Nick LeRoy's critique of the now-standardised "AI SEO sales deck": search-is-fragmenting narrative, an impressive AI-visibility dashboard, then a recommendation to sell AI optimisation as a separate line item with separate pricing. His point is that most of what gets pitched as revolutionary — passage-level structure, structured data, Q&A formatting, earning citations — is core SEO practice repositioned to sound new, and that a deck alone does not close deals; sales and delivery need to align on what a client actually needs before the pitch, not after signature.

**Signal for Orwell:** the most directly relevant story in today's cluster to this year's second deliverable — growing the custom-build client channel into something repeatable. The warning applies squarely to any KlientFlo-type pitch that leans on an "AI is changing everything, buy AI SEO" framing: it is a template other agencies are already running, which weakens it as a differentiator, and the actual sell needs to be grounded in what gets delivered, not the narrative wrapper. Worth keeping in mind when the client-channel pitch material gets built out.
