## Story 1: Google Search Console adds global platform-level tracking for social and video content in Search

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-search-console-platform-properties-are-now-globally-live-483921
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Google Search Console's "Platform properties" feature — which lets site owners monitor how their content performs on Instagram, TikTok, X and YouTube when it surfaces inside Google Search results — is now globally available to all Search Console users, having previously been in limited release. Google published accompanying documentation on analysing this social/video performance data: identifying trending content, comparing formats, and understanding which platform's content is actually winning visibility in Search.

This closes a real gap: until now, site owners had no direct visibility into how content on platforms they don't control (a brand's Instagram or YouTube presence, for instance) performed specifically within Google Search, as distinct from performance on the platform itself.

**Signal for Orwell:** worth checking whether any portfolio or comparison-network site has an active social/video presence (YouTube explainers, Instagram content) that would now show up in this report — if so, it's a free new data source for understanding cross-platform Search visibility that didn't exist yesterday.

## Story 2: "Ghost citations" — 40% of AI search citations never mention the brand name

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/ghost-citation-problem-ai-483748
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

New Writesonic analysis of 16 million brand appearances across AI search engines finds that roughly 40% of AI citations are "ghost citations": the AI engine links to a page as a source, but the generated answer text never actually names the brand. A citation confirms the engine found the content useful; it does not mean a reader will ever notice whose content it was, since most readers focus on the answer text, not the sources panel or footnote links.

The rate varies sharply by platform — from 19% on Microsoft Copilot up to 52% on Perplexity — suggesting real differences in how each engine synthesises and re-voices source material rather than one universal cause. Search Engine Land's practical fixes: track mentions and citations as separate metrics rather than one blended number, invest in named/proprietary research that's harder for an engine to anonymise, and place brand names directly adjacent to key claims on-page rather than merely somewhere nearby.

**Signal for Orwell:** this is the concrete, measurable version of the GEO pressure flagged repeatedly this month — every portfolio and comparison site now needs to ask not just "am I cited?" but "does the AI answer actually say my name?" Worth folding directly into the entity-consistency audit already sitting in the research pipeline, since brand-name proximity to claims is a checkable on-page fix.

## Story 3: How to scale SEO content updates with Claude Code — a working 14-step pipeline

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/scale-seo-content-updates-claude-code-483862
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Agent Org]]

A detailed workflow piece describing a Claude Code-driven pipeline for refreshing existing SEO content at scale: automatically pulling 56-day Google Search Console baselines, tagging content sections as Keep/Fix/Remove/Add against actual query performance, running competitor-gap analysis, validating personas and local knowledge before any rewrite, generating a "delta brief" of only what needs to change, rewriting just those sections, then fact-checking and auditing images before publishing. The author's stated principle: "automation enforces judgment, it doesn't replace it" — the pipeline has hard gates that block progress if persona validation or local-knowledge refresh is skipped, specifically because skipping those steps is what makes AI content sound generic. Running multiple page updates in parallel was found to degrade quality across all of them; the system deliberately processes one page at a time.

**Signal for Orwell:** this is the most directly actionable story in today's report — it's a published, tested version of exactly the kind of content-refresh pipeline the SEO Portfolio sites need, using the same tool (Claude Code) already central to how Orwell operates. Worth a proper look as a template: the GSC-baseline-first, delta-brief, one-page-at-a-time structure is a concrete pattern to borrow rather than reinvent, and the "parallel updates degrade quality" finding is a specific operational warning worth heeding if this gets built.

## Story 4: H1 2026 AI/search halftime report — AI Mode hits 1 billion users, publishers lose a third of referral traffic

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/ai-halftime-report-h1-2026-483912
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Kevin Indig's H1 2026 review: Google's AI Mode has reached 1 billion monthly active users with queries averaging three times longer than traditional search — Google's own framing is "the biggest search box upgrade in 25 years." Publishers took an average 33% referral-traffic loss year-over-year, prompting legal action and regulatory scrutiny, with the traditional content-for-traffic exchange visibly shifting toward content-for-training-data negotiations that remain unresolved on pricing and attribution. Platform competition is fragmenting too: ChatGPT's share of the AI-search space fell from 78% to 56% as Google and Claude gained ground. The report's central line — "AI's economic impact is expanding faster than we can attribute it" — sums up the whole half-year: massive infrastructure investment, publisher pain, and no clean way yet to measure the return.

**Signal for Orwell:** context rather than action — confirms the referral-traffic pressure and platform fragmentation already being tracked, but the 33% publisher traffic-loss figure and the AI Mode scale (1bn MAU) are worth citing as concrete numbers next time the entity-consistency or GEO research note gets written up properly.
