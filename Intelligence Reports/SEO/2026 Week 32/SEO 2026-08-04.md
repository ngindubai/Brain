## Story 1: Microsoft Clarity splits branded vs non-branded AI queries

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/microsoft-clarity-adds-branded-and-non-branded-ai-queries-484185

Microsoft's free analytics tool Clarity now distinguishes branded from non-branded queries inside its AI Citations dashboards, with a separate "Share of Authority" breakdown for each and filtering across reports. Previously, Clarity's AI-citation tracking lumped all AI-search mentions together, useful for a headline "are we showing up in AI answers" number, but useless for telling whether that visibility came from people already searching your brand name versus genuine topical discovery.

That distinction matters more than it sounds. A site can look strong on AI-citation volume purely because existing customers are asking ChatGPT or AI Overviews about a brand they already know, while getting zero new-customer discovery from AI search on unbranded queries. Splitting the two gives a much cleaner read on whether GEO effort is actually growing reach or just riding existing brand awareness. This is the first mainstream free tool to make that split visible; expect Semrush and Ahrefs to follow if it gets traction.

**Signal for Orwell:** Worth a look for any [[SEO Portfolio]] site running Clarity (it's free), particularly ones like pet-transport or funeral-repatriation that depend on non-branded discovery rather than existing brand recognition. Not urgent, but a genuinely useful new lens once someone has ten minutes to check it.

## Story 2: A PPC account rebuild by the book cost a client 40% of revenue

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/ana-kostic-shared-why-best-practice-cost-her-client-40-of-revenue-484178

PPC specialist Ana Kostic inherited a messy Google Ads account and rebuilt it following textbook best practice, clean campaign structure, tidy keyword organisation. The rebuild wiped out years of performance history Google's algorithms had optimised around, and traffic and revenue dropped roughly 40%. Recovery took about two and a half months, with full benefit not showing until nearer six months. Her stated lesson: think about the business first, not the ideal account structure, and make structural changes gradually so new campaigns can learn while the existing ones keep earning.

This isn't an SEO story in the narrow sense, it's Google Ads, but it's a sharp, specific illustration of a mistake that generalises directly to organic work too: a "clean slate" restructure (site migration, URL overhaul, content pruning done all at once) can look correct on a checklist and still tank performance because it throws away accumulated algorithmic trust rather than because the new structure is worse.

**Signal for Orwell:** Directly relevant to any comparison-network or portfolio site running paid campaigns, and a useful cautionary template before any big structural SEO change (site migration, IA overhaul) on [[SEO Portfolio]] or [[Comparison Network]] properties: phase it, don't flip the switch.

## Story 3: Google expands Data Manager API for bulk audience management

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-expands-data-manager-api-with-smarter-audience-management-484191

Google's Data Manager API added a `RemoveAllAudienceMembers` method for clearing an entire audience list in one call, field-level ingestion warnings instead of failing whole requests outright, and expanded address-field support for syncing into Analytics. Minor, developer-facing update, useful for teams managing Customer Match lists across Google Ads, Display & Video 360 and Analytics with less manual cleanup.

**Signal for Orwell:** Background only, no portfolio site currently runs the kind of first-party audience infrastructure this would change.
