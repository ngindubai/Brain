## Story 2: Google's AI leadership reshuffle keeps dominating SEO trade conversation

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-leadership-changes-with-jeff-dean-out-demis-hassabis-role-change-and-more-484387

**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]]

This broke Thursday 6 August, not today, so it's not a new story, but it's still the single most-discussed item across every SEO source checked this morning, and the analysis has moved on from "what happened" to "what it means for ranking systems," which is the genuinely useful bit. Jeff Dean, 27 years at Google and the engineer behind RankBrain and much of the AI Overviews/AI Mode stack, is leaving to found a company targeting AI-driven drug discovery and chip design. Demis Hassabis steps down as DeepMind CEO to become Alphabet chief scientist and DeepMind chair; Koray Kavukcuoglu takes over running DeepMind day to day, reporting straight to Pichai. Google's stock dropped 4% on the news.

The read from search commentators worth noting: nobody expects this to move rankings tomorrow, but Dean's stated bet is that the next efficiency gain comes from automating experimentation rather than periodic human-led model breakthroughs. If that view shapes how DeepMind's successor leadership runs search-adjacent AI, the pace of retrieval/ranking/summarisation change could accelerate with fewer clean "named update" boundaries to react to, which makes attribution (see Story 1) structurally harder going forward, not easier.

**Signal for Orwell:** Nothing actionable today. Filed as a structural watch item, not a to-do.

## Story 3: Reddit v Perplexity DMCA claims survive dismissal motion

**Source:** Multiple, via web search (SDNY Blog, Renascence) | https://www.renascence.io/news/10121/reddit-vs-perplexity-ai-dmca-lawsuit-survives-early-dismissal

**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

A Manhattan federal judge ruled 1 August that Reddit's core DMCA claims against Perplexity and SerpApi can proceed, rejecting the defendants' motion to dismiss. Reddit alleges the pair circumvented Google's anti-bot protections to scrape Reddit content at industrial scale for AI training and retrieval. Not today's news either, but the ruling is still being worked through by SEO/GEO commentators this week as the clearest test case yet of whether AI answer engines can keep treating third-party content as free training and citation fodder without a licence. A finding against Perplexity would harden the direction AI platforms are already moving in (paid licensing deals, e.g. OpenAI-Yelp), which longer term raises the cost of being cited in AI answers and could favour sites with direct licensing relationships over ones relying on open scraping.

**Signal for Orwell:** No portfolio site currently licenses content to an AI platform. Not urgent, but worth a placeholder note if any site develops a large enough proprietary dataset (pricing tables, transport logistics data) to be worth licensing later.
