## Story 1: Google admits Search Console's AI search reporting is inadequate
**Source:** Search Engine Journal | https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-admits-search-console-reporting-for-ai-search-is-inadequate/
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

John Mueller has confirmed publicly what most operators already suspected: Search Console's AI Overviews/AI Mode reporting, rolled out in June and made fully global on 31 August, doesn't give usable position data. Two specific admissions matter. First, impression counts are inflated — a link gets counted as an impression even when the user never saw it, and anything tucked behind a "Show More" expansion is undercounted until someone actually clicks to reveal it. Second, and more fundamental, position reporting tracks where the AI Overview *block* sits on the page, not where an individual link sits within that block. A site could show "position 2" and still be the eighth citation buried at the bottom of the overview, invisible in practice.

Mueller's own framing was that "the old position 1–10 is hard to map, or to make useful for site owners" in an AI-answer world, and he invited the community to suggest better metrics because Google doesn't currently have one. That's a rare direct admission of a reporting gap from Google, not a workaround or a "we're looking into it."

For Orwell this reinforces the standing note that GSC's AI-visibility numbers cannot be trusted as a KPI in isolation. Anyone deciding whether AI Overviews are helping or hurting a portfolio site off these metrics alone is working from bad data. Rank-tracking tools that show per-link position within AI answers (rather than block-level position) are now the only reliable read, which raises the practical value of Ahrefs/Semrush AI-tracking features over Google's own panel.

## Story 2: Automattic confirms Mullenweg back as CEO after board coup attempt
**Source:** Search Engine Journal / TechCrunch (cross-referenced) | https://www.searchenginejournal.com/matt-mullenweg-apparently-back-in-charge-at-automattic/
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[GitHub Library]]

Quick timeline for context, since this moved fast across the end of last week: on 9 September, Automattic's board put founder Matt Mullenweg on paid leave with no reason given, installing CFO Mark Davies as interim CEO, and reportedly gave Mullenweg 50 minutes' notice with no time to get legal advice. The board didn't revoke his Slack access. He took it back, removed the other admins, and declared himself in charge again — reported by TechCrunch on 11 September with visible scepticism ("tells (trolls?) staff"). By 12 September, Automattic's official line had flipped entirely: "Matt Mullenweg is the chairman and CEO of Automattic, with full support of the board." WordPress Executive Director Mary Hubbard and a visible chunk of Automattic staff publicly backed him through the episode.

No public reason for the original ouster has surfaced, and the two Automattic statements read as written by different people, which is its own signal of an unresolved internal fight. This is worth tracking, not acting on yet: Automattic runs WordPress.com, WooCommerce, Jetpack and a large share of the plugin ecosystem the SEO portfolio and comparison-network sites likely touch in some form. Leadership instability at that scale is a slow-burn risk to product roadmaps (security patches, Jetpack SEO features, WooCommerce checkout stability) rather than an immediate one. Flagging for the open loops table rather than the top-priority pile.

## Story 3: Google Merchant Center's AI Performance Insights keeps expanding
**Source:** Relevant Audience (cross-checked against MarTech coverage) | https://www.relevantaudience.com/ecommerce-marketing/merchant-center-ai-performance-three-new-sections/
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Three new sections were spotted in Google's Merchant Center AI Performance Insights report on 9 September, still expanding out from a July pilot. The report tells merchants which terms and product attributes are missing from listings that are eligible to appear in AI Overviews, AI Mode and Gemini shopping answers — effectively Google grading a feed's AI-readiness before the merchant asks. It's still limited to a rolling pilot of US accounts rather than a global rollout.

The direction of travel is the story more than this specific increment: Merchant Center is turning from an ads-eligibility tool into the gatekeeper for whether a product shows up in AI-generated shopping answers at all. Google, AI Mode and Gemini all read the same Shopping Graph, and that graph is populated from structured feed data, not page content. None of the portfolio's current sites are product-feed businesses, but the comparison network (mortgage, money, insurance, company formation) increasingly competes for the same AI-answer real estate on informational-commercial queries, and the underlying lesson — structured data quality now gates AI visibility, not just ad eligibility — applies there too.

## Story 5: Query fan-out roots traced to a 2003 patent framework
**Source:** Search Engine Journal | https://www.searchenginejournal.com/the-2003-framework-that-was-already-doing-query-fan-out/
**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]]

A closer look at Google's AI Mode "query fan-out" technique — where a single search is silently expanded into several sub-queries before an answer is composed — traces the underlying mechanism back to a 2003-era Google patent framework for query expansion and reformulation, not a novel LLM-era invention. The piece argues AI Mode is applying an old retrieval pattern with a new generative layer on top, which matters for how SEOs should think about "optimising for fan-out": the target isn't the literal query typed, it's the cluster of adjacent queries Google's retrieval layer would generate around it.

Relevance is more Agent Org than portfolio-immediate: it's a reminder that content built for a single target keyword is structurally mismatched with how AI Mode actually retrieves, and reinforces the case for topic-cluster content architecture over single-page-per-keyword builds on any new programmatic SEO site.

## Story 6: Le Monde says AI Overviews haven't dented its audience — a counterpoint to the Wikipedia data
**Source:** Search Engine Journal | https://www.searchenginejournal.com/le-monde-says-ai-overviews-havent-hurt-its-audience-so-far/589018/
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Le Monde CEO Louis Dreyfus told French trade title INfluencia (reported by SEJ on 10 September) that the paper has seen no sudden audience drop since AI Overviews launched in France roughly seven weeks earlier, beyond normal seasonal swing. Apps drove 36% of group traffic in August, and Dreyfus's read is that AI Overviews hit web traffic specifically rather than app traffic — Le Monde added 24,000 new subscribers in each of July and August, both record months by the company's own account, and English-edition traffic is up 7% year-on-year for H1.

Worth weighing against yesterday's University of Washington finding that AI Overviews cost Wikipedia roughly 5% of referral traffic. The two aren't really in conflict: Le Monde is a subscription-funnel publisher where app usage, brand loyalty and paid conversion dominate, while Wikipedia is a pure open-referral reference site with nothing else to fall back on. Dreyfus's numbers are also self-reported in an interview, not an independent study, so treat as directional rather than proof AI Overviews are harmless to publishers generally. For the portfolio, which runs closer to Wikipedia's model (organic-referral-dependent, no app or subscription layer to cushion a hit) than Le Monde's, the UW finding remains the more analogous data point — this is a useful caution against over-reading any single publisher's reassurance.
