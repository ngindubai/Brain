## Story 1: Google's August 2026 spam update is rolling — day two, still no visible portfolio impact

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-releases-august-2026-spam-update-485369 and Search Engine Journal | https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-begins-rolling-out-the-august-2026-spam-update/586301/
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Google logged the update on its Search Status Dashboard at 9:27 a.m. Pacific on 18 August, with Google Search Central confirming on X shortly after: "Today we released the August 2026 spam update. We'll update our ranking release history page when the rollout is complete." It is the third spam update of the year, after March and June. Google says it applies globally, to all languages, and "may take a few days to complete" — which puts today, 19 August, squarely inside the live rollout window, not before or after it.

What's confirmed and what isn't: Google did not publish a companion blog post and did not announce any new spam policy. The update enforces existing policy, and reporting explicitly states it does not target link spam or the site reputation abuse (parasite SEO) policy specifically. That leaves the door open to it touching the policies most relevant to Orwell's build model — scaled content abuse, doorway pages, cloaking, expired domain abuse — none of which Google has ruled out. Google's own guidance is that recovery from a spam action can take months, because automated systems need a sustained compliance window before scores recover.

Why this matters here: every SEO Portfolio site (pet transport, funeral repatriation, close protection hire, global bus hire, pest control USA) is built on templated, programmatic content at scale — the exact shape spam updates since 2024's Scaled Content Abuse policy have been tuned to catch, even though the update isn't aimed at "helpful" programmatic sites that pass the human-value bar. The Comparison Network sites carry a different but adjacent risk profile (affiliate-adjacent comparison content, which is closer to the site reputation abuse and thin-affiliate end of the spectrum Google has hit before), though this specific update explicitly excludes site reputation abuse.

Action items:
- Pull Search Console performance for all five SEO Portfolio properties and the five Comparison Network sites now, then again in 5-7 days, to catch the rollout's tail rather than just its start.
- Check Semrush Sensor's relevant industry/geo categories for each site's niche and region daily through this week — no MCP Sensor tool exists (standing gap, see open loops), so this is a manual check for now.
- No content changes on spec. Nothing here indicates any Orwell property has been touched. This is a watch item, not an incident.

## Story 2: Search Console's Generative AI Performance report bug is still unresolved after six days

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-search-console-generative-ai-performance-report-in-search-data-bug-485215
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Agent Org]]

Recap for context: Google confirmed on 17 August that a logging error was undercounting impressions in Search Console's Generative AI performance report, with the drop starting 13 August. John Mueller said it's a reporting artefact, not a real visibility drop, and Google planned to annotate the report while it investigates. As of today, six days after the undercount started and two days after Google confirmed it, no fix or resolution has been announced — the last public word remains Mueller's 17 August reassurance.

This is a distinct fault from the AI Mode conversation-fragment leak into Performance reports covered yesterday (18 August) — that one is about stray URL fragments polluting query data, this one is a straight impression-count logging error in the AI-specific report slice. Both point the same direction: Search Console's newer AI-surfaces reporting is currently the least trustworthy part of the toolset. For anyone leaning on GSC's Generative AI report to judge whether AI Overviews are helping or hurting portfolio visibility, the numbers right now should be treated as directionally unreliable, not zero, but not solid either.

No action needed beyond patience — do not react to any AI-surfaces impression dip in GSC this week as if it were real until Google either fixes the logging or confirms the annotation is live.

## Research pipeline

- Google Merchant Center is overhauling performance reporting from 24 August (retroactive to 1 July): YouTube affiliate traffic split out from "organic," YouTube organic definitions realigned, and expanded product-level Ads reporting. Low direct relevance — no Orwell property runs Merchant Center listings — but flag if any future venture touches shopping feeds.

No portfolio site should be relying on FAQ rich-result API pulls for reporting, but worth a quick check of any dashboard or BigQuery export built against the Search Console API in case FAQ fields are silently going NULL.
