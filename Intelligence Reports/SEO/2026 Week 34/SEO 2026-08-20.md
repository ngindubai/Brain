## Story 1: 41-day experiment finds AI crawlers can't see JavaScript-injected links at all

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/javascript-links-pages-invisible-ai-search-485228
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

A controlled 41-day experiment found that among crawlers tested, only Google's rendering crawler (GoogleOther) followed JavaScript-injected links at all — and even Googlebot, the crawler that actually builds the search index, reached just 2% of them. GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Bingbot, Meta-ExternalAgent and Amazonbot "found exactly zero" JavaScript-linked pages, while all achieved 100% coverage of links present in raw HTML. When the site converted navigation to server-rendered HTML, GPTBot re-crawled within 48 hours; Google took weeks.

This is the single most actionable technical finding this week for the portfolio. If any [[SEO Portfolio]] or [[Comparison Network]] site uses client-side JavaScript for primary navigation or internal linking (common in templated/programmatic builds that lean on frontend frameworks for scale), it is plausibly invisible to every major AI crawler right now, not just ranking lower — completely unreachable. Worth an actual audit: disable JavaScript on each site and check whether navigation and internal links still resolve in raw HTML. A companion piece today (Story 2) makes the fix list concrete.

## Story 2: Search Engine Land lays out 4 technical SEO fundamentals for AI search

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/technical-seo-for-ai-search-fundamentals-485262
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Companion piece to Story 1, published the same day: (1) robots.txt should reflect actual intent toward AI crawlers — check none of the portfolio's five lead-gen sites or five comparison sites are unintentionally blocking ClaudeBot/GPTBot; (2) no AI crawler renders JavaScript, so critical content and structured data need server-side rendering or static generation; (3) schema markup must be present in the initial HTML response, not injected client-side, to be usable for AI citations; (4) entity consistency — a unified brand identity with `sameAs` links to canonical external nodes (Wikidata, LinkedIn) — helps AI systems treat a site as one authoritative source rather than fragmenting it.

Action item: this is a genuine two-hour audit against all ten portfolio/network properties (robots.txt intent, JS-dependency check, schema location, entity consistency) — cheap to run, and directly upstream of whether any of these sites can be cited by AI Overviews, ChatGPT Search or Gemini at all.

## Story 3: Reddit's ChatGPT Search citation share fell 86% in four days — cause unconfirmed

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/reddit-chatgpt-search-citations-fall-report-485473
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Promptwatch data shows Reddit's share of ChatGPT Search citations held around 3.83% from 18 July through 7 August, began sliding 8 August, dropped sharply from 14–17 August, and stabilised at 0.52% — an 86.4% relative collapse. Promptwatch itself says the data "doesn't explain the change" and can't rule out a data-collection issue; Google's AI products showed only a gradual Reddit decline over the same window, suggesting this is specific to ChatGPT Search's source-selection algorithm rather than a broader UGC devaluation.

No direct portfolio exposure (none of the five lead-gen or five comparison sites depend on Reddit citations), but it's a sharp reminder of how unstable individual-platform AI citation share can be week to week — reinforces the "track multiple AI platforms, not one" lesson from Story 4 below.

## Story 4: Study finds Gemini's local AI citations are wildly inconsistent — and diverge completely from ChatGPT's

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/business-websites-gemini-citations-local-ai-search-study-485506
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

A Steady Demand study of 14,472 citations across 1,487 local queries found business websites make up ~60% of Gemini's local citations (Reddit a surprising second at 13.7%, ahead of directories like Angi/Thumbtack combined). The bigger finding is "Grounding Drift": identical repeated queries returned overlapping sources only ~40% of the time (versus Google's local pack at ~90% consistency), and the same top business appeared across repeat queries just 7% of the time. Gemini and ChatGPT also diverge sharply from each other — only 8% domain overlap, 4.2% same-top-business overlap.

Direct relevance for the pet transport, funeral repatriation, close protection, bus hire and pest control sites, all of which are local-intent businesses: being cited once by Gemini or ChatGPT today is not a stable signal of being cited tomorrow, and optimising for one AI platform's citation pattern won't transfer to another. The practical implication is tracking visibility across at least Google AI Overviews, Gemini and ChatGPT separately rather than treating "AI search" as one channel — a real gap given the standing Semrush Sensor MCP tooling limitation noted below.
