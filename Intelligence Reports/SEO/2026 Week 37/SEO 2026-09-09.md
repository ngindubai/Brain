## Story 1: Search Console's Links report has been stuck for a month — backlink data effectively blind since 8 August

**Source:** Search Engine Roundtable, dated 8 September | https://www.seroundtable.com/google-search-console-links-report-delayed-42034.html
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Google Search Console's Links report has not refreshed since around 8 August — roughly a month of stale backlink data across every property that relies on it. The working theory in the SEO community is that this is tied to a 2026 change in how Google processes link data, now refreshed in batches rather than near-real-time, rather than a one-off bug; Google has not issued a fix timeline. This is a distinct, newer thread from the already-tracked GSC generative-AI-logging bug from mid-August — a second Search Console reliability problem running concurrently.

Practical impact for [[SEO Portfolio]] and [[Comparison Network]]: any link-building or disavow decision made off GSC data right now is working from a month-old snapshot. Worth cross-checking anything link-related against Ahrefs or Semrush rather than GSC alone until this resolves, and worth folding into the same standing check as the Cloudflare portfolio audit rather than treating as a one-off.

## Story 2: Gen-AI referral traffic breakdown — ChatGPT takes 56.7% of the pie, Gemini 25.4%, Claude 6.0%

**Source:** Similarweb data via Search Engine Roundtable recap, dated 8 September | https://www.seroundtable.com/recap-09-08-2026-42041.html
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

August 2026 Similarweb data puts ChatGPT at 56.7% of all generative-AI referral traffic to websites, Gemini at 25.4%, Claude at 6.0%, with the remainder split across smaller players. This gives concrete shape to the trend flagged in Monday's report (ChatGPT referral traffic up 48% year-on-year, Bing down 50%): it's not just that AI referral traffic is growing, it's that it is heavily concentrated in one platform. For any [[SEO Portfolio]] or [[Comparison Network]] property building GEO (generative engine optimisation) practice, ChatGPT visibility is now worth roughly nine times the optimisation effort that Claude visibility is, purely on referral-volume grounds — a useful prioritisation signal if resources for AI-visibility work are limited.

## Story 3: Semrush study — 57.5% of AI chatbot users have been talked out of a purchase by the chatbot

**Source:** Semrush via Search Engine Roundtable recap, dated 8 September | https://www.seroundtable.com/recap-09-08-2026-42041.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]], [[SEO Portfolio]]

A Semrush study found 57.5% of people who ask an AI chatbot about a potential purchase report being talked out of it altogether — the chatbot recommending against buying, suggesting cheaper alternatives, or raising concerns that changed the user's mind. This is a genuinely uncomfortable number for any affiliate or lead-gen business model, [[Comparison Network]] included: if a meaningful share of users are now consulting ChatGPT or Gemini before or during a comparison-shopping journey, and that consultation has better-than-even odds of talking them out of converting at all, that's a new leak in the funnel that didn't exist when the competition was other websites rather than a persuasive conversational agent with no commercial incentive to convert the user.

No action needed today, but this is worth treating as a genuine strategic question rather than a stat to file away: does any [[Comparison Network]] product have visibility into whether its own prospective customers are checking with an AI chatbot mid-journey, and if so, what is that chatbot actually telling them about the product category.

## Story 4: Google confirms EU DMA compliance caused its "largest reduction in quality ever" in search results

**Source:** Search Engine Land, dated 8 September | https://searchengineland.com/google-says-dma-changes-in-eu-resulted-in-worse-degradation-of-search-quality-ever-487340
**Related vault notes:** none directly

Google stated publicly that changes made to comply with the EU's Digital Markets Act produced the largest degradation in search result quality the company has measured. No portfolio properties are EU-domiciled or EU-audience-primary, so this doesn't touch [[SEO Portfolio]] or [[Comparison Network]] directly, but it's a significant admission from Google about the real product cost of the DMA's forced changes, and worth watching as a precedent — the UK, and potentially other jurisdictions with comparable market-power regulation on the table, will be watching the same data.

## Story 5: AI Overview and AI Mode product changes — citation panel redesign and paginated follow-ups

**Source:** Search Engine Roundtable recap, dated 8 September | https://www.seroundtable.com/recap-09-08-2026-42041.html
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Two smaller but GEO-relevant product changes surfaced today: Google is testing a thinner redesign of the citations panel inside AI Overview results (affecting how prominently a cited source is displayed), and AI Mode is testing paginated follow-up responses with a skip option rather than a single long answer. Neither is a ranking change, but both affect how much visibility a cited source actually gets once a user is inside an AI-generated answer — the citation panel redesign in particular is worth a screenshot-and-monitor if it reaches any portfolio query set, since a thinner panel could mean lower click-through even for sources that are still being cited.

## Story 6: Search infrastructure is quietly fragmenting — SerpApi and ChatGPT both building independent search indexes

**Source:** Search Engine Roundtable recap, dated 8 September (via Natzir and Peec AI) | https://www.seroundtable.com/recap-09-08-2026-42041.html
**Related vault notes:** none directly

Two separate signals point the same direction: SerpApi has an independent search index in alpha/preview with 3.2 billion pages indexed, positioned as an alternative to scraping Google's results; and ChatGPT is reportedly developing its own proprietary search capability rather than relying entirely on Bing or partner search APIs. Grouped as one story because they represent the same structural shift — the search data layer that GEO and SEO tooling depends on is starting to diversify away from a Google-only or Google/Bing-duopoly foundation. Early-stage and not actionable yet, but worth tracking as these indexes mature, since an independent, non-Google-derived index changes what "ranking" even means for AI-search-visibility tooling.
