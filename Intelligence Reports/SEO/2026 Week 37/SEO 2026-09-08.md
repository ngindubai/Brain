## Story 1: Google's John Mueller warns programmatic SEO risks Google "losing faith" in a site — direct relevance to the SEO Portfolio

**Source:** Search Engine Roundtable, published today | https://www.seroundtable.com/google-lose-faith-42032.html
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Responding to a site owner on Bluesky, John Mueller said programmatic SEO — generating large numbers of pages by combining templates with data like locations, attributes or domain names — "often leads to a site that's either spam, borderline spam, or low quality." His sharpest line: "It's easy to spin something up with many pages, it's hard to provide real value to users." Most significantly for any site already under pressure, he said Google's systems "have possibly lost faith in your site providing good value to users based on the old pages" — and that recovering from that loss of trust "tends to take time & significant effort to show the value," comparable in scale to recovering from a spam action or a core-update hit. His advice to the site owner was to look at how others have handled spam and core-update recovery, since the playbook is similar.

This is worth reading carefully rather than filing away, because it isn't news that programmatic SEO carries risk — it's a specific, on-the-record Google statement about the mechanism of failure (lost trust in old pages, not just algorithmic demotion) and the cost of reversing it (months, not a re-crawl). Every property under [[SEO Portfolio]] is programmatic by design, which is exactly the pattern Mueller is describing. The actionable read isn't "stop doing programmatic SEO" — Google has never said that categorically, and plenty of programmatic sites rank fine — it's that the differentiator Google's systems are watching for is genuine per-page value versus templated thinness, and that if any portfolio site is already seeing soft rankings, the fix is real content investment on the affected pages, not spinning up more of them. Worth an honest audit: does any pet-transport, funeral-repatriation, close-protection, bus-hire or pest-control page look like it could read as "spun up" rather than genuinely useful on inspection? That's a cheaper question to answer now than after a ranking drop makes it urgent.

## Story 2: Google quietly expands two AI Mode ad experiments

**Source:** Search Engine Land, published yesterday (7 Sept) and Search Engine Roundtable, published yesterday | https://searchengineland.com/google-tests-traditional-search-campaigns-in-ai-mode-487199
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Google confirmed (per Ginny Marvin) a "small experiment" letting exact- and phrase-match Search campaigns — previously locked out in favour of AI Max and Performance Max — serve text ads inside AI Mode, but only when Google detects "explicit and direct user intent" matching the advertiser's keywords. No rollout date or permanence has been confirmed; Google frames it as incremental testing, not a replacement for its automated campaign types.

For [[Comparison Network]], this is a bookmark rather than an action: if it expands, it would let tightly-controlled keyword campaigns (the kind a comparison site typically runs to protect margin on paid clicks) reach AI Mode traffic without being forced into Performance Max's more automated, harder-to-audit bidding. Nothing to change today, but worth revisiting if Google confirms a wider rollout.

## Story 3: New data suggests AI Overviews may be reshaping Shopping ad performance in a way that's easy to misread

**Source:** Search Engine Land, published yesterday (7 Sept), citing Smarter Ecommerce and Optmyzr data | https://searchengineland.com/ai-overviews-may-be-affecting-shopping-ad-ctr-and-impressions-487265
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Analysis across hundreds of advertiser accounts (Mike Ryan, Smarter Ecommerce, corroborated by Optmyzr showing a 17% year-on-year CTR rise) found Shopping and Performance Max campaign click-through rates rising roughly 17-20% year-on-year while impressions fall noticeably and clicks stay flat or slightly down. The proposed explanation: Google may be selectively serving AI Overviews for lower-intent queries while reserving Shopping ad placements for higher-intent searches — an "experience switching" pattern that lets both formats coexist without cannibalising each other's revenue.

The catch, and the reason this is worth flagging rather than just logging as a positive CTR trend: rising CTR here doesn't mean ads are performing better, it means fewer, more selective impressions are being shown on the queries Google judges most commercially valuable. Anyone reading a CTR uptick in isolation as "our ads are working better" would be misreading the data. No portfolio product runs Shopping campaigns directly, but the underlying mechanism — Google quietly reallocating which queries get an AI Overview versus a paid placement — is the same one that will eventually touch organic AI Overview exposure for any [[SEO Portfolio]] or [[Comparison Network]] page. Worth remembering as a general caution the next time a CTR metric looks unexpectedly good: check impression volume before concluding anything changed for the better.
