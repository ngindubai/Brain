## Story 1: "People Also Ask" is now almost entirely AI Overviews — up from 12% fourteen months ago

**Source:** Search Engine Roundtable, dated 9 September | https://www.seroundtable.com/ppa-ai-overviews-google-42047.html
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Data cited in Search Engine Roundtable's reporting puts AI Overviews now answering nearly 100% of "People Also Ask" queries, with a more conservative independent tracker (Allintitle's Also Ask Miner) showing 97% of PAA answers AI-generated in the first week of September, up sharply from 86% in August and about 12% fourteen months ago. This is the same underlying trend this report flagged via the Similarweb referral-share data two reports ago (ChatGPT taking 56.7% of gen-AI referral traffic), but from the supply side rather than the demand side: it's not just that users are increasingly asking AI directly, Google's own SERP has now almost fully converted one of its most heavily-optimised-for features into an AI-generated answer surface.

For [[SEO Portfolio]] and [[Comparison Network]], the practical read is that PAA-targeting content strategies — writing to directly answer the exact phrasing of a PAA question in hopes of winning that box — have lost almost all of their addressable surface, because the box itself is no longer a place a normal search result can win; it is now Google's own AI summary, sourced from wherever Google's model chooses to pull from. Genuine GEO work (structuring content so an AI system can extract and cite it accurately) has effectively replaced classic PAA optimisation as of this data point, not as a future prediction but as something that has already happened.

## Story 2: Search Engine Land surveys 131 SEO professionals on what actually drives rankings in 2026

**Source:** Search Engine Land, dated 9 September | https://searchengineland.com/google-top-ranking-factors-survey-131-seo-professionals-487723
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Danny Goodwin's piece surveys 131 working SEO professionals on which ranking factors they believe actually move the needle in practice, rather than Google's own stated guidance. Useful specifically as a counterweight to how much airtime AI Overviews and GEO have taken in recent reports — a reminder that the practitioner consensus still centres on more conventional signals (content quality/relevance, technical health, backlink profile, user experience) even as the AI-visibility conversation dominates industry commentary. No single dramatic finding to flag, but worth reading in full before any [[SEO Portfolio]] resourcing decision that might otherwise over-index on AI-visibility work at the expense of fundamentals that the same 131 practitioners still rate more highly.

## Story 3: Google's spam-update recovery playbook — don't rush, don't self-audit, diversify off Google

**Source:** Search Engine Land, dated 9 September | https://searchengineland.com/google-spam-update-dont-make-recovery-harder-487454
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Kaspar Szymanski's piece lays out recovery guidance for sites hit by the August 2026 spam update: don't delete content wholesale or file for reconsideration before a proper audit (both read as panic responses that hurt more than help), use an independent third party to audit rather than the same team that built the site (structural conflict of interest doing your own audit), and treat full compliance with Google's spam policies as the bar now, not just fixing the one flagged issue. The piece's broader point is the more useful one for [[SEO Portfolio]]: relying on Google for over 50% of traffic is now framed as an active business risk worth deliberately reducing, with brand-building and alternative search engines (Bing named specifically) as the mitigation.

Worth treating as a standing checklist rather than a one-off read: if any portfolio site (pet transport, funeral repatriation, close protection, bus hire, pest control) is ever caught by a future spam action, the instinct to move fast and delete content is explicitly the wrong one per this guidance.

## Story 4: Google Merchant Center adds AI-attribution metrics; Google Ads AI Dashboards begin rolling out

**Source:** Search Engine Roundtable recap, dated 9 September | https://www.seroundtable.com/recap-09-09-2026-42052.html
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Google added three new metrics to Merchant Center's AI Performance Insights — AI Search intent, AI Search terms, and AI attributes data — extending functionality first launched in July, and Google Ads AI Dashboards are now appearing directly in advertiser accounts (first flagged in yesterday's SEL coverage as a rollout-in-progress, now visibly landing). Together these give merchants and advertisers their first real visibility into how much of their traffic and conversions originate from AI-surface queries specifically, rather than lumping it into generic organic or AI Overview referral numbers.

Any [[Comparison Network]] or e-commerce-adjacent [[SEO Portfolio]] property running Google Ads or a Merchant Center feed should check whether these new metrics have appeared in-account yet — this is the first concrete measurement tool for the AI-referral concentration problem flagged in prior reports (Story 2, two reports ago), rather than just industry-level survey data.
