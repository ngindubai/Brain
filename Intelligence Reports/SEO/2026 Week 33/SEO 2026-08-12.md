## Story 1: Nearly 300 French newspapers file fresh competition complaint over Google AI Overviews

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/french-newspapers-complaint-google-ai-overviews-484631

**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

APIG (Alliance de la Presse d'Information Générale), representing close to 300 French newspapers, has filed a complaint with France's competition authority alleging Google rolled out AI Overviews without consent, in breach of a 2022 compensation agreement between Google and French publishers. The complaint's core claim: AI Overviews answer user questions directly inside Search results, cutting referral traffic to the original reporting the summaries draw on. APIG president Marc Feuillee framed it carefully — "The editors are not seeking to halt innovation. They are seeking value sharing and content compensation" — positioning this as a licensing dispute rather than an attempt to block the feature outright. France's competition authority already fined Google €250m in 2024 for non-compliance with the original 2022 deal, and the EU separately opened an investigation in December into whether Google used publisher content for AI services without adequate compensation. This complaint sits on top of both.

**Signal for Orwell:** No direct portfolio action today, but this is now a live regulatory track worth watching specifically rather than folding into general AI Overviews commentary — a competition-authority ruling against Google in France would be the first hard precedent on AI Overviews compensation obligations, and could shape how aggressively Google deploys AI Overviews summarisation on publisher-style content elsewhere, which is directly relevant to [[SEO Portfolio]] sites that depend on that traffic.

## Story 3: A usable 5-layer framework for measuring AI search performance beyond referral traffic

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/ai-search-performance-measurment-framework-484546

**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Worth flagging as a tool, not just a story. The framework: **Layer 1, AI Access** — verify AI crawlers can actually reach your content (reverse DNS lookups, not just user-agent string checks). **Layer 2, AI Visibility** — build a standardised prompt library reflecting real buyer-journey questions and track mention/citation rates in AI answers. **Layer 3, AI Assistants & Referral Traffic** — capture direct GA4 attribution from identifiable LLM referrers (ChatGPT clicks, etc.). **Layer 4, Dark Funnel & Downstream Demand** — watch branded-search clicks and conversions that follow AI discovery through a different channel. **Layer 5, Business Outcomes** — pipeline, closed-won, revenue. The framework's honest core point: no single metric proves AI-driven attribution, but a coherent progression across all five layers builds a credible case.

**Signal for Orwell:** This is the most directly usable piece in today's report — a structured way to actually check whether AI Overviews/AI Mode exposure is doing anything for [[SEO Portfolio]] or [[Comparison Network]] sites, versus the current default of assuming it's a pure headwind. Worth adapting Layer 1 and Layer 2 specifically as a lightweight check across the five portfolio sites next time a proper AEO/GEO audit happens.

## Story 4: LinkedIn publishes a "Credibility Stack" playbook for AI chatbot citations

**Source:** Social Media Today | https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-playbook-offers-guide-to-optimizing-for-ai/827635/

**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

LinkedIn — already the #2 most-cited domain across AI chatbots per Meltwater's analysis of 9.5 million citations, appearing in roughly 11% of AI responses across ChatGPT Search, Perplexity and Google AI Mode — has published its own optimisation guidance for brands. Headline finding: long-form LinkedIn articles outperform feed posts for AI citation, and "answer block" formatting (major sections opening with a tight 30-80 word direct answer) measurably increases citation rates. LinkedIn's own caveat is the important part: no amount of formatting or schema fixes weak content — substance and demonstrated expertise remain the actual gate.

**Signal for Orwell:** The "answer block" formatting finding (30-80 word direct-answer openers per section) is a concrete, testable technique, not just platform-specific advice — worth trialling on portfolio site content generally, not only anything published to LinkedIn itself.

## Story 5: DMCA takedown abuse is being weaponised against legitimate publishers

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/dmca-weapon-fight-fraudulent-takedown-claims-484562

**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

The DMCA's Section 512 safe harbour rewards platforms for removing flagged content fast, with minimal verification — creating an incentive structure bad actors exploit three ways: scraping content and backdating it to claim originality, filing under fabricated organisation names, and burying negative press or reviews with dubious claims. The article's example: investigative reporting on a site-reputation-abuse case vanished from Google worldwide within 48 hours after a fraudulent claim from an entity called "US Webspam." Defence steps: check the Lumen Database for who filed the complaint, gather timestamped proof of prior ownership (CMS logs, Wayback Machine, RSS records, git commits), file a formal counter-notice, wait the statutory 10-14 days, and escalate publicly with Section 512(f) liability exposure for obviously fraudulent claims if needed. Proactive defence: auto-archive on publish, clear bylined schema metadata, monitor Search Console alerts, store originals offline.

**Signal for Orwell:** Worth a direct, practical check — do any of the five [[SEO Portfolio]] sites (pet transport, funeral repatriation, close protection, bus hire, pest control) have auto-archiving and offline originals in place for published content? This is cheap insurance against exactly the kind of attack described, and currently unconfirmed either way.
