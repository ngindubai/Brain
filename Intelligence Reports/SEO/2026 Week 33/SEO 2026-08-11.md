## Story 1: Google tests AI-Mode buttons directly on the search homepage

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-confirms-testing-new-buttons-on-home-page-to-drive-users-to-ai-powered-search-features-484597

**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Google is running a small desktop test adding three buttons — "create images," "ask about files," "brainstorm" — beneath the search box, sitting under a plus icon, each launching AI Mode with a pre-set starting prompt. Google's VP of Product Robby Stein confirmed it directly: "This is a small test on desktop to help people find new things they can do with Search... No impact to how the core search box works." Small test today, but it's a homepage-level nudge toward AI Mode rather than a feature buried in a menu — the kind of change that, if it graduates from test to default, measurably shifts query volume away from a traditional results page before a query is even typed.

**Signal for Orwell:** Nothing to act on today, but this is the leading edge of a trend worth tracking closely for the whole SEO portfolio and comparison network: every homepage-level nudge toward AI Mode is a small permanent tax on organic click-through, on top of the AI Overview CTR drops already logged in prior reports (34-58% on queries that trigger an Overview). Worth a standing watch rather than a one-off note.

## Story 2: Study finds ChatGPT shows ads on 26% of commercial prompts, 14% mismatched

**Source:** Search Engine Land, citing SE Ranking research | https://searchengineland.com/study-chatgpt-ads-appear-on-26-of-commercial-prompts-484590

**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

SE Ranking analysed over 50,000 commercial ChatGPT prompts across 20 niches: roughly one in four surfaced a sponsored placement, and 14.35% of those ads were semantically unrelated to the prompt they appeared against, based on semantic-similarity analysis. Mismatch rates varied wildly by category — 2.6% in Pets versus over 50% in Relationships and News & Politics. ChatGPT's targeting runs on advertiser-supplied natural-language "context hints" rather than keyword matching, and advertisers currently can't see which conversations trigger their ads, making relevance problems hard to fix from the buy side. A separate finding: only 3.63% of advertisers also appeared as cited sources in the AI-generated answer above their ad, meaning buying a ChatGPT ad doesn't meaningfully improve a brand's organic visibility inside the answer itself. Ad density on sensitive categories is notably heavier than Google's AI Mode — healthcare ads appeared in 28.69% of ChatGPT responses versus 2.64% in Google's AI Mode.

**Signal for Orwell:** Early-stage data on a genuinely new ad channel, not an action item — but the finding that ad spend doesn't buy citation visibility is worth remembering if Orwell or a client ever considers ChatGPT Ads as a GEO play: the study suggests it's a separate spend from earning citation placement, not a shortcut to it.

## Story 4: Google expands AI agent features into Ads and Analytics dashboards

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-brings-new-ai-agent-capabilities-to-ads-and-analytics-484542

**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Agent Org]]

Google is expanding its "Ask Advisor" AI assistant across Google Analytics and Google Ads. Analytics now surfaces AI-generated homepage summaries of "key performance changes since a user last logged in," with email/mobile notification options; Ads gets a redesigned homepage with personalised insight cards and natural-language Q&A about competitor impact or campaign trends. A new dashboards feature generates visual reports from a text prompt. Google Ads dashboards are live now; Analytics support is "coming soon."

**Signal for Orwell:** Directly relevant to any Orwell marketing-agent role that currently pulls and interprets Ads/Analytics data manually — this is Google building the same summarisation layer natively that an Orwell agent would otherwise need to build. Worth a look at whether native Ask Advisor output can feed into or replace part of that agent's workflow rather than duplicating it.
