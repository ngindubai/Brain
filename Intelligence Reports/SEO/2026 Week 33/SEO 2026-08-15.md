## Story 1: Gemini 3.7 Flash starts rolling out inside Google Search's AI Mode

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/gemini-3-7-flash-rolling-out-in-google-search-485058

**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Google has begun deploying Gemini 3.7 Flash directly into Search, initially available to AI Mode for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers. This is a faster, more efficient successor to Gemini 3.6 Flash being pushed into the query-answering layer itself rather than just powering the separate Gemini app — a continuation of Google folding its newest models into Search as fast as they ship, rather than keeping Search on a slower, more conservative model cadence.

**Signal for Orwell:** Directly relevant to [[SEO Portfolio]]'s AI Overviews exposure. A faster underlying model in AI Mode likely means broader and quicker AI Mode rollout to non-subscribers over time — worth treating as a leading indicator that AI-mediated answers will keep eating a larger share of query volume across the five portfolio sites' niches, reinforcing yesterday's technical-debt story: the fundamentals (clean extraction, unambiguous structure) matter more as the underlying model doing the extracting gets faster and cheaper to run at scale.

## Story 2: Google sets AI Max auto-upgrade timeline for Search campaigns

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-sets-ai-max-migration-timeline-for-search-campaigns-485006

**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Google announced that eligible standard Search campaigns will begin automatically upgrading to AI Max starting 1 September 2026, retiring the older campaign structure. AI Max enables broader search-term matching and automated text customisation beyond what standard Search campaigns currently allow.

**Signal for Orwell:** A second Google Ads change landing the same week as Thursday's language-targeting removal (also effective late September) — together they're a clear pattern of Google stripping manual control levers out of Search campaigns in favour of automated matching. Worth a single consolidated review of [[Comparison Network]]'s paid campaigns before September rather than reacting to each change separately, since both reduce the same kind of manual control at once.

## Story 3: LLM referral traffic converts differently — and needs different landing pages

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/llm-traffic-converts-differently-what-to-do-484964

**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Piece argues traffic arriving via LLM referrals (ChatGPT, Perplexity, AI Mode citations) converts at meaningfully higher rates than average paid search traffic, but only when the landing page is built for a visitor who already got a synthesised answer elsewhere and is now verifying or completing an action — not a visitor still forming the question. Standard SEO-optimised landing pages built to answer a query from scratch underperform for this segment.

**Signal for Orwell:** Consistent with yesterday's Scrunch data showing AI referral volume is still small in absolute terms for smaller publishers, but this adds a useful qualifier: what little AI-referred traffic the five portfolio sites do get is worth checking against a "does this page assume the visitor already knows the answer" lens, since that's a cheap page-level fix rather than a strategy change.

## Story 5: Somantra launches a "Brand Engagement Score" for AEO/GEO visibility

**Source:** GlobeNewswire | https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/14/3345367/0/en/somantra-launches-brand-engagement-score-for-aeo-and-geo-an-ai-search-metric-that-measures-chatgpt-google-ai-overviews-claude-and-perplexity-engagement-with-a-brand.html

**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Sydney-based AEO/GEO platform Somantra launched a new scoring metric measuring how deeply and favourably an AI search conversation engages with a brand's specific claims and differentiators — not just whether the brand gets named at all — across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Claude and Perplexity.

**Signal for Orwell:** One more entrant in the fast-growing AI-visibility measurement-tool space (following yesterday's flag on Microsoft Clarity's AI Scrape-to-Referral report). Worth noting as a category rather than acting on individually — several vendors are now converging on the same problem (measuring AI-mediated brand presence), which suggests the tooling gap will close on its own before Orwell needs to build or buy anything custom.
