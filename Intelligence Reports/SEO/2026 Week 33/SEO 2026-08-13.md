## Story 1: Federal judge lets SEO sabotage lawsuit over toxic backlinks proceed

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/seo-sabotage-lawsuit-toxic-backlinks-484795

**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Auto transport company Montway sued Nexus AT LLC and its CEO George Arkin, alleging Nexus deployed over 2,350 toxic backlinks — with anchor text like "buy steroids online" and "payday loan services" — specifically to damage Montway's Google rankings between April and October 2025. Federal judge Matthew Kennelly refused to dismiss the case, allowing it to proceed on false advertising claims under the Lanham Act plus trademark and Illinois consumer protection violations (only the California unfair competition claim was dismissed). The court's key finding: the toxic anchor text itself constitutes "literal falsehood" under advertising law, not just a search-ranking manipulation tactic. If the theory survives to a full ruling, it opens a genuinely new legal avenue against SEO sabotage beyond Google's own spam-reporting tools, which have historically been slow and unreliable for victims.

**Signal for Orwell:** Read alongside yesterday's DMCA-weaponisation story, this is the second consecutive day surfacing a real legal mechanism against competitive SEO attacks — worth keeping both in mind together as the practical toolkit if any [[SEO Portfolio]] site (pet transport, funeral repatriation, close protection, bus hire, pest control) is ever targeted with a toxic-backlink or fraudulent-takedown campaign, rather than defaulting straight to Google's disavow tool as the only option.

## Story 2: Query templates — a framework for expanding topical authority

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/query-templates-topical-authority-484676

**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Worth flagging as a working method, not just a story. The core idea: searches cluster around recurring templates — "how to [action]," "[entity] + [attribute]," "[location] + [service]" — and topical authority compounds fastest when a site covers both dimensions at once: every relevant entity and attribute within a topic, across every query-template variation of how people ask for it. The piece frames this against Google's actual cost constraints — "the cost of ranking a site can't exceed the cost of not ranking a site" — arguing sites should structure content into "core" (commercial) and "outer" (informational) sections with internal linking directing authority signals toward the core pages, and should only open a new page when a query involves a genuinely different entity or low semantic overlap with existing content, rather than one page per minor keyword variation.

**Signal for Orwell:** Directly usable for [[SEO Portfolio]]. The five portfolio sites are built around a small number of core service entities (pet transport, funeral repatriation, close protection, bus hire, pest control) with location and service-attribute variations — this framework is close to a direct fit for auditing whether each site's internal linking is actually routing authority toward its core commercial pages, or diluting it across too many thin variation pages.

## Story 3: Google adds Local customer optimization to Performance Max

**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-adds-local-customer-optimization-to-performance-max-484785

**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Performance Max campaigns with store goals can now enable "Local customer optimization," a toggle that prioritises ad delivery to people nearby and actively navigating, trip-planning, or searching locally — surfaced across Google Maps, Waze and local Search formats. The catch: it can't be used alongside Merchant Center product feeds, so advertisers running both have to choose between a dedicated store-goals campaign or dropping the product feed.

**Signal for Orwell:** Relevant to the standing LSA-to-Performance-Max migration open loop — pest-control-usa is in the first migration wave, and pest control is exactly the kind of high local-intent, service-area business this feature targets. Worth checking whether the site's PMax setup is eligible and configured to use it once the migration reaches that stage.
