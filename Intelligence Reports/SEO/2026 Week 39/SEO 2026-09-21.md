## Story 1: Gmail AI Overviews complete their global rollout today

**Source:** Google Workspace Updates | https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/09/gmail-searchs-ai-overviews-now-available-globally.html
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Google announced on 15 September that AI Overviews in Gmail search are expanding globally to paid-plan users with English set as their language, having launched in the US in April. The rollout itself is staged: Rapid Release Workspace domains and consumer accounts started seeing it from 3 September, and today, 21 September, is the day Scheduled Release domains (the slower-moving majority of Workspace tenants) begin their rollout, with visibility expected within one to three days.

This is a Workspace/Gmail feature, not a Search ranking change, so there is no direct portfolio impact today. It's worth a line in the report because it's the same generative-answer pattern Google has been pushing across every surface this year: pull a natural-language answer out of a pile of content (inbox, web pages, product feeds) rather than making the user click through. Every one of Orwell's SEO sites already competes with that pattern in web search; seeing Google extend it into Gmail is confirmation the company treats "answer instead of list of results" as the default UI now, not an experiment.

No action needed. Filed for the GEO watch list, not the portfolio watch list.

## Story 3: Ad-tech antitrust remedies — correcting the open-loop detail, no new movement today

**Source:** AdExchanger | https://www.adexchanger.com/platforms/the-court-just-unsealed-judge-brinkemas-remedies-decision-in-the-google-ad-tech-antitrust-case-heres-your-tldr/
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

No new development today, but worth tightening the open-loop entry since the underlying facts were slightly off. Judge Leonie Brinkema's 106-page remedies opinion was unsealed on 16 September, not held back for a single "judgment date." She rejected the DOJ's push to force Google to divest AdX and/or DFP, opting instead for behavioural remedies: Google must build API integrations connecting AdX and DFP to Prebid, submit AdX bids to rival publisher ad servers on the same terms DFP gets, and operate under a six-year third-party Monitor and Technical Committee (extendable if Google doesn't comply in time).

The 2 October date in the existing open loop is real but is not the final judgment itself — it's the deadline for both DOJ and Google to submit their proposed final judgment language reflecting Brinkema's ruling. The remedy itself only takes effect 60 days after Brinkema signs the official order, so the practical start date is more likely late November or December, not early October. This doesn't touch the comparison network's paid-search buying directly (Google isn't being forced to sell AdX or DFP), but the Prebid integration requirement is a genuine structural change to how publisher-side programmatic works, relevant background if the comparison network ever runs its own display inventory.
