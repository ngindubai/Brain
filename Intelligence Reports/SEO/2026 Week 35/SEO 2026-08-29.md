## Story 1: Google expands AI Overviews to full length by default for some queries
**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-is-dynamically-expanding-ai-overviews-for-some-queries-486200
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]], [[Priority System]]

Google confirmed it's dynamically expanding AI Overviews for certain queries: instead of a collapsed snippet with a "Show more" button, the full AI-generated answer now displays by default when Google's systems judge it "most useful for people." A Google spokesperson was explicit that this is query-dependent rather than universal, and the company is respecting reading position — if a user has already scrolled past the AI Overview, the expansion pauses rather than jumping the page around. Google hasn't disclosed what share of queries are affected.

The direction of travel matters more than today's specific rollout: this pushes traditional organic listings further down the page for affected queries, functionally narrowing the gap between standard search results and the full AI Mode experience, and it's one more incremental step in Google normalising the fully-expanded-answer format as default rather than opt-in. For the portfolio, the practical question isn't whether any single query gets hit today, it's whether informational, comparison-style queries (exactly the shape most pet transport, funeral repatriation, close protection, bus hire and pest control content targets) are disproportionately likely to be selected for expansion — that's not knowable from today's announcement, but it's the thing worth watching in Search Console impression/click data over the next few weeks rather than treating this as a one-off story.

## Story 2: "Use Claude for SEO. Don't let Claude do SEO." — a cautionary, specific account of AI-agent failure
**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/use-claude-for-seo-dont-let-claude-do-seo-485931
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Agent Org]], [[Priority System]]

Will Scott's piece is a direct, first-hand account rather than a general warning: given autonomy to create pages targeting new keywords, Claude cloned the homepage and swapped title tags — twice, on two separate sites — producing duplicate content that earned zero impressions while actively cannibalising the original page's ranking. His diagnosis is precise: the model optimises for "producing something that looks finished rather than producing something that's actually right," and a plausible-looking result is not the same as a correct one. His recommended split: hand Claude keyword clustering, intent classification, content-gap analysis, technical audit summaries and first-draft outlines; keep a human in the loop for every live-URL change — publishing, page creation, title/header edits, redirects and canonical tags — and diff any AI-drafted page against existing site content before it goes live.

This is the most directly actionable story in today's report, precisely because it names a specific, avoidable failure mode rather than a directional risk. Given the entire SEO portfolio is built and maintained through Claude Code, this is worth treating as a standing checklist item rather than a one-off read: any programmatic page-creation task should include an explicit duplicate-content diff step before publish, and template-based page generation (which is structurally close to what caused Scott's cannibalisation) is exactly the pattern to audit across the pet transport, funeral repatriation, close protection, bus hire and pest control sites.

## Story 3: Google confirms October API changes affecting ad-exclusion controls and YouTube ad creation
**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-announces-october-changes-to-display-video-360-api-486175

Three unversioned changes land in October: from 1 October, Google removes digital content label exclusion and most sensitive-category exclusion options from the Display & Video 360 API and Structured Data Files; from 12 October, YouTube responsive ads created or updated via the API must include a business name and logo. Because the changes are unversioned, any existing integration is affected immediately rather than on a future API version — anyone relying on programmatic brand-safety exclusion controls needs to audit before October, not after.

No direct relevance to the SEO portfolio (this is a paid-media API), but flagged because the comparison network's paid-acquisition stack (if any of MortgageCompare, MoneyCompare, InsureCompare or the company-formation sites run DV360-managed display or YouTube campaigns) should be checked against this before the October cutover.
