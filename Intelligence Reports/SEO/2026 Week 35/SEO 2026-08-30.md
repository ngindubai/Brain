## Story 1: Google won't enforce site-reputation-abuse penalties in the EEA
**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-wont-respect-manual-actions-for-site-reputation-abuse-in-european-economic-area-486055 and Search Engine Roundtable | https://www.seroundtable.com/google-site-reputation-policy-eea-41968.html

From 30 August 2026, a site-reputation-abuse (parasite SEO) manual action still gets issued in Search Console for EEA-facing sites, but it no longer suppresses rankings for searchers physically in the European Economic Area. Outside the EEA, the same manual action still directly demotes the penalised section. This follows a European Commission investigation under the Digital Markets Act, after publishers complained the policy was being used to push down news organisations carrying commercial partner content.

Two things worth noting. First, the UK is not in the EEA — Brexit means UK searchers stay fully subject to the penalty, so anyone measuring visibility on blended UK/EU dashboards will see a false split. Second, this is a regulatory carve-out, not a policy reversal: Google's detection and demotion machinery is untouched, only its application in one jurisdiction is switched off. No GCC or UAE equivalent to the DMA exists, so this has no direct bearing on the [[Comparison Network]] or the pest control/bus hire/close protection sites, which don't target EEA searchers specifically. Filed as a market signal: it is proof that sustained regulatory pressure can force Google to fragment ranking enforcement by geography, which is a precedent worth remembering if EU-style scrutiny ever reaches other verticals Orwell touches.

## Story 2: goto redirect wrapping now near-complete across residential IPs
**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-confirms-deploying-goto-url-redirects-to-search-results-links-485926 and anicca.co.uk weekly roundup | https://anicca.co.uk/blog/weekly-update-search-marketing-28-08-2026/

The google.com/goto passthrough redirect on organic result links — first confirmed in prior weeks and carried here as an open loop — has now reached near-complete rollout for residential IP addresses. Google frames it as anti-scraping infrastructure, making it materially harder for third-party tools to harvest clean destination URLs directly off the results page.

Practical read for the portfolio: any home-grown or lightly-configured rank-checking script that scrapes SERP HTML rather than using an API is now more likely to choke or misreport on affected queries. Established tools (Semrush, Ahrefs) will absorb this on their end, but if any project uses a DIY scraper for position tracking, verify it against a paid tool before trusting a sudden ranking swing. This status change moves the open loop from "watch" to "expect visible side effects now."

## Story 3: AI Overviews drop the "Show more" click, jump straight to full AI Mode-style answers
**Source:** Search Engine Roundtable | https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-overviews-push-ai-mode-responses-41974.html and Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-tests-pushing-searchers-from-ai-overviews-show-more-button-to-ai-mode-465523

This is the same underlying change flagged in yesterday's brief (AI Overviews dynamically expanding to full length), now with the mechanism spelled out. For qualifying queries Google no longer shows the "Show more" button at all — the full, AI Mode-style response loads by default, with the "Ask anything" box already open, and organic results get pushed further down the page. Google's line remains "for topics where our systems determine it's most useful for people."

Not a new story so much as confirmation the change removes an interaction step that previously gave publishers a fighting chance at a click. Keep watching Search Console's AI Overview appearance data on the SEO portfolio's highest-volume informational pages — this is the kind of shift that shows up as a click-through-rate drop with impressions unchanged, not as a ranking drop.

## Story 4: ChatGPT–Reddit citation collapse hardens into a Perplexity/ChatGPT split, with hard numbers
**Source:** Web search aggregation (Pierview, explainx.ai analysis of tracked citation data) | https://www.pierview.ai/guides/reddit-citations-chatgpt-perplexity-decline-2026 and https://www.explainx.ai/blog/reddit-citations-chatgpt-search-drop-august-2026

Firmer figures on the thread carried in prior reports: ChatGPT Search's Reddit citations fell from roughly 250/day in early August to 16/day within a week of 14 August — a 94% collapse — after Reddit blocked crawler access to its whole domain via robots.txt, cutting the licensed pipeline it previously fed ChatGPT. Perplexity took the opposite path, roughly doubling its Reddit citations over the same window and now accounting for 71% of tracked Reddit citations between 1 July and 19 August against ChatGPT's 23%. Separately, publishers with an OpenAI licensing deal (confirmed 20 August) earn 48% more ChatGPT citations than those without one.

For any GEO/AI-visibility work on the [[SEO Portfolio]] or [[Comparison Network]], this confirms the two platforms are no longer interchangeable citation sources: Perplexity is the one still pulling from UGC-style community content, ChatGPT increasingly favours licensed and directly-crawlable publisher content. Worth factoring into which platform gets weight in any future AI-visibility tracking build.

## Story 5: Ahrefs retires old Brand Radar volume calculation tomorrow, 31 August
**Source:** Ahrefs via X | https://x.com/ahrefs/status/2085287428550496326 and Ahrefs blog | https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-ahrefs-estimates-ai-search-demand/

Ahrefs is switching Brand Radar from plain Google search volume to "AI-adjusted volume" (parent keyword's Google volume multiplied by a per-platform usage ratio) for prompt volume and impression estimates. The toggle to view the old, unadjusted numbers — in the UI and via the API parameter — is available only until 31 August 2026, tomorrow. After that, all reports and API responses use AI-adjusted volume only, with no way back to the old figures.

Low urgency but a hard deadline: if anyone has pulled or plans to pull Ahrefs volume data for portfolio keyword planning this week, do it today against the old baseline if the comparison matters, since it disappears from tomorrow.

## Story 6: Second Google indexing/serving hiccup in August, 28 August, ~80 minutes
**Source:** Search Engine Roundtable | https://www.seroundtable.com/google-search-indexing-issues-41972.html

Freshly published pages — confirmed indexed in Search Console — weren't surfacing in search results between roughly 6:15am and 7:35am ET on 28 August. Wall Street Journal, New York Times and CNN were among sites affected; Google's own status dashboard showed no incident throughout. This is reportedly the second such indexing/serving gap this month, on top of the standing Search Console generative-AI logging bug open since 13 August.

Individually a non-event — 80 minutes, self-resolved. Cumulatively it's a second data point that Google's real-time indexing pipeline has been less reliable than usual through August, which matters more for programmatic SEO operations publishing pages on a schedule than for a stable content site. Nothing to action, just worth noting as a pattern.
