**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Priority System]]

The Google Search Status Dashboard confirms no new ranking event since the August 2026 spam update closed on 21 August (it ran 18–21 Aug, roughly two days sixteen hours). Ranking, crawling and indexing all show no active incidents as of today. That matches yesterday's read and means the portfolio sites are not sitting inside live volatility right now.

This is a recurring point of confusion every cycle and worth logging because Gareth's own instinct after every "quiet" day is to suspect something moved anyway.

Practically: nothing to action. ## Story 2: Google pushes generative UI into AI Overviews
**Source:** Search Engine Journal (SEO Pulse) | https://www.searchenginejournal.com/seo-pulse-spam-update-generative-ui-ai-overviews-reddit-drop/586597/
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Google is expanding "generative UI" — the tech that builds custom interactive layouts and mini-tools on the fly — out of AI Mode (where it launched in November) and into mainstream AI Overviews, rolling out in English globally. Rather than a block of AI-written text with links underneath, the result page itself starts generating calculators, comparison widgets and interactive elements directly inside the AIO card.

This matters most for anything that competes on a tool or a comparator, which is exactly what half the comparison network is. If Google can generate a mortgage-affordability widget or an insurance-comparison table natively inside the AI Overview, it competes directly with MortgageCompare and InsureCompare's core value proposition — a comparison tool — not just their content. It is a bigger structural threat than a normal AIO text snippet because it doesn't just answer the question, it replaces the interaction the site was built to provide.

No rollout timeline for non-English markets or UAE-specific queries was given, and there's no evidence yet this is live for finance/insurance query types specifically rather than more generic informational ones. Worth a watching brief rather than a fire drill, but this is the clearest early signal that "AI Overviews take the click" is evolving into "AI Overviews take the interaction."

## Story 3: Reddit's ChatGPT Search citations collapse 86% in four weeks, cause unknown
**Source:** Search Engine Journal (SEO Pulse), citing third-party citation-tracking data | https://www.searchenginejournal.com/seo-pulse-spam-update-generative-ui-ai-overviews-reddit-drop/586597/
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]]

Reddit's share of ChatGPT Search citations fell from 3.83% (18 July–7 August) to 0.52% (14–17 August) — an 86.4% drop in about a month. The tracking data flags 8 August as the inflection point, coinciding with an increase in background queries hitting specific websites, but no confirmed cause has been published by OpenAI or independently verified.

This is worth logging rather than acting on. It's a reminder that GEO citation share is volatile and platform-dependent in ways nobody outside OpenAI can fully see or predict — a source (or an entire domain) can lose most of its AI-answer visibility inside a month with zero warning and no public explanation. None of the portfolio sites are meaningfully cited in ChatGPT Search today, so there's no direct exposure, but it's the kind of data point that should temper any strategy built around "get cited in AI answers" as a stable channel rather than a volatile one.

## Story 4: 1.8 million Google Business Profiles study — category specificity and completeness both move rankings hard
**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-business-profiles-local-seo-success-data-485727
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

A study of 1.8 million Google Business Profiles found two clear, quantified levers. First, specific primary categories dramatically outperform generic ones — a 12.5% top-10 appearance rate for specific categories versus 9.2% for generic ones, with an example showing "Hawaiian restaurant" ranking at position 28.8 against "Restaurant" at 49.7. Well-matched additional categories improved rankings by 6–17 positions further; broad catch-all categories like "Service establishment" wasted profile space and did nothing.

Second, profile completeness (a 0–5 index covering website, description, hours, photos and claimed status) correlates strongly with rank: moving from 0 to 5 improved average ranking by roughly 19 positions and tripled the top-10 appearance rate, from 4% to 13%. Home services profiles were the most complete category on average (4.4/5); community organisations the least (3.0/5).

This is directly relevant to any local-services angle across the portfolio and the niche brief categories (pest control, close protection, bus hire, funeral repatriation all have local-service GBP equivalents in target markets). If any portfolio or client business has a Google Business Profile sitting on a generic category or a half-filled listing, this is a cheap, well-evidenced fix — no algorithm risk, no content rebuild, just category and completeness hygiene. Worth a research-note candidate if Gareth wants a checklist built for it.

## Story 5: Google Local Services Ads — direct booking expands 20x, missed calls start costing money 1 October
**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-expands-direct-booking-for-local-services-ads-485807 and https://searchengineland.com/google-local-services-ads-will-charge-for-some-missed-calls-starting-oct-1-485798
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[Comparison Network]]

Two LSA policy moves landed together. Direct booking support expanded from 20 to over 500 "Reserve with Google" partners, letting customers book appointments straight from a Local Services Ad rather than calling. Separately, from 1 October, Google will charge for missed calls: any call lasting more than 20 seconds during business hours counts as a chargeable valid lead even if the business never picks up, with an exception for call-routing systems where the customer must press a key first (the clock only starts after key-press). Follow-up calls that qualify as valid leads are chargeable too, even if the original call wasn't.

Neither article specifies which LSA categories the missed-call billing applies to, so treat it as applying broadly rather than assuming an exemption. This is the kind of change that quietly inflates LSA spend for any business running phone-heavy call tracking without answering promptly — worth a two-line check for anyone running LSA campaigns for pest-control-usa or close-protection-style local lead gen before 1 October: confirm call-answering SLAs and routing setup now, not after the first inflated invoice.

## Story 6: Performance Max gets channel-level controls Google swore it would never need
**Source:** Search Engine Land | https://searchengineland.com/google-tests-channel-prioritization-controls-for-performance-max-485825
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]]

Google is testing new PMax controls letting advertisers adjust channel prioritization — Search, YouTube, Display, Discover, Gmail, Maps — inside what has always been sold as a fully automated, channel-agnostic black box. It's a small but notable concession: years of "trust the algorithm" positioning are giving ground to advertiser demand for at least some steering, likely a response to complaints that PMax over-indexes on cheap inventory (Display/Discover) at the expense of higher-intent Search traffic.

For the comparison network, this is worth testing once it's out of pilot — if PMax campaigns for MortgageCompare or InsureCompare are quietly starved of Search-channel budget in favour of cheaper impressions, a prioritization control could meaningfully shift lead quality without a full campaign rebuild. Not actionable yet; it's in testing with no public rollout date.
