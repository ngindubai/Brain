## 1. US Pest Control

### Aedes aegypti mosquitoes breeding in east London: first confirmed UK breeding event

The UK Health Security Agency confirmed on 3 September that Aedes aegypti mosquitoes and larvae have been found breeding at residential properties in east London. This is the first time the species has been confirmed breeding in the UK, and only the fourth modern detection nationally. The yellow fever mosquito is capable of transmitting dengue, chikungunya, yellow fever, and Zika in regions where those infections already circulate. The UKHSA currently assesses the risk to the public as very low -- no human infections have been attributed to these mosquitoes and no breeding has been confirmed in public spaces. Control measures have been deployed to destroy the larvae, and enhanced surveillance and trapping remain active.

The vector control implication is notable. UKHSA has assessed the find as likely an accidental import via vehicles, baggage, or ornamental plants during the summer heatwave period. The London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine described this as a "rapid reaction" situation, confirming the species can theoretically transmit disease if a dengue-infected traveller were bitten. Climate is the main long-term constraint: the UK's general temperatures remain too cold for Aedes aegypti to establish permanently, but heatwaves are changing the risk profile.

For PCT Online's audience and for SEO operators in the UK pest control market, this is a genuine demand signal. Public concern about invasive mosquitoes will drive search volume. Local authorities and public health bodies will be briefed to advise residents to eliminate standing water -- buckets, containers, paddling pools, plant pots -- creating a residential pest prevention content angle. Specialist vector management services will see enquiry uplift in affected east London postcodes. ([Sources: UKHSA Gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/invasive-mosquito-species-detected-in-east-london), [UKHSA Blog](https://ukhsa.blog.gov.uk/2026/09/03/should-i-be-concerned-that-an-invasive-mosquito-species-has-been-detected-in-east-london/), [LSHTM](https://www.lshtm.ac.uk/newsevents/news/2026/rapid-reaction-mosquitoes-carry-chikungunya-dengue-and-zika-virus-breeding-10))

### UK-EU biocide alignment confirmed: SPS agreement mid-2027 target

The UK Government has confirmed that biocidal product rules will align with EU regulations as part of a new Sanitary and Phytosanitary (SPS) agreement, expected to come into force mid-2027. According to Defra, alignment may affect "product authorisation, labelling, supply chains and operational practices of organisations across the biocides sector." The change follows the UK's post-Brexit retention of EU 528/2012 (GB BPR) and represents a significant compliance shift for pest control operators relying on UK-authorised products that diverge from the EU approval list. The 2026 GB BPR framework currently requires biocidal active substances and formulated products to undergo full HSE evaluation before UK market authorisation. Alignment will effectively return the UK to mutual recognition with the EU active substance approval pipeline.

For operators, this means the 2027 transition will require a product register audit. Products currently UK-authorised under the post-Brexit scheme may need re-evaluation or may benefit from expedited approval via EU mutual recognition. Operators with products in the HSE pipeline should seek confirmation of status before mid-2027. ([Sources: PPC Online](https://ppconline.org/health-safety-and-legislation/government-confirms-uk-eu-alignment-on-biocides-under-new-sps-agreement), [HSE](https://www.hse.gov.uk/biocides/about/regulations-decisions.htm))

### CRRU rodenticide certification: enforcement reminder

Mandatory professional certification for rodenticide purchase under the CRRU (Campaign for Responsible Rodenticide Use) 2026 scheme came into force on 1 January 2026. The requirement that any professional purchasing second-generation anticoagulant rodenticides (SGARs) must hold recognised certification is now in force. Pest controllers who have not yet completed certification are operating in breach. No amnesty period remains. This is a continuing regulatory baseline item -- confirmed active, enforcement assumed. ([Source: CRRU / PGM Pest Control](https://www.pgmpestcontrol.co.uk/post/uk-crru-rodenticide-regulations-2026-complete-guide))

## 2. Pet Transport (IPATA)

### IATA LAR 2026: brachycephalic restrictions tightening across carriers

The 52nd edition of the IATA Live Animals Regulations (in force since 1 January 2026) includes materially tighter guidance on brachycephalic breeds, and the market effect is now visible in operator and airline policy. Key developments this week from search and trade sources:

- The 2026 LAR has specifically tightened container compliance for exotic and wild species and flagged brachycephalic breed transport as an elevated-risk category requiring explicit veterinary fitness documentation.
- Lufthansa and Air France have extended their seasonal brachycephalic embargoes into 2026, banning snub-nosed breeds in cargo hold during summer months. These bans now run longer into September than in prior years.
- Emirates SkyCargo -- recognised as Best Pet Shipping Airline for Middle East/Africa at the 46th IPATA Conference -- has maintained its policy of accepting certain brachycephalic breeds based on IPATA Fit-to-Fly assessments performed by certified veterinarians. This creates a routing opportunity: IPATA member operators can document compliance via the standardised IPATA assessment sheet to access carriers that non-assessed animals cannot use.
- JetBlue and Alaska Airlines rank as the most permissive domestic US carriers in 2026: $125 cabin fee, no breed restrictions on domestic segments, generous quotas per flight.

The practical implication for IPATA members is increasing documentation burden and route planning complexity. The Fit-to-Fly certification programme is no longer a niche offering -- it is a competitive differentiator separating operators who can route brachycephalic pets from those who cannot. ([Sources: Zoospot.nl via IATA](https://zoospot.nl/en/blogs/dog/iata-regels-huisdier-cabine-2026), [0x Cargo pet policies 2026](https://www.0xcargo.com/post/2026-pet-policies-for-major-airlines-where-pets-can-fly-common-restrictions-and-how-0x-cargo-pet), [IPATA Brachycephalic Certification](https://www.ipata.org/brachycephalic-certification-program))

## 4. Close Protection (SIA)

### Consultation week three: the window is live, industry response building

The SIA qualification consultation (opened 28 August, closes 23 October 2026) is three weeks in. No new formal SIA announcements have been published this week -- the authority is in listening mode. The story this week is the industry response ecology that has built up since the consultation opened:

**New close protection refresher training -- mandatory from April 2026 (enforcement note):** The SIA's close protection refresher training requirement, which became mandatory for CP licence renewals from 1 April 2026, is now in its sixth month. Level 3 First Aid at Work has been a renewal prerequisite since the same date. Operatives coming up for renewal who have not completed refresher training will be refused renewal. This is no longer a forthcoming change -- it is active enforcement. Training providers report steady throughput on the refresher qualification. ([Sources: RS Group Training](https://rsgrouptraining.co.uk/sia-announces-new-close-protection-refresher-qualification/), [SIA Training Hub 2026](https://siatraininghub.uk/sia-changes-2026/))

**The 2026 consultation -- what is under review:**

For those joining this story, the five live proposals from the 28 August launch:

1. **First aid as an ongoing licence condition** -- current first aid certificate as a permanent condition of holding a CP licence, not just at initial grant
2. **Independent English language test** -- a new standardised English assessment beyond the current embedded evaluation in training programmes; likely to create barriers for overseas-trained operatives not currently tested to a standardised benchmark
3. **Security-specific first aid qualification** -- a new credential covering incidents more likely in CP deployments (serious bleeding, violence-related trauma)
4. **Advanced physical intervention** -- a new post-licence standard for higher-risk deployments; the consultation asks whether this should be mandatory or voluntary
5. **CP role boundary question** -- whether CP licence holders should retain the ability to perform door supervision and security guarding without additional licensing

The consultation deadline is 23 October 2026. The final specifications will be published in winter 2026 and new qualifications go live in 2027. ([Sources: gov.uk SIA consultation](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/sia-consults-on-changes-to-security-licence-qualifications), [Zems Academy](https://zems.org.uk/2026/07/29/sias-final-qualification-specs-dropping-this-summer-close-protection-content-is-in-the-crosshairs/), [Mango Solutions](https://mangosolutions.uk/navigating-the-new-sia-licensing-landscape-what-security-professionals-need-to-know-about-the-latest-training-changes/), [Proforce Security](https://proforcesecurity.co.uk/blogs/recent-sia-licence-updates-and-their-industry-impact/))

**Physical Intervention Skills refresher specification -- published August 2025, now in force:**

A Physical Intervention Skills for Close Protection Operatives Refresher Training specification was published by the SIA in August 2025 and is now in the training system. This predates the current consultation and is a separate mandatory standard. Refresher-qualified operatives will be better positioned when the 2027 qualification changes land, since the refresher spec aligns closely with the proposed advanced physical intervention requirements in the consultation. ([Source: assets.publishing.service.gov.uk](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68add0a9960e2d135b4c8e3c/sia-cp-physical-intervention-refresher-specification-2025.pdf))
