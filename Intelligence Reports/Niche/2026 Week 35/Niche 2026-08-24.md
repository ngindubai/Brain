## 1. US Pest Control

### Regulation & Policy

**EPA Pesticide General Permit 2026 now locked in**
EPA's 2026 PGP (Pesticide General Permit) is effective 31 October 2026 through October 2031, issued under NPDES. The permit covers point-source discharges of biological and chemical pesticides including mosquito/flying insect control, weed and algae control, animal pest control, and forest canopy pest control. Any PCO applying pesticides near waterways must operate under this permit from October. This is a five-year instrument, not subject to annual renegotiation, so operators should review compliance posture now rather than at the October deadline. Source: [EPA NPDES Pesticide Permitting](https://epa.gov/npdes/pesticide-permitting-2026-pesticide-general-permit).

**TSCA SNURs proposed — comment window closes this week**
EPA proposed significant new use rules (SNURs) for certain chemicals with comment deadlines on 24-31 August 2026. Pest control operators using or distributing products containing these chemical classes should verify whether their product portfolio falls within scope. Source: [Bergeson & Campbell August 2026 Federal Developments](https://www.lawbc.com/recent-federal-developments-for-august-2026/).

**MyPeST v3.0.1 released — digital submission now mandatory infrastructure**
EPA released version 3.0.1 of its MyPeST User Guide (dated 8 July 2026). The platform replaces the old Pesticide Submission Portal and is the required channel for all pesticide label submissions and compliance tracking. Bilingual labelling requirements are phased to full mandatory compliance by 2030, with all submissions running through MyPeST. PCOs sourcing own-brand or speciality-label products need their chemical suppliers operating on this platform. Source: [Bergeson & Campbell](https://www.lawbc.com/recent-federal-developments-for-august-2026/).

**FQPA 30th Anniversary — regulatory retrospective incoming**
3 August 2026 marked the 30th anniversary of the Food Quality Protection Act. A dedicated EPA webinar on 17 September will examine its legacy. Industry bodies are using this moment to push for PRIA reauthorisation. The broader regulatory review webinar on 22 September will cover EPA funding challenges, decision backlogs, and Farm Bill renewal — all of which affect registration timelines for new pest control products. Source: [Bergeson & Campbell](https://www.lawbc.com/recent-federal-developments-for-august-2026/).

**NJ Zika case — first since 2020**
New Jersey's Department of Health confirmed a Middlesex County resident tested positive for Zika, the first case in the state since 2020. Seasonal demand signal for mosquito control PCOs in the Northeast. Zika scares historically drive short-term residential and commercial treatment uptake in affected metro areas. Source: [PCT Online](https://www.pctonline.com/new-jersey-reports-first-case-of-zika-virus-since-2020).

### Pest Intelligence

**First confirmed brown recluse in Habersham County, GA — range signal**
PCO Toby Crowe of Piedmont Pest Management made the first confirmed brown recluse discovery in Habersham County, in the northeast Georgia mountains — a new county record confirmed by the University of Georgia. Brown recluse is historically restricted to northwest Georgia; Habersham sits distinctly further east. Whether this is a transported specimen (shipped goods, furniture) or genuine range expansion is not yet established, but it is significant: a confirmed find in a new county always generates inbound enquiries, and PCOs in that region should be primed for brown recluse identification requests. Source: [PCT Online](https://www.pctonline.com/pco-crowe-makes-first-confirmed-brown-recluse-spider-discovery-in-habersham-county-georgia).

**UCR: pesticides alter ant colony behaviour**
University of California Riverside research found that boric acid and thiamethoxam cause behavioural changes in ants as the chemicals move through a colony — not just lethality but disrupted social behaviour. This has implications for bait-based treatment protocols: the altered behaviour may affect feeding and trail patterns before mortality, which could explain some field reports of bait rejection after initial uptake. Source: [PCT Online](https://www.pctonline.com/ucr-research-finds-pesticides-make-ants-go-crazy).

## 2. Pet Transport (IPATA)

### IATA In-Cabin Pet Guidelines — July 2026 (this week's sector discussion)

The single biggest development in pet transport this week is the continuing industry absorption of IATA's first-ever operational guidelines for in-cabin pet travel, formally published 15 July 2026. The guidelines are voluntary for airlines, but IATA's recommendations historically become de facto mandatory within 12-18 months as airlines align policies to avoid reputational risk.

Key provisions:
- Airlines must provide clear, standardised information covering pet eligibility, carrier specifications, size and weight limits, fees, and cabin restrictions — before booking, not just at check-in.
- Guidelines address the full journey arc: booking, check-in, boarding, in-flight, arrival.
- IATA's 2025 Global Passenger Survey: 41% of pet-owning travellers cited uncertainty about eligibility as their primary pain point; 36% confused by airline policy differences. The guidelines are a direct response to this.
- Primary scope: cats and dogs. Species eligibility per individual airline.

For IPATA members, this is net positive — it reduces the 36% of passenger confusion that currently eats into professional booking volumes. When passengers can't understand airline policy, many abandon the trip or attempt DIY, and end up calling a professional only when things go wrong. Clearer airline-side communication front-ends professional inquiry earlier. Source: [IATA press release](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/07-15-iata-guidance-supports-clearer-more-consistent-in-cabin-pet-travel).

### IATA LAR 52nd Edition — Cargo Hold Rule Tightening

Effective 1 January 2026, the IATA Live Animals Regulations (LAR) 52nd Edition introduced several material changes:
- **Brachycephalic breeds banned from cargo holds** except confirmed service animals. Snub-nosed dogs and cats (pugs, bulldogs, Persian cats, etc.) cannot be transported as cargo. This is a hard stop, not a discretionary airline call.
- **Container sizing for brachycephalic breeds**: those airlines that do allow brachycephalic pets in-cabin must provide containers 10% larger on every dimension.
- Enhanced density and ventilation specs for day-old chicks; new seafood transport provisions.
- Persistent compliance gaps around CR1 container misuse for wild and exotic species are directly addressed.

Significance for IPATA members: clients with brachycephalic breeds can no longer be routed via cargo. This channels all such moves to cabin travel only, which is space-constrained and often unavailable on long-haul. IPATA members handling international relocations of brachycephalic breeds need updated consultation workflows that explicitly route-plan around this restriction, and must advise clients early. Demand for breed-specific routing expertise increases. Sources: [Air Cargo Week (via search)](https://aircargoweek.com/live-animal-transport-rules-tighten-as-iata-prepares-2026-updates/); [IATA LAR addendum](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/80b3908be0ca4ebe8d359292c46cdac2/20260101-lar-addendum-i-v1.0-final_ext.pdf).

### IPATA Airline Liaison — August 2026

IPATA appointed Sue Kazlaw-Nelson as Airline Liaison Consultant in August 2026, specifically to strengthen animal transport standards through direct airline relationships. Timing is not coincidental: with IATA publishing cabin guidelines in July and the LAR tightening cargo rules in January, IPATA is positioning itself as the authoritative intermediary between airlines and professional pet transport providers. Source: web search.

## 4. Close Protection (SIA)

### SIA Final Qualification Specifications — Dropping This Summer

The Security Industry Authority is completing a two-year strategic review of all licence-linked qualifications. Final qualification specifications for close protection are expected to be published this summer (i.e., imminently), with the following confirmed timeline:
- **Autumn–Winter 2026**: Awarding bodies and training providers update syllabi and secure regulatory approval
- **Spring 2027**: Mandatory go-live — all new close protection licence applicants must complete the new qualifications

The revised content will emphasise: enhanced physical intervention for high-threat scenarios; digital surveillance awareness and cyber protection; international travel security and cross-border protocols; counter-terrorism integration aligned with current national security frameworks; advanced first aid with trauma focus. Source: [Zems Academy](https://zems.org.uk/2026/07/29/sias-final-qualification-specs-dropping-this-summer-close-protection-content-is-in-the-crosshairs/).

This is the most significant qualification change in the CP sector in years. Training providers are in a window of competitive advantage right now — those who update first will capture the spring 2027 cohort. There is also a short-term demand spike: candidates who want to enter under current standards (which remain valid for three years) have a window before the mandatory switchover.

### Mandatory Refresher Training — Now Live

From 1 April 2026, mandatory refresher training is required for all close protection licence renewals. This was not previously required. Key requirements:
- Level 3 First Aid at Work (3-day, not Emergency FAW) as prerequisite before sitting the refresher
- Refresher qualification from SIA-approved providers
- Renewal application fee: £204
- Applications can be submitted from 4 months before licence expiry — meaning August 2026 renewals are now in scope

Source: [SIA Changes 2026 summary](https://siatraininghub.uk/sia-changes-2026/); [gov.uk press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/sia-announces-new-refresher-training-for-close-protection-operatives).

### Martyn's Law — Venue Preparation Phase

The Terrorism (Protection of Premises) Act 2025 (Martyn's Law) received Royal Assent April 2025. It will not become legally enforceable until Spring 2027 (24-month implementation period). The current phase is active preparation:
- **Standard tier** (100–799 person capacity): basic security training, written security procedures
- **Enhanced tier** (800+ capacity): comprehensive security measures, security plans, public protection procedures

Close protection operatives working venues or events are being engaged now for planning and training compliance. Demand from event organisers and venue operators for CP consultation is a near-term signal. Source: [Get Licensed](https://www.get-licensed.co.uk/get-daily/martyns-law-for-security-professionals/).

## 5. Bus Hire (Coach & Bus Week)

### Driver Shortage: Structural Crisis, Commercial Opportunity

The UK coach and bus sector faces a documented structural shortage: 3.4% bus driver deficit, 12.4% coach driver deficit — over 4,000 positions unfilled nationally. Coach operators are reporting full order books for inter-city and leisure travel, and bus passenger numbers are rising up to 15% year-on-year in some areas. Demand is outpacing supply. The constraint is not commercial — it is regulatory and demographic.

CPT (Confederation of Passenger Transport) is pressing the Government to remove two specific regulatory barriers:
1. **Age restrictions** that prevent young people from driving category D/DE vehicles
2. **Training pathway inefficiencies** that extend time-to-licence unnecessarily

The EU moved ahead in November 2025 with Directive 2025/2205, reducing the minimum age for Category D/DE licences from 24 to 21 (or 18 with a CPC). The UK has not moved. This divergence creates a competitive disadvantage for UK operators. Source: [CPT news](https://www.cpt-uk.org/news/cpt-calls-for-government-action-to-make-it-easier-to-hire-bus-and-coach-drivers/).

### Scottish Government Fuel Support — £6.2m Total

The Scottish Government has provided £6.2m in total support to bus operators since April 2026 to offset fuel cost increases driven by the ongoing Middle East conflict. This is Scotland-specific — England and Wales operators have received no equivalent support. The support allows Scottish operators to hold pricing, which matters for group hire competitiveness. Source: web search (Coach & Bus Week August 2026 reporting).

### DVSA Earned Recognition — Leap Coaches

Leeds-based Leap Coaches achieved DVSA Earned Recognition status in August 2026. Earned Recognition is a quality signal increasingly used by corporate and public sector buyers as a minimum qualification for approved supplier lists. Growing uptake of the scheme means operators without it may find themselves excluded from tenders. Source: web search.

### Seasonal Demand Context: August

August is peak demand for group hire — school holiday travel, corporate away-days, festival and event transport. The Welsh National Eisteddfod in Llantwd (1–8 August) required extra coach services across an eight-day run. Summer 2026 events season appears to be delivering strong utilisation, consistent with the CPT's "full order books" characterisation. The structural driver shortage is most acutely felt at peak season — operators are turning away work.
