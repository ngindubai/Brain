## 1. US Pest Control

### Rollins Q2 2026: Revenue up, margins down, stock punished
**Source:** PCT Online | https://www.pctonline.com/rollins-second-quarter-revenue-up-7-point-9-percent-yoy
**Related vault notes:** [[SEO Portfolio]], [[pest-control-usa]]

Rollins reported Q2 2026 revenues of $1.1 billion, up 7.9% year-on-year with organic growth of 5.7%. By service line: commercial pest control grew 8.6%, termite and ancillary 10.5%, residential 6.6%. The headline growth number looks strong, but the market reacted badly -- shares dropped 14.33% in after-hours trading to $37.24 after the results missed Wall Street revenue estimates. Non-GAAP profit of $0.32 per share came in 5.8% below consensus. Operating margin compressed 110 basis points to 18.7%.

Management pointed to softer demand in parts of residential pest control as the culprit. The commercial and termite segments are performing well, which is structurally important: commercial contracts are stickier and higher-margin, and termite treatment is a secular growth category tied to housing stock age. The residential softness is a macro signal worth watching -- it tracks closely with consumer discretionary spend and housing transaction volume, both of which are under pressure in 2026.

For [[pest-control-usa]], the Rollins miss does not indicate a broken market. It indicates a market where scale operators are battling margin compression from labour and materials costs while the top line grows. Independent and mid-size operators are the swing group -- and they are the leads our site targets.

## 2. Pet Transport

### IATA releases first-ever in-cabin pet transport guidelines
**Source:** IATA / CAPA | https://centreforaviation.com/news/iata-releases-operational-guidelines-for-in-cabin-pet-travel-1366216
**Related vault notes:** [[pet-transport]], [[SEO Portfolio]]

On 15 July 2026, IATA published its first Operational Guidelines for In-Cabin Pet Transport. This is significant because in-cabin pet travel has historically been governed entirely by individual airline policies, with no industry-wide framework. The guidelines are voluntary but will rapidly become the de facto standard that IPATA members reference and that airlines use to harmonise their policies.

The key provisions:
- Pets (primarily cats and dogs) must remain inside approved carriers throughout the flight
- Carriers must fit under the seat and provide sufficient space for the pet to stand, turn around, and lie down
- Airlines must provide clear, upfront information on eligible pets, carrier specifications, weight and size limits, fees, and cabin restrictions
- Check-in staff must be trained to verify pet eligibility, travel documentation, and carrier compliance before boarding
- Individual airlines retain discretion over which species they permit in the cabin

The guidelines were released as "Edition 1" -- indicating this is a living document IATA intends to revise. The timing (mid-July, northern hemisphere summer peak) is deliberate: airlines and operators will be pressured to align before the next major booking cycle.

For [[pet-transport]], this is a material development. The IATA guidelines create a new category of search queries -- people trying to understand what the new rules mean for their specific journey, breed, or airline. Content explaining the guidelines in plain English, broken down by airline and route, is a strong cluster opportunity.

### IATA LAR 2026 update: brachycephalic breed rules and container standards
**Source:** Air Cargo Week / getTransport blog | https://aircargoweek.com/live-animal-transport-rules-tighten-as-iata-prepares-2026-updates/
**Related vault notes:** [[pet-transport]]

Separately, the 2026 edition of the IATA Live Animals Regulations (LAR) -- effective 1 January 2026 -- contains material changes relevant to the pet transport sector. Specific rules for brachycephalic (flat-faced) breeds are enhanced. Container standards are tightened. The regulations also address persistent compliance gaps around CR1 container misuse for wild and exotic species -- a concern that has periodically embarrassed the industry.

For operators, this means the annual LAR update cycle now carries more weight. Airlines are tightening enforcement of breed restrictions and carrier specifications, and IPATA members who do not stay current with each edition face increased rejection rates at check-in.

## 4. Close Protection

### SIA terminates BIIAB recognition -- the biggest regulatory event in security training in years
**Source:** GOV.UK | https://www.gov.uk/government/news/private-security-regulator-removes-recognition-of-awarding-body
**Related vault notes:** [[closeprotectionhure-com]], [[SEO Portfolio]]

On 14 July 2026, the Security Industry Authority terminated its recognition agreement with BIIAB (British Institute of Innkeeping Awarding Body). With immediate effect, BIIAB is prohibited from registering new learners for SIA licence-linked training. From 10 August 2026, BIIAB cannot issue any new SIA licence-linked qualifications.

The grounds: "serious and persistent material breaches" of the recognition agreement, discovered through Operation RESOLUTE -- an intelligence-led inspection programme involving unannounced visits to training centres. The SIA found widespread malpractice. Ofqual separately issued a Direction under the Apprenticeships, Skills, Children and Learning Act 2009 preventing BIIAB from taking new learners, adding a second regulatory layer on top of the SIA action.

On 9 July (five days before the termination notice), BIIAB had already been removed from the SIA course finder for door supervisor and security guarding qualifications -- so the market had a short warning period.

The practical impact:
- Learners already registered with BIIAB before the cutoff can in principle continue, but BIIAB cannot issue any new qualifications after 10 August -- creating uncertainty about whether existing learners will be processed in time
- Existing SIA licence holders trained through BIIAB are unaffected
- Five other awarding organisations remain approved and are being coordinated with to absorb capacity from BIIAB's circa 650 training providers

For the close protection sector specifically, this affects CP licence candidates in mid-course. The CP licence requires a First Aid qualification and the Level 3 Award for Door Supervisors (or equivalent CP qualification). BIIAB was one of the approved pathways. Candidates who were mid-course through BIIAB will need to assess whether their training can be completed and certificated before 10 August, or whether they need to re-enrol elsewhere.

This is the first time the SIA has fully terminated an awarding body's recognition. It signals a more aggressive regulatory posture under the SIA Strategic Plan 2026-2029, which was also published this month.

## 5. Bus Hire

### PSVAIR compliance deadline: end of July 2026 for rail replacement operators
**Source:** Bus & Coach Buyer / CBW (via search) | https://www.busandcoachbuyer.com/coach-industry-resilient-but-under-pressure-says-operator-study/
**Related vault notes:** [[global-bus-hire]]

Rail replacement coach operators face a PSVAIR (Public Service Vehicle Accessible Information Regulations) compliance deadline at the end of July 2026. Operators running rail replacement services must ensure coaches are compliant with the accessible information requirements by this date or face enforcement action from the DVSA or Traffic Commissioners.

PSVAIR requires operators to provide audio and visual next-stop and destination information on coaches used for scheduled public services, including rail replacement. The deadline has been known for some time but enforcement attention is increasing. This is a narrow but real risk for operators who have not yet retrofitted vehicles or updated booking/information systems.

For [[global-bus-hire]], this is a differentiation signal: operators who are already compliant can use it as a selling point when tendering for rail replacement contracts.

### Driver shortages at near-crisis levels
**Source:** CPT UK | https://www.cpt-uk.org/news/cpt-calls-for-government-action-to-make-it-easier-to-hire-bus-and-coach-drivers/
**Related vault notes:** [[global-bus-hire]]

The UK now has a 3.4% shortage of bus drivers and a 12.4% shortage of coach drivers, equivalent to a combined shortfall of over 4,000 drivers nationwide. The CPT is calling on government to reduce the barriers to driver qualification and recruitment.

This is a structural constraint on the bus hire market's ability to meet demand. Private hire is cited as the biggest opportunity by 80.1% of operators surveyed -- but the ability to fulfil that demand depends on driver availability. The shortage is pushing up driver wages and lead times for bookings, which flows through to higher hire prices and longer advance-booking requirements for customers.

This is directly relevant content for [[global-bus-hire]]: articles explaining why coach hire needs to be booked further in advance than it used to be, and what to do if your quote comes back higher than expected, address real customer questions and signal trust.
