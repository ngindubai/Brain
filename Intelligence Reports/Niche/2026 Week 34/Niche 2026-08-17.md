## 1. US Pest Control

### Bed Bugs: 44% Surge Underlines Peak Season Demand

Rentokil data reported by Pest Magazine (and picked up by PCT Online, 10 August 2026) shows confirmed bed bug activity in the UK rose by an average of 44% year-on-year between 2023 and 2025, with the trend continuing into 2026. UK councils logged over 14,587 callouts in 2023 alone — a 23% rise on the prior year. London is the densest affected market at an estimated 4.6 bed bug incidents per 1,000 residents per month, though Leeds and Oxford are growing faster. The timing is squarely seasonal: school holiday hotel stays and international travel are the primary dispersal vectors. Rentokil's Paul Blackhurst called bed bugs "silent assassins" that build population before detection. The UK figure feeds through to US PMP trade press because Rentokil operates across both markets and the pest control trade press treats UK data as a leading indicator for transatlantic spread.

For US pest control operators, the read-across is clear: August is the peak demand window for bed bug remediation, and the consumer education angle — wash all clothing at 60°C on return from travel, inspect luggage seams — is a proven lead-generation content frame. The UK market is projected to reach US$361.9 million by 2033 (CAGR 5.8% from 2026), driven by post-COVID travel resumption, thermal remediation adoption, and tightening restrictions on synthetic pesticides.

## 2. Pet Transport (IPATA)

### IATA Releases First-Ever In-Cabin Pet Travel Guidelines

On 15 July 2026, IATA published its first voluntary operational guidelines for in-cabin pet transport. The framework covers the full journey: booking transparency, check-in verification, in-flight carrier placement, connecting flight documentation, and animal welfare assessment. Key requirements under the guidance: pet carriers must remain under the seat during taxi, take-off, landing, and turbulence; carriers must provide sufficient space to stand, turn, and lie; airlines are encouraged to require advance notification; check-in staff must be trained to assess animal distress. The guidelines are voluntary — individual airlines retain control over acceptance policies, species lists, weight limits, fees, and permitted routes. National import regulations supersede airline procedures.

For professional pet transport operators, this matters in two directions. First, it gives IPATA-accredited shippers a new compliance reference point to market against: as IATA pushes airlines to standardise, operators who already follow these protocols are ahead of the coming baseline. Second, the voluntary framing means inconsistency will persist across airlines for at least 12–18 months until uptake consolidates — operators managing complex routing will continue to earn their fee from navigating the patchwork.

**Sources:** [Aerospace Global News](https://aerospaceglobalnews.com/news/iata-airline-guidance-pets-in-cabin-2026/) | [Travel Trade Journal](https://traveltradejournal.com/iata-issues-new-operational-guidelines-for-in-cabin-pet-travel/) | [CAPA](https://centreforaviation.com/news/iata-releases-operational-guidelines-for-in-cabin-pet-travel-1366216)

### IATA LAR 52nd Edition: Brachycephalic Restrictions Now Formal

The 52nd edition of the IATA Live Animals Regulations (LAR), effective 1 January 2026, formalises restrictions on brachycephalic (snub-nosed) breeds in cargo holds. Affected breeds — bulldogs, pugs, Boston terriers, Persian and Himalayan cats — may only travel in cargo as confirmed service animals. The edition also introduces enhanced container strength standards, updated density and ventilation specs for day-old chicks, new seafood transport regulations, and clarified species-specific container markings. The brachycephalic cargo ban is the single biggest practical change for pet transport professionals: owners of affected breeds seeking international relocation now have effectively no cargo-hold option and must either route through cabin travel (where the new July 2026 guidelines apply) or explore ground-based alternatives. This creates a distinct consultation and routing service opportunity.

**Sources:** [Air Cargo Week](https://aircargoweek.com/live-animal-transport-rules-tighten-as-iata-prepares-2026-updates/) | [IATA LAR](https://www.iata.org/en/publications/manuals/live-animals-regulations/)

### IPATA Appoints Airline Liaison Consultant

IPATA announced on 1 August 2026 the appointment of Sue Kazlaw-Nelson as Airline Liaison Consultant, a new role created to strengthen standards coordination between IPATA members and airline partners. The appointment signals that IPATA is positioning itself more proactively in airline policy conversations — directly relevant given the IATA in-cabin guidelines launched three weeks earlier. This is the kind of structural move that precedes formal IPATA-IATA alignment on standards, which would eventually create a more predictable operating environment for professional operators.

**Source:** [IPATA](https://www.ipata.org/)

## 3. Funeral Repatriation (NAFD)

### Government Formally Moves on Funeral Sector Regulation

The most significant funeral industry development this year: on 31 July 2026, the UK government issued a press release formally committing to regulate the funeral sector following the sentencing of Hull funeral director Robert Bush on 67 charges including fraud, theft, and preventing lawful burial. The announcement triggers a cross-government workstream led by the Department of Health and Social Care, with the Ministry of Justice and the Law Commission jointly reviewing criminal law protections for deceased persons and identifying legal gaps. Health Secretary Yvette Cooper stated the government would "bring forward comprehensive proposals to ensure respect and dignity for the deceased in every setting." No deadline has been set, but the Law Commission review is now formally commissioned.

Both the NAFD and the National Society of Allied and Independent Funeral Directors expressed support for statutory regulation provided the framework protects deceased persons and families while maintaining market diversity and recognising smaller operators. The key nuance for repatriation specialists: statutory licensing applied to domestic funeral directors will almost certainly extend to repatriation services, which currently sit in a lighter-touch regulatory space. Firms operating in cross-border repatriation that do not hold domestic UK funeral director licences may face a future compliance cliff.

**Sources:** [UKPOL Government Press Release](https://www.ukpol.co.uk/press-release-government-takes-action-to-crack-down-on-rogue-funeral-operators-july-2026/) | [Funeral Directory / NAFD](https://www.funeral-directory.co.uk/guides/repatriation-bringing-a-body-home/)

Parliamentary context running in the background: Hansard records a Commons debate on funeral director regulation on 9 March 2026. ## 4. Close Protection (SIA)

### Close Protection Refresher Training: Now Mandatory for All Renewals

As of April 2026, the SIA enforces mandatory refresher training for all Close Protection licence renewals — no exceptions for prior qualifications. The requirement has two components: a Level 2 Close Protection Refresher (updated intervention techniques, threat awareness, vulnerable person protocols) and a Level 3 First Aid at Work (FAW) qualification that must remain valid for at least 12 months from the booking date. Applications submitted without both qualifications are rejected outright. The previous renewal pathway — where experienced operatives could renew on track record alone — has been eliminated. Prior qualifications from 2021–2022 carry no exemption.

The practical implication: any operative whose licence expires in the next 12 months needs to book training now. SIA licences run three years, so a significant cohort of operatives licensed during the 2023 growth period will hit renewal in 2026. Operators running close protection details should be auditing their team's licence expiry schedules now.

**Source:** [Zems Academy](https://zems.org.uk/2026/08/05/close-protection-refresher-now-mandatory-your-sia-licence-wont-renew-without-it/) | [SIA CP Physical Intervention Refresher Specification](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68add0a9960e2d135b4c8e3c/sia-cp-physical-intervention-refresher-specification-2025.pdf)

### Final Qualification Specifications: Expected This Summer

The SIA is finalising a broader overhaul of Close Protection qualification content through a two-year strategic review. Final specifications are expected to be published this summer (August–September 2026), with training providers then required to update curricula and seek regulatory approval through Autumn–Winter 2026. Mandatory implementation for new licence applicants begins Spring 2027. The updated content emphasises: enhanced physical intervention for high-threat environments, digital surveillance and cyber awareness, international travel security and convoy operations, counter-terrorism integration, and advanced trauma response. Current licence holders will face renewal and top-up requirements aligned with the new standards when their licences come due.

This creates a clear market dynamic for providers: operatives who certify under current standards before Spring 2027 lock in a competitive advantage. The window is approximately 8 months.

## 5. Bus Hire (Coach & Bus Week)

### CT4N Collapses: Sudden Market Vacancy in East Midlands

CT4N, a Nottinghamshire community transport operator running 20 routes and employing 76 staff, ceased trading abruptly on Friday 14 August 2026. Despite creditors approving a Company Voluntary Arrangement in October 2025, rising fuel, insurance, maintenance, and employment costs overwhelmed the business. Most of its 76 staff have lost their jobs with immediate effect. The East Midlands Combined County Authority Mayor has intervened to protect services; Nottingham City Transport is stepping in to replace the key Route 18 (Nottingham–Beeston Rylands–Stapleford) from Monday 17 August, with a full timetable commencing 1 September. Other routes remain unserviced pending commissioning decisions.

The closure illustrates the cost-structure pressure running through the independent and community bus sector: the CVA that bought CT4N time in 2025 proved insufficient against the operating cost inflation that has persisted through 2026. For the global-bus-hire [[SEO Portfolio]] site, this is a peripheral but relevant signal — procurement officers sourcing group transport will route around failed operators, and independent coach hire operators who can demonstrate financial stability are beneficiaries of the confidence gap.

**Sources:** [CBW](https://cbwmagazine.com/ct4n-ceases-trading-with-immediate-effect/) | [West Bridgford Wire](https://westbridgfordwire.com/nottingham-bus-operator-ct4n-ceases-trading-as-most-of-76-staff-lose-jobs/) | [routeone](https://www.route-one.net/news/end-for-ct4n-as-nottingham-operator-ceases-trading-abruptly/)

### Electric Fleet Expansion: Stagecoach East Midlands

Stagecoach East Midlands launched 42 new electric buses on services in Hull this week, one of the larger single-market EV fleet rollouts of 2026. No direct impact on coach hire demand in the near term, but a confirmed mass transit commitment in West Yorkshire would progressively shift urban corridor demand toward rail, with coach hire retaining the first/last mile, event, and rural routes.
