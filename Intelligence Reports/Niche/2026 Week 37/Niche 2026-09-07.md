## 1. US Pest Control

### Pest Index growth softens: 8% YOY in July

The William Blair/PCO Bookkeepers & M&A Specialists total US Pest Index increased 8% year-over-year in July 2026, below the long-term average index growth rate of 10%. This continues a pattern of below-average growth that has made 2026 a more challenging commercial environment than the industry saw in 2024 and 2025. The slowdown is broadly attributed to normalisation after several years of pandemic-era growth and market saturation in the residential segment. ([Source: PCT Online](https://www.pctonline.com/pest-index-up-eight-percent-in-july-2026))

For programmatic lead-gen operators, softer industry growth means the acquisition premium on organic search traffic is lower -- budgets tighten at the operator level before they tighten at the consumer level. ### NPMA Bug Barometer: extended active season ahead

On 1 September, the NPMA released its Fall and Winter 2026 Bug Barometer, forecasting a warmer-than-average autumn across much of the continental US. The practical implication: ticks, ants, and mosquitoes will stay active later into the year before seasonal conditions eventually push cockroaches and rodents indoors to seek warmth. The regional detail that matters for SEO:

- **Mid-Atlantic and New England**: warm, dry conditions extending summer-like tick and ant activity into fall; cooler-season pests (rodents, Asian lady beetles, boxelder bugs) may arrive later than usual
- **Southeast**: warm, wet conditions extending mosquito and cockroach pressure in the Carolinas, Georgia, Alabama, and Florida

The barometer is the industry's clearest seasonal demand signal. It confirms that the autumn rodent push -- typically the highest-search-volume period in the residential pest control calendar -- will arrive, but may arrive later than the historical norm. The content window for autumn pest prevention is open now and runs through October. ([Sources: PCT Online](https://www.pctonline.com/news/npma-releases-fall-and-winter-bug-barometer/), [NPMA Pest World](https://www.pestworld.org/multimedia-center/press-releases/summer-s-not-clocking-out-yet-warmer-fall-forecast-could-extend-pest-activity-across-the-us/))

### New World Screwworm: 47 confirmed cases, federal response activated

A New World Screwworm (NWS) outbreak has reached 47 confirmed cases -- 46 in Texas and one in New Mexico -- as of mid-August, with four active cases in three Texas counties at last count. The NWS is a flesh-eating fly larva that can cause severe and fatal harm to livestock, wildlife, and pets, and represents the most significant exotic pest incursion into the US in decades.

The federal response involves CBP, USDA, and multiple agency partners. CBP has facilitated 96 shipments of sterile NWS pupae through four Texas airports (Del Rio, McAllen, George Bush Intercontinental, Pharr/Hidalgo), deploying the sterile insect technique: sterile male flies mate with wild females, producing no offspring and reducing the population. This is the same method used to eradicate NWS from North America in the 1960s and from Mexico in the 1990s.

For the pest control industry, this is primarily a livestock and wildlife issue rather than a residential pest control issue, but it has industry relevance: PCOs advising agricultural and rural clients should be aware, and any lead-gen content in Texas and New Mexico for outdoor pest management has an elevated search environment. ([Sources: CBP](https://www.cbp.gov/newsroom/national-media-release/cbp-supports-federal-response-new-world-screwworm-threat), [USDA APHIS](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/news/agency-announcements/usda-confirms-presence-new-world-screwworm-united-states))

## 2. Pet Transport (IPATA)

### IPATA: no new announcements this week

### IATA in-cabin pet guidelines: industry adoption phase

IATA published its first-ever Operational Guidelines for In-Cabin Pet Transport on 15 July 2026. This is not new news, but the adoption curve is now what matters -- airlines are now implementing or choosing not to implement the voluntary framework. Key points for operators:

- Airlines should require **advance notification** (48-72 hours minimum) for passengers travelling with cabin pets
- **Staff training** must include visual assessment of whether an animal appears distressed or unfit for travel before boarding
- Pets must remain in **approved carriers** that fit under the seat and allow the animal to stand, turn around, and lie down
- Framework covers booking, pre-arrival, check-in, boarding, in-flight, and arrival -- the full journey
- Applies primarily to cats and dogs; each airline determines which species it accepts in cabin

The voluntary nature is the key constraint. Airlines are not obligated to adopt these guidelines, which means IPATA operators will continue to encounter inconsistency between carriers. The practical implication for the [[SEO Portfolio]] pet transport site: content explaining airline-by-airline variation in in-cabin rules remains high-value, because the guidelines create more confusion, not less, when airlines adopt different elements of them at different speeds. ([Sources: IATA press release](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/07-15-iata-guidance-supports-clearer-more-consistent-in-cabin-pet-travel), [Aerospace Global News](https://aerospaceglobalnews.com/news/iata-airline-guidance-pets-in-cabin-2026/))

### IATA LAR 52nd edition: mid-year addenda watch

The IATA Live Animals Regulations 52nd edition (in force since 1 January 2026) is in mid-year addenda territory. IATA's newly formed Live Animals Working Group is reviewing species lists, terminology, and compliance standards for future editions. No addenda have been published this week, but the working group activity signals that the 2027 edition will carry further changes. Pet transport operators handling specialist species beyond cats and dogs should monitor this closely. ([Source: IATA](https://www.iata.org/en/programs/cargo/live-animals/))

## 4. Close Protection (SIA)

### Consultation week one: industry guidance wave

The SIA qualification consultation (opened 28 August, closes 23 October 2026) is now in its second week. No formal SIA announcements have dropped since the launch -- the authority is in listening mode until October. What has changed this week is the volume of industry-facing guidance being published:

- **Zems Academy** (5 September): "SIA Proposed Training Changes 2027: What Security Operatives Need to Know Now" -- a detailed breakdown advising operatives to verify current first aid certificates, continue existing training without delay, and submit a consultation response before the October deadline. ([Source: Zems Academy](https://zems.org.uk/2026/09/05/sia-proposed-training-changes-2027-what-security-operatives-need-to-know-now/))

([Source: ASIS Online](https://www.asisonline.org/security-management-magazine/latest-news/today-in-security/2026/september/SIA-Proposes-Major-Changes/))
- Multiple training providers (Proforce Security, Mango Solutions, SIA Training Hub) have published their own explainers.

This guidance wave is the search traffic moment. The people reading Zems Academy and ASIS Online are decision-makers in CP operations who are trying to understand what the changes mean for their businesses. The close protection site on the [[SEO Portfolio]] needs to be visible in this search space now.

### Consultation proposals: current state

The five headline proposals remain as outlined in the 28 August launch (covered last week). For completeness this week:

1. **First aid as an ongoing licence condition** -- maintaining a valid first aid certificate as a permanent requirement, not just at initial licensing
2. **Independent English language test** -- a new standardised assessment beyond the current embedded evaluation; likely to create barriers for overseas-trained operatives
3. **Security-specific first aid qualification** -- a new credential for incidents close protection operatives are more likely to encounter (serious bleeding, violence-related injuries)
4. **Advanced physical intervention** -- proposed as a new post-licence standard for higher-risk deployments; the consultation asks whether this should be voluntary or compulsory
5. **Role boundary question** -- whether CP licence holders should retain the right to perform door supervision and security guarding roles without additional licences

The consultation asks for views directly; 5,000+ stakeholders were engaged in the design phase. CP licence holders should respond before 23 October if they have views on any of the above.

## 5. Bus Hire (Coach & Bus)

### West Yorkshire franchising: Weaver Network contracts signed

West Yorkshire's first franchised bus contracts -- the Weaver Network -- have been awarded and signed this week. First West Yorkshire will operate services in Huddersfield and parts of Leeds; Transdev Blazefield takes the Dewsbury services. The network launches in Spring 2027, with the full franchising rollout across Leeds, Wakefield, Bradford, and Calderdale completing by Autumn 2028.

This is the most significant structural change to bus operations in West Yorkshire since deregulation in 1986. For coach hire operators, franchising represents both a threat and an opportunity: route operators face contracted margins and reduced flexibility, while private hire operators who sit outside the franchise structure benefit from the uncertainty around who covers what, particularly for contract, school, and corporate work. ([Source: Bus & Coach Buyer](https://www.busandcoachbuyer.com/west-yorkshire-franchising-contracts-signed/))

### Liverpool: 560 buses, £150m investment as franchising nears

The Liverpool City Region is receiving over 560 new buses in advance of its own franchising transition. The investment exceeds £150 million. This follows the Greater Manchester franchising model and continues the regional devolution trend -- mayors are taking back public transport control across the North of England. Operators in these regions face a fundamental shift in how they compete for business. ([Source: Bus & Coach Buyer](https://www.busandcoachbuyer.com/first-of-560-buses-enter-liverpool-region-as-franchising-nears/))

### £2 fare cap expands to west Scotland from 7 September

As of today (7 September 2026), the Scottish Government's £2 single fare cap extends to west Scotland -- Ayrshire and Strathclyde. Participating operators include McGill's Buses and Stagecoach South Scotland. The First Minister confirmed the extension. Scotland's cap mirrors England's but is devolved and independently funded. The Confederation of Passenger Transport Scotland welcomed the move but flagged that fare caps must be paired with improvements to bus speed and reliability to be sustainable long term. ([Source: Bus & Coach Buyer](https://www.busandcoachbuyer.com/2-fare-cap-expanded-into-west-of-scotland/))

### RHA Coaching Ahead manifesto: coaches are not buses

The Road Haulage Association launched its "Coaching Ahead" manifesto in August 2026 with five priorities: skills, planning, decarbonisation, accessibility, and international operations. The central argument is that coaches are being held back by rules designed for regulated bus services. Specific demands include:

- Recognition of coaches as a distinct transport mode, separate from bus regulation
- Removal of age restrictions blocking 18-19 year olds from PSV licences -- addressing the 5,000-10,000 driver shortage (14% vacancy rate, 25% of drivers over 60)
- A zero-emission coach roadmap to 2050 with manufacturer and operator support
- Reform of Persons with Reduced Mobility Vehicle Accessibility Requirements (PSVAR) to focus on whole journeys rather than individual vehicle specifications
- A Professional Drivers' Exemption from the 90/180-day rule for international operations

The driver shortage point is the most operationally acute: nearly a quarter of the existing driver workforce will retire within a decade, and the pipeline from education into PSV driving is blocked by age rules. For the bus hire broker/comparison model, structural driver shortages are a sustained demand signal -- customers who cannot source reliable capacity from local operators will search for alternatives. ([Sources: RHA](https://www.rha.uk.net/News/News/Detail/RHA-launches-Coaching-Ahead-manifesto-to-support-the-future-of-the-coach-industry), [Bus & Coach Buyer](https://www.busandcoachbuyer.com/stop-treating-coaches-like-buses-says-rha-report/))

### Technology: Queclink 4G LTE router platform, TransMach rebrand

Queclink Wireless Solutions is launching a 4G LTE router platform designed to give bus and coach operators real-time data insights and connected fleet services. TransMach, the smart ticketing provider, has rebranded as it accelerates partnerships with major operators. Both items signal the technology layer in bus and coach operations is evolving -- operators who invest in connected fleets now will have a compliance and efficiency edge when franchising contracts are tendered. ([Source: Bus & Coach Buyer](https://www.busandcoachbuyer.com/new-look-for-transmach-as-growth-accelerates/))
