## 1. US Pest Control

### USDA ARS deploys AI predictive models as New World screwworm reaches Texas

The USDA's Agricultural Research Service published this week the details of its predictive modelling programme for the New World screwworm (Cochliomyia hominivorax) -- a development that marks a significant upgrade in the federal response to what is now a confirmed domestic outbreak. On 3 June 2026, USDA's Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service confirmed the detection of a NWS larvae in a bovine in Zavala County, Texas. That was the first confirmed US presence since the species was declared eradicated from the continental US in 1966.

The ARS models integrate historic habitat data, host preferences, environmental conditions, and livestock movement patterns to forecast where screwworm may establish or spread next. According to ARS, the models correctly predicted a 2025 US entry based on the Central American outbreak trajectory, but prevention efforts -- primarily the Sterile Insect Technique (SIT) barrier maintained in Panama -- bought time. SIT involves releasing sterile flies to interrupt breeding; the ARS is deploying this at scale while the predictive layer directs surveillance and trapping resources.

The pest control industry angle: NWS does not target humans directly, but it is a livestock and wildlife parasite -- and any vector or invasive species outbreak elevates public and regulatory attention to pest management as a professional category. The professional consequence is indirect but real. More immediately, the outbreak is already generating search volume: queries around livestock pest management, screwworm symptoms, and treatment protocols are rising in Texas and the south-west. A pest control operator with agricultural or rural accounts in those states should have content positioning on screwworm awareness. The USDA's public-facing comms are functional but not conversion-optimised -- there is a content gap. ([Sources: USDA ARS](https://www.ars.usda.gov/news-events/news/research-news/2026/tracking-the-path-of-new-world-screwworm), [USDA APHIS](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/news/agency-announcements/usda-confirms-presence-new-world-screwworm-united-states), [Meatingplace](https://meatingplace.com/usdas-ag-research-service-to-use-ai-to-predict-screwworm-movements/))

### Rutgers "mosquito toilet" detects West Nile and unknown hemisphere virus from excrement

A research team at Rutgers University's Center for Vector Biology published a study in Microbiology Spectrum (September 18, 2026) demonstrating that mosquito excrement can be sequenced to detect circulating pathogens before they cause widespread illness. The device -- internally called a "mosquito toilet" -- is a 3D-printed superhydrophobic funnel that collects liquid from mosquito excrement into a tube for genetic analysis. Shotgun metagenomic sequencing reads nearly all genetic material present, then computer analysis stitches millions of fragments against pathogen reference databases.

The results: a complete West Nile virus genome was recovered, and -- more significantly -- the first evidence of a Hedwig-like virus was found in the Americas. Hedwig-like viruses are a group not previously documented in this hemisphere. The system was able to detect pathogens that would not have been screened for under conventional single-target diagnostics.

The practical implications for pest management are substantial at a systems level. If a field-ready version reaches commercial deployment -- which lead researcher Dana Price says is the next development step -- it would give local authorities and pest control operators a surveillance tool that identifies threats before human infection clusters emerge. For an operator running mosquito control programmes, this is a scientific credibility lever: positioning services against a backdrop of "we detect what others miss" is a fundamentally different value proposition than "we spray for mosquitoes." The US market leaders (Rollins, Rentokil) have the R&D budgets to integrate emerging detection tools; mid-tier operators need to watch whether this transitions to a product or a service offering in the next 12-18 months. ([Sources: news-medical.net](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260918/Mosquito-excrement-offers-new-way-to-detect-circulating-viruses.aspx), [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-mosquito-toilet-west-nile-hedwig.html), [Study Finds](https://studyfinds.com/mosquito-toilet-reveals-virus-never-reported-in-americas/))

### IATA LAR Verify: digital compliance platform now live

IATA has launched LAR Verify, a digital platform that provides real-time access to the full IATA Live Animals Regulations and automates compliance checks for live animal shipments. The platform validates documentation requirements, checks container compliance, and is designed to integrate directly into cargo management systems. This is a material operational shift: previously, compliance was a manual, paper-based process relying on operator knowledge and carrier interpretation. LAR Verify standardises the process and reduces the carrier-level discretion that has created the brachycephalic policy fragmentation documented in recent weeks.

The implication for IPATA operators: LAR Verify raises the compliance floor. Operators who are not routinely using the platform risk falling behind carriers and shippers who are. For brachycephalic breed transport specifically, the automated documentation validation is directly relevant -- carriers accepting these breeds conditionally (Emirates, certain US domestic carriers) are likely to adopt LAR Verify as the reference system for clearance decisions. Operators should be documenting IPATA Fit-to-Fly assessments in a format compatible with LAR Verify's validation requirements. ([Sources: IATA](https://www.iata.org/en/programs/cargo/live-animals/), [IATA Knowledge Hub](https://www.iata.org/en/publications/newsletters/iata-knowledge-hub/making-in-cabin-pet-travel-easier-iata-new-initiatives/))
