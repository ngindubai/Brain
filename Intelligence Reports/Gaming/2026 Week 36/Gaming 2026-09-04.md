## Story 1: Xbox goes on record backing Take-Two in GTA VI leak subpoena case, judge grants both requests

**Source:** Dexerto, Windows Central, GameSpot | https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/xbox-responds-after-judge-grants-gta-6-subpoena-request-3401078/
**Related vault notes:** [[Products]]

The 4 September compliance deadline named in yesterday's report has now passed, and it produced the day's one clean development on this thread. A New York district court judge has granted Take-Two's subpoena requests against both Microsoft and Discord, compelling them to hand over account IDs, registration and login IP addresses, phone numbers, device identifiers and (for Microsoft) any OneDrive content referencing GTA, Rockstar or the "Cyberleek" persona behind the map and gameplay leaks. Discord's exposure covers identifying data for users across the named servers going back to 1 June 2026.

The news today is Microsoft's public posture rather than the legal mechanics, which were already known. Xbox CTO Scott Van Vliet put out a statement — "We are working closely with Take-Two and Rockstar Games to support efforts to protect creative works and intellectual property" — which is Microsoft choosing to be seen cooperating rather than quietly complying. That is a meaningfully different signal than a company that fights a subpoena or stays silent; it tells other platform holders (and future leakers) that the industry's biggest publishers are lining up behind IP enforcement ahead of GTA VI's 19 November launch, not treating it as a Rockstar-only problem.

Discord has said it will comply with valid subpoenas as standard practice, which was expected. The interesting unresolved piece is what happens once identities are unmasked — whether Take-Two pursues civil claims, criminal referral, or simply uses the deterrent effect. Nothing on that yet; this is a docket event, not a resolution.

## Story 2: Dungeon Settlers launches Early Access today, six days ahead of its original date

**Source:** Steam News, TurnBasedLovers | https://store.steampowered.com/app/2798330/Dungeon_Settlers/
**Related vault notes:** [[Products]]

Dungeon Settlers went live in Early Access this morning (8:00 AM UTC), having been moved up from its originally announced 10 September date. It's a colony-management/dungeon-crawler hybrid: above ground you run a settlement — housing, food, crafting, research — and below ground you send expeditions deeper into procedurally structured dungeon regions, with the settlement's survival depending on what those expeditions bring back. The developers are pitching roughly two years in Early Access across six planned regions at full release, which is a long runway for a genre (colony sim plus roguelite dungeon delving) that has plenty of established competition — Going Medieval, Going Under, Dwarf Fortress-adjacent titles.

The early-move-up is worth noting as a pattern rather than a one-off: publishers increasingly pull Early Access dates forward by days rather than delaying, presumably to land ahead of a competitor or to catch a wishlist peak before it decays. Nothing here demands action, just a genre-watch data point — base-building/survival hybrids remain a crowded, high-cadence Early Access category on Steam.

## Story 3: Scavland hits 175,000 wishlists on Early Access launch day, six years in the making

**Source:** Insider Gaming, GodIsAGeek, VGTimes | https://insider-gaming.com/survival-rpg-scavland-early-access-september/
**Related vault notes:** [[Products]]

Scavland, a top-down pixel-art survival RPG set in a post-apocalyptic Soviet world, entered Early Access today from solo/small studio NoShadow after a six-year development cycle. The headline number is 175,000 Steam wishlists going into launch — a strong result for an unknown studio with no publisher push behind it, built on explicit STALKER/Tarkov/Darkwood/Ultima Online influences. The developer's own framing ("this milestone belongs to all of us") and the community numbers (8,000+ Steam Community followers, 900 added in the past week alone) point to a slow-build word-of-mouth campaign rather than a marketing-driven launch.

This lands in the middle of Steam's PvE Survival Crafting Fest (running through 7 September), which gives it a visibility tailwind it didn't have to buy. For the survival-crafting genre generally, it's another data point that hardcore, atmosphere-heavy, single-developer or small-team survival games can still break through purely on Steam wishlist mechanics and community trust — no FiveM/GTA-adjacent relevance, but a useful genre signal for anyone tracking what does and doesn't cut through on Steam without paid marketing.
