## Story 1: Cyberpunk 2077 comes to Battle.net, Geralt joins Diablo 4 as a barbarian skin

**Source:** PC Gamer | https://www.pcgamer.com/rss/

CD Projekt and Blizzard announced today that Cyberpunk 2077 will launch on Battle.net later this year, a notable distribution move for a game that has spent its life on Steam, GOG and console storefronts. In the same announcement, The Witcher's Geralt of Rivia will appear in Diablo 4 as a barbarian skin, available to players who preorder The Witcher 3's upcoming DLC. It's a straightforward cross-promotion play for both franchises rather than a genre-relevant story, but it's the clearest sign yet that Blizzard is using Battle.net as a platform expansion vehicle beyond its own IP, worth a line for anyone tracking platform strategy in the space generally.

## Story 2: WoW Forever's design detailed: unified rulesets, new race/class combos, launches 4 November

**Source:** PC Gamer | https://www.pcgamer.com/rss/

Blizzard filled in the design specifics on WoW Forever (announced at BlizzCon as the long-rumoured "Classic Plus" branch) today: unified rulesets replacing the old separate-realm structure, optional transmog settings, new race and class combinations not available in original Classic, and a "Legacy" progression system built around untold vanilla-era stories. The 4 November launch date and beta-this-week timeline both stand from yesterday's report. The unified-ruleset change is the significant new detail, it signals Blizzard is treating WoW Forever as a proper live product with its own design language rather than a straight Classic re-run, a bigger commitment than the initial announcement suggested.

## Story 4: Total War: Warhammer 40,000 shows six minutes of destructible-environment gameplay

**Source:** Windows Central (via search) | https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/warhammer-40-000s-total-war-debut-looks-chaotic-enough-to-win-over-newcomers-like-me

Creative Assembly circulated a fresh six-minute gameplay video today, following its original Bilibili World 2026 showcase, highlighting large-scale, dynamic environment destruction that the studio is calling "the biggest mental shift" for the Total War series in years. The game remains on track for a 2027 release, no date has moved. This is the first extended look specifically at how battlefield destruction will work in a 40k setting rather than a Warhammer Fantasy one, worth watching as the first genuinely new mechanical pitch Creative Assembly has made for the sub-series beyond the faction swap.

## Story 5: Total War: Warhammer III drops the base-game requirement for Immortal Empires from 24 September

**Source:** PC Gamer | https://www.pcgamer.com/games/strategy/come-september-you-wont-need-a-single-total-war-warhammer-game-to-play-its-colossal-trilogy-spanning-immortal-empires-campaign/

When the Lords of the End Times DLC launches on 24 September (bringing Nagash, the Glottkin, Boris Todbringer and Grey Seer Thanquol into the Legendary Lord roster, previously flagged as the biggest DLC in series history), Creative Assembly will also remove the requirement to own any individual Total War: Warhammer game to play the trilogy-spanning Immortal Empires campaign. That's a genuine structural change, not just a content drop: it turns Immortal Empires into a standalone entry point for new players rather than a reward for owning three separate base games, a meaningful move for a franchise 25 years into its life and worth watching as a potential template for how other long-running strategy series consolidate a fragmented back catalogue.
