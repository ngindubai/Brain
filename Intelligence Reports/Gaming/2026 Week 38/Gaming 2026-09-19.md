## Story 1: Tokyo Game Show 2026 opens its public days — first-ever three-day public weekend, Koei Tecmo's Nobunaga's Ambition among the playable strategy titles

**Source:** Tokyo Cheapo / Nikkei xTREND (via search) | https://tokyocheapo.com/events/tokyo-game-show/ | https://xtrend.nikkei.com/sp/gaming/en/feature/atcl/2026/7/index.html | RPG Site | https://www.rpgsite.net/news/21196-koei-tecmos-tokyo-game-show-2026-title-lineup-revealed

TGS 2026's public days began today and, for the first time, run three days (19-21 September, the third a new addition specifically to spread out crowds and free up more trial-play slots) rather than the usual two. The show is marking its 30th anniversary with a record five-day run overall (17-21 September), 3,500 exhibit booths and an expected 300,000 visitors. On the genre front this brief tracks, Koei Tecmo's booth has a new, still Japanese-subtitled Nobunaga's Ambition (Hishou) strategy title playable on the floor alongside four other titles, and WAWE LLC's The Prime Minister's Crest: Path to Power — a pixel-art political-simulation text adventure built around fiscal points, party politics and personal loyalties rather than combat — is also playable at booth N8-03. Neither is a major RTS/grand-strategy release on the scale of Total War, but both are genuine playable-demo confirmations dated to today's public opening rather than trade-day announcements. The show's biggest headline overall was Xbox's reveal that Bill Skarsgård will play the lead in Kojima's PHYSINT, outside this brief's genre lens but worth flagging given the show's scale.

## Story 2: Japanese developers are embracing generative AI at more than double the rate of North American studios

**Source:** PC Gamer | https://www.pcgamer.com/ (Japanese game dev AI adoption survey, 19 September)

A new survey cited today shows 85.8% of Japanese game developers now use generative AI in development, up from 51% a year ago, against just 36% adoption among North American studios. The gap is the interesting part: this isn't a story about AI capability, it's about a structural difference in how two major development cultures are choosing to integrate the same tools a year apart. For a portfolio built on the thesis that AI-native workflows compound advantage over time, a near-2.5x adoption gap between the studios building this year's TGS lineup and the ones building North America's slate is a genuine signal worth revisiting alongside future TGS output — if Japanese output starts visibly outpacing on iteration speed or content volume over the next year, this is the leading indicator that called it.
