## Story 1: Kingmakers resurfaces with a 12-minute gameplay deep dive and opens playtest signups

**Source:** PC Gamer | https://www.pcgamer.com/games/action/after-nearly-a-year-of-silence-following-its-last-minute-delay-kingmakers-resurfaces-with-an-extended-gameplay-video-and-new-playtest/

Publisher tinyBuild and developer Redemption Road broke nearly a year of silence yesterday with an extended gameplay deep dive for Kingmakers, the medieval third-person-shooter/RTS hybrid that delayed out of its original launch window. The video covers missions, weapons and vehicles, progression, resource collection, and — the genre-relevant part — how switching between strategic army command and third-person combat will actually work: players lead armies at a strategic scale, build and manage settlements, and gather resources to fund better equipment, layered under the "hopelessly outclassed enemies" combat hook that's always been the game's central pitch. No release date was given; it's still listed "coming soon" on Steam. A Steam playtest is opening for signups now. Worth tracking as one of the more mechanically distinct base-building/strategy crossovers in the current pipeline, distinct from the wave of straight 4X and RTS revivals.

## Story 2: Wardogs' three-faction base-building shooter draws early balance criticism

**Source:** PC Gamer | https://www.pcgamer.com/rss/

The newly launched tactical shooter Wardogs, which layers competitive FOB-building and defensive-structure construction onto three-team combat, is drawing early community scrutiny over one faction's (Blue team) consistent underperformance — theories range from map-position disadvantages to the uniform's visibility undermining camouflage. Minor on its own, but worth a line for anyone tracking how base-building mechanics interact with competitive balance in a genre that's traditionally been pure combat-focused.

## Story 3: Marvel's Wolverine launches on PS5 to mixed-to-positive reviews

**Source:** Forbes | https://www.forbes.com/sites/brianmazique/2026/09/14/marvels-wolverine-unlocks-soon---heres-the-exact-release-time/ | Game Informer, Complex (via search)

Insomniac's Marvel's Wolverine unlocked globally today at 12am ET as a PS5 exclusive, landing at Metacritic 78/OpenCritic 80 — "generally favourable" but short of the near-universal acclaim Insomniac's Spider-Man titles received. Critics single out the brutal, parry-heavy combat as the game's strongest asset, with the 20-hour linear campaign's grim, violence-forward tone (built around mutant persecution themes) drawing more mixed reactions than Spider-Man's lighter register. Outside genre focus for this report, but worth a line as the week's biggest AAA console release.

## Story 4: Valve's Steam Frame VR headset launches, purchase lottery now live

**Source:** PC Gamer | https://www.pcgamer.com/hardware/vr-hardware/steam-frame-starts-at-usd1-059-with-reservation-lottery-open-now-enter-before-september-17-for-best-chance-of-getting-one-this-year/ | TheSixthAxis (via search)

Valve's standalone wireless VR headset launched yesterday at $1,059 (256GB) / $1,299 (1TB), bundled with two controllers, a 6GHz wireless adapter and a free copy of Half-Life: Alyx. Rather than open sales, Valve is running the same reservation-lottery model it used for the Steam Machine: sign-ups close 17 September at 1pm ET, the list is randomised, and purchase invitations start going out 18 September. Hardware rather than a genre story, included for breadth given how directly it will shape what Valve pushes on Steam over the next year.
