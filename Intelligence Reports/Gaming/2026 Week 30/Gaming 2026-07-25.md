## 1. Seed's AI-powered avatars draw criticism for shallow, buzzword-heavy dialogue
**Source:** PC Gamer | https://www.pcgamer.com/games/sim/mmo-life-sims-seeds-ai-powered-avatars-prove-once-again-that-nothing-shatters-immersion-quicker-than-crappy-chatbot-dialogue/

Klang Games' Seed — the shardless MMO life-sim tracked as an open loop since its 21 July early access launch — is getting its first substantive review coverage, and the verdict on its headline feature is blunt: the AI-powered "seedling" avatars lean hard on filler words ("heavy," "iconic," "vibe") and the tutorial bot mispronounces common words, both immediately immersion-breaking. The one credit given: unlike most chatbots, Seed's AI reportedly doesn't hallucinate game facts, and the tutorial bot gives genuinely useful plain-English answers about mechanics. For a game whose entire pitch is AI-driven social simulation, "the AI is accurate but boring to talk to" is a fairly damning review to get in week one.

Worth noting as a data point beyond gaming: this is a live, public example of the gap between "technically correct LLM output" and "output a user actually wants to read," which is the same failure mode worth guarding against in any Orwell-built agent-facing product.
