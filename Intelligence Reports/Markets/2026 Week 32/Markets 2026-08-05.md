## Editor's note

Two of the three most-hyped earnings prints of the year landed back to back last night, and both broke the same way: beat the headline number, sell the stock anyway. AMD posted record $11.54bn revenue and raised guidance, then lost 8% after hours because the "whisper number" wanted more. SpaceX did the same trick in reverse — a 92% revenue jump, still short of the trillion-dollar afterglow, hammered by AI segment losses and a lock-up expiration landing tomorrow that could put roughly $120bn of insider shares into the market. The more structurally important story is out of China: Washington is drafting a ban on imports of Chinese data-centre optical transceivers, and the CSI300 Telecom Services Index dropped 6% on the news this morning. Oil keeps drifting lower on Hormuz deal optimism, which is the one genuinely good-news thread running through an otherwise jumpy day.

## Story 1: AMD beats on every number, stock craters 8% anyway

**Source:** StockTitan | https://www.stocktitan.net/news/AMD/amd-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-s9qsl4zgkkw3.html ; Yahoo Finance | https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/amd-earnings-beat-estimates-stock-205710559.html ; CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/04/amd-earnings-are-a-key-test-for-chips-and-momentum-stocks-heres-what-the-options-market-is-saying.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Ventures]]

AMD reported record Q2 2026 revenue of $11.54bn (+50% YoY, versus $11.31bn consensus) and adjusted EPS of $1.66 against a $1.62 estimate, and guided Q3 to roughly $13bn. Shares had already run up 8.6% into the print during Tuesday's session, then gave back 8% in after-hours trading once the numbers actually landed. Polymarket had priced a 95%+ probability of a beat going in, so the beat itself was never the question — the market wanted a bigger raise on MI350 shipment numbers and Helios rack-scale order detail than AMD delivered.

This is the same pattern that hit SpaceX hours later (Story 2) and closes out the three-way earnings test flagged in yesterday's report: Palantir and onsemi both beat and rallied Monday night, AMD beat and fell Tuesday night. That makes it two-for-three on "beat triggers a rally" versus one-for-three on "beat gets sold" — not a clean verdict on whether the $3.3tn chip sell-off is sentiment or fundamentals, but the AMD result is the most AI-hardware-pure of the three, and it's the one that broke the wrong way.

Why it matters for the GPU compute venture: a stock up 140% year-to-date selling off on a beat-and-raise is a valuation reset, not a demand signal — the market had priced in more than AMD could confirm even with record numbers. That argues for treating current chip valuations as still stretched relative to what companies can actually prove quarter to quarter, which is the more useful read for entry timing than the raw "beat or miss" scoreboard.

## Story 2: SpaceX's first public earnings beat on revenue, stock falls 7-8% on AI capex and tomorrow's $120bn unlock

**Source:** CNN Business | https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/04/business/spacex-earnings-q2-2026 ; CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/04/spacex-spcx-earnings-live-updates-q2-2026.html ; FX Leaders | https://www.fxleaders.com/news/2026/08/04/spacex-lockup-tsunami-120b-in-shares-ready-to-hit-after-earnings/
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Ventures]], [[Agent Org]]

SpaceX's first-ever public quarterly report landed after the close Tuesday: $7.8bn revenue (+92% YoY, versus $6.8bn consensus) but a wider-than-hoped $541m net loss. Starlink revenue jumped 66%, and the AI segment — folded in via February's xAI/X integration — grew 247% YoY but is still burning heavily, with Q2 capital expenditure hitting $18.4bn. Shares fell as much as 8% in after-hours and late trading despite the top-line beat.

This resolves yesterday's open question with a clean answer: the market read this as a spending story, not a growth story, and the timing makes that read sharper rather than softer. Roughly 911.5 million insider and early-employee shares — about $120bn at current prices — unlock starting tomorrow, 6 August. A weak stock reaction two days before that supply hits the market is a materially worse setup than a strong one would have been; insiders now have one day of trading before their own selling window opens, into a stock that already sold off on the numbers.

Why it matters: this is the cleanest read yet on how the market prices "AI segment bolted onto an existing infrastructure business" — and today's answer is that heavy AI capex without matching revenue gets punished even in a 92%-growth quarter. Worth a same-day follow-up once tomorrow's unlock and the first day of free insider trading are both observable.

## Story 3: Washington drafts ban on Chinese data-centre optical transceivers, Chinese AI hardware stocks slide 6%

**Source:** Bloomberg (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-04/us-drafting-ban-on-china-data-center-components-reuters-says ; Tom's Hardware | https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/us-mulling-ban-on-key-chinese-networking-tech-in-data-center-component-crackdown-white-house-wants-to-impose-restrictions-in-2026-china-says-it-will-respond-if-necessary ; Yahoo Finance | https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/china-ai-hardware-stocks-slump-014228211.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]], [[Ventures]], [[Agent Org]]

The Trump administration is drafting a measure to bar imports of new-model Chinese-made optical transceivers — the components that move data across fibre-optic cable inside AI data centres — reportedly targeting a 2026 rollout. China's CSI300 Telecommunication Services Index tumbled 6% in early trading today on the news, with export-dependent names Zhongji Innolight, Eoptolink Technology and Suzhou TFC Optical Communications all opening sharply lower. The stated rationale is preventing Chinese firms from compromising or disrupting US data centres hosting frontier AI models; the practical effect would raise costs for US cloud operators (AWS named specifically) while benefiting domestic transceiver makers Coherent and Lumentum. Nothing is finalised — officials hope to publish the rule "this year" — and China's commerce ministry has said it will respond "if necessary."

This is a genuinely fresh escalation on a track separate from the rare-earth export-control suspension (which runs to 27 November) and separate from the Uyghur forced-labour import blacklist expansion flagged in [[Geopolitics]] two days ago. Washington and Beijing are now running at least three independent, simultaneously-escalatable trade tracks — rare earths, forced-labour imports, and now data-centre hardware — each on its own timeline.

Why it matters: this directly touches the "Chinese AI divergence from the US sell-off" thread flagged repeatedly this week. A draft-stage US import ban aimed squarely at Chinese data-centre supply chains is a harder-edged risk than anything in that thread so far, and it is a reminder that the "China AI names as a hedge against US chip-stock weakness" trade carries its own escalation risk, not just upside.

This is a genuine tone shift from Monday's picture of stalled talks and Trump's "duplicitous" outburst — see [[Geopolitics]] for the full triangulation, including Iran's continued insistence that it is negotiating shipping-lane fees with Oman, not talking to Washington directly.

Why it matters for Orwell: oil sliding on deal optimism is the mirror image of Monday's spike on stalled talks — same lever, opposite direction. Nothing here is resolved; a "positive talks" characterisation from Iran's foreign ministry has been the least reliable signal all week (see [[Geopolitics]] Story 1). ## What this means for Orwell

Today's real signal is that beating earnings is no longer enough for AI-adjacent names — AMD and SpaceX both delivered strong headline numbers and both got sold, which argues the market has moved from "is AI demand real" to "prove the spend is paying off right now," a much harder bar. That's the single most useful thing for the GPU compute venture's entry-timing question: current valuations still price in more certainty than companies can back up quarter to quarter. The Chinese optical-transceiver ban story is early-stage but structurally important — it opens a third simultaneous US-China trade front alongside rare earths and forced-labour imports, worth watching for compute-supply-chain read-through. Oil's slide on Hormuz optimism is good news for the property/mortgage climate underneath [[Comparison Network]], but treat it as sentiment, not resolution, until an actual framework is signed — see [[Geopolitics]] for why Iran's "positive talks" framing has been unreliable all week.
