## Story 1: SpaceX files its first-ever public earnings, two days before a $116bn lock-up unlock

**Source:** MLQ News | https://mlq.ai/news/spacex-sets-august-4-for-first-ever-earnings-report-triggering-116-billion-lock-up-expiration/ ; Forbes | https://www.forbes.com/sites/investor-hub/article/spacex-first-earnings-what-to-look-out-for/ ; TradingKey | https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/262070253-spacex-first-earnings-wall-street-robust-revenue-growth-morgan-stanley-reiterates-300-target-tradingkey
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Ventures]], [[Agent Org]]

SpaceX reports Q2 2026 results after the close today, with the call at 4:30pm ET — its first earnings release as a public company. This is now a three-segment business (space, connectivity, AI) following February's integration of xAI and X, which makes today's numbers the first audited read on how that merger is actually performing rather than how it was pitched. The company posted a $4.9bn net loss for 2025 and a further $4.28bn loss in Q1 2026 on $4.69bn revenue; analysts are looking for meaningful improvement this quarter, with consensus around $6.88bn in revenue driven by Starlink and the AI segment. Morgan Stanley has reiterated a $300 target.

The timing sharpens the stakes: the first unconditional lock-up tranche unlocks on 6 August, releasing roughly 911.5 million shares (about 20% of eligible insider and employee stock) to trade freely. A weak print two days before that unlock is a materially different setup than a weak print with no supply overhang attached — insiders with a strong incentive to see the stock hold up now have one earnings call to shape sentiment before their own selling window opens.

Why it matters: this is the cleanest available signal on how the market is pricing xAI's contribution inside a non-pure-play wrapper, which is relevant background for anyone tracking how "AI segment bolted onto an existing infrastructure business" gets valued versus a standalone AI lab. Worth a same-day follow-up once the print and the reaction to the lock-up are both in.

## Story 2: AMD reports tonight into a chip tape that's red again in premarket

**Source:** StockTitan | https://www.stocktitan.net/news/AMD/amd-to-report-fiscal-second-quarter-2026-financial-uxy30dtkyyhk.html ; 24/7 Wall St | https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/03/amd-price-prediction-analysts-eye-ai-driven-upside-ahead-of-earnings/ ; Yahoo Finance | https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/mu-skhy-nvda-amd-intc-085017500.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Ventures]]

AMD reports Q2 2026 after the close today, call at 5pm ET, with consensus at $11.34bn revenue (+48% YoY) and $1.61 adjusted EPS. The focus is squarely on AI chip revenue acceleration (MI350 shipments) and whether the company can put quantifiable order numbers behind its Helios rack-scale systems — the same "show me, don't tell me" bar the market set for Palantir and onsemi last night. Polymarket traders are pricing a 95.4% probability of a beat, and options are implying a 10% swing either way. AMD is up 122% year-to-date and 170% over twelve months, so there's real complacency priced in alongside the bullishness.

The backdrop into tonight's print is not friendly: chip stocks are red again in premarket trading, with Micron down roughly 4.9%, Nvidia off about 1.2%, AMD itself down more than 3% ahead of its own report, and the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) on a fourth straight down day. This sits on top of the roughly $3.3tn wiped off global chip valuations since 22 June and the SOX index still around 20% off its June high.

Why it matters for the GPU compute venture: AMD reporting into red premarket tape, immediately after two other chip-adjacent names (Palantir, onsemi) both beat and rallied hard after hours, sets up a genuinely useful test. If AMD also beats and the stock still can't hold a premarket gain, that is a stronger signal the sell-off has shifted from "AI capex fear" to a straightforward valuation reset that good earnings alone won't fix. If it beats and rallies like onsemi did, that argues the entry-timing window for GPU/compute exposure may be closing faster than the doom framing around the $3.3tn figure suggests.

## Story 3: Palantir and onsemi both beat and rallied hard after hours — the sentiment-vs-fundamentals question gets a partial answer

**Source:** FX Leaders | https://www.fxleaders.com/news/2026/08/03/palantir-technologies-q2-earnings-beat-sends-pltr-stock-above-140-as-revenue-growth-accelerates/ ; Benzinga | https://www.benzinga.com/markets/equities/26/08/60894586/palantir-snap-coreweave-on-semiconductor-sofi-stocks-watch ; Investing.com | https://ng.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/onsemi-beats-q2-estimates-guides-q3-revenue-above-consensus-2634650
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Agent Org]], [[Ventures]]

Both names that were flagged as tonight's tests in yesterday's report have now reported, and both beat comfortably. Palantir posted $1.94bn revenue (versus $1.80bn expected, +93% YoY) and $0.41 adjusted EPS (versus $0.35 expected), with US commercial revenue up 149% to $764m — the standout line. The stock closed Monday up 2.1%, then surged a further 15% after hours to above $140, and raised full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $8.15-8.16bn. onsemi beat too: $1.6bn revenue (+9% YoY), non-GAAP EPS of $0.74 against a $0.71 estimate, AI data-centre revenue up 34% sequentially and now expected to more than double for the full year, and shares jumped over 7% after hours to $86.33.

Two beats from two very different businesses — one pure AI-software, one industrial/auto-and-power semiconductors with a growing AI data-centre sliver — is real evidence for the "sell-off is sentiment, not fundamentals" reading Morningstar floated last week. It is not proof: two data points from a sector that's shed $3.3tn is a start, not a conclusion, and AMD's print tonight is a much cleaner AI-pure-play test than either of these two names offered.

Alibaba shares rose about 4-5% in premarket trading today after the company unveiled Qwen 3.8-Max, the largest model in its Qwen series at 2.4 trillion parameters, with full open-source release promised next week. BABA closed around $122.25, up roughly 5.1% on the session. The launch lands the same week the market is watching whether China's domestic AI-chip and AI-model names can keep outrunning the capex-fear rotation hitting US chip stocks — Alibaba's own compute story (reported access to roughly 20,000 Nvidia chips through its infrastructure) makes it a useful bridge case between the "China AI names as a hedge against US export controls" narrative and the more chip-specific Cambricon/Zhongji Innolight/Eoptolink names flagged in yesterday's report.

Why it matters: this is a genuinely fresh, today-dated data point for the Chinese AI divergence thread that yesterday's report flagged as needing better sourcing. It's one company, not a market-wide close, so treat it as supporting evidence rather than resolution — but it's a cleaner signal than yesterday's Futunn substitute.
