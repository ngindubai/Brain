## Story 1: SpaceX shares fall as much as 14% as $101bn of insider shares hit the market

**Source:** Forbes | https://www.forbes.com/sites/jimosman/2026/08/04/nearly-1-billion-spacex-shares-unlock-august-6/ ; The Motley Fool | https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/05/spacexs-lockup-expires-on-aug-6-heres-why-9115-mil/ ; Yahoo Finance (via search)
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Ventures]], [[Agent Org]]

The first tranche of SpaceX's staggered lock-up expired today: up to 911.5 million shares held by employees and early investors — roughly 3.3 times the existing 280.1 million-share public float — became eligible for sale, worth close to $101bn at current prices. The stock, already down 13.6% since its IPO and sitting below its $135 offer price after Tuesday's earnings-day slide, fell as much as 14% further today as the market tried to price in the new supply. An early-unlock provision that would have released an extra 10% of shares required the stock to trade 30% above the IPO price — it never got close, so today's release is the baseline case, not the accelerated one. Smaller tranches continue through October, a second large block follows Q3 earnings, and the full lock-up backstop runs to 8 December.

This is the direct continuation of yesterday's open loop: Tuesday's beat-but-sold-off earnings reaction was a weak setup heading into today's unlock, and that read has now played out in full. The stock has effectively been repricing for three straight sessions on the same underlying question — how much of the "AI segment bolted onto a rocket company" growth story the market is willing to pay for once actual float and actual numbers replace pre-IPO scarcity.

Why it matters for the GPU compute venture: this is now the second AI-adjacent mega-cap in a week (after AMD) where strong headline growth met a valuation reset rather than a rally. A stock this large losing a quarter of its value in three trading days on a mix of "not enough detail" and "too much supply" is a reminder that even the most-hyped 2026 listings are trading on thin, sentiment-driven floats — worth discounting the sticker-price comparisons when benchmarking entry points elsewhere in the sector.

## Story 2: Chip earnings season digests rather than surprises — Micron's record quarter, Broadcom's AI guidance, Nvidia holding steady

**Source:** Yahoo Finance (via search) | https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/article/intel-amd-micron-shares-trim-losses-after-broadcom-results-spark-semiconductor-sector-sell-off-130128361.html ; 24/7 Wall St (via search)
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Ventures]]

No fresh prints today, but the sector is still absorbing this week's numbers: Micron's fiscal Q3 revenue hit $41.46bn (+346% YoY) with HBM4 now in high-volume shipment for the lead AI-accelerator platform, and Q4 guidance calls for $50bn revenue and $31 non-GAAP EPS. Broadcom's guidance points to AI semiconductor revenue growing over 200% YoY to $16bn this quarter. Bank of America reiterated a bullish Micron call yesterday. Nvidia is holding a tight $209-213 range, essentially flat, after Monday's rally — the calmest of the major chip names this week.

Why it matters: the memory and networking side of the AI build-out (Micron, Broadcom) keeps printing numbers that outrun the GPU side's ability to convert headline beats into rallies (see AMD, Story 1 yesterday). That divergence — memory/interconnect demand still under-supplied, compute-layer valuations already stretched — is the more useful signal for GPU compute entry timing than any single day's price move.
