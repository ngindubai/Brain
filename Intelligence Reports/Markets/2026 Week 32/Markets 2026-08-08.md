## Story 1: Trump's team formally revives the effort to fire Fed Governor Lisa Cook — a hard 26 August deadline this time

**Source:** Fortune | https://fortune.com/2026/08/07/trump-fed-governor-lisa-cook-supreme-court-decision-central-bank-independence/ ; Washington Post (via search) | https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/08/07/white-house-revives-bid-remove-fed-lisa-cook-over-mortgage-claims/ ; NPR (via search) | https://www.npr.org/2026/08/07/nx-s1-5925167/trump-lisa-cook-federal-reserve
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Ventures]]

Deputy White House chief of staff Dan Scavino sent Cook a letter dated 5 August, laying out the same mortgage-fraud allegations Bill Pulte first referred last year — that Cook declared two properties, in Ann Arbor and Atlanta, as "primary residence" on separate mortgage applications — and set a formal 26-day deadline for her to respond. This is the sequel to June's 5-4 Supreme Court ruling, which blocked Trump's first attempt to remove Cook but did so on narrow due-process grounds: Chief Justice Roberts explicitly left the door open for the administration to try again with proper notice. This letter is that attempt, procedurally correct in a way the first one wasn't.

Cook's attorney Abbe Lowell called the allegations "as baseless now as they were a year ago." Senate Democrats went further and louder than the legal filing alone would justify — Schumer called it "DC partisan games to rig the economy," Warren called it "an authoritarian power grab" — which tells you this is being fought in public opinion as much as in court. The Supreme Court's June ruling deliberately punted on the substantive question of how high the bar is for firing a sitting Fed governor "for cause," so this round is where that question likely gets tested for real, assuming Cook doesn't resign and the administration follows through past the 26 August deadline.

Why it matters beyond the personalities: this is a live test of Fed independence with a specific clock on it, not an abstract governance debate. The dollar has already been trading soft on the back of last week's weak jobs print and the resulting rate-cut repricing; a credible threat to the Fed's institutional independence is the kind of thing that erodes the dollar and raises the term premium on Treasuries independent of whatever the actual rate path turns out to be. S&P Global flagged exactly this mechanism when it reaffirmed the US AA+ rating earlier this year, citing "the institutional strength and credibility of the Federal Reserve System" as a pillar of US monetary flexibility. If this drags past 26 August into an actual removal attempt and a court fight, expect it to become the dominant Markets story for weeks, not a one-day item — worth tracking as the single most important open loop heading into next week for anything long-duration and rate-sensitive, including the GPU compute venture's financing assumptions.

## Story 2: Berkshire Hathaway posts its second Abel-era earnings release — cash pile and buyback pace are the numbers to watch

**Source:** Berkshire Hathaway investor relations (release posted ~7am CT today) | https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/news/2026news.html ; Businesswire | https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260805236844/en/Berkshire-Hathaway-Inc.-Information-Regarding-Second-Quarter-Earnings-Release ; Yahoo Finance (via search)
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]]

Berkshire's Q2 2026 earnings release and 10-Q went live this morning as scheduled — the second quarterly report filed entirely under CEO Greg Abel since Warren Buffett stepped back at the end of 2025. Going in, analysts were split on the framing: some pegged revenue at roughly $95-96.5 billion (a 3-4% rise) with earnings per share seen falling year-on-year on tougher insurance-underwriting comps, while the number Wall Street was actually watching was share buybacks — Barron's had estimated $5-11 billion of repurchases for the quarter, the cleanest available signal of how Abel is using the near-$400 billion cash pile that had built up under Buffett's final stretch. Q1 under Abel showed 18% operating-earnings growth on insurance strength, favourable FX and interest income, with cash rising to over $397 billion.

Full digestion of today's actual figures — as opposed to the pre-release expectations above — will take the rest of the weekend and into Monday as analysts work through the 10-Q; this report will carry the confirmed operating-earnings and buyback numbers as an open-loop update once available, rather than repeat unconfirmed pre-release estimates as fact.

Why it matters: Berkshire's cash deployment (or lack of it) is one of the cleaner read-throughs on how a famously conservative allocator is pricing risk right now — a business with almost $400 billion in dry powder choosing to sit on it, or choosing to deploy it, says something about valuation levels across the board that's relevant background for anyone else's capital-allocation decisions, including within the portfolio.

## Story 4: Baker Hughes rig count flat at 588 — oil supply-side backdrop stays static while Hormuz risk stays live

**Source:** Oil & Gas 360 | https://www.oilandgas360.com/baker-hughes-rig-count-8-7/ ; EnergyNow (via search)
**Related vault notes:** [[Geopolitics]], [[Comparison Network]], [[Ventures]]

Friday's weekly Baker Hughes count showed the total US rig count unchanged at 588, with oil rigs up one to 451, gas rigs flat at 127. Canada's count fell by three to 216. This is a quiet, unremarkable print in isolation, but it matters as a backdrop check on the Hormuz story: US shale supply is not responding to this week's price strength with a fresh drilling push, which means any further Hormuz-driven price spike has less near-term US supply cushion behind it than a market rallying on a genuine multi-week uptrend would otherwise have.

The underlying Hormuz story itself moved again this week without fully resolving: Iranian state media now describes an emerging arrangement where inbound ships would use a route closest to Iran (under Tehran's coordination) and outbound ships a route closest to Oman, provisionally with no tolls, though Iran has separately signalled it wants to introduce a fee system later. A joint Iran-Oman statement remains, per Tehran, "in the final stage of review and drafting" — the same language used for several days running, with Iranian officials explicitly citing possible "third party" (read: US) obstruction as the risk to signing. No resolution today; carried as an open loop rather than a full story since the mechanics haven't materially changed since Thursday-Friday.

## Story 5: Bessent-linked S&P Global critical-minerals reference-price reports land Friday — a policy-adjacent data point worth tracking

**Source:** MLex | https://www.mlex.com/mlex/energy/articles/2511122 ; search aggregation on Bessent/critical minerals
**Related vault notes:** [[Geopolitics]], [[Ventures]]

S&P Global published five draft market reports on Friday covering gallium, germanium, antimony, neodymium/praseodymium and tungsten — an unusually granular slate of critical-minerals pricing data landing the same week Treasury Secretary Bessent has been pushing for independent reference pricing on these materials, and separately urging the World Bank to redirect funding toward critical-minerals supply chains. This is a slow-moving but structurally important thread: the US is trying to build price-discovery infrastructure for materials China currently dominates, as a precondition for reshoring supply chains rather than staying permanently exposed to Beijing's export-control leverage. See [[Geopolitics]] for the China-export-control side of this story.

Why it matters for Orwell: no direct exposure, but this is the kind of slow institutional building-block that eventually shows up in GPU and chip-supply cost structures — worth a mention in the GPU compute venture's risk register as a multi-quarter thread rather than anything actionable today.
