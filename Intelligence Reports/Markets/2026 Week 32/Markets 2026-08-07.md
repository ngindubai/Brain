## Story 1: July payrolls collapse to 22,000, unemployment hits 4.3% — Fed rate-cut odds surge to ~90% for September

**Source:** Bureau of Labor Statistics Employment Situation release (via search) | https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.htm ; CME FedWatch data (via search)
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Ventures]]

The headline number: US nonfarm payrolls rose by just 22,000 in July, against a consensus estimate that had been sitting in the 75,000-85,000 range. The unemployment rate ticked up to 4.3%, its highest level since October 2021. Wage growth held at +0.3% month-on-month but slipped to +3.7% year-on-year. The private sector alone added only 38,000 jobs. Most damaging of all for the "one soft month" reading: June's already-disappointing 57,000 print was revised down to a net loss, meaning the labour market has now effectively stalled for two consecutive months rather than had one bad print.

This is the clean resolution to yesterday's open loop. The 29 July FOMC held rates at 3.50-3.75% on a 9-3 hawkish-dissent vote — three regional presidents wanted a hike, not a cut — and the entire debate since then has been whether the labour market would validate that hawkish tilt or undercut it. Today's number undercuts it decisively. CME FedWatch has moved to roughly 90% probability of a 25bp cut at the September FOMC, with close to 10% now pricing a 50bp move, a sharp swing from a committee that was three dissenting votes away from tightening ten days ago. Treasury yields, the dollar and rate-sensitive equities are the assets to watch into next week's reaction; the report notes the dollar index was already at a seven-week low before this print landed, so today's data was arguably foreshadowed rather than a total surprise.

Why it matters for the GPU compute venture: a Fed that's clearly leaning toward cutting is straightforwardly supportive for anything long-duration and capital-intensive — data-centre and AI-infrastructure financing gets cheaper as the rate path turns, which is the more durable read-through than any individual stock's earnings reaction this week. It also reframes the whole SpaceX/AMD "beat and get sold anyway" pattern from earlier this week: those were valuation resets against a hawkish-Fed backdrop, and that backdrop just shifted. Worth revisiting entry-timing assumptions on that basis, not as a signal to act today, but as a genuine change in the macro weather rather than noise.

## Story 2: Iran strikes targets inside the Strait of Hormuz overnight — oil extends its gains as the Oman deal stays unsigned

**Source:** Bloomberg (via search, direct fetch blocked 403) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-aug-7 ; Wikipedia 2026 Strait of Hormuz crisis timeline (via search, cross-checked)
**Related vault notes:** [[Geopolitics]], [[Comparison Network]], [[Ventures]]

Oil extended its gains today after reports that Iran struck "hostile targets" inside the Strait of Hormuz overnight, following explosions near Qeshm Island. WTI is trading above $78 a barrel and Brent has settled around $82, both up from Wednesday-Thursday levels. This is a materially worse turn than yesterday's story, which left off with Trump's "deal by Wednesday" deadline simply missed and a projectile hitting a vessel near Al Khasab. Overnight, Iran moved from disputed incidents to an actual acknowledged strike inside the strait, while simultaneously continuing to negotiate a formal arrangement with Oman that would, per the terms reportedly on the table, bar US and Israeli vessels from the waterway and require "hostile" nations to pay compensation before transit — with the strait only fully reopening once the US maritime posture in the Gulf is lifted. Those are not the terms of a de-escalation; they're the terms of a managed stand-off.

The pattern flagged repeatedly this week — oil pricing a resolution ahead of the actual paperwork — has now flipped. Instead of the market discounting a deal that hadn't landed, it's now pricing an active strike that did land, on the same day US labour data would normally be the dominant macro story. That two catalysts this large hit simultaneously (a weak jobs print and a live escalation in the Gulf) and oil still rose rather than falling on rate-cut-driven demand optimism tells you which one the market is weighting more heavily right now.

Why it matters: this sits under [[Geopolitics]] for the full military and diplomatic detail, but the market read is straightforward — a live strike inside the strait is a worse baseline than a missed deadline, and the proposed Iran-Oman terms (blocking US/Israeli vessels, compensation demands) suggest whatever eventually gets signed will not fully restore the pre-crisis flow of Gulf shipping. That's a slower-burn drag on the property/mortgage climate under [[Comparison Network]] than yesterday's framing implied, and worth flagging as a downside risk rather than a base case resolving favourably.

It's a useful cross-check on Story 1 — if the equity reaction to today's payrolls print looks muted or mixed when full session data is available, it won't be because the market didn't believe the data, it'll be because gold, the dollar and rates markets had already priced most of it in over the preceding week.

A jobs report this weak, on top of a downward revision to an already-weak June, has moved the market from "will the Fed hike" to "how big is the September cut" in the space of one release — that's the more durable macro signal for the GPU compute venture than SpaceX's bounce or CoreWeave's pre-earnings wobble, because financing costs for anything long-duration and capital-intensive move directly with that repricing. The Hormuz escalation is the one to actually worry about: an acknowledged Iranian strike inside the strait, alongside Oman-framework terms that would formally restrict US and Israeli shipping, is a worse outcome than yesterday's "deadline missed" framing suggested, and it's a slower-burn risk to the broader Gulf and property/mortgage climate under [[Comparison Network]] if it hardens into a durable arrangement rather than resolving. Nothing here demands action today. The two genuine catalysts ahead are the market's fuller reaction to this jobs print once the session plays out, and CoreWeave's 11 August earnings, which will now land against a meaningfully looser rate outlook than looked likely a week ago.
