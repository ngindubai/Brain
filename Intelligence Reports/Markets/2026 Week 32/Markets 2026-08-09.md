## Story 1: Berkshire's Q2 numbers are in — Abel is now a confirmed net buyer, not a cash hoarder

**Source:** Pulse2 | https://pulse2.com/berkshire-hathaway-equity-purchases-reach-39-4-billion-as-q2-share-buybacks-surge-to-4-5-billion/ ; CNBC (via search) | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/08/berkshire-hathaway-earnings-q2-2026.html ; Seeking Alpha (via search)
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]]

Yesterday's report flagged Berkshire's release as posted but undigested. It's now digested. Q2 share buybacks came in at $4.527 billion, a sharp acceleration from the $235 million spent in Q1 — Abel has clearly decided the "wait and see" phase of his tenure is over. More telling: Berkshire's H1 2026 equity purchases hit $39.4 billion against $27.8 billion in sales, a net roughly $11.6 billion of stock buying for the half, which reverses the net-seller posture Buffett ran through most of last year. Cash and Treasury bills stood at $359.2 billion on 30 June, down from the record near-$400 billion built up over Buffett's final stretch — the first meaningful drawdown of that pile in years. Net earnings reportedly more than doubled to $25.7 billion, though that figure is being cross-checked against the operating-earnings line since Berkshire's headline net number is distorted by mark-to-market swings on the equity book, a standing quirk of GAAP reporting that Buffett spent a decade telling investors to ignore.

Two acquisitions ate a chunk of the deployed capital: OxyChem for roughly $9.4 billion and Taylor Morrison for roughly $6.8 billion. Combined with the buyback acceleration and the swing to net stock buying, the picture is a management team actively spending down a war chest rather than defending it — a genuine change in posture from the last two years of Berkshire's "nothing looks cheap enough" stance.

Why it matters: this is the cleanest available signal of how a famously conservative allocator is now pricing risk across both public equities and outright acquisitions. A near-$400 billion pile finally moving, six months into Abel's sole tenure, reads as a vote of confidence in current valuations from the most risk-averse large allocator in the market — worth noting as background context for anyone else's capital-allocation timing, including inside the portfolio.

## Story 2: Hormuz "deal" hits a parliamentary snag — the draft is tougher than the market has been pricing

**Source:** US News | https://www.usnews.com/news/world/articles/2026-08-08/iran-says-deal-on-strait-of-hormuz-is-close-but-will-not-open-the-waterway-by-itself ; Vantage Markets (via search) | https://www.vantagemarkets.com/market-analysis/crude-oil-price-today-brent-wti-august-3-7-2026/
**Related vault notes:** [[Geopolitics]], [[Comparison Network]], [[Ventures]]

Iran said on Saturday that its shipping-route agreement with Oman is close but explicitly will not by itself reopen the Strait — a meaningful hedge against the market narrative that a signature ends the crisis. Two details that hadn't surfaced by yesterday's report: the arrangement still has to clear Iran's parliament, and the draft under review is harsher than markets have been pricing in — reportedly barring US and Israeli vessels outright and imposing a fine of a fifth of cargo value on violators. Iran is also separately insisting Washington meet additional conditions before it will allow free transit, which keeps a US veto-by-inaction live even if Tehran and Muscat sign.

Why it matters: the market has spent a week trading a "deal is close" narrative that has repeatedly failed to become a signed deal, and now there's a concrete reason for further delay (a hostile-to-Western-shipping draft going through an Iranian legislature that has no incentive to soften it). Brent traded near $82 and WTI near $78 on Friday's close, both still well off the roughly $102 peak from two weeks earlier — the market has been pricing gradual de-escalation. A parliamentary rejection or a further-hardened draft is the scenario that isn't priced, and it is now a distinctly live possibility rather than a tail risk.

## Story 3: CoreWeave's options market is bracing for a 15%+ swing on Tuesday's earnings

**Source:** BigGo Finance | https://finance.biggo.com/news/09395e9c-7fa6-4e5a-85da-48099e1a150a ; TipRanks (via search); Benzinga (via search)
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Markets]]

CoreWeave reports Q2 2026 results Tuesday 11 August, and options traders are pricing a roughly 15.5% move on the print — a genuinely large expected swing for a company already carrying a $99.4 billion remaining-performance-obligations backlog that nearly quadrupled year-on-year in Q1. Consensus expects a loss of $1.27 a share against a year-ago profit of 27 cents, on revenue expected to more than double to $2.56 billion from $1.21 billion — the market is bracing for continued heavy infrastructure spend eating into near-term profitability while revenue growth stays explosive. Analyst positioning has been active into the print: Rosenblatt reiterated Buy with a $250 target on 5 August, and Piper Sandler initiated coverage with Overweight and a $151 target on 3 August — a wide spread that itself signals real disagreement about how to value the backlog versus the burn.

Why it matters: CoreWeave is the clearest public proxy for whether GPU-cloud demand is translating into durable revenue rather than just backlog optics, which matters directly for how the GPU compute venture should be reading the financing environment. A 15%+ priced move either way on Tuesday is a genuine signal event, not noise — worth watching live rather than picking it up after the fact.

## Story 4: Chip-stock momentum sets up the week — Micron and Broadcom carrying the AI-infrastructure trade

**Source:** 24/7 Wall St | https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/08/nvidia-micron-or-broadcom-what-would-raymond-dalio-do/ ; Yahoo Finance (via search)
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Markets]]

Saturday's read across the big four AI-infrastructure names: Micron's HBM4 is now in high-volume shipment for the lead AI-accelerator platform, Morgan Stanley has it as a top 2026 semiconductor pick, and Polymarket traders are assigning roughly 77.5% odds that Micron closes August above $700. Broadcom's Q2 FY26 AI-semiconductor revenue hit $10.8 billion (+143% YoY), with guidance for Q3 AI revenue of $16 billion (+200%+) and a stated target of over $100 billion in AI sales by 2027. Nvidia's Q3 revenue reportedly hit $41.5 billion (+345.7% YoY) with gross margins expanding to 84.6%. AMD's Q2 results are out and the company remains positioned as the consensus "NVDA alternative" rather than a genuine share-taker so far.

Why it matters: this is table-setting, not news of a single event — but the pattern (accelerator-adjacent memory and networking names outgrowing even Nvidia's own eye-watering growth rate) is the clearest read yet that the AI capex cycle is broadening beyond the GPU line item itself into memory, custom silicon and networking. That broadening is the thing to track for the GPU compute venture's cost assumptions — HBM and networking capacity, not just GPU allocation, are becoming the binding constraint.

## Story 5: Ethena's ENA token breaks out on whale buying and a Coinbase Ventures tie-up

**Source:** CoinMarketCap | https://coinmarketcap.com/top-stories/6a759faa4279db6210450601/ ; Coinpedia (via search)
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]]

ENA, the governance token behind the Ethena synthetic-dollar protocol, is up roughly 17% over the past week after Arthur Hayes added another 10.9 million tokens (about $985,000) to a steadily growing position — a continuation of an accumulation pattern rather than a one-off. The token broke above a multi-week descending trendline after defending the $0.07-$0.075 zone, and Coinbase Ventures has separately taken a position in ENA alongside a partnership to expand onchain finance products for Coinbase's retail base, launching next week. Separately, USDe (Ethena's synthetic dollar) now has over $4.3 billion — roughly 60% of its total supply — locked across Pendle and Aave yield strategies, part of a broader stablecoin market that has grown $71 billion (30%) since April 2025 to about $308 billion, largely on the back of last year's GENIUS Act framework.

Why it matters: this is the one genuinely new, today-relevant crypto move — most of the majors (Bitcoin, Ethereum) are just holding Friday's range over the weekend, but ENA is an actual breakout with an institutional catalyst (Coinbase Ventures) behind it, which is the kind of signal worth a note even though it sits well outside anything Orwell has direct exposure to.

## Story 6: The crypto market-structure bill clears its first Senate hurdle — but not before September

**Source:** CoinDesk | https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/08/08/u-s-senate-opens-first-stage-of-crypto-clarity-act-voting-to-give-bill-a-chance-next-month
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]]

Senate Majority Leader John Thune filed a motion to proceed on the Digital Asset Market CLARITY Act in the small hours of Saturday morning, after the Senate missed its window to vote before the August recess. The bill now enters cloture — a procedural chain requiring 60 votes — with the first real vote unlikely before the Senate reconvenes on 14 September. Outstanding disputes include illicit-finance protections, stablecoin reward structures, and a government-ethics provision that would bar senior officials, including the president, from backing crypto projects; that ethics clause needs Trump's sign-off before Democrats will supply the ten votes needed for passage.

Why it matters: this has been dangling as a market overhang for weeks (it was the explicit reason cited for Tuesday's crypto move earlier this week), and the practical effect of Saturday's move is that the overhang now has a hard floor date — nothing happens before mid-September regardless of how negotiations go. That's a genuine data point for anyone timing crypto-adjacent decisions around US regulatory clarity: the "any week now" framing was wrong, and the realistic window has moved out by roughly five weeks.
