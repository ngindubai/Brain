## Story 1: Oil plunges as Trump halts Iran strikes, floats Hormuz reopening deal
**Source:** Oilprice.com | https://oilprice.com/ ; Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/2/why-has-trump-halted-iran-attacks-and-what-is-the-deal-he-is-hinting-at ; TradingEconomics | https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/brent-crude-oil/news/535543
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Ventures]]

Brent crude is trading around $83.56/bbl and WTI around $79.91/bbl in today's session, both down roughly 5% on the day, continuing a slide that started when Trump said he would order a halt to further US strikes on Iran after "parameters" were reached for a deal to end the conflict. The proposed terms: Iran reopens the Strait of Hormuz and the region-wide attacks stop, in exchange for the US lifting its naval blockade and letting Tehran resume oil exports. No deal is signed yet — Iran's state media has already disputed Trump's characterisation of who asked for the pause — but the market has read the de-escalation signal as credible enough to erase a meaningful slice of the risk premium built up since the conflict reignited (Brent had spiked toward $96-113 at various points during the escalation).

This is the market-desk follow-through on the story [[Geopolitics]] is covering from the policy/security angle today — the strike-cancellation announcement itself happened yesterday (2 Aug), today is the first full trading session pricing it. The move is large and fast enough that it qualifies as new information for the markets book even though the political trigger was flagged in yesterday's brief.

Why it matters: a genuine ceasefire-adjacent deal would remove the single biggest tail risk hanging over global freight and fuel costs this year. It is not signed, and Iran's public pushback on the framing is a reason not to treat this as resolved — expect volatility to persist until there's an actual signed document, not just a stand-down.

## Story 2: Palantir reports Q2 tonight — options market pricing a 15% swing either way
**Source:** TipRanks | https://www.tipranks.com/news/palantir-stock-price-forecast-options-traders-expect-a-large-swing-in-pltr-as-earnings-near ; FX Leaders | https://www.fxleaders.com/news/2026/07/30/why-is-palantir-stock-falling-pltr-at-122-before-aug-3-earnings/
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Agent Org]]

Palantir reports Q2 2026 results after the close today, with a 5pm ET webcast. Consensus is $1.81bn revenue (+81% YoY) and $0.34 EPS, up from $0.16 a year ago. Options traders are pricing an implied move of roughly 15% in either direction — a range from about $104 to $140 off a starting price near $122.78. That is an unusually wide expected swing for a company of Palantir's size, and it reflects how contested the stock has become: shares are down about 30% year-to-date as investors have grown warier of paying premium multiples for AI-adjacent names, even ones with genuine government and enterprise revenue growth.

The result lands the same day as onsemi's earnings (Story 3) and the day before AMD's (Story 4), making today/tomorrow a compressed stretch of earnings that will effectively re-price how much benefit of the doubt the market still extends to AI infrastructure and AI-software names after a rough six weeks for the sector.

Why it matters: Palantir is the clearest bellwether for whether enterprise AI software commands a growth premium or gets valued like conventional SaaS. A beat-and-hold reaction would be a modest positive signal for the broader AI trade; a sell-the-news reaction (even on a beat) would confirm the market has moved into "show me the ROI" mode across the board, not just for chip names.

## Story 3: onsemi reports Q2 tonight — a direct read on chip demand amid the memory crisis
**Source:** StockTitan | https://www.stocktitan.net/news/ON/onsemi-to-announce-second-quarter-financial-b9rgs9gn536r.html ; Yahoo Finance | https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/exploring-analyst-estimates-semiconductor-corp-131523174.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Ventures]]

onsemi (ON Semiconductor) reports its own Q2 2026 results after the close today, with a call at 5pm ET. Analysts expect $0.72 EPS (+35.9% YoY) on $1.59bn revenue (+8% YoY). Unlike Palantir, this is a straight industrial/auto-and-power-semiconductor name rather than an AI pure-play, which makes it a useful cross-check on whether chip demand strength is genuinely broad-based or concentrated in the handful of AI-datacentre names (Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron) that have dominated the narrative.

Why it matters: a soft print here, on top of the sector-wide semiconductor sell-off (Story 4), would suggest the weakness is more than sentiment around AI capex — it would point to real end-market softness bleeding into adjacent chip categories. A resilient print would support the view that the recent chip-stock rout is a valuation reset within AI names specifically, not a broader chip-cycle downturn.

## Story 4: Chip sector heads into AMD's Tuesday earnings still down $3.3 trillion from the June peak
**Source:** CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/chip-selloff-sk-hynix-samsung-softbank.html ; The Motley Fool | https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/02/micron-stock-is-down-39-high-history-sugg/ ; Yahoo Finance (Kavout) | https://www.kavout.com/market-lens/what-triggered-the-recent-semiconductor-sell-off
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Ventures]]

Context carried into today's session: the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) is down nearly 20% from its June high, and global chip stocks have shed roughly $3.3 trillion in combined market value since 22 June. Micron alone is down about 39% from its high despite still-tight memory supply, after Broadcom's cautious Q3 AI-chip guidance in late July triggered a sell-the-news reaction across the group, compounded by South Korea curbing leveraged-ETF exposure to chip names and a genuine global memory-chip shortage that is simultaneously crushing smartphone volumes (forecast -13% for 2026) while producers redirect output to higher-margin datacentre customers. Morningstar's read is that the move is "driven largely by sentiment rather than fundamentals" — worth noting given AMD reports tomorrow (Tuesday 4 Aug) into this backdrop, with CoreWeave's Q2 results also expected this week as a GPU-capacity execution test (Bank of America modelling 108% YoY revenue growth for CoreWeave's Q2, accelerating through year-end if power buildout keeps pace).

Why it matters for the GPU compute venture thesis: this is exactly the kind of sentiment-versus-fundamentals gap the venture needs to watch — if the sell-off is sentiment-driven and reverses on a good AMD print, GPU/compute exposure re-rates higher fast; if it is the start of a genuine demand air-pocket, that changes the entry-timing calculus materially.

The 10-year Treasury yield sits around 4.22-4.24% today, essentially flat versus the prior session. Why it matters: nothing actionable today, but a market this undecided on the next Fed move six weeks out is a market vulnerable to a sharp repricing on the jobs number. Worth a one-line flag rather than a deep dive until Friday's data lands.
