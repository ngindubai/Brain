## Story 1: Nvidia's $500bn Wall Street financing push keeps weighing on the stock

**Source:** CNBC (via search) | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/10/nvidia-wall-street-asset-managers-500-billion-ai-push.html ; Forbes | https://www.forbes.com/sites/antoniopequenoiv/2026/08/10/nvidia-stock-loses-130-billion-in-market-value-as-firm-reportedly-enters-500-billion-ai-financing-deal/ ; Bloomberg (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/nvidia-to-team-with-wall-street-on-500-billion-package-ft-says
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Markets]]

The FT reported Monday that Nvidia is working with Apollo Global Management, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to mobilise more than $500bn in third-party capital for hyperscalers, frontier labs and enterprises to build data centres and buy Nvidia hardware, treating compute like a borrowable asset class alongside commercial real estate. The market's reaction ran opposite to what a headline this size would usually produce: Nvidia fell 2.4% to around $218, wiping out $130bn in value, while the Wall Street partners rallied (Apollo +3.6%, Blackstone +3.3%, KKR +1.2%).

The read is straightforward and not a good one for Nvidia specifically: investors are pricing this as Nvidia getting more financially entangled in funding its own customers, reviving the circular-financing worry that's dogged the AI trade all year — if one large buyer stumbles, the ripple runs straight back through the company that financed the purchase. This is still the story moving the chip complex into today's session; there's no material update on the deal itself dated today, but it's the backdrop every other chip-stock and AI-infra story below is trading against.

## Story 2: Anthropic forms Theseus Infrastructure with Macquarie and GIC — a different answer to the same financing question

**Source:** Bloomberg (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-10/anthropic-macquarie-and-gic-form-venture-for-ai-data-centers ; Macquarie Group | https://www.macquarie.com/au/en/about/news/2026/anthropic-mam-gic-data-centre-infrastructure-partnership.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Agent Org]]

Also Monday, Anthropic announced Theseus Infrastructure, a new platform with Macquarie Asset Management and Singapore's GIC to develop, own and lease data-centre capacity back to Anthropic under long-term agreements, with Macquarie and GIC providing the majority of project financing. Functionally this is the AI-lab version of the same problem Nvidia's $500bn push is solving for the supply side: keep the capex off Anthropic's own books, let infrastructure investors take the asset risk, lock in long-term leases as the return. Anthropic has separately committed to absorbing any local electricity-price increases the build-out causes, a direct answer to the community-cost backlash that's been stalling data-centre approvals in several US states.

Two different labs of the AI stack — the chipmaker and the model lab — landed on structurally similar off-balance-sheet financing solutions on the same day. Worth tracking as the emerging template for how the AI buildout actually gets paid for, not just an isolated deal; relevant to how the GPU compute venture might eventually need to structure its own financing.

## Story 4: CoreWeave reports Q2 after the close tonight — the next real test for GPU compute economics

**Source:** TipRanks (via search) | https://www.tipranks.com/news/crwv-stock-coreweave-earnings-will-put-its-99b-backlog-to-the-test ; Seeking Alpha | https://seekingalpha.com/news/4629968-coreweave-q2-2026-earnings-preview
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]]

CoreWeave reports second-quarter results today after market close, with a 5pm ET call. Wall Street consensus is for roughly $2.5bn in revenue (100%+ year-on-year growth) and a per-share loss around $1.17-1.23. The numbers that matter more than the headline beat/miss: whether the $99.4bn revenue backlog holds up, and whether the $31-35bn capex plan gets any more colour. Options markets are pricing an implied post-earnings swing of roughly 13%. CRWV has missed consensus in three of the last four quarters, so a beat isn't the base case priced in.

This is the cleanest live read on neocloud GPU-rental economics available this week, and it lands the same week as the Nvidia financing story above — a weak print would sharpen the circular-financing worry considerably; a strong one would ease it, at least for CoreWeave specifically. Genuinely the single most relevant data point of the week for the GPU compute venture's financing assumptions. Results weren't out as of this report; check tomorrow's brief for the outcome.
