## Story 1: Iran denies direct US talks on Hormuz — oil firms as the "deal soon" story cracks again

**Source:** CNBC (via search) | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/10/oil-prices-today-brent-wti-hormuz-trump-iran.html ; CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/09/saudi-aramco-extinguishes-refinery-fire-houthis-claim-attack.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Geopolitics]], [[Comparison Network]], [[Ventures]]

Over the weekend Iran's foreign ministry explicitly denied any direct negotiation with Washington over reopening the Strait of Hormuz, insisting it is dealing only with Oman on a traffic-management scheme and that Washington still has separate conditions to meet before the strait reopens. That's a harder line than the "deal close" framing pushed by US officials last week, and it's feeding straight into today's session: Brent is up roughly 1.1% to $84.46 and WTI up about 0.8-1% to $78.80-79.00, both firming on renewed doubt that a transit agreement is imminent rather than on any new incident.

This is the same running Hormuz thread flagged in yesterday's report, but the specific new fact today is Iran's on-record denial that Washington is even a direct party to the talks. The pattern is now well established: every "close to a deal" comment compresses the risk premium, every Iranian walk-back reflates it. That whipsaw, not a clean resolution either way, is what's actually priced into Brent and WTI right now — worth remembering for anyone timing options or futures off this complex rather than treating either direction as settled.
