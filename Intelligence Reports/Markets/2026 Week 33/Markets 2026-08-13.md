## Story 1: Cisco beats on Q4 FY26, but shares fall on margin mix despite record AI orders

**Source:** Cisco investor relations | https://investor.cisco.com/news/news-details/2026/CISCO-REPORTS-FOURTH-QUARTER-AND-FISCAL-YEAR-2026-EARNINGS/default.aspx ; market reaction via Investing.com and Yahoo Finance | https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/cisco-q4-fy26-slides-ai-orders-surge-45x-shares-dip-on-margins-93CH-4856377

Cisco reported fiscal Q4 2026 after the close on 12 August, and the numbers were strong by any normal measure. Revenue hit a record $17.3bn, up 18% year-on-year, with product revenue up 24% to $13.5bn. Non-GAAP EPS came in at a record $1.22, up 23%. Full fiscal year 2026 revenue was $63.3bn (+12%), GAAP net income $13.3bn (+30%). Total product orders were up 35% year-on-year in the quarter, or 25% excluding hyperscalers, with double-digit growth in every geography.

The AI number is the one that matters for the GPU compute venture read-through: networking product orders grew 40% year-on-year, the eighth straight quarter of double-digit growth, and Cisco closed the fiscal year with $9.3bn in hyperscaler AI infrastructure orders, up from a $9bn guide. Q4 alone brought in $4bn of AI orders, and management now guides to $7.5bn of AI infrastructure revenue from hyperscalers in fiscal 2027, up from $4bn in fiscal 2026 and $1bn in fiscal 2025. Beyond hyperscalers, neocloud, sovereign and enterprise AI orders hit $1.3bn, up roughly 150% year-on-year.

Despite that, shares fell as much as 4.6% in after-hours trading to $118.18, and were still trading around 4% lower in early Thursday dealing, weighing slightly on Nasdaq 100 futures. The reason is margin, not growth: management flagged that a heavier hardware mix in the AI order book will pressure gross margins going forward, and after a 61% year-to-date rally into the print, the market used the guide as an excuse to take profit rather than extend it. The stock had closed the regular session at $123.95, near its 52-week high of $130.37, so this reads as a normal give-back after a strong run rather than a demand scare.

This is the same signal Cisco has been sending for two quarters running: hyperscalers are still ordering networking gear at an accelerating pace, which is one of the cleaner proxies for actual capex intent rather than allocation noise from any single GPU vendor. The margin worry is worth tracking, though, because it is the first time a bellwether AI infrastructure name has flagged that the mix of what's being bought (more hardware, less high-margin software/services) is starting to bite profitability. If that shows up in Broadcom or Arista's numbers next, it becomes a genuine theme rather than a one-off.

## Story 2: July CPI lands exactly on consensus, market reaction confirmed

**Source:** CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/12/cpi-inflation-report-july-2026.html ; Investing.com/Reuters | https://www.investing.com/news/economic-indicators/market-shrugs-off-inline-july-cpi-report-4855116
**Related vault notes:** [[Priorities]]

Closing yesterday's open loop: July CPI, released 12 August, printed headline +0.1% month-on-month and +3.4% year-on-year, core +0.2% month-on-month and +2.5% year-on-year, both down 0.1 point from June and exactly in line with the Dow Jones consensus. No surprise in either direction. The market's reaction was correspondingly muted, "shrugged off" per Reuters' framing: futures rose modestly, Treasury yields eased across the curve, and the S&P 500 closed the 12 August session up 0.3%, the Nasdaq Composite up roughly 0.5%, the Dow essentially flat. The in-line print keeps the "no need to hike" narrative intact following last week's weak jobs report, and reinforces market expectations that the Fed holds again in September rather than moving in either direction.

Nothing here changes the macro backdrop for AI infrastructure financing: rates stay where they are, the September meeting stays a hold, and Jackson Hole (27-29 August) remains the next point where the Fed could signal a shift. Filed as resolved, not carrying forward as an open loop.

## Story 3: Nvidia earnings date confirmed for 26 August as CDS spread keeps drifting wider

**Source:** TradingEconomics, Yahoo Finance/moomoo aggregation of CDS pricing | https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/nvidia-rising-cds-talk-wall-123955612.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]]

Two small but genuine updates to the running Nvidia story. First, Nvidia's next earnings report is now confirmed for 26 August, meaning the market has under two weeks before the next real catalyst on AI demand sustainability; peers reporting well this cycle (Cisco, CoreWeave, Supermicro, Micron) are being read as a supportive signal ahead of that date. Second, the credit market side of the $500bn financing story that dominated Monday's news is still moving: Nvidia's five-year CDS spread was quoted around 79.8bp today, up from the roughly 77.2bp level flagged in yesterday's report and off the 82bp intraday spike hit in late July. The direction is still widening, not stabilising, though the pace has slowed.

The stock itself was little changed to marginally lower today. Nothing here is a new thesis, just a continuation: credit markets remain more sceptical of the "circular financing" structure (Nvidia backstopping capital that flows back to Nvidia via chip purchases) than equity holders are. Worth watching whether the CDS spread breaks back above 80bp before the 26 August print; that would be the first sign the credit market is pricing real distress risk rather than just a highly geared balance sheet.

## Story 4: Oil snaps five-day rally on Hormuz negotiation signals and a large inventory build

**Source:** TradingEconomics, Bloomberg (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-12/latest-oil-market-news-and-analysis-for-aug-13
**Related vault notes:** [[Geopolitics]]

WTI fell 1.39% to $82.11/bbl today, Brent down 1.19% to $87.92, snapping a five-day advance. Two things drove it: mixed diplomatic signals on the Strait of Hormuz standoff (Pakistan's defence minister said the US and Iran are "close to some sort of arrangement," while Iran-Oman talks are reportedly advanced, even as Trump repeated that the US has "total control" over the waterway and negotiations remain formally deadlocked), and a large EIA inventory build of 17.4m barrels last week, the biggest weekly rise since early 2023. Full geopolitical context sits in today's [[Geopolitics]] report; flagging here only for the market read-through. A sustained pullback in oil, if it holds, is a mild tailwind for input costs and inflation expectations generally, but the Hormuz situation is unresolved enough that this reads as a pause, not a resolution.
