## Story 1: July retail sales hold up, markets head into data-heavy Friday on a three-week win streak

**Source:** Census Bureau Advance Monthly Retail Sales / Yahoo Finance / financecalendar.com | https://www.census.gov/retail/marts/www/marts_current.pdf
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Priorities]]

The Census Bureau's Advance Monthly Retail Sales report for July landed at 8:30am ET today. Headline sales rose roughly 0.3% month-on-month, extending a run of consecutive monthly gains and broadly matching the consensus call of +0.2-0.3%. Core retail sales (ex-autos, ex-restaurants) came in around +0.3% month-on-month and roughly +4.7% year-on-year — a tenth straight month of growth on the National Retail Federation's parallel tracking measure, which showed +0.32% m/m and +5.15% y/y.

The print lands into a market that closed Thursday at a fresh S&P 500 record above 7,800, on track for a third straight winning week — the first such streak since May. The University of Michigan's preliminary August consumer sentiment reading is due at 10am ET today; consensus expected a soft print (around 54, down slightly from July's 55.2) with one-year inflation expectations holding near 4.2%. That sentiment number, plus the retail sales beat-in-line, is this morning's real tape-setter, not any single stock story.

For the venture: consumer resilience matters less directly than the read-through to Fed positioning. A retail sales print that's neither hot enough to revive rate-hike chatter nor soft enough to force an emergency cut keeps the "no move until Jackson Hole" consensus intact — the base case the GPU compute venture's financing assumptions should keep running on. Nothing here changes that base case; it confirms it.

## Story 2: Applied Materials beats EPS, misses on revenue, guides strong — stock slides anyway

**Source:** Benzinga / Investing.com / TradingKey | https://www.benzinga.com/markets/earnings/26/08/61193840/applied-materials-beats-q3-estimates-issues-strong-guidance-unprecedented-demand
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]]

Applied Materials reported fiscal Q3 results after Thursday's close, with the stock reaction still playing out in today's session. EPS came in at $3.50 versus $3.45 expected, a beat, but revenue of $9.115bn missed the $9.176bn consensus. Shares fell close to 4% in the after-hours/early session despite CEO Gary Dickerson calling it "another record-breaking quarter" with "the highest sequential revenue growth in the company's history."

The guide was the strong part of the print: Q4 revenue of $9.75-10.75bn and non-GAAP EPS of $3.82-4.22, both above where the Street sat, plus a raise to the calendar-2026 Semiconductor Systems growth outlook to "more than 30%." CFO Brice Hill pointed to an eight-quarter order backlog as evidence of two-year forward visibility on AI-driven fab spend. The sell-off looks like profit-taking after AMAT's roughly 200% rally off its late-July low near $436, not a demand problem.

This is a genuine AI-capex checkpoint, one layer upstream of the GPU compute venture: semicap equipment orders are the leading indicator for the fab capacity that eventually becomes GPU and HBM supply. A record backlog and raised growth guide, delivered even as the stock wobbles on the print, is a bullish signal for anyone positioned on multi-year AI infrastructure buildout rather than the next quarter's print. Watch whether Lam Research and KLA, which report later in the cycle, confirm the same backlog story.

## Story 3: Memory sector rally still rippling through Friday's session

**Source:** 24/7 Wall St / Yahoo Finance | https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/13/sandisk-unveils-multi-year-financial-model-and-growth-strategy-memory-stocks-soar/
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]]

Still-developing thread, not a new story: SanDisk's Thursday investor day set out a long-term financial model targeting mid-to-high-teens revenue growth and roughly 80% non-GAAP gross margins through fiscal 2028-30, underpinned by enterprise data-centre flash demand it sees reaching 1.2 zettabytes by 2030. The stock jumped 15% on the day; Micron, SK Hynix and Western Digital all moved 6-8% higher in sympathy, and the read-through lifted equipment names Lam Research, Applied Materials and Camtek too.

There's no fresh dated catalyst today — this is Thursday's news continuing to be digested in Friday trading rather than a new development. It's worth carrying as an open loop because it reinforces the same memory-supply-tightness thesis behind Micron's trillion-dollar market cap milestone earlier in the week. HBM and enterprise flash are both direct cost inputs for any GPU compute build; a sector-wide reset toward structurally higher margins on tight supply is relevant to venture economics even without new news today.

## Story 4: CoreWeave and Nebius re-rating still the dominant AI-infrastructure story of the week

**Source:** CNBC / Yahoo Finance | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/11/coreweave-crwv-q2-earnings-report-2026.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]]

Also a carry-forward rather than new news: CoreWeave's Q2 print (revenue $2.58bn, +112% y/y, backlog nearly doubling to $104bn) and Nebius's Q2 print (cloud revenue $575-582m, up over 500% y/y, four separate $1bn+ contracts signed in the quarter) both landed Tuesday/Wednesday, driving CoreWeave up ~19% and Nebius up ~34% on the day. Neither company has put out fresh news today; the stocks remain elevated as the market keeps digesting what the backlog numbers imply for GPU rental economics.

The signal that matters for the venture: two "neocloud" GPU-rental operators just showed contracted demand outrunning even bullish Street numbers, with backlog growth (CoreWeave nearly 2x since November) outpacing revenue growth. That's the closest public proxy available to the GPU compute venture's own market — it says GPU rental pricing power and utilisation are, for now, better than the bears expected. Worth revisiting in a fortnight for any capacity, financing, or power-constraint commentary from either company, which is where the real risk to that thesis would show up first.
