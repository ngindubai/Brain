## Story 1: August payrolls blow past consensus at 162K, flipping the Fed's September debate from cut to hike-risk

**Source:** Yahoo Finance | https://finance.yahoo.com/news/us-nonfarm-payrolls-surge-august-124328262.html and CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/04/jobs-report-august-2026.html

Thursday's report flagged this as pending; it's now resolved, and the resolution matters more than a routine data day usually would. The Bureau of Labor Statistics' August print came in at +162,000 nonfarm payrolls, roughly triple the +53,000 to +58,000 consensus and the strongest monthly gain since March, with unemployment holding steady at 4.1%. That's a sharp reversal from Wednesday's soft ADP private-payrolls miss (+38,000 against a +47,000 consensus), which had set up expectations for a weak number.

The market reaction was immediate and went the opposite way from what a "coin flip" framing would suggest: futures moved lower and short-end Treasury yields rose sharply, since a labour market this strong hands ammunition to the "higher for longer" camp rather than the rate-cut camp. Fed attention now shifts to next week's CPI and PPI prints as the final inputs before the 16-17 September FOMC meeting, and at least one tracked source now puts hike odds (not cut odds) at over 60% on the FedWatch-style tool — a genuine reversal from two weeks ago's straightforward easing narrative, and from yesterday's report's "coin flip" framing.

This is the one item today with a clean read-through to the GPU compute venture's rates exposure and to anyone running Fed-linked options or futures: the setup for September has flipped from "probably cuts" to "real chance of a hike," and that repricing happened in a single data release rather than gradually. Worth revisiting once CPI lands next week rather than trading on this print alone.

## Story 2: Oil holds its post-escalation range into the weekend as Mitsui warns Hormuz disruption won't normalise this year

**Source:** Vantage Markets | https://www.vantagemarkets.com/market-analysis/brent-wti-crude-oil-price-september-4-2026/ and oilprice.com

Friday's close had Brent at $97.36 and WTI at $92.39, capping a week in which Brent gained roughly 8% on renewed Iran hostilities and the continued near-shutdown of Hormuz shipping (six vessel transits Wednesday against a roughly 13-a-day ten-day average). Nothing escalated further today — no new strikes reported since Friday — but the one incremental data point worth flagging is Japan's Mitsui O.S.K. Lines, the world's largest tanker operator, now saying it expects Hormuz disruption to persist without normalisation through year-end. That's a shipping-industry operator making a planning assumption, not a trader's guess, and it argues for treating the standing floor under oil prices (as flagged in yesterday's report) as a multi-month planning assumption rather than a near-term risk premium that resolves on any particular news day.

No new information today changes the picture from yesterday's correction (Hormuz effectively closed, not "cooling"); this is a continuation, tracked in the open loops table.
