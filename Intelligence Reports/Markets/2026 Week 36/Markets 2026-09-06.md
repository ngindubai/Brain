## Story 1: CENTCOM disables three more Iranian tankers after IRGC missile attack on two US warships — the tanker war's sharpest escalation yet

**Source:** ABC News | https://abcnews.com/International/live-updates/iran-live-updates-centcom-targeted-iranian-forces-posed/?id=136080582 and Gulf Times | https://www.gulf-times.com/article/732614/international/uslatin-america/centcom-announces-targeting-three-iranian-crude-oil-tankers

US Central Command said its forces struck three Iranian crude oil tankers after Iran's IRGC fired ballistic missiles at two US Navy vessels — a carrier and a guided-missile destroyer — patrolling in the Gulf. Both US ships evaded the missiles with no personnel injured. In response, CENTCOM permanently disabled two IRGC-linked tankers (the M/T Downy off Kharg Island and the M/T Stark 1 near Jask) and struck a third, the M/T Kylo, in the Gulf of Oman. CENTCOM commander Admiral Brad Cooper's statement was blunt: "If you shoot at two of our ships, we will impose an even higher economic cost — taking out three of yours."

This is a materially sharper escalation than the "standing floor, no new escalation" framing this report carried yesterday: Iran moved from harassing commercial shipping to firing directly at US Navy warships, and the US response directly degrades more of Iran's own tanker fleet rather than just intercepting attacks. For anyone running Hormuz-linked oil exposure or the GPU compute venture's rates thesis, this is the single most important data point of the weekend — it argues the "disruption through year-end" read from Mitsui O.S.K. (carried in yesterday's report) understates the trajectory if the exchange keeps escalating rather than plateauing. Full detail on the regional and military read-through is in today's [[Geopolitics]] report; this entry covers the market and shipping-cost angle specifically. Expect this to be the lead input for Monday's Asia-Pacific open and Brent's first print of the week.

## Story 2: Bitcoin ETFs post $986.9m net inflow for the week, the strongest institutional signal yet behind August's rally

**Source:** KuCoin/AMBCrypto aggregation | https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/bitcoin-etf-sees-101m-net-inflow-ethereum-etf-records-48-2m-net-outflow and https://ambcrypto.com/bitcoin-etfs-see-770m-in-inflows-is-september-breaking-its-curse/

US spot Bitcoin ETFs took in $986.9 million in net inflows for the week ending 5 September, built on a run of daily prints that swung from a $236.5 million outflow on 1 September to a $730.8 million single-day inflow on 3 September — the strongest single day since 14 January. Bitcoin itself sits around $79,700-$80,000, still testing the same resistance level flagged yesterday.

The weekly aggregate is the more useful number than any single day: a week that opened with outflows and closed with nearly $1 billion net in is a stronger institutional-demand signal than yesterday's report's single-day read, and it's worth tracking whether that flow continues once equities reopen Monday. No direct Orwell product exposure, but worth a glance for anyone holding crypto-linked positions.

## Story 3: Fed rate debate hardens into a specific date — 11 September CPI now the explicit swing factor for the 16-17 September FOMC

**Source:** Axios | https://www.axios.com/2026/09/03/rates-waller-jobs-inflation-policy** and Morningstar | https://www.morningstar.com/economy/warsh-sounds-hawkish-will-there-be-september-rate-hike

Yesterday's report flagged the payrolls-driven flip toward hike-risk as needing "CPI/PPI next week" to resolve; the sourcing today narrows that to a specific date. Fed Governor Christopher Waller said at the Reuters NEXT Newsmaker event that with the labour market and economic activity "in good shape," he'd support holding rates steady — unless the 11 September inflation report comes in worse than expected. That's a direct contrast with Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole framing, which had markets pricing a hike as the base case going into the August payrolls surprise. Fed funds futures were pricing roughly 58% odds of a hike as of the most recent tracking, essentially the coin-flip this report described three days ago, now with a firmer trigger date attached.

Nothing resolves this before Thursday. The practical takeaway for anyone with Fed-linked options or futures exposure: 11 September is now the date that matters, not a vague "next week."
