## Story 1: TJX beats on Q2 but shares slide on store expansion plan and soft Q3 guide

**Source:** Bloomberg (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-19/tj-maxx-owner-s-plan-to-open-more-stores-hits-shares-after-beat and The Motley Fool | https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/19/why-tjx-stock-dropped-today/ and Investing.com | https://www.investing.com/news/earnings/tjx-earnings-beat-by-003-revenue-topped-estimates-4867204

TJX reported second-quarter EPS of $1.22 against a $1.19 estimate, on revenue of $15.2bn versus $15.19bn consensus, with same-store sales up 4%, ahead of expectations. Full-year guidance was raised: diluted EPS to $5.31–$5.36 (from $5.08–$5.15) and pretax margin to 12.3–12.4%. By any normal reading that is a clean beat-and-raise.

The stock fell as much as 5% anyway. Two things did it: CEO Ernie Herrman said TJX will accelerate new-store openings to 4% growth a year starting next year, which investors read as capital going into square footage at a moment when tariff-driven cost pressure is already squeezing margins elsewhere in retail; and third-quarter adjusted EPS guidance came in below Street expectations even as the full-year number went up, implying a choppier next 13 weeks than the annual figure suggests. The market is pricing execution risk on the expansion plan more than it is crediting the beat.

For context this resolves an open loop flagged in yesterday's report — TJX's headline beat was known, the share reaction was not. It landed negative, in the same direction as Target below, which is the more interesting pattern today: strong prints, weak stock reactions, across the board in retail.

## Story 2: Target beats and raises guidance, stock falls again

**Source:** StockTitan | https://www.stocktitan.net/news/TGT/target-corporation-reports-second-quarter-whi1a63iatnc.html and Investing.com | https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-target-beats-q2-2026-estimates-shares-slip-premarket-93CH-4867580 and CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/19/target-tgt-q2-2026-earnings.html

Target's Q2 was a genuine beat: EPS of $4.11 against a $2.33 estimate (note the scale of the beat is unusually wide — worth sanity-checking against Target's own release before quoting externally, but multiple outlets independently report the same figures), revenue of $26.54bn versus $26.13bn expected, comparable sales up 3.8% against a 2.4% consensus, comparable traffic up 3.6%, digital comps up 8.7% and same-day delivery up more than 25%. Target raised full-year net sales growth guidance to around 5%, a full point above its prior forecast.

Shares still fell — down 1.64% premarket to $149.99 from a $152.48 close, and down as much as 3.5% intraday. The stock had run hard into the print (near two-year highs per pre-earnings previews), so this reads as profit-taking and "sell the beat" positioning rather than any flaw in the numbers themselves. Combined with TJX, the pattern across US big-box and off-price retail today is that strong operating results are not buying share price support — the market wants to see whether the strength holds into the holiday season before re-rating these names higher.

## Story 3: Lowe's cuts full-year outlook but shares jump 5% on Pro strength

**Source:** StockTitan | https://www.stocktitan.net/news/LOW/lowe-s-reports-second-quarter-2026-sales-and-earnings-ru02tuuuuh3w.html and Yahoo Finance | https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/lowes-q2-2026-earnings-outlook-115020871.html and Benzinga | https://www.benzinga.com/markets/market-summary/26/08/61307306/crude-oil-moves-higher-lowes-shares-gain-after-q2-earnings

Lowe's reported Q2 sales of $26.0bn, up 8.3% year on year (helped by the Foundation Building Materials and Artisan Design Group acquisitions), with GAAP net income of $2.4bn ($4.27/share, flat year on year) and adjusted diluted EPS up 1.6% to $4.40. Comparable sales rose just 0.2%, with Pro and home-services growth and a 15.7% jump in online sales offsetting continued weakness in DIY spending.

The company trimmed guidance: full-year sales now guided to $92bn (the bottom of the prior $92–94bn range), comp sales guidance cut from "flat to up 2%" to flat, and adjusted EPS guidance narrowed to $12.25 from a $12.25–12.75 range — all below Street estimates. Despite the cut, shares gained roughly 5% on the day. The read here is the mirror image of TJX and Target: the market already had DIY softness priced in, so a guide-down that still shows Pro-channel resilience and M&A integration on track was received as a relief rather than a disappointment.

Taken together, the three prints say the same thing from different angles: US consumer spending is bifurcating sharply along income and category lines (value-seeking traffic and Pro/trade spend holding up, discretionary DIY and general merchandise softer), and share reactions this week are being driven more by forward guidance quality and capital allocation choices than by the headline beat or miss.

## Story 4: Alibaba's cloud unit accelerates to 26% growth as it reports Q1 FY27

**Source:** X/Bloomberg-sourced data via search aggregation (algotradingdesk) | https://x.com/algotradingdesk/status/2087897426443342241 and Pepperstone preview | https://pepperstone.com/en-gb/insights/market-analysis/equities/alibaba-q1-fy27-earnings-preview-wall-street-is-overwhelmingly-bullish-now-ai-and-cloud-have-to-prove-it

Alibaba reported fiscal Q1 2027 results before the US open today. Group revenue came in at RMB 247.7bn, up a modest 2% year on year, but Cloud Intelligence Group revenue reached RMB 43.2bn, up 26% year on year — a clear acceleration from prior quarters and the number the market was watching. Adjusted EBITA was RMB 26.5bn. Management reiterated that AI-related product revenue within the cloud unit is targeting its twelfth consecutive quarter of triple-digit growth, and that AI infrastructure demand is now the primary driver of cloud consumption growth, alongside continued margin pressure from AI capex and quick-commerce investment.

This lands directly on top of yesterday's Baidu story (Baidu down 13% on a soft print, reported in yesterday's brief) and sharpens the split emerging across Chinese AI stocks this week: Alibaba and Tencent both showing investors a credible AI-monetisation story (cloud growth, capex discipline improving), Baidu still struggling to show its AI pivot converting to revenue. Alibaba shares had already rallied 34% in Hong Kong this quarter into the print, so today's cloud acceleration gives the bulls the number they needed, though the flat 2% group revenue growth and ongoing margin compression from AI and quick-commerce spend are the counter-argument bears will point to. This also sits inside a broader Bloomberg theme running today — that China's AI ecosystem is "rapidly narrowing" the gap with Silicon Valley on price and market share, which is worth tracking against the SEO and comparison-network cost base if any of the underlying model or inference pricing feeds into vendor choices there.
