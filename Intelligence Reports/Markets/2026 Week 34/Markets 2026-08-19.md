## Story 1: FOMC minutes confirm historic hawkish split, rate-cut odds keep shrinking

**Source:** Newsquawk, CNBC, InteractiveCrypto | https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/preview-fomc-minutes-due-wednesday-19th-august-2026-at-1900bst1400edt | https://www.interactivecrypto.com/fomc-minutes-on-august-19-set-to-clarify-fed-s-rate-path-amid-mixed-economic-signals-aug-2026
**Related vault notes:** [[Priorities]], [[Ventures]]

The Fed released the minutes of its 28-29 July meeting at 2pm ET today. They confirm what the initial vote already signalled: a 9-3 hold with three regional presidents — Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari and Dallas's Lorie Logan — dissenting as a unified bloc in favour of a quarter-point hike. That is the first time since September 2016 three FOMC members have aligned on the same directional dissent.

The minutes add colour rather than surprise: committee members flagged persistent above-target inflation, a widening federal deficit, and elevated oil prices (Hormuz-risk driven) as reasons the hawks pushed for a hike rather than a hold. Market pricing for a rate move by year-end has been drifting in the Fed's hawkish direction since the July meeting — CME FedWatch now shows roughly a 64% probability of a hike by year-end, up sharply from the low-30s just after the meeting.

This closes yesterday's open loop directly: the "CPI due today" framing was slightly off (July CPI actually printed 12 August at a mild 3.4% y/y, 0.1% m/m), but today's real catalyst — the FOMC minutes — was always scheduled for 19 August and delivered the more consequential signal. Treasury yields across the curve continued climbing into the release: the 30-year hit a fresh 19-year high on Tuesday and the 10-year sits near 4.7%.

For a solo operator this is background noise rather than a lever to pull, but it matters for financing costs anywhere Orwell or the GPU compute venture would touch debt or variable-rate infrastructure spend — a hawkish Fed into September keeps the cost of capital for compute-heavy bets higher for longer.

## Story 2: Home Depot beats and reaffirms guidance — tariff pass-through question answered, for now

**Source:** StockTitan, Investing.com, Yahoo Finance | https://www.stocktitan.net/news/HD/the-home-depot-announces-second-quarter-fiscal-2026-results-w7n9jy4uowii.html | https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-home-depot-beats-q2-2026-estimates-shares-rise-93CH-4865627
**Related vault notes:** [[Priorities]]

Home Depot's Q2 fiscal 2026 numbers, reported yesterday and reacted to into today's session: net sales up 5.7% to $47.9bn (beating consensus by roughly $630m), comparable sales up 1.7%, adjusted EPS of $4.92 versus $4.73 expected (a 4% beat). Online comparable sales grew 11% for a fifth straight quarter of double-digit growth.

The stock's reaction was modest rather than euphoric — shares up roughly 0.75% in premarket to $340.40 off a $337.88 close. That muted move answers yesterday's open question about tariff pass-through: management held guidance for the full year and framed the beat as share-gain in a still-weak home-improvement market, while flagging that some of the margin benefit came from Section 338 tariff refunds that may not repeat. Investors are pricing that caveat in rather than chasing the headline beat.

This closes the open loop cleanly: no fresh interim-CEO stumble, no guidance cut, tariff question answered with "manageable but not repeatable."

## Story 4: Baidu craters 13% on Q2 miss; Alibaba and Tencent rally on the opposite AI story

**Source:** SEC EDGAR (Baidu 6-K), TradingKey, Yahoo Finance, Bloomberg Technology (via search) | https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001329099/000119312526326771/d173235dex991.htm | https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/baidu-sinks-13-soft-results-171813626.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]]

Baidu's Q2 revenue came in at RMB31.3bn, down 4% year-on-year and below the RMB31.95bn estimate; EPS of roughly ¥7.22 missed the ¥9.84 estimate by nearly 27%. The stock fell 13% to $90.39, taking it to its lowest level of the year and down more than 35% year-to-date — the fifth straight quarter of revenue decline, per Bloomberg Technology's framing ("Baidu Posts Fifth Straight Revenue Drop After AI Lag Widens").

The split inside the numbers is the real story: core AI-powered business revenue grew 25% to RMB12.5bn, and AI Cloud Infra revenue was up 50% with GPU Cloud specifically up 283%. But online marketing — still the bulk of the business — fell 19%, and that overwhelmed the cloud strength. Baidu is the clearest case study yet of a legacy platform where AI infrastructure growth is real but not yet large enough to offset core-business erosion.

The contrast lands the same day: Alibaba rose 6% and Tencent gained 4% as their in-house models (Kimi K3, Qwen3.8 Max) were assessed as matching top US systems, triggering a sector rotation within Chinese AI names — capital moving from the laggard (Baidu) to the perceived leaders (Alibaba, Tencent) rather than out of the China-AI trade altogether. Worth watching for the comparison-network thesis on how fast AI-cloud revenue lines can actually offset core declines — Baidu's numbers argue "not fast enough yet," a useful benchmark when sizing similar transitions elsewhere.

## Story 5: July housing starts and import prices both miss — printed yesterday, resolves the loop

**Source:** InvestingLive, US Census Bureau | https://investinglive.com/news/us-july-housing-starts-xxm-vs-1-350m-expected/ | https://www.census.gov/construction/nrc/pdf/newresconst_202407.pdf

Both prints landed on 18 August (after yesterday's report filed) rather than today, but neither has been covered yet, so this closes the loop now. Housing starts came in at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 1.239m, 12.4% below the revised June estimate of 1.415m and well short of the 1.35m consensus — a sharp miss. Import prices fell 0.4% month-on-month against an expected 0.1% rise.

Read together with Toll Brothers' results (Story 3) and Home Depot's tariff caveats (Story 2), this is a coherent picture: housing demand is softening faster than the builders' own guidance implied even after two rounds of downward revisions, while import price weakness suggests some of the tariff-driven inflation pressure the Fed is watching may be easing on the goods side even as services and shelter costs keep the hawks vocal. That tension is exactly what today's FOMC minutes (Story 1) describe internally.

## What this means for Orwell
