## Story 1: Trump pauses Iran strikes after 13 nights as Oman-brokered Hormuz talks advance — oil eases off its highs

**Source:** Axios (via Iran International) | https://www.iranintl.com/en/202607250090 ; SCMP | https://www.scmp.com/news/world/middle-east/article/3361857/us-pauses-nightly-iran-strikes-houthis-clash-saudis ; CBS News | https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-us-trump-strait-hormuz-middle-east/
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Comparison Network]], [[Geopolitics]]

After 13 consecutive nights of US strikes on Iranian military targets, CENTCOM reported no new strikes overnight Friday into Saturday — the first quiet night since the campaign against Iran's infrastructure began. Trump told reporters Friday the US and Iran "are talking" and that Tehran is "getting more and more serious," and two regional sources told CBS News that Omani mediators travelled to Tehran on Friday and made genuine progress on reopening the Strait of Hormuz and clarifying territorial-water protocols — the coincidence of that outreach and the strike pause is unlikely to be accidental. A Pentagon source described operations as "on hold," not ended, and it remains unclear whether this is a real diplomatic off-ramp or a pause before renewed escalation; the Houthis attacking a Saudi tanker in the Red Sea on the same day (unclaimed as of this report) is a reminder the picture is not clean. Oil responded: Brent eased to roughly $97-98/bbl and WTI below $88 on Friday, both down for the day even as they remain roughly 10% higher for the week overall.

This is the clearest signal all week that the "simultaneous Hormuz/Red Sea closure" scenario the Dubai property and mortgage book has been bracing for might have a near-term ceiling rather than an open-ended trajectory — though a single quiet night after two weeks of escalation is not confirmation of anything yet. Full military/diplomatic detail in [[Geopolitics]]; this entry is deliberately the economic-transmission read.

## Story 2: DeepSeek pauses second funding round after founder's leaked comments on China's AI lag go viral

**Source:** Bloomberg (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-25/deepseek-said-to-tell-backers-of-funding-pause-after-viral-posts ; Fortune | https://fortune.com/2026/07/25/deepseek-liang-wenfeng-backers-fundraising-pause-viral-posts-investors/
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[GitHub Library]]

DeepSeek told prospective backers it is suspending its second funding round — targeting at least 10 billion yuan at a pre-money valuation of 480 billion yuan — days after a leaked transcript of founder Liang Wenfeng's comments to first-round investors went viral. In the transcript, Liang reportedly acknowledged China remains 12-18 months behind US frontier labs and discussed the company's reliance on Nvidia chips, comments at odds with the triumphalist framing that's driven Chinese AI stock rallies this month. The first round closed in June at $7bn. DeepSeek may resume the process later and has separately begun IPO preparation, possibly filing as soon as this year.

Read-through: this is a rare instance of the China AI-catch-up narrative getting punctured by the lab itself rather than by external skepticism, and it lands the same week Kimi K3's release triggered a US policy response (Story 6). Worth flagging as a data point against any assumption that Chinese labs are close to parity — Liang's own words say otherwise.

## Story 3: Samsung signs $200bn, five-year chip supply MOU with Broadcom spanning memory, 2nm foundry and packaging

**Source:** Bloomberg (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-25/samsung-inks-200-billion-chip-supply-with-broadcom ; CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/25/samsung-electronics-wins-200-billion-broadcom-ai-chip-partnership.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[GitHub Library]]

Samsung Electronics and Broadcom signed a memorandum of understanding worth more than $200bn running through 2030, covering Samsung's 2-nanometer-and-below foundry process for Broadcom-designed chips, memory supply, and joint development of next-generation high-bandwidth memory and advanced packaging. It's an MOU rather than a binding contract, but the strategic logic is explicit: Broadcom is diversifying its manufacturing away from near-total reliance on TSMC, which still controls more than 90% of leading-edge chip production. The deal was announced alongside the South Korean president's US visit.

For the GPU compute venture, this is the clearest evidence yet that foundry concentration risk is now a boardroom-level concern for the AI infrastructure buildout's biggest customers, not just an analyst talking point — a second source of leading-edge capacity becoming viable is a structural, multi-year shift in how AI compute gets sourced.

## Story 4: CXMT prices $8.6bn IPO, Asia's largest of 2026, ahead of Monday's Shanghai STAR Market debut

**Source:** Bloomberg (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-14/cxmt-prices-ipo-in-marquee-moment-for-china-s-chip-revolution ; CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/24/cxmt-china-ipo-listing-chip-memory.html ; Nikkei Asia | https://asia.nikkei.com/business/tech/semiconductors/cxmt-to-raise-8.5bn-in-largest-chinese-chip-ipo
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]]

ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT), China's largest memory chipmaker and the world's fourth-largest DRAM maker (roughly 7.7-8% global share, behind Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron), is set to raise about 57.9 billion yuan ($8.55-8.6bn) at 8.66 yuan/share when it lists on Shanghai's STAR Market on Monday 27 July — the largest Chinese A-share semiconductor offering ever, ahead of SMIC's 2020 raise. Revenue grew sevenfold in H1 2026 on what a Nomura analyst called structurally under-supplied memory demand from the AI buildout. CNBC's pre-listing coverage flags cash-drain fears for the broader market given the IPO's size, though the consensus view is that hyperscaler capex is deep enough to absorb the liquidity pull.

This is the clearest read yet on how far China's chip self-sufficiency push has come specifically in memory — a sector the DeepSeek story (Story 2) suggests China is genuinely behind on for logic/compute, but where CXMT's scale-up looks real. Watch Monday's listing performance as the actual market verdict.

## Story 5: Anthropic launches Claude Opus 5 — a cheaper, effort-togglable model positioned as the default for everyday office work

**Source:** Bloomberg (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/anthropic-unveils-more-cost-efficient-model-for-everyday-tasks ; Fortune | https://fortune.com/2026/07/24/anthropic-debuts-claude-opus-5-with-feature-that-lets-users-toggle-between-cost-and-capability/ ; VentureBeat | https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/anthropic-launches-claude-opus-5-a-cheaper-ai-model-for-coding-agents-and-enterprise-workflows
**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]], [[GitHub Library]]

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5 on Friday, priced identically to the prior Opus 4.8 ($5/million input tokens, $25/million output) but pitched as approaching Fable 5-level capability on many benchmarks at half the effective cost, with a new low/medium/high effort toggle letting users trade capability for cost per task. On Zapier's AutomationBench — which measures whether a model can complete a business task start-to-finish — Opus 5's pass rate runs roughly 1.5x the next-best model at the same cost-per-task. Anthropic is explicitly positioning it as the default model for day-to-day enterprise and knowledge-work use, not a frontier flagship.

Directly relevant to Orwell's economics: a model this competitive at this price point on business-task completion is exactly the input variable that determines how cheap a humanless department can get. Worth a short evaluation note on whether Opus 5's effort-toggle changes any current agent cost assumptions in [[Agent Org]].

## Story 6: Nvidia and Microsoft lead a coalition urging Washington against broad restrictions on open-weight AI models, after Kimi K3 rattles the field

**Source:** Bloomberg (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/nvidia-microsoft-lead-call-for-open-weight-ai-models-after-kimi ; Fox Business | https://www.foxbusiness.com/technology/nvidia-microsoft-urge-us-avoid-broad-restrictions-open-ai-models
**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]]

A coalition of tech companies led by Nvidia and Microsoft published a joint letter Friday arguing that open-weight AI models strengthen US competitiveness and cybersecurity and should not face broad policy restrictions — a direct response to Moonshot AI's Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter open-weight model that beat Claude Fable 5 on the Frontend Code Arena benchmark and rattled US policymakers into discussing a crackdown on Chinese open-weight releases. The letter's core argument: restricting open models would cede ground to Chinese labs rather than protect US leadership, since the compute and talent gap (see Story 2) is real but the weights themselves are freely redistributable regardless of US policy.

This is a genuine policy fork worth tracking — if Washington moves toward restricting open-weight model access or distribution, it would directly affect any Orwell tooling built on open-weight infrastructure rather than proprietary API access.

## Story 7: Midjourney's Co-Star acquisition surfaces — bootstrapped image-gen lab quietly bought a 4.3m-user astrology app to build consumer apps

**Source:** TechCrunch | https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/24/midjourney-acquired-the-astrology-app-co-star/ ; Bloomberg (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-24/ai-startup-midjourney-buys-astrology-app-co-star-and-is-building-its-own-apps
**Related vault notes:** [[GitHub Library]]

News broke Friday that bootstrapped, profitable Midjourney quietly acquired Co-Star — the blunt-horoscope social astrology app with 4.3m monthly active users — in a deal that closed this spring but wasn't disclosed until now. Co-Star's 24 employees, including founder Banu Guler (now Midjourney's chief design officer), bring consumer app-development experience Midjourney lacks, having operated almost entirely through Discord since launch. It's part of a broader expansion teased last month: Midjourney's building its first standalone image-generation app.

Minor story but a useful data point on AI-lab consolidation logic: a profitable, well-capitalised lab buying distribution and consumer-product expertise rather than raising a round to build it — a different playbook from the mega-round-fuelled labs dominating this week's other funding headlines.
