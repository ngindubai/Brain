## Editor's note

US and Asian equity markets don't trade on a Saturday, so today's report leans on the three things that do move on a weekend: oil (driven by the Hormuz/Red Sea chokepoint, see [[Comparison Network]] and [[Ventures]]), crypto (trades continuously), and Friday-evening corporate news that lands after the close and hasn't been covered yet. The one genuine catalyst is the physical one: Strait of Hormuz transit has now collapsed to roughly 6-15 ships a day against a pre-war baseline of 88, which is a materially worse number than anything reported this week and the reason oil is not following its usual weekend quiet. Against that, Verizon's $1bn dark-fibre deal with Google and a fresh Crunchbase tally of AI mega-rounds are the week's clearest evidence that capital markets are pricing AI infrastructure demand as structural, not cyclical, even as the physical energy system it depends on gets more fragile by the day. Next week's Big Tech earnings (Microsoft and Meta on Wednesday, Apple Thursday) will be the next real test of that thesis.

## Story 1: Hormuz transit collapses to 17% of normal as the chokepoint stops being a risk premium and starts being an actual supply cut

**Source:** War on the Rocks (via search) | https://warontherocks.com/global-risks-heating-up/ ; NPR | https://www.npr.org/2026/07/24/g-s1-135417/us-iran-war-updates ; TradingEconomics | https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/brent-crude-oil
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Comparison Network]]

The number that matters today: only six ships transited the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday, down from 15 the day before, against a pre-war baseline of roughly 88 vessels a day — commercial transit is running at under a fifth of normal. This is a step change from earlier in the week, when the story was "risk premium," and it lands the same week CENTCOM confirmed a 13th consecutive night of strikes on Iranian military targets (coastal surveillance, drone storage, command centres) and the Houthis reopened a second front against Saudi tankers in the Red Sea. Brent eased to $98.38 on Friday (down 2.29% on the day) and WTI to $87.88 (down 4.67%), giving back some of Thursday's spike, but the pullback reads as profit-taking on an already-extreme move rather than any actual de-risking — the underlying transit numbers are worse, not better.

For the GPU compute venture this is background macro noise rather than a direct hit, but for the Dubai property and mortgage book this is the scenario that has been flagged for weeks arriving as the base case: two simultaneous Gulf chokepoint disruptions, not a tail risk. See [[Geopolitics]] for the full military and diplomatic picture — this entry is deliberately the economic-transmission read only, to avoid re-telling the war's blow-by-blow twice in one day's briefs.

## Story 2: Verizon signs $1bn-plus dark-fibre deal with Google, stock jumps 4%, more AI infrastructure deals promised by year-end

**Source:** Yahoo Finance | https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/verizon-signs-1-billion-dark-180950648.html ; The Motley Fool | https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/07/24/verizon-just-signed-a-more-than-1-billion-dark-fib/
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[GitHub Library]]

Verizon and Google signed a dark-fibre agreement worth more than $1bn, announced Friday on Verizon's post-earnings call, under which Verizon supplies the physical fibre connectivity linking Google's data centres. CEO Dan Schulman described it as "several of our dark fibre routes for well in excess of a billion dollars" and said further multi-billion-dollar infrastructure deals would be announced by year-end. Verizon shares climbed roughly 4% on the news.

This is a useful data point for the GPU compute venture from an adjacent angle: telecom carriers are now positioning dark fibre and connectivity — not just power and chips — as a monetisable layer of the AI buildout. It corroborates the broader thesis that AI infrastructure demand is deep enough to pull in capacity from vendors with no direct compute exposure, similar to the read-through from Intel's foundry beat earlier this week.

## Story 3: Bitcoin retreats toward $65k as oil-driven inflation expectations push Treasury yields higher

**Source:** Yahoo Finance | https://finance.yahoo.com/personal-finance/investing/article/bitcoin-and-ethereum-prices-today-friday-july-24-2026-crypto-prices-retreat-on-higher-us-treasury-yields-152200068.html ; Fortune | https://fortune.com/article/current-price-of-bitcoin-for-july-24-2026/
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]]

Bitcoin opened Friday at $65,047.87, down 1.6% from Thursday, and was trading around $65,590 through the day — crypto is one of the few assets that actually trades through the weekend, so it's the cleanest read on risk appetite today. The proximate driver is the same one hitting equities: higher oil prices are lifting inflation expectations, pushing US Treasury yields up, and pulling institutional money out of risk assets generally. This is the same mechanism flagged in yesterday's report around the elevated VIX (still around 18.8-19.5 today) — a genuinely wide, oil-driven risk-off mood rather than anything crypto-specific.

Read-through for Orwell: nothing directly actionable, but it confirms the oil/Hormuz story is now the dominant cross-asset macro driver this week, ahead of both the FOMC meeting (28-29 July) and next week's Big Tech earnings.

## Story 4: AI mega-rounds keep coming — Atoms leads a $1.7bn physical-AI raise in a "varied week" for large deals

**Source:** Crunchbase News | https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/biggest-funding-rounds-physical-ai-fintech-defense-atoms/
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[GitHub Library]]

Crunchbase's weekly biggest-rounds tally, published Friday, put physical-AI startup Atoms in the lead spot with a $1.7bn raise led by Andreessen Horowitz. Other names in the same round-up: Meshy AI ($400m), battery technology firm Sila ($300m), and AI-inference company Etched ($300m) — the same Etched round flagged in yesterday's report as one of several AI-adjacent rounds this week, now confirmed as part of the formal weekly tally rather than a one-off. Separately, General Catalyst overtook Y Combinator in Q2 2026 fintech deal count, backing 12 deals of $5m-plus — a smaller but relevant signal that AI-adjacent fintech is pulling venture attention away from pure consumer plays.

None of this is a single today-dated catalyst on its own; it's confirmation that the funding backdrop for anything Orwell might raise against or partner around in AI infrastructure and tooling remains extremely permissive, even in a week dominated by war-driven macro volatility.

## What this means for Orwell

Nothing here demands a decision today, but the picture is sharpening rather than staying static. The Hormuz transit collapse (17% of normal) is the clearest sign yet that the "simultaneous Hormuz/Red Sea closure" scenario isn't a tail risk anymore — it's this week's reality, and it's the direct driver of both the oil price and the risk-off mood hitting crypto and (when markets reopen Monday) equities. Against that, the AI-infrastructure capital cycle keeps running on its own track: Verizon-Google and the Crunchbase funding tally both say investors are still treating AI buildout as a multi-decade structural trend, unbothered by this week's war-driven volatility. The one thing worth actually watching into next week is whether that decoupling holds once Microsoft, Meta, Amazon and Apple report Wednesday and Thursday — a genuinely bad Hormuz week colliding with nervous AI-capex commentary from the Big Four would be the first point where the two stories properly collide.
