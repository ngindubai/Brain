## Story 1: Bessent calls US-China talks "successful", both sides agree to launch an AI dialogue

**Source:** Bloomberg | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-21/bessent-hails-very-successful-china-talks-on-ai-threats-trade

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer spent Sunday in hours of talks with Vice Premier He Lifeng at JPMorgan's Manhattan headquarters, and the readout landed today: both sides agreed to set up a formal dialogue aimed at reaching "common understanding on goals and threats" in AI, and Greer said the US-China Board of Trade has now been "operationalised" to help run ongoing trade negotiations. China separately voiced "serious concern" over new US export curbs during the same talks, per the South China Morning Post. This is the direct precursor to Trump and Xi's 24 September summit, and it's the first concrete structural step (a standing AI dialogue) to come out of the diplomatic track this month, rather than just rhetoric. For a solo operator watching from Dubai, the AI-dialogue detail matters more than the trade language: whatever guardrails come out of that channel will eventually shape how Chinese open-weight models (Qwen, Z.AI, MiniMax, all live threads in this report) can be built on, distilled from, or sold into outside China.

## Story 3: Fed futures erase rate-cut odds entirely, price a coin-flip on another hike

**Source:** CME FedWatch (via web search) | https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/interest-rates/cme-fedwatch-tool.html

CME FedWatch now shows a 0% probability of a rate cut at the 28 October FOMC meeting, down from roughly 54% odds of a cut priced as recently as 19 August, and a 57.6% probability of a further 25bp hike versus 42.4% for holding. That's a materially sharper read than the "~42% odds of two hikes" figure carried in yesterday's report; the market has now essentially closed the door on any near-term easing and is leaning toward tightening again. This follows the unanimous 25bp hike to 3.75-4.00% on 16 September and continues to run directly against Trump's public push for Chair Warsh to cut toward "1% or less." For the GPU compute futures venture and anything leveraged, a market pricing further hikes rather than cuts is a headwind on financing costs precisely as CME's own compute-futures contracts prepare to launch on 5 October.

## Story 4: Oil eases back under $100 as Saudi's Oman workaround takes hold

**Source:** AGBI / Yahoo Finance (via web search) | https://www.agbi.com/oil-and-gas/2026/09/oil-supply-fears-ease-on-saudi-workaround-via-oman/

WTI has slipped to around $98-99 a barrel and Brent to roughly $102-103, down from Thursday's $101.91/$104.82 prints, as satellite tracking shows Saudi Arabia moving up to 2.8 million barrels a day through ship-to-ship transfers off Oman's Sohar port, well beyond the Strait of Hormuz chokepoint, versus just 700,000 bpd in August. That's a real logistics fix, not just a rhetorical reassurance, and it's the first clear evidence the East-West pipeline outage (since 10-11 September) isn't going to translate into the scale of shortfall the market feared two weeks ago. The move doesn't resolve the underlying outage; the pipeline itself is still down and repair estimates remain weeks to months. But the reroute is working well enough that oil is now trading meaningfully off its post-attack highs heading into the week, which take some of the immediate inflation-pass-through pressure off ahead of the Fed's next meeting.

## Story 5: Nvidia, Amazon and Alphabet pull the Nasdaq higher while Apple and Microsoft lag

**Source:** StrongBuyAnalytics market outlook / Trading Economics (via web search) | https://strongbuyanalytics.com/stock-market-outlook

Today's session shows a clear split inside big tech rather than a uniform rally: Nvidia, Amazon and Alphabet are leading Nasdaq futures and high-beta growth names (QQQ, SMH, ARKK) higher, alongside gains in Bitcoin and Ethereum, while Apple, Microsoft, Meta and Tesla are trading weaker and defensive sectors (staples, utilities, communications) lag. The VIX is lower on the day. This is dispersion, not conviction; the "AI winners" (chips, hyperscaler cloud/AI capex names) are being bid while the more consumer-facing or diversified mega-caps sit out the move. It's consistent with a market that's still fundamentally an AI-infrastructure trade rather than a broad tech rally, and it lines up with the Fed story above: investors are willing to keep funding the AI buildout specifically even as the broader rate outlook turns less friendly.
