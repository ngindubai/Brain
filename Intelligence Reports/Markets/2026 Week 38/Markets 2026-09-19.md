## Story 1: Nvidia-backed Nscale files publicly for a US IPO seeking up to $3bn

**Source:** Bloomberg (via CNBC/Yahoo Finance syndication) | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/18/nscale-ai-cloud-provider-ipo-nscl.html | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/nvidia-backed-data-center-firm-nscale-files-publicly-for-us-ipo

Nscale, a London-based AI data-center and cloud operator backed by Nvidia, filed publicly with the SEC on Friday to list on the NYSE under the ticker NSCL, seeking to raise as much as $3bn. Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley are leading the offering. Nscale builds and operates GPU-dense data centers specifically for AI compute rental — the same commercial category the GPU compute venture research has been tracking via the CME/Silicon Data futures thread. This is the first pure-play AI-infrastructure operator of this scale to go through a full public filing rather than a private mega-round, which means its S-1 will be the first genuinely public disclosure of unit economics for GPU-rental infrastructure: utilisation rates, contract terms, capex-to-revenue ratios. That's a direct research input once the filing's financial statements are parseable — worth pulling the S-1 itself once posted on EDGAR rather than relying on press coverage.

## Story 2: Anthropic's annualised revenue reported to top $100bn in 2026, IPO could land as soon as November

**Source:** Bloomberg (via Axios) | https://www.axios.com/2026/09/18/anthropic-100-billion-revenue | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/anthropic-s-annualized-revenue-to-top-100-billion-in-2026-nyt

The New York Times reported Friday that Anthropic's annualised revenue run rate will exceed $100bn in 2026, a claim that — if it holds — represents roughly a 50% jump from the $65bn run rate confirmed at the end of July and a more than tenfold increase from the ~$9bn run rate at the end of 2025. The same reporting has Anthropic's public trading debut potentially landing as soon as November, materially sooner than the "later this year" framing tracked in prior briefs. This is the single clearest data point yet that frontier-lab revenue growth hasn't slowed even as the sector absorbs a wave of capex-financing stress elsewhere (see Story 3). Treat the $100bn figure as a projection sourced to NYT via Anthropic, not an audited number — but the trajectory (9bn to 65bn to 100bn+ inside twelve months) is consistent enough across separate reports that the scale, if not the precise figure, looks solid.

## Story 3: Meta-tied data center's debut junk bond draws 4x demand — first sign of real investor appetite for AI-infrastructure high-yield debt

**Source:** Bloomberg | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/meta-tied-data-center-draws-blowout-demand-for-debut-junk-bond

CleanSpark Inc.'s roughly $2.28bn five-year note sale — proceeds fund a 175-megawatt AI data-center campus in Sandersville, Georgia, fully leased to a Meta subsidiary under a $6.6bn, 20-year deal — attracted around $10bn in orders, more than four times the deal size. The notes priced at 98.5 cents on the dollar to yield 8.25%, roughly 1.75 points above the average for BB-rated debt. This is the first US high-yield bond tied specifically to a Meta data-center project, and the blowout demand is a genuine signal: money managers are willing to take BB-grade credit risk on AI infrastructure at a real yield premium rather than treating it as speculative overreach. Read alongside BlackRock's earlier $12.3bn investment-grade bond sale for a different Meta data center (24 July), the financing stack for hyperscaler AI infrastructure is now spanning the full credit spectrum from investment-grade to junk — a sign the market believes these facilities' 20-year lease cashflows are bankable even before the AI capex thesis is fully proven out.

## Story 4: Bank of Japan's 1.25% hike confirmed — yen weakens instead of rallying, a genuinely contrarian market reaction

**Source:** CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/18/japan-raises-rates-30-year-high-yen-jgb.html | Bloomberg | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/boj-split-vote-sends-bearish-signal-on-yen-strategists-say

Friday's Bank of Japan decision — flagged as pending in yesterday's brief — is now confirmed: the BoJ hiked its policy rate 25bp to 1.25%, the highest since 1995, in a 7-2 split vote. The result defied the standard playbook: the yen weakened past 157 against the dollar rather than strengthening, while the Nikkei 225 gained 1.5%. The mechanism is the split vote and Governor Ueda's cautious press-conference tone — two dissents plus no explicit commitment to further tightening told markets this was a one-off pre-emptive move rather than the start of a sustained hiking cycle, which is bearish for the currency even as the policy rate itself rises. Strategists also flagged that the BoJ's two most hawkish board members are due to step down next summer, which markets are already reading as a structurally more dovish board ahead. This closes the loop from yesterday's brief; the practical read is that the yen-funded carry trade this year's global risk-asset positioning has leaned on is not obviously under threat from today's hike, contrary to the initial assumption.

## Story 5: Disney names its first-ever corporate CTO — poaches Character.AI's CEO

**Source:** Bloomberg (via Variety, americanbazaaronline) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/disney-names-ai-executive-as-first-ever-chief-technology-officer | https://variety.com/2026/biz/news/disney-cto-karandeep-anand-character-ai-1236866528/

Disney named Karandeep Anand — until now CEO of Character.AI, the chatbot company Disney had separately threatened with legal action over unauthorised use of its characters — to the newly created, company-wide role of Chief Technology Officer, effective 2 October, reporting directly to CEO Josh D'Amaro. Anand previously built Azure at Microsoft over 15 years and was president/chief product officer at Brex. Disney has had business-unit CTOs before but never a single corporate-wide technology chief; the mandate covers enterprise tech, infrastructure, data, AI platforms, product and engineering. The hire-while-threatening-to-sue dynamic is the notable detail: it reads as Disney deciding the fastest way to control AI-generated-character risk to its IP is to own the capability internally rather than fight it in court indefinitely. Worth watching as a template for how legacy IP-heavy companies are choosing to resolve the AI-character licensing problem — build in-house rather than litigate to a settlement.

## Story 6: Crunchbase's weekly funding roundup — AI infrastructure leads a sparser week, Temporal Technologies' $550m the biggest single round

**Source:** Crunchbase News | https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/biggest-funding-rounds-ai-space-fintech-temporal/

Crunchbase's weekly biggest-rounds roundup, published today, confirms the week of 12-18 September's largest US round was a $550m Series E for Temporal Technologies (Bellevue, WA), an open-source platform for building and operating long-running AI agents, at a $12.55bn valuation, backed by Lightspeed, Wellington Management, Goldman Sachs Alternatives and Tiger Global. Second was Impulse Space's $308m round (space vehicles, not AI). Other AI-adjacent rounds above $50m this week: Ridgeline ($250m, AI-driven investment management), Cornelis Networks ($205m, AI-workload networking infrastructure), Factory ($200m, AI software-development tools), and Profound ($180m, AI marketing tech). Separately confirmed: Crusoe's previously-reported multibillion-dollar AI-infrastructure raise (co-led by Atreides, Valor Equity, Mubadala Capital) was officially announced this week. No single round this week matches the multi-billion-dollar mega-rounds of recent months — a genuinely sparser week for headline-grabbing AI funding, with the money instead spreading across infrastructure, agent tooling and vertical AI applications.
