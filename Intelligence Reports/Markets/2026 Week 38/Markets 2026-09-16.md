## Story 1: The Fed hikes into an oil shock, not out of one
**Source:** FedRateCalc | https://fedratecalc.com/fed-rate-decision/ and Kiplinger | https://www.kiplinger.com/investing/live/fed-meeting-updates-and-commentary-september-2026
The FOMC's two-day meeting concludes today with a rate decision at 2pm ET (18:00 UTC) and a press conference from Chair Kevin Warsh at 2:30pm ET. Markets are pricing roughly a 92% probability of a 25 basis point hike, taking the federal funds target range from 3.50-3.75% to 3.75-4.00%. That would be the Fed's first hike since 2023, and it is happening for an unusual reason: a steady labour market plus energy-driven inflation, not an overheating economy in the classic sense. The proximate trigger cited across sources is Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole speech, but the real driver sitting underneath it is the Hormuz-linked oil spike (see Story 3). A central bank raising rates because a shipping chokepoint is jamming crude supply is a different animal to one raising rates because growth is too hot, and it changes the read-through for risk assets: this is a inflation-defence hike, not a confidence hike. The dot plot and Warsh's guidance on October/December matter more than the 25bp itself, since the hike is almost fully priced. Watch specifically whether Warsh frames this as a one-and-done move against an oil shock, or opens the door to a longer tightening cycle — the two scenarios produce very different reactions in equities, gold and the dollar this afternoon.

**Source:** Cboe / market data via search, Saxo Bank Options Brief | https://www.home.saxo/en-ch/content/articles/options/vol-collapses-skew-climbs---options-brief---14092026
Heading into decision day, the VIX sat around 17.7, with options volume running well above average and calls outnumbering puts roughly 3:1 — a market positioning for upside relief as much as downside shock, consistent with a hike being fully priced and Warsh's tone being the real swing factor. Volatility futures show the term structure still stressed: the near-dated (18 September) VIX future was quoted at a premium to spot that widened from 0.19 points to 0.82 points session-on-session, meaning traders are paying up for protection through the announcement and immediately after, not for the months beyond. This is a textbook "event risk" curve: elevated at the front, calmer further out. It is directly relevant to the mechanics behind the CME/Silicon Data GPU compute futures launching 5 October (see Open Loops) — that market will trade the same way, spiking around AI capex data points and hyperscaler earnings, and today's Fed-day options behaviour is a decent template for what compute futures volatility will look like around news events once that market exists.

## Story 3: Hormuz data confirms the official "two-thirds restored" claim does not hold up
**Source:** IMF PortWatch and Strait of Hormuz shipping trackers | https://portwatch.imf.org and https://straits.live/
Fresh tracker data as of today shows the strait still effectively closed to normal commercial traffic. The most recent granular count (6 September) recorded just 6 transits against an 85-per-day pre-crisis baseline, and as of 05:56 UTC this morning 401 vessels were holding position away from berth rather than transiting. War-risk insurance for tankers is now pricing at 40 times pre-crisis levels, and six protection and indemnity clubs have withdrawn cover altogether, which is a bigger practical bottleneck than the strait's physical width — insurers, not the US Navy, are choking the traffic. This directly contradicts the claim from Energy Secretary Chris Wright and President Trump earlier this week that roughly two-thirds of normal Hormuz traffic has been restored. The gap between the political claim and the shipping-tracker reality is now wider, not narrower, and it matters because oil pricing today is being set on the assumption that the disruption is easing. If it is not easing, Brent and WTI have more room to run regardless of what the Fed does this afternoon.

## Story 4: Chip stocks tread water into decision day, but Micron is the odd one out
**Source:** Yahoo Finance and various analyst notes via search
Nvidia was soft in early trade, hovering near $213, broadly holding Tuesday's level rather than extending Monday's slide. That slide traced back to Anthropic's Dario Amodei and other tech figures publicly calling for a slowdown in frontier AI capability development, which spooked chip names on fears of throttled demand — a story that has now been sitting for two sessions with no fresh catalyst today. Micron is the clear divergence: analysts have been sharply raising targets on the back of what's described as a memory supercycle, with UBS lifting its target from $535 to $1,625 and other desks following with similarly aggressive upgrades, consensus now sitting at "Strong Buy." The bull case rests on $18 billion in customer cash deposits and roughly $100 billion in contracted minimum revenue already locked in — real, structural tightness in memory supply rather than sentiment. The split matters: it says the market is separating "AI compute demand might slow" (bad for logic/GPU names on safety-slowdown fears) from "AI memory is structurally short regardless" (good for Micron specifically). Micron reports fiscal results 30 September, which is the next real test of that thesis.

## Story 5: The dollar and Treasury yields are pricing the hike as done — the risk is what comes after
**Source:** TradingEconomics | https://tradingeconomics.com/united-states/currency
The dollar index is trading near 99.7, its highest level in about a month, as the market fully leans into a 25bp move. That sits alongside the 10-year Treasury yield holding near the multi-decade highs it touched this week (above 5% intraday Monday, closing near 4.987% Tuesday, a level not seen since 2007). A dollar rallying into a hike that's already 92% priced is not unusual, but it leaves very little room for a "buy the rumour, sell the fact" reversal unless Warsh is notably more hawkish than expected on future meetings. The more interesting risk sits in credit, not FX: hyperscaler bond issuance (Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle) has already hit roughly $132 billion year-to-date through the end of July, cover ratios on those deals have fallen from near 5x in February to below 2x in July, and spreads on 2-4 year paper have widened from 30bp to 40bp. That was all happening before this week's yield spike. A confirmed hike today, on top of an oil-driven inflation scare, tightens financial conditions on exactly the balance sheets funding the AI data-centre build-out — this is the channel through which today's Fed decision reaches the AI capex story most directly, more so than through equity multiples.
