## Story 1: Jensen Huang tells CBS Nvidia will "go as fast as we can"

**Source:** CBS News | https://www.cbsnews.com/news/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-ai-development-fast-as-we-can/

Huang's interview with CBS's Jo Ling Kent airs today on *Sunday Morning*. Asked directly whether AI development needs to slow down, he said "we should go as fast as we can irrespective of anybody else," while adding Nvidia would "never ever ship products before they're ready" or "deliver products that are unsafe." The framing matters because it's a direct rebuttal, timed to land in the same week as Dario Amodei's "We Must Pace the Frontier" essay (12 September) and California Governor Newsom's executive order pushing a frontier-model "kill switch" (18 September); both still live threads from earlier in the week. Huang is the industry's most exposed voice on capex: Nvidia sits above a $5tn valuation and every dollar of that is a bet that the buildout keeps accelerating, not pacing itself. His comments give no new numbers on chip sales or guidance, but the positioning is the story; Nvidia is now publicly, deliberately on the opposite side of the safety-slowdown coalition from Anthropic, which puts the two biggest AI names in direct rhetorical conflict rather than just competing on product.

## Story 2: Transwarp Technology ("StarRing") set to debut on HKEX tomorrow, Stock Connect eligible day one

**Source:** Futunn News (via web search) | https://news.futunn.com/en/post/79506671/starring-technology-to-list-on-hkex-tomorrow-the-chinese-databricks

Transwarp Technology (06727.HK / 688031.SH), pitched as a Chinese answer to Databricks, lists on the HKEX Main Board on 21 September and will be eligible for Stock Connect from its first day of trading, unusually fast inclusion that opens it to mainland capital immediately rather than after the usual waiting period. It's an enterprise AI-infrastructure and GPU-native database software provider, positioned in the same commercialisation-stage bracket Databricks and Snowflake have already grown out of. The read here: Hong Kong's AI-infrastructure IPO pipeline hasn't slowed despite the broader Chinese AI stock selloff (see Story 4); issuers are still finding a window, and Stock Connect eligibility on debut signals HKEX is actively smoothing the path for AI-infrastructure names specifically. Worth tracking whether the debut trades up or down tomorrow as a read on investor appetite for Chinese AI-infra plays right now, versus the beaten-down foundation-model names.

## Story 4: Chinese AI stocks split; Alibaba up, Z.AI and MiniMax still down

**Source:** Multiple (CNBC, Bloomberg, Investing.com via web search)

Alibaba is reportedly up around 3% in a broader Chinese internet-stock rally, with the company planning a revenue-share model for large commercial users of its forthcoming Qwen3.8-Max open-weight model (2.4tn parameters, second-largest open-weight model in China) rather than pure cloud-usage billing, a monetisation pivot worth watching if it works, since it's a different model to how US labs charge for open-weight releases. No new data point today changes the Z.AI/MiniMax trajectory; flagging the divergence rather than re-telling the selloff.

## Story 5: Oil holds elevated on East-West pipeline outage, Hormuz reroute continuing

**Source:** CNBC (via web search) | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/17/oil-prices-today-wti-brent-hormuz-iran-war.html

Brent last printed around $104.82 and WTI around $101.91 at Thursday's (17 September) close, as Saudi Arabia continues rerouting crude through the Strait of Hormuz to offset the 11 September shutdown of the East-West pipeline (previously carrying ~39% of the kingdom's crude exports around Hormuz). US officials call the outage "brief," independent analysts say weeks to months; consensus repair estimate is a partial restart at 40–60% capacity. Oil markets don't print over the weekend, so there's no new today figure, but this is the backdrop level heading into Monday's reopen; still comfortably above the $100 mark that's been the story all month, and the risk stays skewed toward a bigger disruption if repairs slip past September or if Iran-aligned proxies escalate. No fresh news today; carrying the Thursday print forward as the number that matters for Monday's open.
