## Editor's note

Today is the day the Fed's two-day FOMC meeting formally opens, and it opens into the worst chip-stock session in months: Monday's full close showed the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down 5.9-6%, Intel off as much as 7%, AMD down 6%, and the 10-year Treasury yield briefly touching 5% for the first time since October 2023. None of that was visible in yesterday's report, which only had pre-market futures. The genuinely new read today is the shape of the unwind: hyperscalers (Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta) rose while the chipmakers that sell to them fell, a split analysts are calling a rational repricing of who actually carries AI capex risk, not a verdict on AI itself. Layer that onto oil still pinned near $107/103 on Hormuz and the Saudi pipeline shutdown, and gold pulling back as yields climb, and Wednesday's 2pm ET rate decision is now carrying more macro weight than any Fed meeting this year.

## Story 1: FOMC's two-day meeting opens with a 10-year yield at a five-year high and chip stocks bleeding into decision week

**Source:** Seoul Economic Daily | https://en.sedaily.com/international/2026/09/15/us-10-year-treasury-yield-tops-5-percent-for-first-time | CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/14/10-year-us-treasury-is-closing-in-on-5percent.html | FedRateCalc | https://fedratecalc.com/fed-rate-decision/

The Fed's two-day meeting began today in Washington, with the rate decision due tomorrow at 2pm ET and Chair Warsh's press conference at 2:30pm. Markets walk in with the 10-year Treasury yield having briefly crossed 5% on Monday (peaking at 5.014%, last at 4.987%) for the first time since October 2023 and only the second time since 2007 — a level driven by energy-linked inflation rather than growth optimism, which is the less comfortable kind of yield spike. CME-tracked odds of a 25bp hike to 3.75-4.00% sit at 83-93% depending on the source, up sharply after Chair Warsh's hawkish 28 August Jackson Hole speech and firm August jobs data, even as headline CPI has actually eased for two months running to 3.4% in July. That tension — a Fed hiking into softening headline inflation because energy and tariff pass-through keep pushing the underlying number up — is the real story behind tomorrow's decision, more than the AI-stock noise dominating headlines.

## Story 2: Hyperscalers rise, chipmakers fall — Monday's AI-pacing selloff reveals who actually carries the risk

**Source:** Fortune | https://fortune.com/2026/09/14/ai-slowdown-stocks-nvidia-meta/ | Yahoo Finance | https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/chip-stocks-tumble-ai-pacing-132334555.html

Yesterday's report flagged Trump's rejection of the Amodei/Altman/Musk "pace the frontier" call and premarket futures moves; Monday's actual close adds a materially different and more useful data point. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 5.9-6% on the day — Intel down 7% to $95.96, AMD down 6% to $486.80, Nvidia down a comparatively modest 3% to $212.50, Micron and Broadcom both off more than 4% — while Alphabet rose 2%, Microsoft gained 1.6% and Meta added roughly 1.4%. Amazon fell 1.6% but still outperformed the chip names. Analyst Gil Luria's read, echoed across coverage: if AI development genuinely slows, hyperscalers can simply pause new data-centre construction and monetise what they've already built, while chipmakers depend on sustained new capex to keep growing. The unusual pattern within chips — Intel (least AI-exposed) fell hardest, Nvidia (most AI-exposed) fell least — points to a positioning unwind and profit-taking on AMD's large year-to-date gains rather than genuine demand destruction. Separately, Altman publicly backed Amodei's slowdown call Monday morning, a notable alignment given OpenAI's own commercial incentives to keep scaling.

## Story 3: Oil stays bid near $107/103 as US "total control" claims collide with shipping data

**Source:** Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/14/us-says-its-clearing-hormuz-traffic-why-are-oil-futures-beyond-100

Brent closed Monday at $107.82 (+$3.21) and WTI at $103.22 (+$3.17), still climbing on the combination of Sunday's tanker strike near Qeshm and Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline shutdown (see today's [[Geopolitics]] report for the full diplomatic picture). The genuinely new element today is the gap between US messaging and the data: Energy Secretary Chris Wright claimed 10 million barrels a day are now passing through Hormuz on average, roughly two-thirds of pre-conflict flow, and Trump said the US is in "total control" of the strait and escorting ships through it. Shipping-tracking data tells a different story — vessel transits fell to single digits daily over the weekend, against a pre-conflict baseline of over 100 vessels a day carrying roughly 20 million barrels. That's not a rounding gap, it's two incompatible pictures of the same chokepoint, and it matters directly for how much further oil has to run into Wednesday's Fed decision and any CBUAE read-through.

## Story 4: Gold retreats as yields climb into Fed week

**Source:** Fortune (gold price tracker, via search) | https://fortune.com/article/current-price-of-gold-09-01-2026/

Gold fell 1.25% Monday to $4,284.48/oz, its third straight weekly loss after peaking near $4,380 on Friday, as the rising 10-year yield and a firmer dollar ahead of the Fed decision pulled safe-haven demand away from the metal even with Hormuz and Red Sea risk both live. Gold's 2026 run has still been extraordinary — it cleared its inflation-adjusted 1980 high of roughly $850 (nominal terms) back on 7 September and touched an all-time high above $5,500 earlier in the year — so this is a pullback within a structurally strong year, not a trend change, but it's a reminder that even genuine geopolitical risk premium can lose to rate expectations in the short run.

## What this means for Orwell

Today is largely about positioning ahead of tomorrow's Fed decision, not new information in its own right — but the two genuinely fresh data points both matter. First, the hyperscaler-vs-chipmaker split is a cleaner read on how the market is actually pricing AI capex risk than yesterday's "Trump rejects slowdown" framing was: it suggests professional money sees this as a chipmaker-specific repricing, not a verdict on AI demand generally, which is the more relevant distinction for anyone building on the stack rather than trading the chip names. Second, the gap between US government claims about Hormuz traffic and actual shipping data is worth tracking closely over the next few days — if the "two-thirds restored" claim is wrong, oil has further to climb; if it's right, some of the current risk premium is due to unwind, with direct implications for CBUAE-linked mortgage pricing the Comparison Network depends on. Nothing here requires action today. Tomorrow's Fed decision, taken together with whatever the Hormuz data shows by then, is the point where this week's real signal arrives.
