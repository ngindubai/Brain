## Story 1: Marvell posts record $2.74bn revenue — and drops 7.6% after hours on a margin miss

**Source:** ts2.tech / 247WallSt (via search) | https://ts2.tech/en/marvell-stock-falls-7-6-after-hours-despite-2-74-billion-revenue-record/
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]]

Marvell delivered exactly the kind of number that should have validated the 88%-beat-probability setup this brief flagged yesterday: record quarterly revenue of $2.74 billion, data-centre revenue up 46% year-on-year, and Q3 guidance that beat consensus by roughly 4%. The stock fell 7.6% in after-hours trading anyway. The reason is the one line beneath the headline: non-GAAP gross margin guidance came in about 90 basis points below the prior quarter's midpoint — a real cost, not a rounding error, at a company carrying a 58x forward P/E and a 179% year-to-date run into the print. This is the clean read on where AI-infrastructure sentiment actually sits right now: revenue growth alone no longer buys a pass, the market is pricing margin durability as the harder question, and Marvell just became this earnings season's example of a beat that wasn't good enough. Broadcom, which reports 2 September, now faces a market primed to scrutinise its own margin line just as hard.

## Story 2: Nvidia's beat closes out a real session — Nasdaq +1.57%, S&P +0.72%, Nvidia itself +8.74%

**Source:** CNBC (via search) | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/26/stock-market-today-live-updates.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Priorities]]

Thursday's full close confirms what Wednesday's after-hours pop only implied: the Nasdaq Composite finished up 1.57% to 26,541.35, the S&P 500 rose 0.72% to 7,730.99, and the Dow added 105.56 points (0.2%) to 53,569.44, with Nvidia itself closing at $227.98, up 8.74% on volume roughly double its three-month average. That's a market genuinely repricing on the 70%-FY28-growth guide, not a pre-market headline that faded by lunch. Read alongside Marvell's margin-driven stumble the same evening, the picture for the GPU compute venture's backdrop is: demand confidence at the top of the stack (Nvidia) remains intact and rewarded, but the market is starting to discriminate on execution further down the chain (Marvell, and by extension Broadcom next week) rather than riding every AI-infrastructure name on Nvidia's coattails.

## Story 3: Prediction markets brace for Warsh's first Jackson Hole keynote today

**Source:** CNBC / Axios (via search) | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/27/what-warsh-will-say-and-how-the-market-will-react-according-to-prediction-markets.html | https://www.axios.com/2026/08/27/warsh-guidance-jackson-hole
**Related vault notes:** [[Priorities]]

New detail since yesterday's preview: Kalshi prices only an 8% chance Warsh says "rate cut" in his 10am ET address, and a CNBC survey found 45% of respondents don't expect him to expand on the rate outlook at all — Warsh told reporters in July his remarks would focus on long-term structural questions, not near-term guidance. Fed funds futures now price a 66% chance of a September hold against a 34% chance of a hike — a real repricing toward hawkish risk, not just softer hold conviction, and notably no cut probability of any size showing up in the mix. The read-through both ways carries real force: hawkish framing hits rate-sensitive equities and long bonds fast; a dovish tone (unlikely per positioning) would hit the dollar. Neutral, which is what Warsh has signalled he intends, is priced in rather than safe — a genuinely neutral speech still moves markets because "neutral" itself wasn't universally expected.

## Story 4: Oil holds its slide as Iran and Oman work toward a permanent Hormuz administration

**Source:** CNBC (via search) | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/24/oil-price-today-wti-brent-us-sanctions-iran.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Comparison Network]]

WTI is trading in the low $80s and Brent below $88, both still pressured by this week's Hormuz revenue-sharing mechanics. Trump added a specific claim overnight: 10 million barrels passed through the strait on Tuesday, and mines in the waterway have been cleared. Iran and Oman are now working toward a genuinely permanent structure beyond this week's temporary corridor — a mechanism covering long-term administration, information-sharing, traffic management and security services, with talks continuing over the 30-60 day window already flagged. Separately, fresh US sanctions on Iran landed less aggressively than markets had braced for, sparing Iran's trading partners from the toughest measures — itself a mild bearish-for-oil signal, since a softer sanctions regime implies less supply disruption risk priced in.

Broadcom rose 3.33% Thursday on renewed enthusiasm after OpenAI publicly validated its Broadcom-designed custom chip. ## Story 7: China's AI stocks rally on a homegrown-chip model beating DeepSeek's benchmark

**Source:** Bloomberg / CNBC (via search)
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]]

China's CSI300 and Shanghai Composite both gained (+0.5%, +0.6%) on an AI-and-hardware rally, with memory-chip maker CXMT up 5.3% and materials supplier Shengyi Technology up 10%. The standout is Z.ai (Zhipu AI), up as much as 12% after confirming it built Ox Alpha, the stealth model that topped online usage charts last weekend, and releasing GLM-5.3-Flash — a model that runs entirely on domestic semiconductors and ranks 10th on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, ahead of DeepSeek V4 Pro Max. This is a genuine data point for the "China closing the compute gap with domestic silicon" thread this brief has tracked loosely all year, not just another Chinese-stock pop.
