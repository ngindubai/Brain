## Story 3: Iran and Oman name an interim framework to reopen Hormuz, including joint mine-clearance

**Source:** gcaptain.com | https://gcaptain.com/iran-and-oman-unveil-framework-for-future-management-of-strait-of-hormuz/ | Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/25/iran-oman-discuss-temporary-shipping-lane-through-strait-of-hormuz
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Comparison Network]], [[Priorities]]

Iranian FM Abbas Araghchi and Omani FM Badr Albusaidi issued a joint statement following yesterday's Tehran meeting (flagged as developing in yesterday's Geopolitics report) naming a two-part "interim framework": a temporary joint navigation corridor through the strait, and a joint mine-clearance project to restore safe passage. Technical talks continue toward a permanent corridor, a future administration mechanism for the strait, and traffic-management/information-sharing arrangements. Context matters here — Hormuz has been effectively closed to normal traffic since February when the US and Israel attacked Iran and Tehran responded with a blockade of its own, disrupting a waterway that normally carries roughly a fifth of global oil and LNG exports; the plan implements provisions of the June 17 US-Iran memorandum of understanding that required Iran to negotiate safe passage with Oman specifically. The catch: no coordinates, no reporting mechanism, no security procedures have been published yet, and the US Joint Maritime Information Center still flags "continued risk of drifting or uncharted mines" despite Trump's earlier claim the Navy had cleared international waters. Iran also maintains that full reopening still requires the US to lift its naval counter-blockade, remove oil sanctions and unfreeze Iranian assets — so this is a framework for de-escalation, not a resolved reopening. WTI traded near $81 today, down roughly 6% over three sessions, and Brent closed below $89 yesterday, both extending the decline this news triggered.

The more durable development is the Hormuz framework: mine-clearance plus a named interim corridor is the first genuinely mechanical (not just rhetorical) step toward de-escalation in six months, and if it holds it matters more for the oil/property/CBUAE-rate chain than a single Nvidia guidance number.
