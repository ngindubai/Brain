## Story 1: Warsh goes hawkish at his first Jackson Hole keynote — 2-year yield jumps, September hike odds surge to 45.7%

**Source:** CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/28/kevin-warsh-jackson-hole-federal-reserve-inflation.html | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/28/treasury-yields-jackson-hole.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Priorities]], [[Ventures]]

Yesterday's report flagged this as the pending resolution; it landed clean. Warsh told the Jackson Hole symposium he's "impressed" with the economy's overall strength but is troubled that underlying inflation trends haven't improved, and said the Fed "has more work to do" — explicit language pointing toward hikes rather than cuts. He stopped short of committing to a specific reaction function, using the speech more as a governance statement than forward guidance, which is why Paul Krugman dismissed it as "utterly conventional." Markets didn't treat it as conventional. The 2-year Treasury yield, the maturity most sensitive to near-term policy, jumped more than 6 basis points to 4.298%, while the 30-year eased 2bp to 5.168% — a flattening move consistent with the market pricing near-term tightening risk without abandoning the long-run disinflation case. CME FedWatch's implied probability of a September hike rose from 35.4% to 45.7% in the session, and BMO's rates desk called it "a deliberately hawkish speech that will put to rest any concerns about the Fed's willingness to raise rates." For the GPU compute venture's macro backdrop, this is the more consequential data point of the week: a real, priced repricing of near-term rate risk, not a rhetorical exercise.

That's consistent with the margin-scrutiny theme this brief flagged around Marvell's earnings miss — AI-infrastructure equities are trading on a shorter, more nervous leash than the index they sit inside.

## Story 3: Prediction markets now price Anthropic's IPO as bigger than SpaceX's

**Source:** Motley Fool (via search) | https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/28/prediction-markets-ai-anthropic-unseat-spacex-ipo/
**Related vault notes:** [[Priorities]], [[Ventures]]

New today: prediction markets have shifted to expect Anthropic to unseat SpaceX as the largest IPO of 2026, a genuine change in relative positioning rather than just another valuation headline. The scale driving it: Anthropic's annualised run-rate sales went from roughly $9 billion at the end of 2025 to $65 billion by the end of July, alongside a $65 billion Series H round that valued the company at $965 billion post-money. A confidential draft S-1 was filed with the SEC back on 1 June, no share price or listing date is set yet, and reporting now puts the target IPO valuation at $2 trillion or more. Worth logging without overreading: this is prediction-market sentiment, not a confirmed valuation, and the timeline still depends on market conditions holding. But the direction of travel — from "will Anthropic IPO this year" to "will it be the biggest listing of the year" — is itself the signal.

## Story 4: Record short bets pile up against China's AI leaders even as they out-innovate on domestic chips

**Source:** Bloomberg (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-25/record-short-bets-hit-china-s-ai-model-duo-on-fierce-competition
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]]

A genuinely two-sided story worth separating from yesterday's Z.ai coverage. Short interest against Z.AI Co. and MiniMax Group has climbed to records ahead of their earnings — 20% of MiniMax's free float and 6% of Z.AI's, per S&P Global data — reflecting investor worry that China's AI rivalry is becoming a margin-destroying price war even as the technology itself advances. That's the tension: Z.ai's GLM-5.3-Flash, running entirely on domestic Chinese silicon, ranks ahead of DeepSeek V4 Pro Max on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (covered yesterday), while MiniMax posted 283% revenue growth in H1 and still saw its Hong Kong shares only manage a muted 3% gain on the news. The market is applying the same "growth alone doesn't buy a pass" logic to Chinese AI names that it applied to Marvell this week — genuine technical progress, priced skeptically on unit economics. Read together with the domestic-silicon story, this is the more complete picture of where China's AI sector actually sits: technically credible, financially unproven.

## Story 5: Oil eases toward $83 as Hormuz supply recovery firms up, but Iran insists no reopening yet

**Source:** CNBC / Al Jazeera (via search) | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/24/oil-price-today-wti-brent-us-sanctions-iran.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Comparison Network]]

Crude slipped toward the low $80s on Friday, on track to end the week lower as Gulf supply keeps normalising: exports from the Persian Gulf region have recovered to roughly two-thirds of pre-war levels, with total regional crude exports back up to 15-16 million barrels a day. That's a materially different data point from "mines cleared, talks continuing" — actual barrels are moving again at scale. Qatar's prime minister met Iran's foreign minister and other senior officials in Tehran on Thursday specifically to discuss the temporary Hormuz shipping corridor and demining plan, with Iran's side describing a return to diplomacy as "not impossible" but still insisting the strait won't fully reopen until the US meets its commitments under the June interim deal. Net read: the mechanical de-escalation this brief has tracked all week is now showing up in actual export volumes, not just diplomatic mechanics, which is the more durable bullish-for-supply, bearish-for-price signal.

## Story 6: Bitcoin prints a fresh multi-month high, then gives most of it back

**Source:** Yahoo Finance / Fortune (via search)
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]]

Bitcoin briefly touched $81,455 overnight Thursday into Friday — its highest level since 15 May — before pulling back sharply to around $77,800-79,500 through Friday morning, a bigger round-trip than the gentle pullback this brief tracked earlier in the week. Friday's open at $80,262 was still 1.5% above Thursday's, so the underlying August rally (tracking toward its strongest month since November 2024) hasn't broken, but the size of the reversal off the new high is worth flagging as a genuine change from "pause, not reversal" to "test the rally's conviction." Crypto remains unusually sensitive to the Jackson Hole "financial innovation" theme this brief noted yesterday, and Warsh's hawkish tone is a plausible proximate cause for the pullback — tighter policy expectations are generally a headwind for risk assets priced on liquidity, bitcoin included.
