## Story 1: Nvidia posts $96.2bn revenue, guides to 70% FY28 growth — the beat the market actually needed

**Source:** CNBC / S&P Global / Kiplinger (via search) | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/26/nvidia-nvda-earnings-report-q2-2027-live-updates.html | https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/2026/08/nvidia-earnings-preview-q2-2027
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Priorities]]

Nvidia's Q2 FY27 results, reported after Wednesday's close, landed well clear of the ~$91-92 billion consensus flagged in yesterday's report: $96.2 billion in revenue, up 106% year-on-year, with EPS of $2.22 against a $2.08-2.09 estimate. Edge Computing revenue (the Blackwell workstation line) grew 27.5% annually and 13% quarter-on-quarter to $7.2 billion, offset by softer consumer PC sales on higher memory and systems prices — a small crack in an otherwise dominant print. The number that matters more than the beat is the guide: management pointed to Q3 FY27 revenue of $108 billion (plus or minus 2%), above 89% year-on-year growth, and CFO Colette Kress signalled roughly 70% revenue growth for the whole of FY28. CEO Jensen Huang framed it as AI having "reached its inflection point," with the pool of companies needing large GPU clusters expanding rather than plateauing.

The market reaction is the real tell. Nvidia has fallen after each of its last four reports despite beating every time — the pattern this brief has tracked all month — because beat-and-raise stopped being sufficient; investors wanted guidance that exceeded already-elevated expectations. This time they got it: Nvidia stock extended its after-hours gain to roughly 4% at $218.77, and Nasdaq 100 futures are up about 1% in early Thursday trading on the read-through. That's a genuine regime change in sentiment, not just a good number — it eases the "AI spending boom is losing momentum" narrative that had been building through the seven-session losing streak Nvidia only snapped on Tuesday. Marvell reports today after the close and Broadcom follows on 2 September; both were trading purely on Nvidia's tone heading in, and today's guide raises the bar they now have to clear.

## Story 3: Iran-Oman Hormuz talks produce a revenue-sharing agreement — oil falls for a third session

**Source:** Bloomberg / gcaptain.com (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-26/iran-oman-agree-to-share-strait-of-hormuz-revenue-irgc-says | https://gcaptain.com/iran-oman-agree-to-share-strait-of-hormuz-revenue/
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Comparison Network]], [[Priorities]]

Beyond the "interim framework" language covered in yesterday's report, Iran's IRGC confirmed a genuine mechanical detail on Wednesday evening: an agreement on each country's share of the strait's waters and on splitting the revenue from managing them. The structure — a seven-mile-wide transit corridor, with inbound traffic routed through Iranian waters and outbound through Omani waters, military vessels excluded — was detailed by Iran's deputy foreign minister for legal affairs, Kazem Gharibabadi. Coordinated mine-clearance is expected to run over the following 30-60 days. Iran continues to caveat that this is not a reopening: Tehran says normalising traffic still requires the US to honour the lapsed 17 June memorandum of understanding — lifting the naval counter-blockade, sanctions relief, unfreezing assets.

Markets are trading the direction of travel rather than waiting for the caveats to resolve. Brent crude is trading below $88 a barrel today and WTI near $82, both down for a third consecutive session — oil has fallen roughly 6% over that stretch on renewed optimism that Hormuz will become safe enough for normal transit. This is the most price-relevant Middle East development for the GPU compute venture's backdrop this week, more durable than any single earnings print, because it's compressing the risk premium baked into crude rather than just reacting to one data point.

## Story 4: PCE comes in hot — Fed rate path muddier heading into Jackson Hole

**Source:** Forbes / Invezz (via search) | https://www.forbes.com/sites/tylerroush/2026/08/26/feds-preferred-inflation-meter-unchanged-in-july-heres-why-interest-rates-hikes-are-now-more-likely | https://invezz.com/news/2026/08/26/us-pce-in-july-higher-than-expected-at-3-7-but-core-pce-matches-forecast/
**Related vault notes:** [[Priorities]]

Yesterday's Personal Income and Outlays report showed headline PCE inflation at 3.7% year-on-year and 0.2% month-on-month, both above expectations, while core PCE matched forecast at 3.3% year-on-year. Both readings sit well above the Fed's 2% target. Reaction was split: Navy Federal's Heather Long said the US "still has an inflation problem," while LPL Financial's Jeffrey Roach argued retailers absorbing tariff costs into margins could pull core inflation below 3% as soon as October. Futures markets moved on the print — the probability of the Fed holding rates steady at the 16 September FOMC meeting eased from around 67% a week ago to roughly 60% today, a modest but real repricing toward less certainty of a hold. This is the backdrop Warsh inherits for tomorrow's keynote: a print that gives him no clean case for either an aggressive easing signal or a hawkish one.
