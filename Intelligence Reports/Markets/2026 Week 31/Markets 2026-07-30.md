## Story 1: Microsoft beats on Azure strength, Azure crosses $100bn annualised revenue

**Source:** Microsoft Source | https://news.microsoft.com/source/2026/07/29/microsoft-cloud-and-ai-strength-fuels-fourth-quarter-results-4/ ; Fortune | https://fortune.com/2026/07/29/microsoft-azure-100-billion-annual-revenue-earnings-revenue-cloud-ai/ ; Axios | https://www.axios.com/2026/07/29/microsoft-earnings-azure-2026
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Ventures]]

Microsoft's fiscal Q4 (reported after the close 29 July) came in well ahead of estimates: revenue of $90.0bn (+18% YoY) against a consensus of roughly $86-87.5bn, operating income of $40.6bn (+18%), and net income of $35.8bn (+31% GAAP), or $4.81 per share versus the ~$4.22-4.24 Street consensus flagged in yesterday's report. Microsoft Cloud revenue reached $59.3bn, up 27%, and the company said annual Azure revenue has now passed $100bn for the first time. CFO Amy Hood told analysts she expects capital expenditure growth to continue into fiscal 2027, citing "demand signals across our portfolio" — a line the market read as bullish rather than a capex-fear trigger, in contrast to how Meta's guidance landed a few hours later.

Two additional details matter. First, the results included a $3.2bn gain from Microsoft's investment in Anthropic, a reminder of how tightly Microsoft's own earnings are now levered to the AI-lab ecosystem it partly owns and increasingly competes with. Second, Microsoft said it now offers 11,000 models through Azure AI Foundry, spanning OpenAI, Anthropic and Mistral — positioning itself as the neutral model marketplace even as it builds its own frontier ambitions.

Shares rallied roughly 7% in after-hours trading. This is the cleanest of the earnings set today: revenue, margin and forward capex commentary all landed well, and it stopped the semiconductor-driven risk-off mood (SOX -4.5% on Tuesday) from carrying straight through into Big Tech earnings week.

## Story 2: Meta misses EPS despite revenue beat, capex guide climbs to $135-145bn, stock sinks after hours

**Source:** KuCoin News | https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/meta-q2-2026-earnings-miss-estimates-capex-guidance-rises-to-145-billion ; CryptoBriefing | https://cryptobriefing.com/meta-stock-sinks-earnings-capex-guidance/ ; Investing.com transcript | https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-meta-misses-eps-in-q2-2026-as-stock-sinks-after-hours-93CH-4821910
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Ventures]]

Meta's Q2 revenue of $60.8bn narrowly beat the $60.2bn consensus (advertising revenue $59.3bn vs $59.07bn expected), but EPS of $6.18 badly missed the $7.14-7.24 consensus flagged in yesterday's report — a large enough gap that it dominated the reaction despite the top-line beat. The bigger issue for the market was capex: Meta raised its full-year 2026 guidance to $135-145bn, up from $125-145bn, meaning the low end alone jumped $10bn in a single quarter. Shares dropped roughly 8% in after-hours trading. Meta had already fallen for nine straight sessions into the print, its longest losing streak on record, so today's move extends rather than reverses the recent trend.

The pattern here echoes Tuesday's Corning/KLA sell-off-on-good-numbers and reinforces a theme building through the week: investors are no longer rewarding revenue beats on their own if capex guidance keeps ratcheting up without a matching profit signal. Given Meta doesn't have an Azure-style external cloud business to point to as return on that spend, the market has less to hold onto than it did with Microsoft's numbers a few hours earlier. Worth flagging for the GPU compute venture thesis: Meta's higher capex number is more demand for GPUs and data centre capacity industry-wide, even as its own stock gets punished for spending it.

## Story 3: Fed holds at 3.50-3.75% but three dissent for a hike; market reads it as hawkish, 10-year yield through 4.6%

**Source:** CNN Business | https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/29/business/live-news/federal-reserve-interest-rate-07-29-26 ; NPR | https://www.npr.org/2026/07/29/nx-s1-5910558/federal-reserve-interest-rates-inflation ; Yahoo Finance live blog | https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/live/fed-meeting-live-federal-reserve-july-interest-rate-decision-141813444.html ; Federal Reserve press feed | https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260729a.htm ; Fortune | https://fortune.com/2026/07/29/kevin-warsh-treasury-yields-stocks-rate-hikes/
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]]

Resolving yesterday's open item: Chair Kevin Warsh's FOMC held the federal funds rate at 3.50-3.75% for a fifth straight meeting, but the vote was not clean. Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari and Dallas's Lorie Logan all dissented in favour of a 25bp hike — the most dissents pushing in one direction since September 2016. Warsh, who has stripped forward guidance out of the post-meeting statement since taking over, told reporters "I asked for a good family fight and I got one," and separately said the Fed "won't hesitate" to act against inflation and does not have a "soft inflation target."

Markets read the combination of a hold plus three hawkish dissents plus no forward guidance as net hawkish rather than dovish: the 10-year Treasury yield rose roughly 5bp to about 4.657%, pushing above the closely watched 4.6% level, while some fixed-income desks are now pricing meaningful odds of a September hike rather than a cut. The Dow fell over 1,100 points (about 2.2%) on the day, with the S&P 500 down 1.5% and Nasdaq down about 1.7%, compounded by the chip sell-off and an oil spike on Iran-related Middle East tensions (full geopolitical detail in today's [[Geopolitics]] report). The read-through for Orwell is limited directly, but a Fed that is now plausibly hike-biased into September changes the discount-rate backdrop for every long-duration AI capex story the market is currently trying to price — including Meta's guide a few hours later.

## Story 4: CBUAE mirrors the Fed, holds UAE base rate at 3.65%

**Source:** Central Bank of the UAE | https://www.centralbank.ae/media/kc0p2tuf/cbuae-maintains-the-base-rate-at-3-65-en.pdf ; Gulf News | https://gulfnews.com/business/banking/uae-keeps-base-rate-steady-after-federal-reserve-leaves-rates-unchanged-1.500578304
**Related vault notes:** [[Comparison Network]], [[Markets]]

Following through directly for Dubai: the Central Bank of the UAE held its base rate on the overnight deposit facility at 3.65%, and kept the standing credit facility rate at 50bp above that, tracking the Fed hold exactly as the dirham peg requires. This is a mechanical follow-through rather than a surprise, but it confirms the UAE funding-cost backdrop stays unchanged for now — relevant if the Fed does tilt toward a September hike given the CBUAE has essentially no independent discretion here.
