## Story 1: Oil craters and equity futures rally as the Iran strike pause holds into a second day

**Source:** Bloomberg (via search) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-26/oil-tumbles-as-us-and-iran-pause-military-strikes-markets-wrap ; CNBC pre-market wrap (via search)
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Comparison Network]], [[Geopolitics]]

Brent crude fell as much as 7.4% to below $90/bbl as the US and Iran both refrained from strikes for a second straight night, extending Saturday's pause into a genuine trend rather than a one-off lull. US equity futures followed: Dow futures up roughly 0.5%, S&P futures up 0.6%, Nasdaq 100 futures up 1.2% pre-market. Asia-Pacific markets carried the same relief through Monday's session — Nikkei +0.24%, Topix +0.60%, Kospi +0.44%, Kosdaq +1.49%, ASX 200 +0.97%. This is the clearest market confirmation yet that Friday's diplomatic signal (Oman-brokered Hormuz talks, see [[Geopolitics]] for the full military/diplomatic read) is being priced as durable rather than tactical — a two-day pause is a much stronger signal than the one quiet night the market shrugged off on Saturday.

For the GPU compute venture and the wider portfolio, a genuine unwind of the Hormuz risk premium is the single most consequential macro variable in play right now, more than any individual AI story below. Worth watching whether the rally holds once markets have had a full session to digest it, and whether Tuesday's Trump-Zelenskyy/Netanyahu meetings (see [[Geopolitics]]) introduce any fresh Iran-adjacent risk.

## Story 2: China's CXMT opens up 472% on Shanghai debut, becomes mainland China's most valuable listed company, drags Micron and SK Hynix lower

**Source:** SCMP | https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3361926/chinas-cxmt-shares-rise-472-star-market-debut-valuing-dram-maker-us489-billion ; CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/27/cxmt-china-market-debut-chipmaker-ipo.html ; TrendForce | https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/07/24/news-chinas-dram-maker-cxmt-to-debut-jul-27-ipo-to-raise-rmb-57-9b-largest-on-star-market-since-launch/
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[GitHub Library]]

ChangXin Memory Technologies opened trading on Shanghai's STAR Market this morning at 49.50 yuan against its 8.66 yuan IPO price — a 472% pop that instantly made it the most valuable company listed on the mainland Chinese market at roughly 3.31 trillion yuan ($489bn), on the back of the largest STAR Market IPO ever (RMB 57.9bn raised, surpassing SMIC's 2020 record). The market reaction wasn't confined to China: Micron fell about 5% to $862 and SK Hynix's US ADR dropped roughly 7% to $164 in early Monday trading, as investors repriced how much competitive pressure a state-backed Chinese DRAM maker with 7.6-8% global market share (up from 4.7% one quarter earlier) puts on the incumbent memory oligopoly.

This is the clearest single data point yet that China's memory self-sufficiency push is real and monetisable, distinct from the more contested picture in logic/compute chips. Read together with last week's DeepSeek funding-pause story — which suggested China is genuinely behind on frontier compute — CXMT's debut narrows that gap specifically in memory, a sector directly load-bearing for the AI buildout the GPU compute venture is watching. Worth a short note on whether Micron/SK Hynix's pricing power assumptions need revisiting given a well-capitalised Chinese competitor now has $8.6bn of fresh war chest.

## Story 3: Moonshot AI releases Kimi K3's full weights today — the largest open-weight AI model release yet, under a Modified MIT licence

**Source:** VentureBeat/CNBC (via search) | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/17/moonshot-ai-kimi-k3-model-openai-anthropic-china.html ; Hugging Face | https://huggingface.co/blog/ResterChed/kimi-k3-model-overview-mxfp4-quantization-open-wei ; TECHi | https://www.techi.com/kimi-k3-open-weights-inference-economics/
**Related vault notes:** [[Agent Org]], [[GitHub Library]]

Moonshot AI released Kimi K3's full model weights today as promised, a 2.8-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model (roughly 50bn parameters active per token across 16 of 896 experts) under a Modified MIT licence — meaning any organisation can now host and modify a model whose benchmarks put it neck-and-neck with proprietary systems from Anthropic and OpenAI, though Moonshot itself says it still trails Claude Fable 5 and GPT 5.6 Sol on overall performance. The catch flagged by multiple outlets: the weights are a 1.4TB download, a real practical barrier to casual redistribution even under a permissive licence.

This lands directly on top of last week's Nvidia/Microsoft-led coalition letter arguing against broad US restrictions on open-weight models (still an open policy question, no resolution today). A model this capable, this large, and now genuinely open puts pressure on that debate in real time — every day Kimi K3's weights are downloadable without a US policy response is itself a data point in the argument that restriction is impractical. Worth a short evaluation note in [[Agent Org]] on whether a self-hostable model at this capability tier changes any build-vs-API-access assumptions for Orwell's agent stack, independent of the geopolitics.
