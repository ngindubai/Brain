## Editor's note

Amazon and Apple gave the market two different earnings stories on the same night, and Amazon won decisively: AWS reaccelerated to its fastest growth since 2021 and Andy Jassy raised the full-year capex guide to $220bn, sending the stock up double digits after hours, while Apple's headline numbers were genuinely strong but got overshadowed by China-demand concerns and fell after hours. Layer on a Q2 GDP print that missed badly (1.5% vs 2.1% expected) even as core PCE held at 3.3%, and a chip sector that ripped higher into the print (AMD +13%, Micron +18%, SOX +8%) after Wednesday's SK Hynix-triggered rout — and the week closes with the "AI capex pays off" thesis intact for the names with a visible cloud business, on shakier ground for everyone else, against a growth backdrop that is now genuinely soft rather than just noisy.

## Story 1: Amazon crushes on AWS reacceleration, raises capex guide to $220bn, shares jump double digits after hours

**Source:** CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/30/amazon-amzn-q2-earnings-report-2026.html ; TradingKey | https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/262026432-amazon-amzn-stock-forecast-july-2026-aws-28-percent-capex-q2-earnings-tradingkey
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]], [[Ventures]]

Amazon's Q2 2026 results (reported after the close 30 July) beat across the board: net sales of $200.6bn (+20% YoY) against a $196.47bn consensus, and EPS of $5.75. The standout number is AWS: revenue of $42.2bn against $40.54bn expected, growth accelerating to 37% YoY — the unit's fastest pace since 2021 and well ahead of the 31% analysts were modelling. CEO Andy Jassy called AWS "booming," flagging that both its AI services and homegrown chip business (Trainium) have each individually crossed a $25bn annual revenue run rate.

The capex number is the one worth sitting with: Jassy raised full-year guidance to $220bn, up from the $200bn guided previously, explicitly citing higher memory costs rather than just more build-out. That is the same memory-cost dynamic that has been squeezing margins across the chip supply chain all month (SK Hynix's disappointing outlook triggered Tuesday's sell-off). Shares rose more than 8-10% in extended trading. Against yesterday's Microsoft/Meta split, this reinforces the pattern the market is now pricing hard: capex increases get rewarded when there is a visible, accelerating revenue line behind them (Azure, now AWS), and punished when there isn't (Meta).

## Story 2: Apple posts "strongest June quarter ever" by revenue but shares slip after hours on China concerns

**Source:** TradingKey | https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/262065111-apple-earnings-report-q3-2026-aapl-iphone-mac-tim-cook-china-services-tradingkey ; CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/30/apple-earnings-live-updates.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]]

Apple's fiscal Q3 revenue climbed 16.4% YoY to $109.42bn, with EPS of $2.02 (including 11 cents from US tariff refunds). iPhone revenue grew roughly 22% YoY to $54.25bn, beating the $53.86bn Street estimate, and Mac revenue jumped about 29% to $10.35bn. Tim Cook called it the company's "strongest June quarter ever," citing double-digit growth across iPhone, Mac, Services and every geographic segment. Despite that, shares fell over 4% after hours to $319.09, with weak China sales cited as the drag — a reminder that Apple's China exposure remains the market's single biggest point of doubt on the stock regardless of headline strength elsewhere.

The Apple/Amazon divergence is the cleanest read-through of the week's whole earnings run: the market is no longer rewarding revenue beats on their own — it wants either an accelerating AI-infrastructure story (Amazon, Microsoft) or a clean growth narrative with no China asterisk, and right now Apple has neither fully.

## Story 3: Chip stocks rip higher into Big Tech earnings, reversing Wednesday's rout

**Source:** CNBC (via search index) | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/30/chip-stock-rally-lam-research-micron-amd.html ; Benzinga | https://www.benzinga.com/markets/equities/26/07/60813042/stock-market-today-microsoft-stock-earnings-reaction-ai-chip-rebound
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Markets]]

A sharp reversal from Tuesday's SK Hynix-triggered semiconductor sell-off (SOX -4.5%): AMD surged 13%, Micron jumped 18%, Nvidia rose roughly 3%, Lam Research climbed 17% and SanDisk was up over 20%, with the iShares Semiconductor ETF popping around 8% on the session. The trigger was Microsoft's Wednesday earnings and Thursday's Amazon beat confirming that AI infrastructure demand is still accelerating rather than plateauing, which the market read as validating chip demand even as memory-cost inflation squeezes near-term margins. This is the clearest signal yet that Tuesday's chip weakness was a memory-cost/guidance scare rather than a demand-side one — worth tracking for the GPU compute venture thesis as a read on how quickly sentiment can swing in this sector.

## Story 4: Q2 GDP misses badly at 1.5%, core PCE holds at 3.3% — a soft-growth, sticky-inflation print

**Source:** BEA.gov | https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-advance-estimate-2nd-quarter-2026 ; CNBC | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/30/us-economy-slowed-to-1point5percent-growth-rate-in-q2-june-core-inflation-at-3point3percent.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]]

Resolving yesterday's pending item: the BEA's advance estimate put Q2 2026 real GDP growth at an annualised 1.5%, well below the 2.1% consensus and down from Q1's 2.1%, with rising imports cited as the main drag. June core PCE — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge — came in at 3.3% YoY, essentially unchanged from May's 3.4% three-year high and in line with expectations. The combination is an uncomfortable one for the Fed, which held rates Wednesday against three hawkish dissents: growth that is now genuinely soft, alongside inflation that isn't cooling fast enough to justify a near-term cut. This tightens the bind flagged in yesterday's report — a Fed that looks hike-biased into September now has weaker growth data to explain away if it wants to hold that line.

## Story 5: Microsoft's $450bn single-day gain is the largest in stock market history

**Source:** Bloomberg (via search index) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-30/microsoft-eyes-history-with-490-billion-pop-in-market-value
**Related vault notes:** [[Markets]]

A footnote to yesterday's earnings story worth flagging on its own: Microsoft's regular-session share price rose 16% on 30 July — its biggest one-day gain since October 2008 — adding roughly $450bn in market capitalisation in a single day, eclipsing Nvidia's prior record ($440bn, set on the 2025 tariff-pause rally) as the largest single-day market-value gain by any stock ever. The scale of the move underlines just how much conviction the market now has in Azure's 43% growth rate specifically, versus AI infrastructure spend generally.

## What this means for Orwell

Nothing here requires action today, but the picture is clarifying rather than clearing up: the market has now drawn a hard line between AI capex that shows up as accelerating cloud revenue (Amazon's AWS, Microsoft's Azure) and capex that doesn't yet (Meta, and to a lesser extent Apple's China-exposed growth). For the GPU compute venture thesis, Amazon's $220bn guide and the chip-sector rebound both point the same direction — underlying demand for compute is not the problem, memory-cost inflation and capital intensity are. The soft Q2 GDP print alongside still-sticky core PCE is the more genuinely new signal today: it argues for a Fed stuck between a weakening real economy and inflation that isn't cooperating, which is a harder policy spot than either a clean hike or cut case. Oil holding near $90 on sustained Hormuz risk (see [[Geopolitics]]) remains the more directly relevant number for the Dubai cost base than anything in the equity market today.
