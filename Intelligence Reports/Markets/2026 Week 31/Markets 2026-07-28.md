## Story 1: Kospi trips a circuit breaker as the CXMT-driven chip selloff goes market-wide

**Source:** Korea Times | https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/economy/20260728/bourse-operator-issues-circuit-breaker-for-kospi-on-sharp-fall ; Korea JoongAng Daily | https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/kospi-trading-halted-after-circuit-breaker-follows-sellside-sidecar/12759341 ; TradingKey | https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/more/262057458-skhynix-samsung-kioxia-dram-hbm-kospi-tradingkey ; CNBC (via search) | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/28/sk-hynix-plunges-semiconductor-selloff-deepens-samsung-softbank.html
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]], [[Comparison Network]]

The Korea Exchange triggered a Level 1 marketwide circuit breaker at roughly 10:14am local time after the Kospi plunged 8.02% intraday to 6,213.51 — by some counts the seventh or eighth such halt this year. SK Hynix fell as much as 11-13% intraday, Samsung Electronics 9-10%, and SoftBank's Tokyo listing around 5-9% (sources disagree on the precise figure). Japan's Nikkei and Taiwan's benchmark each fell close to 4%, and the MSCI Asia Pacific gauge slipped 3%. The stated cause is a combination of investor scepticism over whether AI infrastructure spending is actually generating returns, and intensifying Chinese memory competition — this is the direct next-day escalation of yesterday's CXMT Shanghai debut, now repricing the entire Asian memory and HBM supply chain rather than just the two most obviously exposed names.

For the GPU compute venture this is the single most consequential data point in today's report: SK Hynix is Nvidia's key HBM supplier, and a 20-minute market-wide trading halt is a genuine volatility signal, not noise. Worth tracking whether this stabilises by Wednesday or whether it starts bleeding into US-listed chip names beyond the premarket dip noted in Story 2.

## Story 2: US futures dip on Asia contagion, but Nvidia itself decouples and trades green

**Source:** CNBC live blog (via search) | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/27/stock-market-today-live-updates.html ; Benzinga | https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/07/60572383/whats-going-on-with-nvidia-stock-tuesday-5
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]]

S&P 500 futures were down 0.3%, Nasdaq 100 futures down 0.2%, and Dow futures roughly flat (+0.05%) ahead of today's US open, tracking the Asia selloff. Notably, Nvidia itself was up 1.09% to $205.50 in premarket per Benzinga Pro data — a clean decoupling from the Asian memory and foundry names taking the brunt of Story 1's rout.

The split matters more than either number alone: the market is distinguishing between "AI compute demand" risk (Nvidia, largely unaffected today) and "memory oversupply and Chinese competition" risk (SK Hynix, Samsung, and by extension Micron), rather than treating this as a single undifferentiated AI-stock selloff. That's a more useful framing for anyone thinking about the GPU compute venture's supply-side exposure than a single "chip stocks down" headline would suggest.

## Story 3: Corning reports Q2 today as a high-implied-volatility AI-capex bellwether

**Source:** Investing.com | https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/corning-stock-outlook-40-drawdown-divided-wall-street-and-a-july-28-earnings-wildcard-93CH-4805733 ; TradingKey | https://www.tradingkey.com/news/market-movers/262056946-market-movers-glw-20260727 ; MarketBeat | https://www.marketbeat.com/earnings/reports/2026-7-28-corning-incorporated-stock/
**Related vault notes:** [[Ventures]]

Corning shares fell nearly 5% to around $139 heading into today's pre-market print, down roughly 46% from June's record closing high near $255. Options pricing implied up to a 10% post-earnings move. Consensus going in: EPS of $0.76 (up 26.7% year-on-year) and core sales guided to roughly $4.6bn (+14% YoY), driven by optical-communications demand tied to generative-AI data centre buildout. Results were not confirmed via a clean source at time of research — do not treat any beat/miss claim surfacing later today as verified without a direct check.

Corning is a genuine AI-capex bellwether through its optical fibre business, distinct from the chip-supply story dominating the rest of today's report. Today's print and the market's reaction to what multiple outlets are calling an "earnings wildcard" is worth a same-day follow-up rather than waiting for tomorrow's report, given the size of the implied move.
