## Story 1: Mistral raises $3.5bn at $24bn, Samsung leads Europe's biggest AI round

**Source:** Crunchbase News | https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/europe-record-setting-mistral-ai-raise/

Paris-based Mistral AI closed a $3.5bn Series D, nearly doubling its valuation to $24bn from $13.7bn roughly a year ago. Samsung Electronics led, with Scaleup Europe Fund (managed by EQT) and existing investor PSG Equity co-leading. Mistral has now raised $7.5bn since its 2023 founding and remains the only European frontier-AI lab valued above $20bn among private competitors — a marker of just how far the valuation gap between US and European labs has stretched, given Anthropic and OpenAI are both tracking toward the trillion-dollar range on the same calendar.

This didn't make yesterday's report because Crunchbase's RSS feed hadn't picked it up by the time that edition was filed; it's genuinely new to this report rather than a re-tread, and it's the single biggest AI funding round this report has carried in weeks. Samsung's lead is the detail worth sitting with: a hardware and memory giant leading a foundation-model round is a vertical-integration play, not passive VC allocation, and sits alongside Samsung's other AI-adjacent moves already touched on in this report's chip coverage. ## Story 2: AMD talks up a $2-3 trillion AI total addressable market at Citi's Global TMT conference

**Source:** Investing.com | https://uk.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/amd-shares-surge-as-chipmaker-eyes-2-trillion-ai-market-4861063

AMD shares jumped 6-7% on Tuesday after management used Citi's Global TMT Conference to lift its long-run AI total addressable market estimate to $2-3 trillion by 2030, up from roughly $2 trillion previously. The framing shift is notable: AMD management said inference — running trained models rather than training them — has become "the majority driver of AI computing" over the past twelve months, a claim that reframes AMD's MI450 GPU and server-CPU roadmap around a different growth curve than the training-bound story Nvidia has dominated. AMD is guiding to $70bn in data-centre revenue by 2027 off that thesis.

This is a repositioning claim rather than a hard data print, and it lands the same week Micron and Broadcom are also mid-cycle on AI-driven demand (Micron's forward P/E of 6 against $18bn in customer cash deposits and $100bn in contracted minimum revenue, reported earlier this week, is the more concrete number in the sector right now). Read together, the chip complex is pricing in a multi-year AI infrastructure cycle rather than a one-off spike — relevant background for the GPU compute futures venture even though that specific thread had no movement today.
