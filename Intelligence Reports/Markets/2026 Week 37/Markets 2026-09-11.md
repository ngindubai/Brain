## Story 2: Nvidia hit with DOJ antitrust probe into its $17-20bn Groq licensing deal

**Source:** Bloomberg (via search aggregation) | https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-10/doj-probes-nvidia-s-license-deal-with-groq-on-antitrust-concerns ; Axios | https://www.axios.com/2026/09/10/doj-nvidia-groq-antitrust

The Department of Justice is investigating whether Nvidia structured its roughly $17-20bn non-exclusive licensing deal with Groq — agreed last December, and which came bundled with Nvidia hiring Groq founder Jonathan Ross and other executives — as a de facto acquisition designed to dodge antitrust review. The DOJ has sent Nvidia a formal information request; Nvidia denies wrongdoing, framing the arrangement as "the American system working as designed." The likelier outcome, per the coverage, is a fine rather than the deal being unwound, but it's a new and direct regulatory thread on the company that dominates AI compute — worth tracking for the GPU compute venture as a precedent-setting test of how far chip-licensing structures can go before triggering merger review.

## Story 3: Micron falls on premarket weakness plus a fresh Netlist patent action

Micron fell roughly 3% in today's premarket trading, tracking the broader chip-sector pullback (rising bond yields, elevated oil), but layered with company-specific news: Netlist has filed new ITC and federal-court patent actions against Micron, seeking exclusion orders that could block key DDR5 memory products from US import and sale. That's a genuine legal overhang on top of the macro selloff, distinct from yesterday's largely macro-only chip weakness. Analyst consensus is still Buy with an average target around $1,522; next earnings catalyst is 30 September. Memory supply is a direct input to the GPU compute venture's cost stack.

## Story 4: Broadcom drags on $100bn AI debt-financing reports and continued Marvell/Google pressure

**Source:** Search aggregation of AVGO quote/news coverage | https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AVGO/

Broadcom shares fell today despite a strong 2 September Q3 print ($29.6bn revenue, raised full-year AI semiconductor guidance). Two overhangs are doing the damage: reports that Broadcom is pursuing debt financing as large as $100bn to back AI chip commitments to Anthropic and others, after a BofA note flagged a $370bn modelled ceiling on its AI-financing platform by 2029; and continued fallout from Marvell's custom-silicon deal with Google (roughly $12bn in warrants, up to $120bn of implied silicon revenue through FY2033), which chips away at Broadcom's position as Google's dominant custom-chip partner. The Marvell/Google deal itself is a mid-August story, not new today — today's story is the price still absorbing it alongside the financing report. Relevant to the GPU compute venture on both the debt-financing-for-AI-infra angle and the custom-silicon competitive landscape.
