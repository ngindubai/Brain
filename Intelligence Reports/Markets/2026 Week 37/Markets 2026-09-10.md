## Story 1: Brent tops $100/bbl as the Hormuz tanker war escalates to direct hits on US hardware

**Source:** CBS News live updates | https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-us-tankers-attacks-strikes-strait-of-hormuz-oil-price/ and Al Jazeera | https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/9/us-destroys-five-iranian-tankers-iran-retaliates-with-attacks-on-jordan-base

Brent crude broke above $100 a barrel this week for the first time since late May, and the escalation behind it is real rather than sentiment: the US struck three Iranian oil tankers on 5 September and five more on 8-9 September, Iran retaliated by hitting ten ships near Hormuz and firing on the US-operated Al-Azraq base in Jordan, and by CBS's live blog (timestamped 9:43pm ET, 9 September) roughly eight US F-15s took light damage and one A-10 lost a wing. Traffic through the Strait has fallen to its lowest level since May. The full military and diplomatic picture belongs in today's [[Geopolitics]] report; the Markets read is narrower but sharper — this has stopped being a background risk line and is now the direct driver of this week's equity weakness (VIX up 5.47% into Wednesday's close) and a live input into next Wednesday's Fed decision, since a sustained oil shock cuts against any case for a rate hold.

For the GPU compute futures venture, an oil price this elevated alongside an AI infrastructure buildout that is itself power- and capital-intensive is worth tracking as a cost input on the compute-financing side, even though there is no direct mechanical link yet.

## Story 5: BRICS finance ministers wrap their Mumbai track today — de-dollarisation is the quiet macro backdrop

**Source:** NewsOnAir | https://newsonair.gov.in/final-brics-finance-ministers-and-central-bank-governors-meeting-kicks-off-in-mumbai/ and background reporting on BRICS local-currency trade

BRICS finance ministers and central bank governors close their two-day Mumbai meeting today, the last preparatory session before Saturday's leaders' summit in New Delhi (full geopolitical detail in today's [[Geopolitics]]). The market-relevant thread running under the summit theatre: BRICS bloc members reportedly settled 67% of intra-bloc trade in national currencies in 2025, with some estimates for BRICS+ settlement now at 85-90%, and a 100-unit cross-border settlement pilot ("The Unit") already trialled since late October 2025 — though India's own commerce minister has publicly ruled out backing a shared BRICS currency. None of this moves markets today specifically, but it's the slow-burn structural story sitting underneath a summit that will otherwise be covered as diplomatic theatre: a genuine, gradual erosion of dollar-invoicing share among a bloc that includes two of the world's top three oil consumers.
