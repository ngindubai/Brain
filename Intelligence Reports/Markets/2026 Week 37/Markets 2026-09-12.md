## Story 1: August CPI confirmed, headline steady at 3.4%, core cools, hike odds barely move

**Source:** Benzinga, BLS, CNBC (via search) | https://www.benzinga.com/markets/economic-data/26/09/61736703/august-inflation-report-2026 | https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm

The confirmed number: CPI rose 0.4% month-on-month in August, putting the annual rate at 3.4%, in line with estimates and unchanged from July. Core CPI rose 0.3% on the month, slightly hotter than forecast, but the annual core rate actually eased to 2.4% from 2.5%, the lowest reading since March 2021. Gasoline (+3.9%) did over a third of the monthly work. This is the number that was still unconfirmed when yesterday's report went out, and it lands as a mixed signal rather than a clean resolution: headline inflation is stuck well above target, but the core disinflation trend that Fed Chair Warsh said at Jackson Hole he wanted to see "meaningfully improve" did, on this reading, improve. Markets did not read it as dovish enough to shift the hike call, CME FedWatch odds for a 16 September 25bp hike sit around 62-69%, barely moved from yesterday's above-70%, and the 10-year Treasury yield finished Friday at 4.96%, effectively flat against yesterday's 4.95% and still on the cusp of 5% amid a broader global bond selloff. For the GPU compute venture and any options/futures thinking: a Fed that hikes into a cooling core print is an unusual and higher-uncertainty setup, it raises the odds of a policy error in either direction, which is exactly the kind of macro noise that widens implied vol on rate-sensitive and AI-capex-sensitive names heading into the FOMC. Nothing to action yet, but 16 September is now a hard date to watch closely.

## Story 3: Oil retreats from highs as Iran signals Gulf talks over Hormuz

**Source:** CNBC (via search) | https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/oil-price-today-iran-brent-wti-trump.html

Brent settled Friday down 2.8% to $104.61 a barrel; WTI fell 2.4% to $100.05. The trigger was Iranian state media confirming Tehran will meet Gulf states in Oman to discuss the Strait of Hormuz, the first concrete diplomatic signal since the tanker-war tension that pushed oil above $107 earlier in the week. Despite Friday's pullback, both benchmarks still posted sharp weekly gains: Brent +8.7% week-on-week, WTI +9.4%, both still comfortably above the $100 mark that took four sessions to reclaim. This is a genuine, if fragile, de-escalation signal rather than a reversal, a single mooted meeting is not a resolved crisis, and oil giving back less than 3% after a near-9% weekly run is not a market convinced the risk has gone away. See today's [[Geopolitics]] report for the Saudi pipeline story that complicates this further. For Orwell there's no direct exposure, but it is worth flagging as background: sustained sub-$100 oil would ease one of the macro tailwinds currently supporting the Fed's hawkish case, while renewed Hormuz escalation would do the opposite and complicate the rate picture further.
